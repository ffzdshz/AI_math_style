## Proposition 7.19
**Statement:** If four numbers be proportional, the number produced from the first and fourth will be equal to the number produced from the second and third; and, if the number produced from the first and fourth be equal to that produced from the second and third, the four numbers will be proportional.

**Step 1.** Let A, B, C, D be the four numbers in their given order. Form E = AD, F = BC, and G = BD, with the left-hand factor multiplying the right-hand factor in each product. These products are understood as repeated additions. [D7.15] Our path is to compare both E and F with the auxiliary number G, avoiding any interchange of factors.

**Step 2.** Since A and B multiply the same number D to produce E and G, respectively,
A : B = E : G.
Here 7.18 applies directly because the common factor D is the number multiplied. [7.18]

**Step 3.** Since B multiplies C and D to produce F and G, respectively,
C : D = F : G.
Here 7.17 applies directly because the common factor B is the multiplier. [7.17]

**Step 4.** First suppose that the four numbers are proportional, so that A : B = C : D. [D7.20] Combining this hypothesis with Steps 2 and 3, ratios equal to the same ratio are equal to one another; hence
E : G = F : G. [5.11]

**Step 5.** Things having the same ratio to the same thing are equal. Therefore E = F, that is, AD = BC: the product of the first and fourth equals the product of the second and third. [5.9]

**Step 6.** Conversely, suppose that AD = BC, so E = F. Equal quantities have the same ratio to the same quantity; therefore
E : G = F : G. [5.7]

**Step 7.** Combining Step 6 with the identities established in Steps 2 and 3 gives A : B = C : D. [5.11] Thus the four numbers are proportional. [D7.20] Both implications are proved.
