## Proposition 7.1
**Statement:** Two unequal numbers being set out, and the less being continually subtracted in turn from the greater, if the number which is left never measures the one before it until an unit is left, the original numbers will be prime to one another.

**Step 1.** Let A and B be the unequal numbers, with A greater than B. Consider the prescribed succession of subtractions, which by hypothesis reaches a unit u without an earlier remainder measuring its predecessor. We take the direct route through the definition of numbers prime to one another: we will exclude any common measure that is a number. [Hypothesis; D7.12]

**Step 2.** Suppose, for a contradiction, that a number d measures both A and B. Thus each of A and B consists of a whole collection of copies of d. [D7.5]

**Step 3.** Consider any individual subtraction in the prescribed process, and suppose that d measures both the greater quantity X and the lesser quantity Y. Write each as a collection of whole copies of d. The collection making X must contain more copies: equally many copies would give equal totals by repeated addition of equals, and fewer copies would give a smaller total. Remove from the collection making X exactly as many copies as make Y. By subtraction of equals, the copies left have total X − Y. Since X is greater than Y, at least one copy remains. Consequently d measures the remainder X − Y. [D7.5; CN2; CN5; CN3]

**Step 4.** Initially d measures both numbers by Step 2. After each subtraction, it still measures the unchanged quantity and, by Step 3, also the remainder. Interchanging their roles does not alter this fact. Applying Step 3 successively through the given process therefore shows that d measures its final remainder, the unit u. [Hypothesis; Steps 2–3]

**Step 5.** But d, being a number, is a multitude of units, whereas u is one unit; hence d is greater than u. A positive collection of whole copies of d is either equal to d or greater than d, and therefore cannot equal u. Thus d cannot measure u, contradicting Step 4. [D7.1; D7.2; CN5; D7.5]

**Step 6.** Therefore no number measures both A and B. The unit does measure each, since every number is composed of units. Hence A and B have an unit alone as a common measure, and so the original numbers are prime to one another. [D7.2; D7.12]
