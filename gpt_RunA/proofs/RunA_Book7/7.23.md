## Proposition 7.23
**Statement:** If two number be prime to one another, the number which measures the one of them will be prime to the remaining number.

**Step 1.** Let A and B be numbers prime to one another, and let C measure A. Thus the unit alone measures A and B in common. We use this defining common-measure criterion directly, rather than passing through proportions. [D7.12]

**Step 2.** Suppose that C and B are not prime to one another. Then there is a common measure D other than the unit: D measures both C and B. [D7.12]

**Step 3.** Since C measures A, A consists of an exact finite number of copies of C. Since D measures C, each such copy consists of an exact finite number of copies of D. Replacing every copy of C by its equal sum of copies of D, and adding these equal sums, expresses A as an exact sum of copies of D. Hence D measures A. If either measurement is an equality, the corresponding sum simply has one copy. [D7.5, CN2]

**Step 4.** Consequently D measures both A and B, although D is not the unit. This contradicts their being prime to one another. Therefore C and B are prime to one another, as required. [D7.12]
