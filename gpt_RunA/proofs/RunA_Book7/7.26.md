## Proposition 7.26
**Statement:** If two numbers be prime to two numbers, both to each, their products also will be prime to one another.

**Step 1.** Let A and B each be prime to each of C and D, as given. Let E be the product of A and B, and F the product of C and D (D7.15). We must prove that E and F are prime to one another.

**Step 2.** Since A and B are both prime to C, their product E is prime to C (7.24). The direct path is to apply 7.24 repeatedly, since it combines two factors prime to a fixed number into their product.

**Step 3.** Likewise, since A and B are both prime to D, their product E is prime to D (7.24).

**Step 4.** Being prime to one another is symmetric: the common measures are unchanged when the two numbers are interchanged (D7.12). Thus C and D are both prime to E, by Steps 2 and 3.

**Step 5.** Therefore the product F of C and D is prime to E (7.24). By the same symmetry (D7.12), E and F are prime to one another. Hence the products of the two given pairs are prime to one another, as required.
