## Proposition 7.11
**Statement:** If, as whole is to whole, so is a number subtracted to a number subtracted, the remainder will also be to the remainder as whole to whole.

**Step 1.** Let A and B be the whole numbers, and let C and D be subtracted from them respectively, with A:B = C:D. Write R = A − C and S = B − D for the positive remainders. By D7.20, the hypothesis expresses the same multiple, the same part, or the same parts relation. We choose the direct numerical route: apply 7.7 to the part case and 7.8 to the parts case, reducing the multiple case to the part case.

**Step 2.** If A = B, then C = D by the given proportion and D7.20. Subtracting equals from equals gives R = S by CN3. Hence R:S = A:B by D7.20. We may therefore consider the unequal cases.

**Step 3.** Suppose A is the same part of B that C is of D. By 7.7, the remainder R is the same part of the remainder S that A is of B. Therefore R:S = A:B by D7.20.

**Step 4.** Suppose A is the same parts of B that C is of D. By 7.8, the remainder R is the same parts of the remainder S that A is of B. Therefore R:S = A:B by D7.20.

**Step 5.** Suppose A is the same multiple of B that C is of D. Then B measures A as many times as D measures C; thus B is the same part of A that D is of C (D7.3, D7.5). Apply 7.7 to these whole numbers B and A, subtracting D and C respectively. It follows that S is the same part of R that B is of A. Consequently R is the same multiple of S that A is of B (D7.3, D7.5). Hence R:S = A:B by D7.20.

**Step 6.** The cases supplied by D7.20 have all been established. Therefore (A − C):(B − D) = A:B: the remainder is to the remainder as whole to whole.
