## Proposition 7.22
**Statement:** The least numbers of those which have the same ratio with them are prime to one another.

**Step 1.** Let A and B be the least numbers among those having their ratio. Suppose they are not prime to one another. Then they have a common measure C other than the unit. [Hypothesis; D7.12]

**Step 2.** Let a count how many times C measures A, and let b count how many times C measures B. Thus a multiplying C produces A, and b multiplying C produces B. [D7.15]

**Step 3.** Since a and b multiply the same number C, the products A and B have the same ratio as the multipliers: A:B = a:b. [7.18] Path choice: the common-multiplicand proposition applies directly to these counts; any common measure suffices, so finding the greatest common measure is unnecessary.

**Step 4.** Since C is a number other than the unit, each copy of C contains more than one unit. Selecting one unit from each of the a copies composing A gives a proper part consisting of a units; hence a < A. Likewise, selecting one unit from each of the b copies composing B gives b < B. [D7.2; CN5]

**Step 5.** Therefore a and b are respectively smaller than A and B but have the same ratio, contrary to the assumed leastness of A and B. Consequently A and B have no common measure other than the unit, and so are prime to one another. [Hypothesis; Step 3; Step 4; D7.12]
