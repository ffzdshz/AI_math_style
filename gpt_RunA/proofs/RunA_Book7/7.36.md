## Proposition 7.36
**Statement:** Given three numbers, to find the least number which they measure.

**Step 1.** Let A, B, C be the three given numbers. By 7.34, find D, the least number measured by A and B. Thus A and B both measure D. Our path is to apply the two-number construction successively, since it applies directly to the given data. [7.34]

**Step 2.** Again by 7.34, find E, the least number measured by D and C. Thus D and C both measure E. [7.34]

**Step 3.** A and B also measure E. Indeed, E consists of copies of D, and each copy of D consists of copies of A. Replacing each copy of D by this equal sum shows that E consists of copies of A. The same reasoning applies to B. Consequently A, B, C all measure E. This argument includes a single copy whenever two of the numbers concerned are equal. [D7.5, D7.15, CN2; Steps 1–2]

**Step 4.** Let N be any number measured by A, B, C. Since A and B measure N, their least common multiple D also measures N, by 7.35. Therefore D and C both measure N. [7.35; Step 1]

**Step 5.** Since E is the least number measured by D and C, and N is measured by both, E cannot be greater than N. Thus no number smaller than E is measured by all three given numbers. Together with Step 3, this proves that E is the required least number which A, B, C measure. [7.34; Steps 2–4]
