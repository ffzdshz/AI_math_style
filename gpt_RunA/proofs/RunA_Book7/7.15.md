## Proposition 7.15
**Statement:** If an unit measure any number, and another number measure any other number the same number of times, alternately also, the unit will measure the third number the same number of times that the second measures the fourth.

**Step 1.** Let e be the unit, and let A, B, C be the second, third, and fourth terms respectively. Suppose e measures A exactly r times and B measures C exactly r times. Thus A consists of r units, while C consists of r groups each equal to B. We take the direct path of regrouping units, without treating the unit as a number. (Hypothesis; D7.1, D7.2.)

**Step 2.** Let B consist of s units; thus e measures B exactly s times. In each of the r groups composing C, list these s units in order. This gives r rows, each containing s units; the rows merely record the unit-decompositions of the given groups. (D7.2.)

**Step 3.** For each position in a row, collect the unit occupying that position from every row. Each resulting column contains exactly r units, and therefore equals A: its units equal the corresponding units of A, so their sums are equal. There are exactly s such columns. (D7.2; CN2, repeatedly applied.)

**Step 4.** Every unit of C belongs to exactly one column, and every unit in a column belongs to C. Consequently the columns together coincide with the original collection of units composing C, and their total equals C. Since each column equals A, C is therefore the sum of exactly s copies of A. (CN4; CN2.)

**Step 5.** Hence A measures C exactly s times, while e measures B exactly s times. Thus, alternately, the unit measures the third term the same number of times that the second measures the fourth, as required. (D7.2, D7.3; Steps 2 and 4.)
