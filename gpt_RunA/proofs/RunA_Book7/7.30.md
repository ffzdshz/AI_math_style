## Proposition 7.30
**Statement:** If two numbers by multiplying one another make some number, and any prime number measure the product, it will also measure one of the original numbers.

**Step 1.** Let A and B multiply one another to produce C, and let a prime number p measure C. Suppose, for a contradiction, that p measures neither A nor B. [Hypotheses; assumption for contradiction.]

**Step 2.** Since p is prime and does not measure A, it is prime to A; likewise, it is prime to B. Thus A and B are each prime to p, since having only the unit as a common measure is a symmetric relation. [7.29; D7.12.]

**Step 3.** Therefore the product C of A and B is also prime to p. We choose the product-coprimality route because the conclusions of Step 2 give exactly the hypotheses of 7.24, without requiring a proportion construction. Consequently, the unit alone is a common measure of C and p. [7.24; D7.12.]

**Step 4.** But p measures C by hypothesis and measures itself once. Hence p is a common measure of C and p, and, being a number—a multitude of units—it is not the unit. This contradicts Step 3. The supposition that p measures neither factor is therefore false: p measures A or B, as required. [Hypothesis; D7.2; D7.12.]
