## Proposition 7.13
**Statement:** If four numbers be proportional, they will also be proportional alternately.

**Step 1.** Let A, B, C, D be proportional numbers, so that A:B = C:D. By D7.20, A is the same multiple, the same part, or the same parts of B that C is of D. We use the arithmetic results 7.9 and 7.10 directly, distinguishing precisely these cases rather than transferring the question to magnitudes. [D7.20]

**Step 2.** Suppose A is the same part of B that C is of D. By 7.9, alternately, whatever part or parts A is of C, B is the same part or parts of D. Therefore A:C = B:D. Here 7.9 is the directly applicable result for the single-part case. [7.9, D7.20]

**Step 3.** Suppose A is the same parts of B that C is of D. By 7.10, alternately, whatever parts or part A is of C, B is the same parts or part of D. Therefore again A:C = B:D. [7.10, D7.20]

**Step 4.** Suppose A is the same multiple of B that C is of D. If this is the onefold case, then A = B and C = D, so A:C = B:D by substitution. Otherwise B measures A as many times as D measures C; thus B is the same part of A that D is of C. Applying 7.9 to B, A, D, C gives B:D = A:C, which is the required equality read in the opposite order. [D7.5, D7.3, 7.9, D7.20]

**Step 5.** The cases in D7.20 are exhausted, and in every case A:C = B:D. This compares antecedent with antecedent and consequent with consequent, so the four numbers are proportional alternately. [D7.20, D5.12]
