## Proposition 7.7
**Statement:** If a number be that part of a number, which a number subtracted is of a number subtracted, the remainder will also be the same part of the remainder that the whole is of the whole.

**Step 1.** Let A be a part of B, and let C and D be subtracted from A and B respectively, where C is the same part of D that A is of B. Write R = A − C and S = B − D. By the meaning of the same part, B consists of k copies of A and D consists of k copies of C, for the same count k greater than one. We use this measuring count directly, since the hypothesis supplies it without any conversion to ratios. [D7.3]

**Step 2.** Each copy of A consists of a copy of C together with a copy of R. Let T consist of k copies of R. Adding these decompositions over the k copies of A, the whole B equals the k copies of C together with T. The k copies of C constitute D; therefore B = D + T. [CN2]

**Step 3.** Subtract D from both sides of B = D + T. The remainders are equal, so S = T. Thus S consists of k copies of R. Since k is greater than one, R is less than S. [CN3, CN5]

**Step 4.** Consequently R measures S exactly as many times as A measures B. Therefore the remainder R is the same part of the remainder S that the whole A is of the whole B, as required. [D7.3]
