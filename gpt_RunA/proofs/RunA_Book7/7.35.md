## Proposition 7.35
**Statement:** If two numbers measure any number, the least number measured by them will also measure the same.

**Step 1.** Let A and B measure N. By 7.34, take L to be the least number measured by both A and B. Since N is itself measured by both, L is not greater than N. **Path choice:** use the leastness of L directly through repeated subtraction, without introducing proportional numbers. [7.34]

**Step 2.** Starting with N, subtract L repeatedly whenever the remaining quantity is at least L. A number is a finite multitude of units, and each subtraction removes a positive collection of those units. Thus this process terminates, either with nothing remaining or with a nonempty remainder R less than L. This constructs the remainder directly; no division theorem is assumed. [D7.2; CN5]

**Step 3.** Every nonempty remainder in this process is measured by both A and B. Indeed, initially N consists of whole copies of A, and L also consists of whole copies of A. Whenever L is subtracted, cancel from the current grouping exactly the copies of A making up L; because the current quantity is at least L, these copies are available. The difference therefore consists of whole copies of A, unless nothing remains. The same argument, grouping separately into copies of B, applies to B. Repeating this reasoning at each subtraction proves the assertion. [D7.15; D7.5; CN3; CN5]

**Step 4.** If the terminal remainder R were nonempty, Step 3 would make it a number measured by both A and B, while Step 2 gives R less than L. This contradicts the leastness of L established in Step 1. Therefore nothing remains when the subtraction process ends. [7.34; Steps 1–3]

**Step 5.** Adding back the copies of L removed in the process reconstructs N exactly. Hence N consists of whole copies of L, so L measures N. Therefore, if two numbers measure any number, the least number measured by them also measures that same number. [CN2; D7.15; D7.5]
