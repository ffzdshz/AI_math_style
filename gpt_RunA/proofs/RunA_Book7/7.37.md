## Proposition 7.37
**Statement:** If a number be measured by any number, the number which is measured will have a part called by the same name as the measuring number.

**Step 1.** Let N be measured by M, and let u be a unit. If N equals M, then N consists of as many equal unit parts as there are units in M; a unit is therefore the required part named after M. Hence suppose M is less than N. [D7.1, D7.2]

**Step 2.** Since M measures N, N is a multiple of M. Let Q consist of as many units as there are repetitions of M in N. Since M is less than N, these repetitions are more than one, so Q is a number. Thus u measures Q the same number of times that M measures N. [D7.5, D7.2]

**Step 3.** Apply 7.15 to u, Q, M, N. Alternately, u measures M the same number of times that Q measures N. This is the direct path: 7.15 interchanges the measurement counts already obtained, without requiring a passage through products. [7.15]

**Step 4.** Therefore N consists of equal copies of Q, as many as there are units in M. Since M is a number, there is more than one such copy, and Q is less than N. [D7.2, CN5] Since Q also measures N, Q is a part of N. [D7.3] Its denomination is precisely M, because it measures N as many times as there are units in M. Thus the number measured has a part called by the same name as the measuring number.
