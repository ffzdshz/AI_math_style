## Proposition 7.6
**Statement:** If a number be parts of a number, and another be the same parts of another, the sum will also be the same parts of the sum that the one is of the one.

**Step 1.** Let A be parts of B, and let C be the same parts of D. Unpack the hypothesis by taking counts r<s and equal-sized groups u and v such that A consists of r copies of u and B of s copies of u, while C consists of r copies of v and D of s copies of v. Each of u and v may itself be a number or a unit. We choose direct counting of corresponding equal parts, so that unit-sized parts require no separate case. [Hypothesis; D7.1, D7.2, D7.3, D7.4.]

**Step 2.** Pair each u-group with one v-group, and call their sum w. Every such pair equals w, since adding equal u-groups to equal v-groups gives equal sums. Moreover, w is a number, being composed of at least two units. [CN2; D7.1, D7.2.]

**Step 3.** Pairing the groups constituting A and C produces exactly r copies of w; pairing those constituting B and D produces exactly s copies of w. These regroupings leave the constituent units unchanged. Hence A+C consists of r copies of w, and B+D consists of s copies of w. In particular, w is the part of B+D that measures it s times. [D7.2; CN4; D7.3.]

**Step 4.** The number A+C does not measure B+D. For suppose that B+D consisted of k copies of A+C. By Step 3, its s copies of w would then equal k blocks of r copies of w. Different counts of the same nonempty group cannot have equal totals, since the larger collection contains the smaller as a proper part. Thus s would be exactly the count in k blocks of r groups. Grouping the s copies of u constituting B into those same blocks would make B consist of k copies of A, contrary to the hypothesis that A is parts, rather than a part, of B. [CN5; D7.2, D7.4.]

**Step 5.** Since r<s, A+C is less than B+D; and, by Step 4, it does not measure B+D. It is therefore parts of B+D. More precisely, Step 3 shows that it comprises r of the s equal parts of B+D, exactly as A comprises r of the s equal parts of B. Therefore A+C is the same parts of B+D that A is of B, as required. [CN5; D7.3, D7.4.]
