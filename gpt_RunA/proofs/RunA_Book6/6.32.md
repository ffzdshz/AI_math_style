## Proposition 6.32
**Statement:** If two triangles having two sides proportional to two sides be placed together at one angle so that their corresponding sides are also parallel, the remaining sides of the triangles will be in a straight line.

**Step 1.** Label the triangles ABC and DCE in their prescribed adjoining placement at C, with AB:AC = DC:DE, AB parallel to DC, and AC parallel to DE. In this placement CA lies inside angle BCD, and CB and CE lie on opposite sides of CD. We must prove that BC and CE form one straight line. [Given proportionality and placement.]

**Step 2.** Since AB is parallel to DC, the transversal AC gives ∠BAC = ∠ACD. Since AC is parallel to DE, the transversal CD gives ∠ACD = ∠CDE. Hence ∠BAC = ∠CDE. [1.29, CN1]

**Step 3.** The triangles ABC and DCE therefore have equal included angles and proportional sides about those angles. They are consequently equiangular, and in particular ∠ABC = ∠DCE, these angles being opposite the corresponding sides AC and DE. The direct choice is 6.6, because precisely its included-angle and two-side hypotheses have now been established. [6.6]

**Step 4.** Since AB is parallel to DC, the transversal BC makes the interior angles ABC and BCD on the same side together equal to two right angles. Thus ∠ABC + ∠BCD equals two right angles. [1.29]

**Step 5.** Add ∠BCD to the equal angles ∠ABC and ∠DCE from Step 3. Using Step 4, it follows that ∠DCE + ∠BCD equals two right angles. [CN2, CN1]

**Step 6.** At C, the straight lines CB and CE lie on opposite sides of CD and make with it the adjacent angles BCD and DCE, whose sum is two right angles. Therefore CB and CE are in a straight line. Thus the remaining sides BC and CE of the two triangles form one straight line, as required. [1.14]
