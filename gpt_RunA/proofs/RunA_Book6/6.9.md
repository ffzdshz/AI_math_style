## Proposition 6.9
**Statement:** From a given straight line to cut off a prescribed part.

**Step 1.** Let AB be the given straight line, and let the prescribed part be its nth part, where n ≥ 2. We must construct a segment which, repeated n times, equals AB; this is the required part in the sense of D5.1 and D5.2.

**Step 2.** Draw from A a straight line not lying along AB, and produce it as necessary. On it mark successive points A₁, A₂, …, Aₙ so that AA₁, A₁A₂, …, Aₙ₋₁Aₙ are all equal: each successive point is obtained by a circle centered at the preceding point with the fixed radius AA₁, taking the intersection in the forward direction [P1, P2, P3, D1.15]. Put E = A₁ and C = Aₙ. Then E lies between A and C, and AC is the sum of n copies of AE, so AC = n·AE [CN2, D5.2].

**Step 3.** Join CB. Through E draw a straight line parallel to CB, meeting AB at D, between A and B [P1, 1.31].

**Step 4.** In triangle ACB, the parallel ED cuts the sides proportionally. Hence CE:EA = BD:DA [6.2]. This is the direct path: the constructed parallel immediately supplies the required proportion, without an additional angle or similarity argument.

**Step 5.** By composition of the proportion in Step 4, (CE + EA):EA = (BD + DA):DA. Since these sums are CA and BA respectively, AC:AE = AB:AD [5.18].

**Step 6.** Apply the definition of equal ratios to this proportion, taking one copy of each antecedent and n copies of each consequent. Since AC equals n·AE by Step 2, AB must likewise equal n·AD [D5.5]. Thus AD measures AB exactly n times, and, since D lies between A and B, AD is the prescribed nth part cut off from AB [D5.1, D5.2].
