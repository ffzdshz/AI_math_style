## Proposition 6.30
**Statement:** To cut a given finite straight line in extreme and mean ratio.

**Step 1.** Let AB be the given finite straight line. By 2.11, cut AB at an interior point C so that the rectangle contained by AB and CB equals the square on AC. This rectangle–square construction is the direct route because it supplies exactly the equality needed for a three-term proportion. [2.11]

**Step 2.** By the converse of 6.17, applied with AB and CB as the extremes and AC as the mean,
AB : AC = AC : CB.
The three-line criterion applies directly because the given rectangle equals a square on the mean. [6.17]

**Step 3.** Since AC is a proper part of AB, AB > AC. In the proportion just obtained, the first magnitude therefore exceeds the third; hence the second exceeds the fourth. Thus AC > CB. [CN5, 5.14]

**Step 4.** Consequently, as the whole AB is to the greater segment AC, so is AC to the less segment CB. Therefore AB has been cut at C in extreme and mean ratio, as required. [D6.3]
