## Proposition 6.25
**Statement:** To construct one and the same figure similar to a given rectilineal figure and equal to another given rectilineal figure.

**Step 1.** Let F be the given rectilineal figure to which the required figure must be similar, and G the given rectilineal figure to which it must be equal. Choose a side a of F. Construct squares Q and R equal in area to F and G respectively, and denote their sides by p and q. [2.14]

**Step 2.** To the three straight lines p, q, a construct a fourth proportional b, so that
p : q = a : b. [6.12]

**Step 3.** On b construct a rectilineal figure H similar and similarly situated to F, with b corresponding to the side a of F. [6.18]

**Step 4.** The squares Q and R are similar: their corresponding angles are equal because all are right angles, and their sides about equal angles are proportional because each square has all its sides equal. [D1.22, P4, D5.5, D6.1] Regard them as similarly described on p and q. Thus Q and R are similar figures described on p and q, while F and H are similar and similarly described on a and b. Since p : q = a : b, it follows that
Q : R = F : H. [6.22]
The direct ratio-transfer proposition 6.22 is the natural choice here because the construction has supplied precisely its four proportional straight lines and two pairs of similar figures.

**Step 5.** Substituting Q = F and R = G into this proportion gives
F : G = F : H. [D5.5]
Magnitudes to which the same magnitude has the same ratio are equal; therefore G = H. [5.9] By Step 3, H is also similar to F. Hence the one figure H is similar to the given rectilineal figure F and equal to the other given rectilineal figure G, as required. [6.18, 5.9]
