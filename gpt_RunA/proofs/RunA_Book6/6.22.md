## Proposition 6.22
**Statement:** If four straight lines be proportional, the rectilineal figures similar and similarly described upon them will also be proportional; and, if the rectilineal figures similar and similarly described upon them be proportional, the straight lines will themselves also be proportional.

**Step 1.** Let the four straight lines be a, b, c, d, and let the rectilineal figures described upon them be F, G, H, K, respectively. Suppose F and G are similar and similarly described upon a and b, and H and K upon c and d; thus these lines are corresponding sides. We must prove that a:b = c:d if and only if F:G = H:K. [D6.1, D5.6]

**Step 2.** Construct third proportionals x and y such that a:b = b:x and c:d = d:y. [6.11] Consequently, a:x and c:y are the duplicate ratios of a:b and c:d, respectively. [D5.9] Therefore F:G = a:x and H:K = c:y. [6.20] This is our path choice: express duplicate ratios by ordinary ratios of constructed lines, using the theorem for general rectilineal figures.

**Step 3.** First suppose a:b = c:d. Since a:b = b:x and c:d = d:y, we also have b:x = d:y. [5.11] Applying ex aequali to the triples a, b, x and c, d, y gives a:x = c:y. [5.22] Together with Step 2, this gives F:G = H:K. [5.11] Thus proportional straight lines yield proportional figures.

**Step 4.** Conversely, suppose F:G = H:K. Step 2 then gives a:x = c:y. [5.11] Construct a fourth proportional e to a, b, c, so that a:b = c:e; then construct a third proportional f to c, e, so that c:e = e:f. [6.12, 6.11]

**Step 5.** Since b:x = a:b = c:e = e:f, we have b:x = e:f. [5.11] Hence, from a:b = c:e and b:x = e:f, ex aequali gives a:x = c:f. [5.22]

**Step 6.** Comparing Steps 4 and 5 gives c:f = c:y. [5.11] Magnitudes to which the same magnitude has the same ratio are equal; therefore f = y. [5.9] Substituting this equality into c:e = e:f gives c:e = e:y. [5.7, 5.11] We consequently have both c:d = d:y and c:e = e:y.

**Step 7.** Inverting c:d = d:y gives d:c = y:d; inversion preserves equality of ratios by the defining equimultiple comparisons. [D5.5] Thus d:c = y:d and c:e = e:y. These are precisely the perturbed proportions between the triples d, c, e and e, y, d. Perturbed ex aequali therefore gives d:e = e:d. [5.23] This form of ex aequali is chosen because the matching ratios occur in perturbed order.

**Step 8.** In the equality d:e = e:d, take one-fold multiples of all four terms. The definition of equal ratios requires the comparison of d with e to agree with the comparison of e with d. Neither strict inequality can satisfy this, so d = e. [D5.5] Consequently c:e = c:d, and a:b = c:e yields a:b = c:d. [5.7, 5.11] Thus proportional figures yield proportional straight lines. Both directions are proved. [D5.6]
