## Proposition 6.17
**Statement:** If three straight lines be proportional, the rectangle contained by the extremes is equal to the square on the mean; and, if the rectangle contained by the extremes be equal to the square on the mean, the three straight lines will be proportional.

**Step 1.** Let the three straight lines, in order, be a, b, c. Write R(x,y) for the rectangle contained by x and y, and S(b) for the square on b. The square on b is precisely the rectangle contained by b and b, so S(b) = R(b,b). [D1.22, D2.1] **Path choice:** apply 6.16 to the four entries a, b, b, c. Repeating the mean makes that theorem directly applicable in both directions, without an auxiliary construction.

**Step 2.** Suppose the three straight lines are proportional; that is, a:b = b:c. [D5.6] Thus the four entries a, b, b, c are proportional. By 6.16, the rectangle contained by their extremes equals the rectangle contained by their means: R(a,c) = R(b,b). By Step 1, this is R(a,c) = S(b). Therefore the rectangle contained by the extremes equals the square on the mean. [6.16, D1.22, D2.1]

**Step 3.** Conversely, suppose R(a,c) = S(b). Since S(b) is R(b,b), this says that the rectangle contained by the extremes of a, b, b, c equals the rectangle contained by their means. [D1.22, D2.1] The converse direction of 6.16 therefore gives a:b = b:c. Hence the three straight lines a, b, c are proportional. [6.16, D5.6] Both assertions are proved.
