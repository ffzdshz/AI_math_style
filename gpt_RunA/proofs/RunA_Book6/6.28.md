## Proposition 6.28
**Statement:** To a given straight line to apply a parallelogram equal to a given rectilineal figure and deficient by a parallelogrammic figure similar to a given one: thus the given rectilineal figure must not be greater than the parallelogram described on the half of the straight line and similar to the defect.

**Step 1.** Let AB be the given straight line, F the given rectilineal figure, and S the given parallelogram specifying the defect. Bisect AB at E (1.10), and on EB construct a parallelogram EBCD similar and similarly situated to S (6.18). Call this parallelogram H, and write [Z] for the area of a figure Z. By hypothesis, [F] ≤ [H].

**Step 2.** Produce CD through D, and draw through A a parallel to ED, meeting the produced line at G (P2, 1.31). The parallelograms AEDG and EBCD have equal bases AE and EB and lie between the same parallels AB and GC; therefore their areas are equal (1.36). If [F] = [H], AEDG is the required application, with EBCD as its defect. Moreover, 6.27 establishes the necessity of the stated upper bound: the half-line application is greatest among applications with the specified similar defect. It remains to treat [F] < [H].

**Step 3.** Construction choice: use 6.25, since it directly supplies both prescribed area and prescribed similarity. First construct a parallelogram J similar to H and equal to F (6.25). This will allow the excess of H over F to be represented by an actual rectilineal remainder.

**Step 4.** The adjacent sides of J corresponding to DC and DE are both shorter than those sides of H. Indeed, similarity makes the corresponding adjacent sides proportional (D6.1), and consequently both pairs compare in the same sense (5.14). If they were equal, a copy placed at the corresponding angle D would coincide with H; if greater, it would contain H. Either possibility contradicts [J] = [F] < [H] (CN4, CN5). Cut off the two shorter lengths along DC and DE and complete a copy of J inside H by parallels (1.3, 1.31). This copy has the same area as J: the triangles formed by its adjacent sides agree with the corresponding triangles of J by 1.4, and those triangles are halves of their parallelograms by 1.34 (CN2). Henceforth let J denote this copy at D.

**Step 5.** Let R be the remainder of H outside J. Since J occupies the corner D, R is a rectilineal figure (D1.19), and [R] + [F] = [H] (CN2). Also, 0 < [R] < [H] (CN5). Construct a parallelogram K similar to H and equal to R (6.25). By the same side comparison and copying construction as in Step 4, place K inside H at D, similarly situated to H. Denote it DUXV, with U on DC, V on DE, and X opposite D. Thus
[H] − [DUXV] = [F] (CN3).

**Step 6.** Join DB (P1). Since DUXV is similar and similarly situated to H and has the common angle D, it is about the same diameter DB; in particular, X lies on DB (6.26).

**Step 7.** Produce UX to meet EB at T. Produce VX in one direction to meet BC at W, and in the other direction to meet AG at L (P2). The parallelisms of the construction, together with transitivity of parallelism (1.30), make ATXL and TBWX parallelograms. Their union is the parallelogram ABWL on the whole given line AB. Thus ATXL is applied to AB with defect TBWX; it remains to establish its area and the similarity of its defect.

**Step 8.** Within H, TBWX is the parallelogram about the diameter DB opposite DUXV. Therefore TBWX is similar to H (6.24), and hence similar to the given parallelogram S (6.21).

**Step 9.** The other two parallelograms in the division of H, namely ETXV and UCWX, are the complements about the diameter DB. Consequently,
[ETXV] = [UCWX] (1.43).

**Step 10.** The parallelograms AEVL and EBWV have equal bases AE and EB and lie between the same parallels AB and LW. The equal-base theorem applies directly, giving
[AEVL] = [EBWV] (1.36).

**Step 11.** Add the equalities from Steps 9 and 10 (CN2). Using the indicated divisions of ATXL and H, and then Step 5, gives
[ATXL] = [AEVL] + [ETXV]
= [EBWV] + [UCWX]
= [H] − [DUXV]
= [F] (CN3).
Therefore ATXL is equal to the given rectilineal figure and is applied to AB deficient by TBWX, which is similar to S by Step 8. Together with the equality case in Step 2, this completes the construction under precisely the stated bound.
