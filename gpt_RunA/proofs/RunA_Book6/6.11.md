## Proposition 6.11
**Statement:** To two given straight lines to find a third proportional.

**Step 1.** Let the two given straight lines have lengths a and b. Draw two noncollinear rays from A. On the first mark B and C, in the order A, B, C, so that AB = a and BC = b; on the second mark D so that AD = b. These placements are made by transferring the given lengths and cutting them off on the rays, produced as necessary. [P1, P2, 1.2, 1.3]

**Step 2.** Join BD. Through C draw a straight line ℓ parallel to BD. [P1, 1.31]

**Step 3.** The ray of ℓ on the side of AC containing D makes with CA an angle equal to ∠ABD. [1.29] But ∠BAD and ∠ABD together are less than two right angles. [1.17] Therefore ℓ meets the ray AD at a point E on that side of AC. [P5] Moreover, E lies beyond D: if E were D, or were between A and D, the segment CE would meet BD, in the latter case by entering triangle ABD across BD. This is impossible because ℓ is parallel to BD. [D1.23]

**Step 4.** In triangle ACE, B lies on AC, D lies on AE, and BD is parallel to CE. Hence AB : BC = AD : DE. [6.2] This is the direct path: the parallel gives the required ratio of the side-segments immediately, without passing through equiangular triangles.

**Step 5.** Substituting AB = a, BC = b, and AD = b into that proportion gives a : b = b : DE; replacing magnitudes by equal magnitudes preserves the defining comparisons of their equimultiples. [D5.5] Thus the given first line, the given second line, and DE are continuously proportional. Therefore DE is the required third proportional. [D5.6]
