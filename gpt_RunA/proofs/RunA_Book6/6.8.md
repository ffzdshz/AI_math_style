## Proposition 6.8
**Statement:** If in a right-angled triangle a perpendicular be drawn from the right angle to the base, the triangles adjoining the perpendicular are similar both to the whole and to one another.

**Step 1.** Let ABC be a triangle right-angled at A, and let the given perpendicular AD meet the base BC at D. The angles BDA and ADC are right angles, so each equals the right angle BAC. [D1.10, P4]

**Step 2.** Since D lies on BC, the angles DBA and ABC coincide and are therefore equal. [CN4] The angle sums of triangles DBA and ABC are equal, each being two right angles. Subtracting their equal right angles and then their equal angles at B gives ∠BAD = ∠BCA. Thus triangles DBA and ABC are equiangular, with correspondence D ↔ A, B ↔ B, A ↔ C. [1.32, CN1, CN3]

**Step 3.** Path choice: apply 6.4 directly, since angle equalities have already been established. The sides about the equal angles of triangles DBA and ABC are proportional; hence these triangles are similar. [6.4, D6.1]

**Step 4.** Likewise, the angles DCA and BCA coincide and are equal. [CN4] The angle sums of triangles DAC and ABC are equal, each being two right angles. Subtracting their equal right angles and then their equal angles at C gives ∠DAC = ∠ABC. [1.32, CN1, CN3] Consequently, triangles DAC and ABC are equiangular, with correspondence D ↔ A, A ↔ B, C ↔ C. Their sides about the equal angles are therefore proportional, and they are similar. [6.4, D6.1]

**Step 5.** The two smaller triangles are also equiangular: ∠BDA = ∠ADC because both are right angles; ∠DBA = ∠DAC because both equal ∠ABC; and ∠BAD = ∠DCA because both equal ∠BCA. Their correspondence is therefore D ↔ D, B ↔ A, A ↔ C. [P4, CN1; Steps 2 and 4]

**Step 6.** Applying 6.4 to triangles DBA and DAC, their sides about the equal angles are proportional; hence they are similar. [6.4, D6.1] Thus the triangles adjoining the perpendicular are similar both to the whole triangle and to one another, as required.
