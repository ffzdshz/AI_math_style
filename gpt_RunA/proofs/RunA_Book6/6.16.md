## Proposition 6.16
**Statement:** If four straight lines be proportional, the rectangle contained by the extremes is equal to the rectangle contained by the means; and, if the rectangle contained by the extremes be equal to the rectangle contained by the means, the four straight lines will be proportional.

**Step 1.** Let the four given straight lines, in their stated order, be a, b, c, d. Let R denote the rectangular parallelogram contained by a and d, and S the rectangular parallelogram contained by b and c. Thus R is the rectangle contained by the extremes, and S is the rectangle contained by the means (D2.1).

**Step 2.** Since all their angles are right angles, R and S are equiangular (P4). We choose 6.14 directly because it concerns equiangular parallelograms themselves, without requiring auxiliary triangles. For these rectangles, reciprocal proportionality of the sides about their equal angles is precisely a:b = c:d: a side of R is to a side of S as the remaining side of S is to the remaining side of R (D6.2).

**Step 3.** Suppose first that the four straight lines are proportional. Then a:b = c:d (D5.6). Consequently, the sides about the equal angles of R and S are reciprocally proportional, as identified in Step 2. By 6.14, R and S are equal. Hence the rectangle contained by the extremes equals the rectangle contained by the means (D2.1).

**Step 4.** Conversely, suppose that the rectangle contained by the extremes equals the rectangle contained by the means; that is, R = S (D2.1). These parallelograms are also equiangular by Step 2. Therefore, by 6.14, their sides about the equal angles are reciprocally proportional, giving a:b = c:d. Thus the four straight lines are proportional (D5.6). Both implications are proved.
