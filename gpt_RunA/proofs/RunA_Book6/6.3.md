## Proposition 6.3
**Statement:** If an angle of a triangle be bisected and the straight line cutting the angle cut the base also, the segments of the base will have the same ratio as the remaining sides of the triangle; and, if the segments of the base have the same ratio as the remaining sides of the triangle, the straight line joined from the vertex to the point of section will bisect the angle of the triangle.

**Step 1.** Let ABC be a triangle, and let D lie between B and C. Join AD (P1). We must prove that ∠BAD = ∠DAC if and only if BD:DC = AB:AC. Path choice: use one auxiliary parallel and 6.2, which directly supplies the required ratio of segments and serves both implications.

**Step 2.** Produce BA beyond A to X (P2). Through C draw a straight line parallel to AD (1.31), and let CY be its ray on the side of AC opposite B. By 1.29, ∠ACY = ∠DAC. By 1.13, ∠XAC + ∠BAC equals two right angles. Since ∠DAC is a proper part of ∠BAC, it is smaller (CN5); consequently ∠XAC + ∠ACY is less than two right angles. Therefore AX and CY, produced if necessary, meet at E on that side of AC (P5). Thus B, A, E are collinear, A lies between B and E, and CE is parallel to AD.

**Step 3.** In triangle BEC, the straight line AD is parallel to the side EC and cuts BE and BC at A and D. Hence
BA:AE = BD:DC.
This is precisely the proportional division supplied by 6.2.

**Step 4.** Since AD is parallel to CE and B, A, E lie in that order on one straight line, the corresponding angles give ∠BAD = ∠AEC. With AC as transversal, the alternate angles give ∠DAC = ∠ACE (1.29).

**Step 5.** First suppose that AD bisects ∠BAC, so that ∠BAD = ∠DAC. The equalities in Step 4 yield ∠AEC = ∠ACE (CN1). Therefore the sides opposite these equal angles in triangle AEC are equal: AE = AC (1.6). Here 1.6 applies directly because equality of angles has already been established.

**Step 6.** Since AE = AC, the same magnitude AB has the same ratio to them: AB:AE = AB:AC (5.7). Combining this with Step 3 gives
BD:DC = AB:AC
(5.11). This proves the first implication.

**Step 7.** Conversely, suppose that BD:DC = AB:AC. Step 3 also gives BD:DC = AB:AE; hence AB:AE = AB:AC (5.11). Magnitudes to which the same magnitude has the same ratio are equal, so AE = AC (5.9).

**Step 8.** In triangle AEC, the equality AE = AC gives ∠AEC = ∠ACE (1.5). Combining this with the angle equalities in Step 4 yields ∠BAD = ∠DAC (CN1). Thus AD bisects ∠BAC. Both implications are proved.
