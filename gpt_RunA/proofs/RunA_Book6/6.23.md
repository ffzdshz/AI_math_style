## Proposition 6.23
**Statement:** Equiangular parallelograms have to one another the ratio compounded of the ratios of their sides.

**Step 1.** Let the given equiangular parallelograms be ABCD and AEFG, placed with their equal angles DAB and GAE coinciding, so that AB and AE lie along one ray and AD and AG along another. Such placement preserves their areas and side lengths (CN4). Write P and Q for their respective areas. We shall insert an intermediate parallelogram: this makes the equal-height theorem 6.1 directly applicable to both comparisons.

**Step 2.** Through E draw a straight line parallel to AD, and through D draw a straight line parallel to AB; let these meet at H (1.31). Then AEHD is a parallelogram. Denote its area by R.

**Step 3.** The parallelograms ABCD and AEHD have bases AB and AE on the same straight line, and their opposite sides CD and DH lie on the same parallel through D. They therefore have the same height. Hence
P : R = AB : AE
by 6.1.

**Step 4.** Regard AD and AG as the bases of AEHD and AEFG. These bases lie on the same straight line, while their opposite sides EH and EF lie on the same parallel through E. Thus these parallelograms also have the same height, and
R : Q = AD : AG
by 6.1.

**Step 5.** Find a fourth proportional straight line t to AD, AG, and AE, so that
AD : AG = AE : t
(6.12). Combining this with Step 4 gives
R : Q = AE : t
(5.11). Thus the three areas P, R, Q and the three straight lines AB, AE, t have their successive ratios equal.

**Step 6.** By proportion ex aequali,
P : Q = AB : t
(5.22). The ratio AB : t is compounded of AB : AE and AE : t: the sizes of these successive ratios, multiplied together, give the size of the first-to-last ratio (D6.5). Since AE : t = AD : AG, it follows that P : Q is compounded of AB : AE and AD : AG. These are the ratios of the corresponding sides about the equal angles. Therefore equiangular parallelograms have to one another the ratio compounded of the ratios of their sides.
