## Proposition 6.4
**Statement:** In equiangular triangles the sides about the equal angles are proportional, and those are corresponding sides which subtend the equal angles.

**Step 1.** Let ABC and DEF be equiangular triangles, with ∠BAC = ∠EDF, ∠ABC = ∠DEF, and ∠ACB = ∠DFE. We first prove AB : AC = DE : DF. The chosen route is a parallel cut, since 6.2 directly supplies the needed segment proportions.

**Step 2.** Interchanging the triangles if necessary, suppose AB ≥ DE; this merely interchanges the two sides of the desired equality of ratios. If AB = DE, the equal angles at A, B and D, E give AC = DF and BC = EF by 1.26. All the required proportions then follow from equality of corresponding magnitudes and D5.5. It remains to consider AB > DE.

**Step 3.** Cut off AP = DE on AB, with P between A and B [1.3]. Through P draw PQ parallel to BC, meeting AC at Q, between A and C [1.31].

**Step 4.** Since AP and AQ lie along AB and AC, ∠PAQ = ∠BAC [CN4]. Also ∠APQ = ∠ABC because PQ is parallel to BC [1.29]. Hence ∠PAQ = ∠EDF and ∠APQ = ∠DEF by the given angle equalities [CN1]. Together with AP = DE, these equalities give AQ = DF by 1.26.

**Step 5.** Because PQ is parallel to BC, it cuts the other two sides proportionally:
AP : PB = AQ : QC [6.2].
Alternating gives
AP : AQ = PB : QC [5.16].

**Step 6.** By 5.12, the ratio of the sums of these antecedents and consequents equals their common ratio:
(AP + PB) : (AQ + QC) = AP : AQ.
Since AP + PB = AB and AQ + QC = AC, this is AB : AC = AP : AQ. Substituting AP = DE and AQ = DF gives
AB : AC = DE : DF [D5.5].
Here 5.12 is the direct choice because the sums are precisely the two whole sides required.

**Step 7.** The argument in Steps 2–6 applies to any chosen pair of corresponding vertices. Applying it with (A, B, C; D, E, F) relabelled as (B, A, C; E, D, F), and then as (C, A, B; F, D, E), gives respectively
BA : BC = ED : EF,
and
CA : CB = FD : FE.
Thus the sides about every pair of equal angles are proportional [D5.6].

**Step 8.** The corresponding terms in these proportions pair AB with DE, AC with DF, and BC with EF [D5.11]. These pairs subtend, respectively, the equal angles at C and F, at B and E, and at A and D. Therefore, in equiangular triangles the sides about the equal angles are proportional, and the corresponding sides are those which subtend the equal angles.
