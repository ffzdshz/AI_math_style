## Proposition 6.26
**Statement:** If from a parallelogram there be taken away a parallelogram similar and similarly situated to the whole and having a common angle with it, it is about the same diameter with the whole.

**Step 1.** Let ABCD be the whole parallelogram and AEFG the parallelogram taken away, with vertices named in corresponding order and common angle at A. Thus E lies on AB and G lies on AD. Since the parallelograms are similar and similarly situated, AE:EF = AB:BC and ∠AEF = ∠ABC. [Hypotheses; D6.1]

**Step 2.** Join AF and AC. [P1] We use the side–angle–side criterion 6.6 directly: the proportional sides and equal included angles in Step 1 already supply its hypotheses for triangles AEF and ABC, so no auxiliary parallelogram is needed.

**Step 3.** Triangles AEF and ABC are therefore equiangular, with A corresponding to A, E to B, and F to C. Hence ∠EAF = ∠BAC. Since AE and AB are the same ray, this gives ∠BAF = ∠BAC. [6.6; Step 1]

**Step 4.** The rays AF and AC both lie inside the common angle DAB. If they were distinct, one of ∠BAF and ∠BAC would be a proper part of the other and therefore smaller, contradicting Step 3. [CN5] Thus A, F, and C are collinear. Since AEFG is contained in ABCD, its diameter AF lies on the diameter AC of the whole. Therefore the parallelogram taken away is about the same diameter with the whole, as required.
