## Proposition 6.18
**Statement:** On a given straight line to describe a rectilineal figure similar and similarly situated to a given rectilineal figure.

**Step 1.** Let AB be the given straight line, and let C₁C₂…Cₙ be the given rectilineal figure, with C₁C₂ chosen as the side corresponding to AB. Draw noncrossing internal diagonals dividing the figure into triangles, beginning with the triangle incident to C₁C₂ [P1]. We choose the angle-copying route: reproduce these triangles successively, so that proportionality follows directly from their being equiangular.

**Step 2.** Set A₁ = A and A₂ = B. Whenever a side AᵢAⱼ corresponding to a side CᵢCⱼ of one of the triangles has already been constructed, copy at Aᵢ and Aⱼ the angles at Cᵢ and Cⱼ of that triangle, placing both copied angles on the corresponding side of AᵢAⱼ [1.23]. The two original angles together are less than two right angles [1.17]; therefore the newly drawn straight lines meet on that side, at a point Aₖ [P5]. This constructs the triangle AᵢAⱼAₖ corresponding to CᵢCⱼCₖ.

**Step 3.** These two triangles have their two constructed angles equal. Since the angles of each triangle together equal two right angles, their remaining angles are also equal [1.32, CN2, CN3]. Consequently, their sides about corresponding equal angles are proportional [6.4]. Here 6.4 is the natural choice because equiangularity has already been established by the construction.

**Step 4.** Begin with the triangle on C₁C₂, whose corresponding side is AB, and continue the construction across the diagonals of the dissection. Whenever two original triangles adjoin along a diagonal, place their copies on opposite sides of the corresponding diagonal, retaining the original cyclic order of the vertices. Each attachment is possible by the construction in Step 2 [1.23, 1.17, P5]. In this way obtain the corresponding triangular division and its boundary A₁A₂…Aₙ, with A₁A₂ = AB.

**Step 5.** At each vertex, the angle of the whole rectilineal figure is the sum of the angles of the triangles incident there. Each such triangular angle equals its corresponding copied angle by Step 3. Adding these equal angles shows that the whole angles at Cᵢ and Aᵢ are equal, for every i [CN2].

**Step 6.** Fix a vertex Cᵢ. In their order through its interior angle, write the lengths of the incident sides and diagonals as e₀, e₁, …, eᵣ; thus e₀ and eᵣ are the two boundary sides at Cᵢ. Write e′₀, e′₁, …, e′ᵣ for the corresponding lengths at Aᵢ. Each successive pair belongs to a pair of corresponding equiangular triangles, so

eⱼ : eⱼ₊₁ = e′ⱼ : e′ⱼ₊₁

for every j from 0 to r − 1 [6.4]. Hence, ex aequali,

e₀ : eᵣ = e′₀ : e′ᵣ [5.22].

If only one triangle is incident at the vertex, this equality follows directly from 6.4. Thus, at every pair of corresponding equal angles, the two boundary sides are proportional.

**Step 7.** The figures C₁C₂…Cₙ and A₁A₂…Aₙ therefore have their angles severally equal and the sides about their equal angles proportional; hence they are similar [D6.1]. They are also similarly situated, because their corresponding vertices occur in the same cyclic order and their corresponding triangles were attached on matching sides of every diagonal. Since A₁A₂ is the given straight line AB, the required rectilineal figure has been described on AB.
