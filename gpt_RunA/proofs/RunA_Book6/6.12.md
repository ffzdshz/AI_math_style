## Proposition 6.12
**Statement:** To three given straight lines to find a fourth proportional.

**Step 1.** Let the three given straight lines, in their given order, be a, b, and c. We must construct a straight line x such that a:b = c:x (D5.6). We choose the parallel-cut construction: 6.2 naturally applies first because it gives the required ratio directly between segments of two sides.

**Step 2.** Draw two rays from O which do not lie in one straight line. On the first mark A and B, in the order O, A, B, so that OA = a and AB = b. On the second mark C so that OC = c. These lengths are cut off after producing the straight lines sufficiently (P1, P2, 1.3).

**Step 3.** Join AC. Through B draw a straight line ℓ parallel to AC (P1, 1.31).

**Step 4.** The ray of ℓ on the same side of OB as C makes with BO an angle equal to ∠OAC (1.29). In triangle OAC, the angles ∠AOC and ∠OAC together are less than two right angles (1.17). Therefore ℓ meets the ray OC at a point D on that side of OB (P5). Moreover, D lies beyond C: if it were at C, or between O and C, BD would meet AC, contrary to their being parallel (D1.23).

**Step 5.** In triangle OBD, A lies on OB and C lies on OD, and AC is parallel to BD. Hence the sides are cut proportionally:
OA:AB = OC:CD (6.2).

**Step 6.** Substituting the equalities constructed in Step 2 into the proportion of Step 5 gives a:b = c:CD. Thus CD is a fourth proportional to the three given straight lines, as required (D5.6).
