## Proposition 6.19
**Statement:** Similar triangles are to one another in the duplicate ratio of the corresponding sides.

**Step 1.** Let ABC and DEF be similar triangles, with A, B, C corresponding respectively to D, E, F. Choose BC and EF as an arbitrary pair of corresponding sides. Write [XYZ] for the area of triangle XYZ. By D6.1, ∠ABC = ∠DEF and AB:BC = DE:EF. By alternation (5.16),
AB:DE = BC:EF.

**Step 2.** By 6.11, construct a third proportional t to BC and EF, so that
BC:EF = EF:t.
This construction is the direct choice because duplicate ratio is defined by three proportional magnitudes (D5.9). Produce BC if necessary (P2), and mark G on the ray BC using the circle with centre B and radius t (P3). Thus BG = t and
BC:EF = EF:BG.
Join AG (P1).

**Step 3.** Combining the proportions in Steps 1 and 2 by 5.11 gives
AB:DE = EF:BG.
Thus the sides AB, BG and DE, EF are reciprocally proportional.

**Step 4.** Since the rays BG and BC coincide, ∠ABG = ∠ABC (CN4). Hence ∠ABG = ∠DEF by Step 1 and CN1. The triangles ABG and DEF therefore have an equal included angle and reciprocally proportional sides about that angle. By 6.15,
[ABG] = [DEF].

**Step 5.** Triangles ABC and ABG have the common vertex A and bases BC and BG on the same straight line, so they have the same height (D6.4). Consequently, by 6.1,
[ABC]:[ABG] = BC:BG.

**Step 6.** Since [ABG] = [DEF], the ratios [ABC]:[ABG] and [ABC]:[DEF] are equal (5.7). Combining this with Step 5 by 5.11 yields
[ABC]:[DEF] = BC:BG.

**Step 7.** Since BC:EF = EF:BG, the ratio BC:BG is the duplicate ratio of BC:EF (D5.9). Therefore [ABC]:[DEF] is the duplicate ratio of BC:EF. Since BC and EF were an arbitrary pair of corresponding sides, similar triangles are to one another in the duplicate ratio of their corresponding sides.
