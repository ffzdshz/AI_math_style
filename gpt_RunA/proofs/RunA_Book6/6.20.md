## Proposition 6.20
**Statement:** Similar polygons are divided into similar triangles, and into triangles equal in multitude and in the same ratio as the wholes, and the polygon has to the polygon a ratio duplicate of that which the corresponding side has to the corresponding  side.

**Step 1.** Let A_1A_2…A_n and B_1B_2…B_n be similar polygons, their vertices being named in corresponding order. Their corresponding angles are equal, and the sides containing those angles are proportional (D6.1). If n = 3, each polygon already consists of one triangle, and the duplicate-ratio assertion is 6.19. Hence suppose n ≥ 4.

**Step 2.** Join A_1 to A_3,…,A_{n−1}, and B_1 to B_3,…,B_{n−1} (P1). These diagonals divide the polygons into corresponding fans
T_i = triangle A_1A_iA_{i+1}, and U_i = triangle B_1B_iB_{i+1},
for i = 2,…,n−1. Thus each division contains n−2 triangles. We shall prove that T_i and U_i are similar for every i.

**Step 3.** In T_2 and U_2, the included angles at A_2 and B_2 are equal, and
A_1A_2 : A_2A_3 = B_1B_2 : B_2B_3
(D6.1). Therefore these triangles are equiangular (6.6), and their corresponding sides are proportional (6.4); hence they are similar (D6.1). This use of 6.6 is direct: an equal included angle and proportional containing sides are precisely the data already given.

**Step 4.** Suppose T_{i−1} and U_{i−1} have been proved similar, where 3 ≤ i ≤ n−1. Their angles at A_i and B_i are equal (D6.1). Subtract these equal angles from the equal polygon angles at those vertices. The remaining angles are therefore equal:
∠A_1A_iA_{i+1} = ∠B_1B_iB_{i+1}
(CN3).

**Step 5.** The preceding pair of similar triangles gives
A_1A_i : A_{i−1}A_i = B_1B_i : B_{i−1}B_i
(6.4). The similarity of the original polygons gives
A_{i−1}A_i : A_iA_{i+1} = B_{i−1}B_i : B_iB_{i+1}
(D6.1). Consequently, ex aequali,
A_1A_i : A_iA_{i+1} = B_1B_i : B_iB_{i+1}
(5.22). Together with the angle equality in Step 4, this makes T_i and U_i equiangular (6.6), with corresponding sides proportional (6.4), and therefore similar (D6.1). Starting with Step 3 and repeating this argument proves the similarity of every corresponding pair. The polygons have thus been divided into similar triangles equal in multitude.

**Step 6.** Write t_i and u_i for the areas of T_i and U_i. For each i = 2,…,n−2, the adjacent triangles T_i and T_{i+1} share the side A_1A_{i+1}, while U_i and U_{i+1} share its corresponding side B_1B_{i+1}. By 6.19, both ratios t_i : u_i and t_{i+1} : u_{i+1} are the duplicate ratio of the very same ratio
A_1A_{i+1} : B_1B_{i+1}
(D5.9). They are therefore equal; chaining these equalities gives
 t_2 : u_2 = t_3 : u_3 = … = t_{n−1} : u_{n−1}
(5.11). Using the shared diagonals allows 6.19 to apply directly, without an auxiliary proportional-line construction.

**Step 7.** When any number of antecedents have the same ratio to their respective consequents, the sum of the antecedents has that ratio to the sum of the consequents (5.12). Hence
(t_2 + … + t_{n−1}) : (u_2 + … + u_{n−1}) = t_i : u_i
for every i. The two sums are exactly the areas of the respective polygons, by the divisions in Step 2. Thus every corresponding pair of triangles is in the same ratio as the whole polygons.

**Step 8.** In particular, the ratio of the polygons equals t_2 : u_2. Since T_2 and U_2 are similar and have corresponding sides A_1A_2 and B_1B_2, this triangle ratio is the duplicate ratio of A_1A_2 : B_1B_2 (6.19). Therefore the polygon has to the polygon the duplicate ratio of that corresponding side to its corresponding side (5.11). The initial side A_1A_2 could have been any side, with B_1B_2 its correspondent. Thus the conclusion holds for any corresponding sides. The similar polygons are divided into similar triangles equal in multitude and in the same ratio as the wholes, and the wholes are in the duplicate ratio of corresponding sides, as required.
