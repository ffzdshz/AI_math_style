## Proposition 6.7
**Statement:** If two triangles have one angle equal to one angle, the sides about other angles proportional, and the remaining angles either both less or both not less than a right angle, the triangles will be equiangular and will have those angles equal, the sides about which are proportional.

**Step 1.** Let ABC and DEF be the given triangles, with ∠BAC = ∠EDF and AB:BC = DE:EF. Suppose that the remaining angles ∠ACB and ∠DFE are either both less than a right angle or both not less than a right angle. These are the hypotheses. Write R for a right angle; all right angles are equal. [P4]

**Step 2.** If ∠ABC = ∠DEF, the remaining angles are also equal, by subtracting the two pairs of equal angles from the angle sums of the triangles, and the conclusion follows. [1.32, CN3] Suppose, therefore, that ∠ABC and ∠DEF are unequal. Interchanging the triangles if necessary, assume ∠ABC > ∠DEF.

**Step 3.** At B construct a ray BG inside ∠ABC such that ∠ABG = ∠DEF. [1.23] Since ∠ABG < ∠ABC, we have ∠BAC + ∠ABG < ∠BAC + ∠ABC < 2R. [CN5, 1.17] Consequently this ray meets AC on the side of AB containing C. [P5] Because the ray lies strictly inside ∠ABC, its intersection G lies strictly between A and C.

**Step 4.** Since AG lies along AC, ∠BAG = ∠BAC = ∠EDF. [CN4, hypothesis] Together with ∠ABG = ∠DEF, this gives ∠AGB = ∠DFE by the angle sums of triangles ABG and DEF. [1.32, CN3] Thus those triangles are equiangular.

**Step 5.** Path choice: apply 6.4, since the auxiliary triangles have already been shown equiangular. It gives AB:BG = DE:EF. [6.4] Combining this with the given proportion AB:BC = DE:EF yields AB:BG = AB:BC. [5.11] Therefore BG = BC, since magnitudes to which the same magnitude has the same ratio are equal. [5.9]

**Step 6.** Triangle BGC is consequently isosceles, so ∠BGC = ∠GCB. [1.5] Since G lies between A and C, ∠GCB = ∠ACB. [CN4] The two equal base angles of BGC have sum less than 2R. [1.17] Hence each is less than R: otherwise both would be at least R and their sum would be at least 2R. [P4, CN2, CN5] In particular, ∠ACB < R.

**Step 7.** Since A, G, C lie on one straight line, ∠AGB + ∠BGC = 2R. [1.13] As ∠BGC < R, its supplementary angle ∠AGB is greater than R. [P4, CN3, CN5] But ∠AGB = ∠DFE by Step 4, whereas ∠ACB < R by Step 6. Thus one of the original remaining angles is less than a right angle and the other is greater than a right angle, contradicting either permitted alternative in the hypothesis.

**Step 8.** Therefore ∠ABC = ∠DEF. Together with the given equality ∠BAC = ∠EDF, the angle sums now give ∠ACB = ∠DFE. [1.32, CN3] Hence ABC and DEF are equiangular, and in particular the angles ABC and DEF, about which the given proportional sides lie, are equal. This proves the proposition.
