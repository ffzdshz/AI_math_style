## Proposition 6.5
**Statement:** If two triangles have their sides proportional, the triangles will be equiangular and will have those angles equal which the corresponding sides subtend.

**Step 1.** Let ABC and DEF be triangles whose corresponding sides are AB and DE, BC and EF, and CA and FD. By hypothesis their sides are proportional; thus AB:BC = DE:EF and BC:CA = EF:FD. We must prove that their corresponding angles are equal. [Hypothesis; D5.6]

**Step 2.** We choose an equiangular auxiliary triangle on EF, so that 6.4 will supply proportions involving this shared side. At E and F, on the same side of EF, construct angles GEF and EFG equal respectively to ABC and BCA. [1.23] Since angles ABC and BCA together are less than two right angles, the constructed arms meet on that side of EF at a point G. [1.17; P5]

**Step 3.** The sums of the angles of triangles ABC and GEF are equal, since each equals two right angles. [1.32; CN1] The two pairs of copied angles have equal sums. [CN2] Subtracting these equal sums gives ∠BAC = ∠EGF. Consequently ABC and GEF are equiangular, with A corresponding to G, B to E, and C to F. [CN3]

**Step 4.** The sides opposite the equal angles in these equiangular triangles are proportional. Hence AB:BC = GE:EF and BC:CA = EF:FG. [6.4]

**Step 5.** Comparing these proportions with the hypothesis, ratios equal to the same ratio are equal to one another. Therefore DE:EF = GE:EF and EF:FD = EF:FG. [5.11]

**Step 6.** Apply 5.9 directly: the first equality of ratios has the same consequent EF, so DE = GE; the second has the same antecedent EF, so FD = FG. Thus triangles DEF and GEF have all three corresponding sides equal, EF being common. [5.9]

**Step 7.** Applying the side–side–side angle-equality proposition at each corresponding vertex gives ∠EDF = ∠EGF, ∠DEF = ∠GEF, and ∠DFE = ∠GFE. This is the direct next step because all three corresponding sides have just been shown equal. [1.8]

**Step 8.** Combining these equalities with the copied angles and the remaining-angle equality from Step 3 yields ∠BAC = ∠EDF, ∠ABC = ∠DEF, and ∠ACB = ∠DFE. These are respectively the angles subtended by the corresponding sides BC and EF, CA and FD, and AB and DE. Therefore the given triangles are equiangular, and the angles subtended by corresponding sides are equal. [CN1]
