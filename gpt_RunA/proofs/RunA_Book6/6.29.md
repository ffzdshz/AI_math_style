## Proposition 6.29
**Statement:** To a given straight line to apply a parallelogram equal to a given rectilineal figure and exceeding by a parallelogrammic figure similar to a given one.

**Step 1.** Let AB be the given straight line, let ℱ be the given rectilineal figure, and let 𝒟 be the given parallelogram. Write [X] for the area of a figure X. Bisect AB at M (1.10), and on MB construct a parallelogram MBRS similar to 𝒟 (6.18). Denote this parallelogram, also named SRBM, by Q. We shall construct a larger similar parallelogram and use its equal complements; this makes the required excess appear directly.

**Step 2.** Construct a parallelogram equal to ℱ (1.45), and adjoin it externally to Q along a nonzero boundary segment, with their interiors disjoint. Their union is a rectilineal figure 𝒢 satisfying [𝒢] = [Q] + [ℱ] (CN2).

**Step 3.** By 6.25 construct a parallelogram similar to Q and equal to 𝒢. Set a copy of it in the angle RSM, calling it STUV, with its corresponding sides ST and SV on the rays SR and SM respectively. This setting is constructed by copying the adjacent side-lengths onto the rays and completing parallels (P2, 1.3, 1.31). Corresponding angles agree by 1.29; joining the diagonals between the endpoints of those adjacent sides gives equal triangles by 1.4, and hence equal parallelograms by 1.34 and CN2 (P1). Thus STUV is similar and similarly situated to Q, and [STUV] = [Q] + [ℱ] (6.25, CN1).

**Step 4.** Similarity gives ST:SV = SR:SM (D6.1). Consequently ST compares with SR in the same way that SV compares with SM (5.14). If ST were no greater than SR, then SV would be no greater than SM, and STUV would be contained in Q or coincide with it. It could not then have greater area than Q (CN5). But [STUV] = [Q] + [ℱ] > [Q] (CN5). Therefore ST > SR and SV > SM: R lies between S and T, M lies between S and V, and Q lies within STUV.

**Step 5.** Join SU (P1). Since Q is similar and similarly situated to STUV and has the common angle at S, it is about the same diagonal SU; hence B lies on SU (6.26). Produce MB beyond B to meet TU at X, and RB beyond B to meet VU at Y (P2). The parallel sides of the two parallelograms give the resulting parallel grid (1.30). In particular, M, B, X occur in that order, and the four parts of STUV are Q, RTXB, MBYV, and BXUY.

**Step 6.** Through A draw a parallel to TU, meeting VU produced beyond V at Z (1.31, P2). Then AXUZ is a parallelogram, partitioned into the three parallelograms AMVZ, MBYV, and BXUY; their opposite sides are parallel by construction and 1.30. Its base AX contains AB and extends beyond B to X.

**Step 7.** In STUV, the parallelograms Q and BXUY are about the diagonal SU. The other two parts, RTXB and MBYV, are therefore its complements, so [RTXB] = [MBYV] (1.43).

**Step 8.** Since AM = MB, the parallelograms AMVZ and MBYV have equal bases and lie between the same parallels AB and ZU. Hence [AMVZ] = [MBYV] (1.36), and therefore [AMVZ] = [RTXB] (CN1). Here 1.36 is the natural first choice: equal bases between the same parallels already give the required equality, without a proportional-area argument.

**Step 9.** Subtract Q from the equality [STUV] = [Q] + [ℱ]. The partition in Step 5 gives
[ℱ] = [RTXB] + [MBYV] + [BXUY] (CN3).
Replace RTXB by the equal parallelogram AMVZ from Step 8. By addition of equals and the partition in Step 6,
[ℱ] = [AMVZ] + [MBYV] + [BXUY] = [AXUZ] (CN2, CN1).

**Step 10.** The parallelograms BXUY and Q are about the diagonal of STUV, and are therefore similar to one another (6.24). Since Q is similar to 𝒟, BXUY is similar to 𝒟 (6.21). The line BY separates AXUZ into the parallelogram ABYZ on the given line AB and the excess BXUY beyond B. Thus AXUZ is a parallelogram equal to ℱ, applied to AB and exceeding by a parallelogram similar to the given one, as required.
