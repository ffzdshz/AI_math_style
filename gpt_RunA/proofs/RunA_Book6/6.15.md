## Proposition 6.15
**Statement:** In equal triangles which have one angle equal to one angle the sides about the equal angles are reciprocally proportional; and those triangles which have one angle equal to one angle, and in which the sides about the equal angles are reciprocally proportional, are equal.

**Step 1.** Let ABC and DEF be triangles with ∠BAC = ∠EDF. Write [XYZ] for the area of triangle XYZ. We shall prove that [ABC] = [DEF] if and only if AB : DE = DF : AC, which expresses reciprocal proportionality of the sides about the equal angles. [D6.2]

**Step 2.** On the rays AB and AC take points G and H respectively such that AG = DE and AH = DF, extending the sides and cutting off the required lengths when necessary. If a required length equals the existing side, use its endpoint. Join GC and GH. [P2, 1.3, P1]

**Step 3.** Since AG and AH lie along AB and AC, we have ∠GAH = ∠BAC = ∠EDF. Together with AG = DE and AH = DF, this gives [AGH] = [DEF] by the side-angle-side proposition. [1.4]

**Step 4.** Path choice: use the intermediate triangle AGC to compare common-height triangles directly; the common-height theorem 6.1 applies immediately, without introducing parallelograms. Triangles ABC and AGC have bases AB and AG on the same straight line and the same vertex C. Triangles AGH and AGC have bases AH and AC on the same straight line and the same vertex G. Thus each pair has the same height, and
[ABC] : [AGC] = AB : AG,
[AGH] : [AGC] = AH : AC. [D6.4, 6.1]

**Step 5.** First suppose [ABC] = [DEF]. By Step 3, [ABC] = [AGH]. Equal magnitudes have the same ratio to the same magnitude, so
[ABC] : [AGC] = [AGH] : [AGC].
Combining this with Step 4 gives AB : AG = AH : AC. Substituting AG = DE and AH = DF yields AB : DE = DF : AC. Hence the sides about the equal angles are reciprocally proportional. [CN1, 5.7, 5.11, D6.2]

**Step 6.** Conversely, suppose AB : DE = DF : AC. Since AG = DE and AH = DF, this gives AB : AG = AH : AC. Therefore Step 4 yields
[ABC] : [AGC] = [AGH] : [AGC].
Magnitudes having the same ratio to the same magnitude are equal, so [ABC] = [AGH]. By Step 3, [AGH] = [DEF]; hence [ABC] = [DEF]. Thus the triangles are equal, and both assertions are proved. [5.7, 5.11, 5.9, CN1]
