## Proposition 6.31
**Statement:** In right - angled triangles the figure on the side subtending the right angle is equal to the similar and similarly described figures on the sides containing the right angle.

**Step 1.** Let ABC be right-angled at A. Denote by F_BC, F_AB, and F_AC the areas of the similar and similarly described rectilineal figures on BC, AB, and AC, respectively; these supporting sides are corresponding sides (D6.1). Path choice: transfer the established equality for squares to the given figures by the general polygon-ratio theorem.

**Step 2.** Describe squares on BC, AB, and AC, and denote their areas by Q_BC, Q_AB, and Q_AC (1.46). These squares are similar: their angles are equal right angles, and their corresponding sides are proportional because all the sides within each square are equal (D1.22, P4, D6.1).

**Step 3.** By 6.20, each of the ratios F_AB : F_BC and Q_AB : Q_BC is the duplicate ratio of AB : BC. Likewise, each of F_AC : F_BC and Q_AC : Q_BC is the duplicate ratio of AC : BC. Therefore, by 5.11,
F_AB : F_BC = Q_AB : Q_BC,
and
F_AC : F_BC = Q_AC : Q_BC.
Here 6.20 is the natural choice because the given figures are arbitrary polygons, not necessarily triangles.

**Step 4.** Adding the antecedents in these two proportions, while retaining their respective common consequents, gives
(F_AB + F_AC) : F_BC = (Q_AB + Q_AC) : Q_BC
(5.24).

**Step 5.** Since ABC is right-angled at A, the square on BC equals the squares on AB and AC together:
Q_AB + Q_AC = Q_BC
(1.47). Substitution into Step 4 therefore gives
(F_AB + F_AC) : F_BC = Q_BC : Q_BC.

**Step 6.** Apply the definition of equal ratios, taking both multipliers to be one. Since the two magnitudes in Q_BC : Q_BC are equal, the corresponding magnitudes F_AB + F_AC and F_BC must also be equal (D5.5). Thus F_BC = F_AB + F_AC: the figure on the side subtending the right angle equals the similar and similarly described figures on the sides containing that angle, taken together.
