## Proposition 6.21
**Statement:** Figures which are similar to the same rectilineal figure are also similar to one another.

**Step 1.** Let F and G each be similar to the rectilineal figure H. Match the vertices and sides of F and G through their respective correspondences with H. By the definition of similarity, each given correspondence has equal corresponding angles and proportional sides about those angles. [D6.1]

**Step 2.** Take any three corresponding angles, one in each figure. The angle in F equals the angle in H, and the angle in G equals that same angle in H. Therefore the angles in F and G are equal. Since the corresponding angles were arbitrary, F and G have their angles severally equal. [D6.1, CN1]

**Step 3.** Let a, b be the sides about any chosen angle in F; let c, d be their corresponding sides in H; and let e, f be their corresponding sides in G, taken in the same order. The given similarities yield a:b = c:d and e:f = c:d. Ratios which are the same with the same ratio are also the same with one another; hence a:b = e:f. Thus the sides of F and G about every pair of corresponding equal angles are proportional. The direct path is 5.11, since the two ratios already agree with the same ratio in H. [D6.1, 5.11]

**Step 4.** Consequently F and G have their angles severally equal and the sides about the equal angles proportional. They are therefore similar. Hence figures which are similar to the same rectilineal figure are also similar to one another. [D6.1]
