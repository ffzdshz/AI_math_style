## Proposition 6.2
**Statement:** If a straight line be drawn parallel to one of the sides of a triangle, it will cut the sides of the triangle proportionally; and, if the sides of the triangle be cut proportionally, the line joining the points of section will be parallel to the remaining side of the triangle.

**Step 1.** Let ABC be a triangle, with D in the interior of AB and E in the interior of AC. Join DE, BE, and CD. Write [XYZ] for the area of triangle XYZ. We shall prove that DE is parallel to BC if and only if BD:DA = CE:EA. [P1]

**Step 2.** Triangles BDE and ADE, with bases BD and DA on AB, have the same perpendicular height from E. Likewise, triangles CDE and ADE, with bases CE and EA on AC, have the same perpendicular height from D. Hence
[BDE]:[ADE] = BD:DA,
and
[CDE]:[ADE] = CE:EA.
[D6.4, 6.1]

**Step 3.** First suppose DE is parallel to BC. Triangles BDE and CDE are on the same base DE and between the same parallels DE and BC. Therefore [BDE] = [CDE]. The common base makes the same-base area theorem the direct choice. [1.37]

**Step 4.** Equal magnitudes have the same ratio to the same magnitude, so
[BDE]:[ADE] = [CDE]:[ADE]. [5.7]
Combining this equality with the two proportions in Step 2 gives BD:DA = CE:EA. [5.11]
Thus the sides AB and AC are cut proportionally. [D5.6]

**Step 5.** Conversely, suppose BD:DA = CE:EA. The proportions established in Step 2 then give
[BDE]:[ADE] = [CDE]:[ADE]. [5.11]

**Step 6.** Magnitudes having the same ratio to the same magnitude are equal. Consequently [BDE] = [CDE]. [5.9]

**Step 7.** Since D and E lie inside AB and AC respectively, B and C are on the same side of DE. Thus BDE and CDE are equal triangles on the same base DE and on the same side of it. The straight line BC joining their remaining vertices is therefore parallel to DE. The same-base converse applies directly here. [1.39]
Together with Step 4, this proves both assertions.
