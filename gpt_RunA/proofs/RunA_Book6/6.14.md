## Proposition 6.14
**Statement:** In equal and equiangular parallelograms the sides about the equal angles are reciprocally proportional; and equiangular parallelograms in which the sides about the equal angles are reciprocally proportional are equal.

**Step 1.** Choose corresponding equal angles of the two parallelograms. Place their vertices together at O and their corresponding arms along the same two rays. Denote the parallelograms, in cyclic order, by OABC and ODEF, with A and D on one ray and C and F on the other. This placement preserves lengths and areas by coincidence (CN4). The required reciprocal proportion is OA:OD = OF:OC (D6.2).

**Step 2.** Let the straight lines BC and DE, produced if necessary, meet at G (P2). This intersection exists: BC is parallel to OA, and DE is parallel to OC; if BC and DE were parallel, transitivity of parallelism would make OA and OC parallel (1.30), contrary to their meeting at O (D1.23). Now OD is parallel to CG, and OC is parallel to DG, so ODGC is an auxiliary parallelogram.

**Step 3.** Write p, q, and r for the areas of OABC, ODEF, and ODGC respectively. **Path choice:** compare both given parallelograms with this single auxiliary parallelogram, using the same-height theorem 6.1. The parallelograms OABC and ODGC have bases OA and OD and lie between the same parallels OA and BC. Therefore
p:r = OA:OD. (6.1)

**Step 4.** Taking OF and OC as bases, the parallelograms ODEF and ODGC lie between the same parallels OC and DE. Therefore
q:r = OF:OC. (6.1)

**Step 5.** First suppose that the given parallelograms are equal, so p = q. Equal magnitudes have the same ratio to the same magnitude; hence p:r = q:r (5.7). Combining this equality with Steps 3 and 4 gives
OA:OD = OF:OC (5.11).
Thus the sides about the chosen equal angles are reciprocally proportional (D6.2).

**Step 6.** Conversely, suppose that the sides about the corresponding equal angles are reciprocally proportional, so OA:OD = OF:OC (D6.2). Steps 3 and 4 then give p:r = q:r (5.11). Magnitudes having the same ratio to the same magnitude are equal, so p = q (5.9). Here 5.9 is the direct choice because the two area ratios have the common consequent r. Consequently the given parallelograms are equal. Both assertions are proved.
