## Proposition 12.15
**Statement:** In equal cones and cylinders the bases are reciprocally proportional to the heights; and those cones and cylinders in which the bases are reciprocally proportional to the heights are equal.

**Step 1.** Let X and Y be two cones, or two cylinders, with circular bases B_X and B_Y and heights h_X and h_Y. Construct a solid Z of the same kind on B_Y with height h_X: revolve a right triangle, respectively a rectangle, whose perpendicular sides are the radius of B_Y and h_X (D11.18, D11.21). Our path is to compare X with Z at equal height first, and then Y with Z on the same base; this treats both kinds directly.

**Step 2.** Since X and Z have equal heights, they are to one another as their bases (12.11). Thus
X : Z = B_X : B_Y.

**Step 3.** Since Y and Z have the same base, they are to one another as their heights (12.14). Thus
Y : Z = h_Y : h_X.

**Step 4.** First suppose that X and Y are equal. Equal magnitudes have the same ratio to the same magnitude, so X : Z = Y : Z (5.7). Combining this with Steps 2 and 3 by equality of ratios (5.11), we obtain
B_X : B_Y = h_Y : h_X.
Therefore the bases of the equal solids are reciprocally proportional to their heights.

**Step 5.** Conversely, suppose that the bases are reciprocally proportional to the heights, so that
B_X : B_Y = h_Y : h_X.
The ratios in Steps 2 and 3 are consequently equal: X : Z = Y : Z (5.11). Magnitudes having the same ratio to the same magnitude are equal (5.9); hence X = Y. Since the argument applies to two cones and to two cylinders, both assertions are proved.
