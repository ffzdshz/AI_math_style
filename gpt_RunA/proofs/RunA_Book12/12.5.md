## Proposition 12.5
**Statement:** Pyramids which are of the same height and have triangular bases are to one another as the bases.

**Step 1.** Let P and Q be pyramids of the same height, with triangular bases of areas A and B. We use the direct exhaustion route: compare the inscribed prism collections supplied by 12.3–12.4, and then verify the definition of equal ratios, D5.5. Subdivide each pyramid by 12.3, and repeatedly subdivide every remaining pyramid in the same manner. After k corresponding stages, let X_k and Y_k be the total prisms obtained, and R_k and S_k the total remaining pyramids. Thus P = X_k + R_k and Q = Y_k + S_k. (12.3; CN2.)

**Step 2.** The corresponding prism collections contain equal numbers of prisms. Therefore, at every stage,
X_k : Y_k = A : B.
This is precisely the comparison furnished by 12.4 for the corresponding collections. Moreover, for k > 1, X_k > X_1 and Y_k > Y_1, since the later collections include additional prisms. (12.4; CN5.)

**Step 3.** At each further stage, more than half of every remaining pyramid is removed as prisms. Consequently, more than half of the total remainder is removed. The same holds for any fixed equimultiple of that remainder. Hence, by 10.1, for any positive solid magnitude δ and any fixed positive integers m and n, sufficiently many stages give both mR_k < δ and nS_k < δ. We may also require k > 1, since further subdivision only decreases the remainders. (12.3; CN2; CN5; 10.1.)

**Step 4.** For k > 1, Step 2 and 5.11 give
X_k : Y_k = X_1 : Y_1.
Subtracting the corresponding first-stage collections, 5.19 gives
(X_k − X_1) : (Y_k − Y_1) = X_k : Y_k = A : B.
Here 5.19 is the natural choice: the wholes and the parts being removed already have the same ratio. (5.11; 5.19.)

**Step 5.** Let m and n be arbitrary positive integers, and suppose mA > nB. By D5.5 applied to the proportions in Steps 2 and 4,
mX_1 > nY_1,
and, for every k > 1,
m(X_k − X_1) > n(Y_k − Y_1).
Put δ = mX_1 − nY_1, a positive solid magnitude. Adding the corresponding parts gives
mX_k > nY_k + δ. (D5.5; CN2; CN3.)
Choose k > 1 so that nS_k < δ, as permitted by Step 3 and 10.1. Then
nQ = nY_k + nS_k < nY_k + δ < mX_k < mP.
Thus mA > nB implies mP > nQ. (10.1; CN2; CN5.)

**Step 6.** If mA < nB, interchange (P, A, m) and (Q, B, n) in Step 5. The identical comparison of equimultiples and exhaustion argument gives nQ > mP. Thus mA < nB implies mP < nQ. (Step 5; D5.5; 10.1.)

**Step 7.** Suppose instead that mA = nB. By Step 2 and D5.5, mX_k = nY_k at every stage. If mP > nQ, put δ = mP − nQ and choose k with mR_k < δ, using Step 3 and 10.1. Then
mX_k = mP − mR_k > mP − δ = nQ > nY_k,
contrary to mX_k = nY_k. The inequality mP < nQ is excluded by the same argument with the two pyramids interchanged. Therefore mP = nQ. (D5.5; 10.1; CN3; CN5.)

**Step 8.** For every pair of positive integers m and n, the equimultiples mP and nQ exceed, equal, or fall short of one another exactly as mA and nB do. By D5.5,
P : Q = A : B.
Therefore pyramids of the same height with triangular bases are to one another as their bases.
