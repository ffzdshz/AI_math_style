## Proposition 12.16
**Statement:** Given two circles about the same centre, to inscribe in the greater circle an equilateral polygon with an even number of sides which does not touch the lesser circle.

**Step 1.** Let the greater and lesser circles be Γ and γ, with common centre O and radii R and r, where R > r. Choose A on γ, and produce OA to meet Γ at E. Through A draw the perpendicular to OE, meeting Γ at B and C. Since A is inside Γ, it lies between B and C. Thus OA = r and OA is perpendicular to BC. Let θ be the interior angle BOC of triangle BOC. [P1, P2, D1.15, 1.11]

**Step 2.** Produce OE through O, and draw through O its perpendicular, producing this also to meet Γ in both directions. These two lines divide the circle into four sectors whose central angles are equal right angles. [P2, 1.11, P4]

**Step 3.** Subdivide every central angle into four equal angles by bisecting it and then bisecting both halves. Repeat this operation simultaneously in all sectors. At each operation the common central angle is reduced to one quarter of its previous magnitude: the amount removed is three quarters, which is greater than one half. Consequently, by 10.1, a finite stage is reached at which the common central angle α is strictly less than θ. If it is already smaller, no subdivision is needed; if equality occurs, one further subdivision makes it smaller. This angular subdivision is chosen because it preserves equality of the central angles throughout. [1.9, CN5, 10.1]

**Step 4.** Join the successive points where the resulting rays meet Γ, in their order around the circumference. All vertices of the resulting polygon lie on Γ, so the polygon is inscribed in Γ. After n subdivision stages it has 4·4ⁿ sides, divisible into two equal groups, and therefore an even number of sides. Moreover, the triangles formed by O and successive pairs of vertices have their two radial sides respectively equal and their included angles all equal to α. Their bases are therefore equal, so the polygon is equilateral. [P1, D4.3, D7.6, D1.15, 1.4]

**Step 5.** Let UV be any side of this polygon. In triangles OBC and OUV, OB = OU and OC = OV, since all four are radii of Γ. But ∠BOC = θ > α = ∠UOV. Therefore BC > UV. The included-angle comparison applies directly here, without requiring any comparison of arc lengths. [D1.15, 1.24]

**Step 6.** Draw OH perpendicular to the straight line containing UV. Since BC and UV are chords of the same circle and BC > UV, BC is nearer the centre than UV. Hence OH > OA = r. We use the chord-distance comparison here because it gives exactly the required inequality between perpendicular distances. [1.12, 3.15, D3.5]

**Step 7.** Let X be any point of the line containing UV. If X = H, then OX = OH > r. Otherwise, triangle OHX is right-angled at H; its angle at X is less than that right angle, and consequently OX > OH > r. Thus every point of every side of the polygon has distance greater than r from O, whereas every point of the circumference of γ has distance r from O. No side therefore meets the lesser circle. The constructed polygon is consequently an equilateral polygon with an even number of sides, inscribed in the greater circle and not touching the lesser circle, as required. [1.17, 1.19, D1.15]
