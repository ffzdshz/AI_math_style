## Proposition 12.8
**Statement:** Similar pyramids which have triangular bases are in the triplicate ratio of their corresponding sides.

**Step 1.** Let T = ABCD and T′ = A′B′C′D′ be the given similar triangular pyramids, with corresponding vertices indicated by primes. Their corresponding triangular faces are similar [D11.9], so corresponding face angles are equal and corresponding sides are proportional [D6.1]. By alternation, all pairs of corresponding edges have the same ratio, namely AB:A′B′ [5.16, 5.11].

**Step 2.** Complete a parallelepiped P having AB, AC, AD as its three edges at A, and a parallelepiped P′ having A′B′, A′C′, A′D′ as its three edges at A′. Draw the necessary parallels by 1.31; the opposite face planes are parallel by 11.15. We choose this route because 11.33 directly supplies the required triplicate ratio for similar parallelepipeds.

**Step 3.** In each completed parallelepiped, the side lengths of every face equal the lengths of the appropriate generating edges, by the equalities of opposite sides of parallelograms [1.34]. Its angles are the angle between those generating edges and its supplement [11.10, 1.29]. Step 1 therefore gives equal corresponding angles and proportional corresponding sides in the faces of P and P′. Thus their corresponding faces are similar [D6.1], and P and P′ are similar solids [D11.9].

**Step 4.** In P, let E and F be the vertices corresponding to B and C across the edges parallel to AD; thus DE is parallel to AB, DF to AC, and BE and CF to AD. The diagonal plane BCEF divides P into two equal triangular prisms, one of which is ABC–DEF [11.28]. The three-pyramid division of this prism consists of the equal pyramids ABCD, BCDE, and CDEF [12.7]. Hence this prism is three times T, and P is six times T. The identical construction in P′ gives P′ six times T′ [CN2].

**Step 5.** Since P and P′ are the same multiples of T and T′ respectively, the pyramids have the same ratio as the parallelepipeds:
T:T′ = P:P′ [5.15].

**Step 6.** The similar parallelepipeds P and P′ are in the triplicate ratio of their corresponding edges AB and A′B′ [11.33]. Consequently, by Step 5, T and T′ are also in the triplicate ratio of AB:A′B′ [5.11]. Since Step 1 shows that every pair of corresponding pyramid edges has this same ratio, similar pyramids having triangular bases are in the triplicate ratio of their corresponding sides. ∎
