## Proposition 12.1
**Statement:** Similar polygons inscribed in circles are to one another as the squares on the diameters.

**Step 1.** Let F and G be the given similar polygons, with consecutive corresponding vertices A, B, C and A′, B′, C′. These vertices lie on their respective circles [D4.3]. Join AC and A′C′ [P1].

**Step 2.** By similarity of the polygons, AB:BC = A′B′:B′C′ and ∠ABC = ∠A′B′C′ [D6.1]. Hence triangles ABC and A′B′C′ are equiangular [6.6]. At least one of ∠BAC and ∠ACB is acute, since their sum is less than two right angles [1.17]. Reverse the labels A and C, and simultaneously A′ and C′, if necessary, so that ∠ACB and its equal corresponding angle ∠A′C′B′ are acute. This choice will permit a same-segment comparison without a supplementary-angle case.

**Step 3.** Find the centres of the two circles [3.1]. Draw the straight lines from A and A′ through their respective centres, producing them to meet the circumferences again at D and D′. Thus AD and A′D′ are diameters [P1, P2, D1.17]. Join BD and B′D′ [P1].

**Step 4.** Since ∠ACB is acute, C lies on the arc of the greater segment cut off by AB, and AB is not a diameter; the corresponding assertions hold for A′B′ and C′ [3.31]. Consequently D ≠ B and D′ ≠ B′. The angles ABD and A′B′D′ are right, being angles in semicircles [3.31]. Therefore ∠ADB and ∠A′D′B′ are acute [1.17], so D and D′ also lie on the arcs of the greater segments cut off by AB and A′B′ respectively [3.31]. Thus C and D belong to the same segment on AB, and C′ and D′ to the same segment on A′B′.

**Step 5.** Angles in the same segment are equal, so ∠ADB = ∠ACB and ∠A′D′B′ = ∠A′C′B′ [3.21]. The angles on the right are equal by Step 2; hence ∠ADB = ∠A′D′B′ [CN1]. Also ∠ABD = ∠A′B′D′, since both are right [P4]. The remaining angles are equal by subtracting these equal angles from the two-right-angle sums of the triangles [1.32, CN3]. Thus triangles ABD and A′B′D′ are equiangular, and consequently AB:AD = A′B′:A′D′ [6.4]. Alternating gives
AB:A′B′ = AD:A′D′ [5.16].

**Step 6.** Describe squares S and T on AD and A′D′ respectively [1.46]. Their corresponding angles are equal, since all are right, and their corresponding adjacent-side ratios are equal, since the sides within each square are equal [D1.22, P4, D5.5]. Hence S and T are similar [D6.1].

**Step 7.** Path choice: apply the four-line proportion theorem directly, since Step 5 supplies precisely its hypothesis. The polygons F and G are similar and similarly described on the homologous sides AB and A′B′, while the squares S and T are similar and similarly described on AD and A′D′. Therefore the proportion AB:A′B′ = AD:A′D′ gives F:G = S:T [6.22]. Since AD and A′D′ are diameters [D1.17], the similar polygons are to one another as the squares on the diameters, as required.
