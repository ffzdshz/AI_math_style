## Proposition 12.9
**Statement:** In equal pyramids which have triangular bases the bases are reciprocally proportional to the heights; and those pyramids in which the bases are reciprocally proportional to the heights are equal.

**Step 1.** Let the two pyramids be S₁A₁B₁C₁ and S₂A₂B₂C₂. Write p₁, p₂ for their solid magnitudes, b₁, b₂ for their triangular base areas, and h₁, h₂ for their perpendicular heights (D6.4). We must prove that p₁ = p₂ if and only if b₁ : b₂ = h₂ : h₁. **Path choice:** pass to enclosing parallelepipeds, since 12.7 supplies the required relation between triangular prisms and pyramids, while 11.34 gives exactly the reciprocal-base-and-height criterion for parallelepipeds.

**Step 2.** For each i = 1, 2, complete the triangle AᵢBᵢCᵢ to the parallelogram AᵢBᵢDᵢCᵢ. Complete the three edges AᵢBᵢ, AᵢCᵢ, AᵢSᵢ to a parallelepiped Kᵢ by drawing parallels (1.31). Denote its upper vertices corresponding to Aᵢ, Bᵢ, Dᵢ, Cᵢ by Sᵢ, B′ᵢ, D′ᵢ, C′ᵢ. The upper and lower faces are parallel, since two intersecting edges in one are respectively parallel to two intersecting edges in the other (11.15). Since the upper face contains Sᵢ, the height of Kᵢ is hᵢ (D6.4).

**Step 3.** Write βᵢ for the base area of Kᵢ. The diagonal BᵢCᵢ bisects the parallelogram AᵢBᵢDᵢCᵢ into equal triangles (1.34). Therefore βᵢ = 2bᵢ (CN2).

**Step 4.** The plane through the corresponding diagonals BᵢCᵢ and B′ᵢC′ᵢ divides Kᵢ into two equal triangular prisms (11.28). Consider the prism with bases AᵢBᵢCᵢ and SᵢB′ᵢC′ᵢ. Apply the division of 12.7 starting with its lower triangular base and the upper vertex Sᵢ: its three equal pyramids are SᵢAᵢBᵢCᵢ, SᵢBᵢCᵢB′ᵢ, and SᵢCᵢB′ᵢC′ᵢ. Thus this prism is three times the given pyramid. Since Kᵢ consists of two equal such prisms, its solid magnitude vᵢ satisfies vᵢ = 6pᵢ (12.7, CN2).

**Step 5.** Equimultiples have the same ratio as the original magnitudes (5.15). Consequently,
β₁ : β₂ = b₁ : b₂,
and
v₁ : v₂ = p₁ : p₂.

**Step 6.** Suppose first that the pyramids are equal, so p₁ = p₂. Their sixfold multiples are equal, hence v₁ = v₂ (CN2). Equal parallelepipeds have their bases reciprocally proportional to their heights (11.34), so β₁ : β₂ = h₂ : h₁. Substituting the base ratio from Step 5 gives b₁ : b₂ = h₂ : h₁, as required.

**Step 7.** Conversely, suppose b₁ : b₂ = h₂ : h₁. By Step 5, β₁ : β₂ = h₂ : h₁. Therefore the parallelepipeds are equal by the converse in 11.34, giving v₁ = v₂. But v₁ : v₂ = p₁ : p₂ by Step 5. Taking both multipliers to be one in the definition of equal ratios shows that p₁ = p₂ (D5.5). Thus the pyramids are equal precisely when their bases are reciprocally proportional to their heights.
