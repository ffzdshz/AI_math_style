## Proposition 12.12
**Statement:** Similar cones and cylinders are to one another in the triplicate ratio of the diameters in their bases.

**Step 1.** Let C₁ and C₂ be similar cylinders, with base diameters a and b and heights h and k. Their axes are their heights, so h:k = a:b by the definition of similar cylinders (D11.21, D11.22, D11.24). Let T be the cylinder having the same base as C₂ and height h (D11.21). We choose this auxiliary cylinder so that the equal-height comparison 12.11 applies directly; after proving the cylinder case, we shall transfer it to cones by 12.10.

**Step 2.** We establish the comparison T:C₂ = h:k directly from the definition of equal ratios. Take arbitrary positive integers m and n. Place m congruent copies of T successively along one axis, and likewise n congruent copies of C₂. The resulting solids represent mT and nC₂ (D5.2, CN2, CN4). By the generating-rectangle definition of a cylinder, these stacks are cylinders on the same base, with heights mh and nk respectively (D11.21). Place their lower bases together and their axes in the same direction. If mh exceeds nk, the second stack is a proper part of the first; if mh equals nk, the stacks coincide; and if mh falls short of nk, the first is a proper part of the second. Thus mT respectively exceeds, equals, or falls short of nC₂ exactly when mh respectively exceeds, equals, or falls short of nk (CN4, CN5). Since m and n were arbitrary, D5.5 gives T:C₂ = h:k. Therefore T:C₂ = a:b by Step 1 and 5.11.

**Step 3.** Write B₁ and B₂ for the circular bases of C₁ and C₂. Since C₁ and T have the same height, and T has base B₂, 12.11 gives C₁:T = B₁:B₂. By 12.2, B₁:B₂ is the ratio of the square on a to the square on b. Hence C₁:T equals that ratio of squares (5.11).

**Step 4.** Construct c as a third proportional to a and b, and d as a third proportional to b and c (6.11). Thus a:b = b:c = c:d. The squares on a and b are similar polygons, so their ratio is the duplicate ratio of a:b (6.20). The similar-polygon theorem is the direct choice here because the figures being compared are squares. Since a:b = b:c, this duplicate ratio is a:c (D5.9). Consequently Step 3 gives C₁:T = a:c (5.11).

**Step 5.** Step 2 gives T:C₂ = a:b, while Step 4 gives a:b = c:d; hence T:C₂ = c:d (5.11). Combining this with C₁:T = a:c, we obtain C₁:C₂ = a:d ex aequali (5.22). Since a, b, c, d are continuously proportional, a:d is the triplicate ratio of a:b (D5.10). Thus similar cylinders are in the triplicate ratio of their base diameters.

**Step 6.** Now let K₁ and K₂ be any two similar cones, and let U₁ and U₂ be the cylinders having their respective bases and heights. The axes of these cones and cylinders are their heights (D11.18, D11.19, D11.21, D11.22). Their corresponding axis and diameter ratios are therefore unchanged, so U₁ and U₂ are similar cylinders (D11.24). By 12.10, U₁ is three times K₁ and U₂ is three times K₂. Magnitudes have the same ratio as their corresponding equimultiples, so K₁:K₂ = U₁:U₂ (5.15). By Step 5, this is the triplicate ratio of their base diameters. Therefore similar cones and cylinders are to one another in the triplicate ratio of the diameters in their bases.
