## Proposition 12.7
**Statement:** Any prism which has a triangular base is divided into three pyramids equal to one another which have triangular bases.

**Step 1.** Let the given prism have opposite triangular faces ABC and DEF, with corresponding vertices A, D; B, E; and C, F. Its remaining faces are the parallelograms ABED, BCEF, and CADF. [D11.13]

**Step 2.** Join AF, BF, and AE. The triangles ABF and AEF lie in planes; use these as cutting sections of the prism. [P1, 11.2]

**Step 3.** The section ABF, bounded by the edge AB and the face diagonals BF and AF, separates the triangular pyramid ABCF from the pyramid whose vertex is F and whose base is the parallelogram ABED. In the latter pyramid, the section AEF divides its base along AE into triangles ABE and ADE, and therefore divides that pyramid into the triangular pyramids ABEF and ADEF. Thus the three pyramids
T₁ = ABCF, T₂ = ABEF, and T₃ = ADEF
have disjoint interiors and together exhaust the prism. [D11.12, D11.13, 1.34]

**Step 4.** The diagonal BF bisects the parallelogram BCEF, so the triangles BCF and BEF are equal. Regard T₁ and T₂ as pyramids with these respective bases and the common vertex A. Their bases lie in the same plane, so their heights are the same perpendicular from A to that plane. [1.34, D6.4]

**Step 5.** Apply the triangular-base theorem 12.5 directly: it is the first applicable pyramid-ratio result, and no polygonal-base generalization is needed. It gives
T₁ : T₂ = triangle BCF : triangle BEF.
Since the two bases are equal, the definition of equal ratios, taking each multiple once, gives T₁ = T₂. [12.5, D5.5]

**Step 6.** The diagonal AE bisects the parallelogram ABED, so triangles ABE and ADE are equal. Regard T₂ and T₃ as pyramids with these respective bases and the common vertex F. They have the same height, namely the perpendicular from F to the plane ABED. Hence
T₂ : T₃ = triangle ABE : triangle ADE,
and therefore T₂ = T₃. [1.34, D6.4, 12.5, D5.5]

**Step 7.** Consequently T₁, T₂, and T₃ are equal to one another. By the dissection in Step 3, they constitute the entire given prism, and each has a triangular base. Thus any prism with a triangular base is divided into three equal pyramids having triangular bases. [CN1, D11.12]
