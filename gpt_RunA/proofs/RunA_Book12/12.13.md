## Proposition 12.13
**Statement:** If a cylinder be cut by a plane which is parallel to its opposite planes, then, as the cylinder is to the cylinder, so will the axis be to the axis.

**Step 1.** Let the original cylinder have axis AB, and let the cutting plane meet AB at C. Denote the two resulting solids by K and L, corresponding respectively to AC and CB. In every position of the generating rectangle, its intersections with the cutting plane and the base planes are parallel (11.16). Thus the cut divides that rectangle into two rectangles having the same radial breadth (1.29, 1.34). By the rotational definitions of a cylinder, its axis, and its bases, K and L are therefore cylinders with the same base radius and with axes AC and CB respectively (D11.21, D11.22, D11.23).

**Step 2.** We use the equimultiple criterion for equal ratios directly: repeated coaxial copies can be compared by coincidence or inclusion, so no formula for the magnitude of a cylinder is needed (D5.5). Let m and n be arbitrary positive integers. Extend the axis beyond A as necessary, and mark points M and N on the same ray from A so that AM consists of m successive lengths equal to AC, while AN consists of n successive lengths equal to CB (P2, P3). Thus AM = m·AC and AN = n·CB (D5.2).

**Step 3.** Starting from the original base at A, assemble m congruent copies of K consecutively along AM. Separately, starting from that same base, assemble n congruent copies of L consecutively along AN. Each assembly is itself a cylinder, since its generating rectangles join end to end into a single rectangle with the original radial breadth (D11.21). Call these cylinders X and Y. Each copy equals its original by coincidence, and addition of the copies gives X = mK and Y = nL (CN4, CN2, D5.2).

**Step 4.** The cylinders X and Y have the same starting base, the same axis ray, and the same radius. Consequently, their generating rectangles show that if AM > AN, then Y is a proper part of X, and hence X > Y; if AM < AN, then X is a proper part of Y, and hence X < Y (D11.21, CN5). If AM = AN, their generating rectangles, and therefore the cylinders themselves, coincide, so X = Y (D11.21, CN4).

**Step 5.** Hence, for the arbitrary integers m and n, the magnitudes mK and nL exceed, equal, or fall short of one another exactly as m·AC and n·CB do. By the definition of equal ratios, K:L = AC:CB (D5.5). Therefore, as the cylinder is to the cylinder, so is the axis to the axis, as required.
