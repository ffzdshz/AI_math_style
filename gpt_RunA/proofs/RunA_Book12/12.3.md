## Proposition 12.3
**Statement:** Any pyramid which has a triangular base is divided into two pyramids equal and similar to one another, similar to the whole and having triangular bases, and into two equal prisms; and the two prisms are greater than the half of the whole pyramid.

**Step 1.** Let ABCD be the given pyramid, and denote the whole solid by W. Bisect AD, BD, CD, AB, BC, CA at E, F, G, H, I, J respectively. Draw the joins required below and take the planes of the resulting triangles. [1.10, P1, 11.2]

**Step 2.** In any face of ABCD, the straight line joining two side-midpoints is parallel to the third side and equal to its half: parallelism follows from 6.2, and the equality follows from the equal corresponding angles and proportional sides. [6.2, 1.29, 6.4] In particular,

EJ = FI = GC = CD/2,
HB = EF = JI = AB/2,
HJ = FG = BI = IC = BC/2.

Within each displayed group the segments are parallel and, when read in the indicated order of their endpoints, have the same direction.

**Step 3.** Consider the four corner pyramids, listing their vertices in correspondence with D, A, B, C:

T_D = (D,E,F,G),
T_A = (E,A,H,J),
T_B = (F,H,B,I),
T_C = (G,J,I,C).

These are triangular-based pyramids. [D11.12] Every edge of each is half the corresponding edge of ABCD. Consequently their corresponding triangular faces are similar to those of ABCD, and all four pyramids are similar to ABCD. [6.5, D6.1, D11.9] Moreover, corresponding faces of any two of these smaller pyramids have equal corresponding sides and equal included angles, and therefore are equal as well as similar. Thus

T_D = T_A = T_B = T_C,

and the four pyramids are equal and similar to one another. [1.4, D11.10]

**Step 4.** Since EF and JI are parallel to AB, while EJ and FI are parallel to DC, EFIJ is a plane parallelogram. [11.9] Cut W first by its plane. The sections with the four faces are EF, FI, IJ, JE; this cut separates the edge DC from the edge AB. [11.3]

From the part containing DC, cut off T_D along triangle EFG. From the part containing AB, cut off T_A along triangle EHJ. Call the remaining solids U and V respectively. Their boundary faces are:

U: triangles EFG and JIC, and quadrilaterals EFIJ, FGCI, GEJC;
V: triangles HEJ and BFI, and quadrilaterals HEFB, EJIF, JHBI.

These cuts leave no further piece and give no overlapping interiors. Hence

W = T_D + T_A + U + V. [11.3, D11.12, CN2]

**Step 5.** The triangular ends EFG and JIC of U are equal and similar by Step 3. Their planes are parallel, since EF is parallel to JI and EG to JC. [11.15] Their corresponding joining edges EJ, FI, GC are equal and parallel by Step 2, so their three lateral faces are parallelograms. [1.33] Therefore U is a prism. [D11.13]

Likewise, the triangular ends HEJ and BFI of V are equal and similar. Their planes are parallel, since HE is parallel to BF and HJ to BI. [11.15] The joining edges HB, EF, JI are equal and parallel, so the lateral faces are parallelograms. Thus V is also a prism. [1.33, D11.13]

**Step 6.** To compare these prisms, complete EFG and JIC to parallelograms EFGK and JICL, respectively. This is done by drawing the appropriate parallels in their planes. [1.31] Their opposite sides give

EK = FG = IC = JL,

with all four segments parallel and similarly directed. [1.34, Step 2] Since EJ, FI, GC are also equal and parallel, KL is equal and parallel to them. [1.33] The six faces

EFGK, JICL, EFIJ, FGCI, KGCL, EJLK

therefore bound a parallelepiped Z; the opposite face-planes are parallel. [11.15]

Path choice: compare diagonal halves of this one parallelepiped. This makes 11.28 directly applicable, without requiring any formula for the magnitude of a pyramid.

**Step 7.** In the opposite faces EFGK and JICL, EG and JC are diagonals. The plane through them cuts Z into two equal parts, one of which is precisely U. Thus U is half of Z. [11.28]

In the other pair of opposite faces EJLK and FICG, JK and IG are diagonals. Their plane likewise bisects Z. Let R be the half having triangular ends JKL and IGC. Thus R also is half of Z, and therefore

U = R. [11.28, CN1]

**Step 8.** Superpose V upon R by moving its vertices according to

H → J, E → K, J → L,
B → I, F → G, I → C.

This is one common displacement: the six joining segments HJ, EK, JL, BI, FG, IC are equal, parallel, and similarly directed, by Steps 2 and 6. Under this displacement the triangular ends HEJ and BFI coincide with JKL and IGC, and the corresponding lateral faces also coincide. Hence V coincides with R and is equal to it. [CN4] Together with Step 7 this gives

U = V. [CN1]

**Step 9.** The corner pyramid T_C, with vertex G and base JIC, is a proper solid part of U. Similarly, T_B, with vertex H and base BFI, is a proper solid part of V. In each case the prism has a whole triangular end opposite the base, whereas the contained pyramid reaches that end only at its vertex; the remaining region is therefore nonempty as a solid. Consequently,

U > T_C and V > T_B. [D11.12, D11.13, CN5]

Since T_C = T_D and T_B = T_A by Step 3, addition gives

U + V > T_D + T_A. [CN1, CN2, CN5]

**Step 10.** Put S = U + V and T = T_D + T_A. The dissection gives W = S + T, while Step 9 gives S > T. Hence

2S > S + T = W,

so S is greater than half of W. [CN2, CN5]

Thus the original triangular-based pyramid is divided into the two equal and similar triangular-based pyramids T_D and T_A, each similar to the whole, and the two equal prisms U and V; and the two prisms together are greater than half of the whole pyramid.
