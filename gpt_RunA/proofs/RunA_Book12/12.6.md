## Proposition 12.6
**Statement:** Pyramids which are of the same height and have polygonal bases are to one another as the bases.

**Step 1.** Let P and Q be pyramids with polygonal bases A and B and equal heights. We also use these letters for their respective solid and plane magnitudes. The height is the perpendicular distance from the vertex to the base plane. [D11.12, D6.4] Path choice: triangulate the bases and apply 12.5; then use 5.24, the natural addition theorem because the component comparisons will have fixed consequents.

**Step 2.** Draw noncrossing diagonals dividing A into triangles A₁, …, Aₘ and B into triangles B₁, …, Bₙ. Through each diagonal and the corresponding pyramid’s vertex take the plane determined by them. These planes divide P into triangular-based pyramids P₁, …, Pₘ with bases A₁, …, Aₘ, and Q into triangular-based pyramids Q₁, …, Qₙ with bases B₁, …, Bₙ. [P1, 11.2, D11.12] The pieces fill their respective figures without overlapping interiors, so
A = A₁ + ⋯ + Aₘ,  B = B₁ + ⋯ + Bₙ,
P = P₁ + ⋯ + Pₘ,  Q = Q₁ + ⋯ + Qₙ. [CN2]

**Step 3.** Each component pyramid has the same vertex and base plane as its original pyramid, and therefore the same height. Consequently all the component pyramids have equal heights. [D6.4] Fix Q₁ as a reference pyramid. By the triangular-base result, for every i and j,
Pᵢ : Q₁ = Aᵢ : B₁,
and
Qⱼ : Q₁ = Bⱼ : B₁. [12.5]

**Step 4.** Add the antecedents in the first family of proportions, retaining the fixed consequents Q₁ and B₁. Repeated application of 5.24, followed by the partition identities in Step 2, gives
P : Q₁ = A : B₁.
Doing the same with the second family gives
Q : Q₁ = B : B₁. [5.24, CN2]

**Step 5.** Invert the latter proportion to obtain
Q₁ : Q = B₁ : B.
This follows directly from the definition of equal ratios: interchanging the two groups of arbitrary equimultiples reverses both corresponding inequalities and preserves equality. [D5.5]

**Step 6.** We now have
P : Q₁ = A : B₁
and
Q₁ : Q = B₁ : B.
Therefore, ex aequali,
P : Q = A : B. [5.22]
Thus pyramids of the same height with polygonal bases are to one another as their bases.
