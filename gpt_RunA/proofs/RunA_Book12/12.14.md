## Proposition 12.14
**Statement:** Cones and cylinders which are on equal bases are to one another as their heights.

**Step 1.** Let C₁ and C₂ be cylinders on equal circular bases B₁ and B₂, with heights h₁ and h₂ respectively. Their heights are the lengths of their axes [D11.21, D11.22]. We first establish the cylinder case using 12.13, since it directly compares the two portions of a cylinder with their axes; we will then transfer the result to cones by 12.10.

**Step 2.** Produce the axis of C₁ beyond its upper base sufficiently far, and cut off on the produced portion a segment equal to h₂ [P2, 1.3]. Extend the generating rectangle of C₁ accordingly, keeping its breadth unchanged, and rotate the extended rectangle about the extended axis. This produces a single cylinder T consisting of C₁ and an additional cylinder E of height h₂ [D11.21, D11.22]. The bases of E are equal to B₁, since they are circles generated by the same radius [D11.23, D3.1]. The original upper-base plane of C₁ separates these two portions and is parallel to the end planes of T, since all three planes are perpendicular to the same axis [11.14].

**Step 3.** Apply 12.13 to this division of T. The two cylinders are to one another as their respective axes; hence
C₁ : E = h₁ : h₂ [12.13].

**Step 4.** The cylinders E and C₂ have the same height h₂, so 12.11 is the direct comparison available here. It gives
E : C₂ = B₁ : B₂ [12.11].
Since B₁ = B₂, the equality clause in the definition of equal ratios, taking one copy of each magnitude, yields E = C₂ [D5.5].

**Step 5.** Equal magnitudes have the same ratio to any fixed magnitude, and a fixed magnitude has the same ratio to equal magnitudes. Thus C₁ : C₂ = C₁ : E [5.7]. Combining this with Step 3 gives
C₁ : C₂ = h₁ : h₂ [5.11].
This proves the assertion for cylinders without any restriction on the relative sizes of their heights.

**Step 6.** Now let K₁ and K₂ be cones on equal bases with heights h₁ and h₂, and take cylinders C₁ and C₂ on their respective bases with those same respective heights [D11.21]. Each cone is one third of its corresponding cylinder, so C₁ and C₂ are respectively the same multiples—namely triples—of K₁ and K₂ [12.10]. Magnitudes have the same ratio as their corresponding equimultiples; therefore K₁ : K₂ = C₁ : C₂ [5.15]. By the cylinder result and equality of ratios,
K₁ : K₂ = h₁ : h₂ [5.11].
Consequently, cones on equal bases are to one another as their heights, and cylinders on equal bases are likewise to one another as their heights.
