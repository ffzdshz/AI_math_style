## Proposition 5.3
**Statement:** If a first magnitude be the same multiple of a second that a third is of a fourth, and if equimultiples be taken of the first and third, then also ex aequali the magnitudes taken will be equimultiples respectively, the one of the second and the other of the fourth.

**Step 1.** Let A be the same multiple of B that C is of D, and let E and F be equimultiples of A and C respectively. Thus, for some common number n of copies, E consists of n copies of A and F consists of n copies of C. [Hypotheses; D5.2]

**Step 2.** For each k from 1 through n, denote the sum of k copies of A by S_k, and the sum of k copies of C by T_k. In particular, S_1 = A and T_1 = C, so S_1 is the same multiple of B that T_1 is of D. Also, S_n = E and T_n = F. [Hypotheses; D5.2]

**Step 3.** We use the addition rule 5.2, since these sums are formed by adjoining corresponding copies. Suppose k < n and S_k is the same multiple of B that T_k is of D. Since A is also the same multiple of B that C is of D, proposition 5.2 gives that S_k + A is the same multiple of B that T_k + C is of D. These sums are precisely S_{k+1} and T_{k+1}. [Hypothesis; 5.2]

**Step 4.** Starting with the first pair in Step 2 and applying Step 3 successively for every further copy, S_n is the same multiple of B that T_n is of D. If n = 1, this is already the initial hypothesis. Since S_n = E and T_n = F, E and F are therefore equimultiples of B and D respectively, as required. [5.2; D5.2]
