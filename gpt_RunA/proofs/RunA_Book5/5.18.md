## Proposition 5.18
**Statement:** If magnitudes be proportional separando, they will also be proportional componendo.

**Step 1.** Let A exceed B and C exceed D, and suppose (A − B):B = (C − D):D. Put X = A − B and Y = C − D. Thus A = X + B, C = Y + D, and X:B = Y:D. We must prove A:B = C:D, the required proportion componendo. [D5.15, D5.14] Path choice: use the equimultiple criterion directly; this does not require magnitudes belonging to different ratio-pairs to be of the same kind. [D5.5]

**Step 2.** Let m and n be arbitrary positive whole numbers. Take the m-fold multiples of A and C and the n-fold multiples of B and D. Repeated addition of A = X + B and C = Y + D gives
mA = mX + mB and mC = mY + mD.
Here multiplication denotes repeated addition of a magnitude. [D5.2, CN2]

**Step 3.** Suppose first that n ≤ m. Then mB ≥ nB and mD ≥ nD: when m = n the respective multiples coincide, and when m > n the smaller multiple is a proper part of the larger. Moreover, mA exceeds its part mB, and mC exceeds its part mD. Consequently mA > nB and mC > nD. [D5.2, CN5]

**Step 4.** Suppose instead that n > m, and put r = n − m, a positive whole number. By repeated addition,
nB = mB + rB and nD = mD + rD. [D5.2, CN2]
Since X:B = Y:D, the defining criterion for equal ratios says that mX and rB have the same comparison as mY and rD: they alike exceed, are alike equal to, or alike fall short of one another in corresponding order. [D5.5]

**Step 5.** Adding the same magnitude to both sides preserves a comparison: equality is preserved by CN2, while a strict inequality is preserved because the larger sum consists of the smaller sum together with the original positive excess. [CN2, CN5] Thus, adding mB to the comparison between mX and rB, and mD to the comparison between mY and rD, shows that
mX + mB and rB + mB have the same comparison as mY + mD and rD + mD.
By the identities in Steps 2 and 4, this says exactly that mA and nB have the same comparison as mC and nD. [CN2, CN5]

**Step 6.** In both cases, therefore, the arbitrary multiples mA and mC alike exceed, are alike equal to, or alike fall short of nB and nD respectively. Hence A:B = C:D by D5.5. The magnitudes are consequently proportional componendo, as required. [D5.6, D5.14]
