## Proposition 5.13
**Statement:** If a first magnitude have to a second the same ratio as a third to a fourth, and the third have to the fourth a greater ratio than a fifth has to a sixth, the first will also have to the second a greater ratio than the fifth to the sixth.

**Step 1.** Let A, B, C, D, E, F be the six magnitudes, with A:B = C:D and C:D > E:F (given). We use the direct definitional route: obtain equimultiples witnessing the greater ratio by D5.7, then transfer the necessary comparison using D5.5.

**Step 2.** Since C:D > E:F, there are positive whole-number multipliers m and n such that mC exceeds nD, but mE does not exceed nF. Here mC and mE are equimultiples of C and E, while nD and nF are equimultiples of D and F. [D5.7]

**Step 3.** Take also the m-fold multiple mA of A and the n-fold multiple nB of B. Since A:B = C:D, the equimultiples mA and mC must alike exceed, equal, or fall short of nB and nD respectively. As mC exceeds nD, mA therefore exceeds nB. [D5.5]

**Step 4.** Thus the equimultiples mA and mE of A and E, compared respectively with the equimultiples nB and nF of B and F, satisfy: mA exceeds nB, but mE does not exceed nF. Therefore A has to B a greater ratio than E has to F, as required. [D5.7]
