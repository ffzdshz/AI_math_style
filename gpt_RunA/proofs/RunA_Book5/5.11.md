## Proposition 5.11
**Statement:** Ratios which are the same with the same ratio are also the same with one another.

**Step 1.** Let the ratios A:B and C:D each be the same as E:F. We shall prove that A:B is the same as C:D. We use the defining equimultiple criterion directly, since both hypotheses supply comparisons with the same pair of magnitudes. [D5.5]

**Step 2.** Let m and n be arbitrary positive whole numbers. Take the equimultiples mA, mC, mE of A, C, E, and the equimultiples nB, nD, nF of B, D, F. These are the arbitrary multiples required by the criterion. [D5.5]

**Step 3.** Since A:B is the same as E:F, the comparison of mA with nB agrees with that of mE with nF: they alike exceed, are alike equal, or alike fall short. [D5.5]

**Step 4.** Since C:D is also the same as E:F, the comparison of mC with nD likewise agrees with that of mE with nF. Consequently, using Step 3:
- If mA > nB, then mE > nF, and hence mC > nD.
- If mA = nB, then mE = nF, and hence mC = nD.
- If mA < nB, then mE < nF, and hence mC < nD.
Thus mA and mC alike exceed, are alike equal to, or alike fall short of nB and nD respectively. [D5.5]

**Step 5.** Because m and n were arbitrary, the defining criterion gives that A:B is the same as C:D. Therefore ratios which are the same with the same ratio are also the same with one another. [D5.5]
