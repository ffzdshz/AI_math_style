## Proposition 5.16
**Statement:** If four magnitudes be proportional, they will also be proportional alternately.

**Step 1.** Let A, B, C, D be the given proportional magnitudes, of the same kind so that the alternate ratios are defined (D5.3). Thus A:B = C:D (D5.6). We must prove A:C = B:D, which is the alternate proportion (D5.12).

**Step 2.** Take arbitrary equimultiples mA, mB of A, B, and arbitrary equimultiples nC, nD of C, D, where m and n are independently chosen positive whole numbers (D5.2).

**Step 3.** By 5.15,
A:B = mA:mB and C:D = nC:nD.
This is the direct path: 5.15 permits each original ratio to be multiplied by its own common multiplier. Combining these equalities with A:B = C:D by 5.11 gives
mA:mB = nC:nD.

**Step 4.** Apply 5.14 to this last proportion. If mA > nC, then mB > nD; if mA = nC, then mB = nD; and if mA < nC, then mB < nD.

**Step 5.** Thus arbitrary equimultiples mA, mB of A, B alike exceed, equal, or fall short of arbitrary equimultiples nC, nD of C, D, respectively. By D5.5, A:C = B:D. Therefore the four magnitudes are proportional alternately (D5.6, D5.12).
