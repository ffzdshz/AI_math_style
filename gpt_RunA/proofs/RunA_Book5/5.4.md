## Proposition 5.4
**Statement:** If a first magnitude have to a second the same ratio as a third to a fourth, any equimultiples whatever of the first and third will also have the same ratio to any equimultiples whatever of the second and fourth respectively, taken in corresponding order.

**Step 1.** Let A have to B the same ratio as C has to D. Let E and G be any equimultiples of A and C respectively, and let F and H independently be any equimultiples of B and D respectively. We must prove that E has to F the same ratio as G has to H. Here multiples are understood in the sense of D5.2.

**Step 2.** Take arbitrary equimultiples X and Z of E and G respectively, and independently take arbitrary equimultiples Y and W of F and H respectively. By D5.5, it suffices to show that X and Z alike exceed, are alike equal to, or alike fall short of Y and W respectively.

**Step 3.** Apply 5.3 directly: it is the natural tool because these test magnitudes are multiples of multiples. Since E and G are equimultiples of A and C, and X and Z are equimultiples of E and G, X and Z are equimultiples of A and C. Likewise, since F and H are equimultiples of B and D, and Y and W are equimultiples of F and H, Y and W are equimultiples of B and D. [5.3]

**Step 4.** Since A has to B the same ratio as C has to D, these equimultiples satisfy the corresponding comparisons: if X exceeds Y, Z exceeds W; if X equals Y, Z equals W; and if X falls short of Y, Z falls short of W. [D5.5]

**Step 5.** Thus arbitrary equimultiples of E and G have matching comparisons with arbitrary equimultiples of F and H respectively. Therefore E has to F the same ratio as G has to H. Since E, G and F, H were any equimultiples of the original magnitudes in the required order, the proposition follows. [D5.5]
