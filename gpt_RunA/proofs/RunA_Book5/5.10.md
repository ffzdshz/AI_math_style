## Proposition 5.10
**Statement:** Of magnitudes which have a ratio to the same, that which has a greater ratio is greater; and that to which the same has a greater ratio is less.

**Step 1.** Path choice: use the defining equimultiples directly, since they turn each hypothesis into a comparison of magnitudes without requiring an auxiliary comparison theorem for ratios. Write kX for the magnitude formed from k equal copies of X (D5.2). Suppose A has to C a greater ratio than B has to C. By D5.7, there are positive whole numbers m,n such that mA > nC while mB ≤ nC. Consequently mA > mB.

**Step 2.** If A = B, repeated addition of equals gives mA = mB (CN2), contradicting Step 1. If A < B, each copy of B consists of a part equal to A together with a nonzero remainder. Adding these m decompositions makes mA a proper part of mB (CN2), and hence mA < mB (CN5), again contradicting Step 1. Thus A is neither equal to nor less than B, and therefore A > B. This proves the first assertion.

**Step 3.** Now suppose C has to A a greater ratio than C has to B. By D5.7, there are positive whole numbers p,q such that pC > qA while pC ≤ qB. Consequently qA < qB.

**Step 4.** If A = B, repeated addition of equals gives qA = qB (CN2), contradicting Step 3. If A > B, each copy of A consists of a part equal to B together with a nonzero remainder. Adding these q decompositions makes qB a proper part of qA (CN2), and hence qA > qB (CN5), again contradicting Step 3. Thus A is neither equal to nor greater than B, and therefore A < B. Hence, of magnitudes having a ratio to the same, that which has the greater ratio is greater; and that to which the same has the greater ratio is less.
