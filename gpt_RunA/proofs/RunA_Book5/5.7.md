## Proposition 5.7
**Statement:** Equal magnitudes have to the same the same ratio, as also has the same to equal magnitudes.

**Step 1.** Let A and B be equal magnitudes, and let C be any magnitude for which the stated ratios are defined. We use the defining criterion of equal ratios directly for both assertions, rather than a transformation of ratios (D5.5).

**Step 2.** Write kX for the magnitude formed by adding k copies of X, with 1X = X; this is its k-fold multiple (D5.2). Since A = B, adding the same number of these equal magnitudes gives kA = kB by repeated application of CN2; for k = 1, the equality is the hypothesis.

**Step 3.** Let m and n be arbitrary positive integers. By Step 2, mA = mB. Consequently, mA exceeds, equals, or falls short of nC exactly when mB respectively exceeds, equals, or falls short of nC. These are precisely the corresponding comparisons between arbitrary equimultiples for the pairs (A, C) and (B, C). Therefore A:C = B:C by D5.5.

**Step 4.** Again let m and n be arbitrary positive integers. By Step 2, nA = nB. Consequently, mC exceeds, equals, or falls short of nA exactly when it respectively exceeds, equals, or falls short of nB. Applying D5.5 to the pairs (C, A) and (C, B) therefore gives C:A = C:B. Together with Step 3, this proves that equal magnitudes have to the same magnitude the same ratio, and that the same magnitude has to equal magnitudes the same ratio.
