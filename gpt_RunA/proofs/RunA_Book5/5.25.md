## Proposition 5.25
**Statement:** If four magnitudes be proportional, the greatest and the least are greater than the remaining two.

**Step 1.** Name the four proportional magnitudes A, B, C, D so that A:B = C:D and A is the greatest; thus A>B and A>C. Such naming is legitimate by exchanging the equal ratios and, if necessary, reversing both: the defining equimultiple comparisons remain equivalent when both ratios are reversed. [D5.6, D5.5]

**Step 2.** Since A:B = C:D and A>C, we have B>D. [5.14] Also, since A>B, the same-ratio definition, with both multipliers equal to one, gives C>D. [D5.5] Consequently D is the least magnitude.

**Step 3.** Let X be the remainder after subtracting C from A, and Y the remainder after subtracting D from B. Both are positive by Steps 1–2. The whole magnitudes A, B have the same ratio as the subtracted parts C, D; therefore
X:Y = A:B. [5.19]
Path choice: the remainder theorem applies directly to the given proportion and produces precisely the two excesses needed, without an additional transformation of ratios.

**Step 4.** Since A>B and X:Y = A:B, the defining comparisons with both multipliers equal to one give X>Y. [D5.5] Thus X consists of Y together with a positive excess E.

**Step 5.** By the definitions of the remainders, A=C+X and B=D+Y. Substituting X=Y+E and adding equal magnitudes gives
A+D = C+Y+E+D = B+C+E. [CN2]
Hence B+C is a proper part of A+D, so A+D>B+C. [CN5] Therefore the greatest and the least together are greater than the remaining two.
