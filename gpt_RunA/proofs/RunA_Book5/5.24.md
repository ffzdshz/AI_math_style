## Proposition 5.24
**Statement:** If a first magnitude have to a second the same ratio as a third has to a fourth, and also a fifth have to the second the same ratio as a sixth to the fourth, the first and fifth added together will have to the second the same ratio as the third and sixth have to the fourth.

**Step 1.** Let the first, second, third, fourth, fifth, and sixth magnitudes be A, B, C, D, E, and F, respectively. By hypothesis, A:B = C:D and E:B = F:D. We must prove (A + E):B = (C + F):D.

**Step 2.** The second hypothesis gives B:E = D:F by the definition of equal ratios. Indeed, for any positive integers m and n, nE has the same comparison with mB as nF has with mD. Reversing both comparisons, mB has the same comparison with nE as mD has with nF. This is precisely B:E = D:F. [D5.5]

**Step 3.** Apply ex aequali to the triples A, B, E and C, D, F. Since A:B = C:D by Step 1 and B:E = D:F by Step 2, we obtain A:E = C:F. Ordinary ex aequali is the natural choice here because the ratios occur in successive, rather than perturbed, order. [5.22]

**Step 4.** By composition of the proportion in Step 3, (A + E):E = (C + F):F. [5.18]

**Step 5.** Apply ex aequali to the triples A + E, E, B and C + F, F, D. Their successive ratios are equal by Step 4 and the hypothesis E:B = F:D. Therefore (A + E):B = (C + F):D: the first and fifth together have to the second the same ratio as the third and sixth together have to the fourth, as required. [5.22]
