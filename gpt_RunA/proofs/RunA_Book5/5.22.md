## Proposition 5.22
**Statement:** If there be any number of magnitudes whatever, and others equal to them in multitude, which taken two and two together are in the same ratio, they will also be in the same ratio ex aequali.

**Step 1.** Let A₁, …, Aₙ and B₁, …, Bₙ be the two collections, with Aᵢ:Aᵢ₊₁ = Bᵢ:Bᵢ₊₁ for each i from 1 to n−1. We must prove A₁:Aₙ = B₁:Bₙ, which is precisely the required ratio ex aequali. For two terms this is already the hypothesis. [D5.17]

**Step 2.** First consider three corresponding terms, denoted A, B, C and D, E, F, satisfying A:B = D:E and B:C = E:F. Let m and n be any positive whole-number multipliers, and write mX for the m-fold multiple of a magnitude X. By 5.4, the two given proportions yield
(mA):(mB) = (mD):(mE),
(mB):(nC) = (mE):(nF).
The direct path is to use 5.4 because it permits the independently chosen multipliers required by the definition of equal ratios. [5.4, D5.5]

**Step 3.** Apply 5.20 to the triples mA, mB, nC and mD, mE, nF, whose successive ratios agree by Step 2. If mA exceeds nC, then mD exceeds nF; if mA equals nC, then mD equals nF; and if mA falls short of nC, then mD falls short of nF. This uses the comparison theorem in the given, unperturbed order. [5.20]

**Step 4.** Since m and n were arbitrary, any equimultiples of A and D alike exceed, equal, or fall short of any equimultiples of C and F, respectively. Therefore A:C = D:F. Thus the required conclusion has been established for any three corresponding terms. [D5.5]

**Step 5.** Return to the original collections. The equality A₁:A₂ = B₁:B₂ is given. Whenever A₁:Aⱼ = B₁:Bⱼ has been established and j < n, combine it with the given equality Aⱼ:Aⱼ₊₁ = Bⱼ:Bⱼ₊₁. Applying the three-term argument of Steps 2–4 to A₁, Aⱼ, Aⱼ₊₁ and B₁, Bⱼ, Bⱼ₊₁ gives A₁:Aⱼ₊₁ = B₁:Bⱼ₊₁. Repeating this finite process reaches A₁:Aₙ = B₁:Bₙ. Hence the collections are in the same ratio ex aequali, as required. [Steps 2–4, D5.17]
