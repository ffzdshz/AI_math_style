## Proposition 5.6
**Statement:** If two magnitudes be equimultiples of two magnitudes, and any magnitudes subtracted from them be equimultiples of the same, the remainders also are either equal to the same or equimultiples of them.

**Step 1.** Let A and B be equimultiples of X and Y respectively, and let C and D, subtracted from A and B, also be equimultiples of X and Y respectively. Denote the remainders by R and S. By the meaning of multiple, A and B consist respectively of the same number n of copies of X and Y, while C and D consist respectively of the same number m of copies of X and Y. We use these equal-copy decompositions and cancellation directly, rather than an argument about ratios. [Hypotheses; D5.2]

**Step 2.** Since C is subtracted as a proper part of A, C is less than A. Consequently m is less than n: if m equalled n, adding the corresponding equal copies would give C = A; if m exceeded n, the first n copies constituting C would equal A, and C would be greater than A because it contains further copies. Both alternatives contradict C being less than A. Thus at least one copy remains after m copies are removed from n copies. [CN5; CN2]

**Step 3.** In the decomposition of A, let U be the sum of its first m copies of X and T the sum of its remaining copies. Likewise, in the decomposition of B, let V be the sum of its first m copies of Y and W the sum of its remaining copies. Adding corresponding equal copies gives U = C and V = D. Moreover, T and W consist of the same positive number of copies of X and Y respectively, since the same number m has been removed from the same number n. [D5.2; CN2; Step 2]

**Step 4.** Subtracting the equal magnitudes C and U from the same whole A gives equal remainders, so R = T. Similarly, subtracting D and V from B gives S = W. [CN3]

**Step 5.** If precisely one copy remains in each decomposition, then T = X and W = Y, hence R = X and S = Y. If more than one copy remains, R and S are measured respectively by X and Y the same number of times, and therefore are equimultiples of them. Thus the remainders are either equal to the original magnitudes respectively or equimultiples of them, as required. [CN1; D5.2; Steps 3–4]
