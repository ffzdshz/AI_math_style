## Proposition 5.23
**Statement:** If there be three magnitudes, and others equal to them in multitude, which taken two and two together are in the same ratio, and the proportion of them be perturbed, they will also be in the same ratio ex aequali.

**Step 1.** Let the three magnitudes be A, B, C, and the other three D, E, F, with A:B = E:F and B:C = D:E, as prescribed by perturbed proportion (D5.18). We must prove A:C = D:F, the required ratio ex aequali (D5.17).

**Step 2.** Let m and n be arbitrary positive integers, and write kX for the k-fold multiple of a magnitude X (D5.2). Take mA, mB, mD and nC, nE, nF. In particular, mA and mD are arbitrary equimultiples of A and D, while nC and nF are arbitrary equimultiples of C and F, as required for testing A:C = D:F by D5.5.

**Step 3.** By 5.15, mA:mB = A:B and nE:nF = E:F. Together with the hypothesis A:B = E:F, these give mA:mB = nE:nF by 5.11. Here 5.15 is the natural choice because both terms within each ratio have been multiplied equally.

**Step 4.** Apply 5.4 to the hypothesis B:C = D:E, taking m-fold multiples of its antecedents and n-fold multiples of its consequents. Thus mB:nC = mD:nE.

**Step 5.** Consequently, the triples mA, mB, nC and mD, nE, nF satisfy the perturbed proportions of D5.18. The direct route is therefore to apply the perturbed comparison theorem 5.21. It gives all three corresponding comparisons: if mA > nC, then mD > nF; if mA = nC, then mD = nF; and if mA < nC, then mD < nF.

**Step 6.** Since m and n were arbitrary, the equimultiples of A and D alike exceed, are alike equal to, or alike fall short of the respective equimultiples of C and F. Therefore A:C = D:F by D5.5. Thus the original triples are in the same ratio ex aequali, as required (D5.17).
