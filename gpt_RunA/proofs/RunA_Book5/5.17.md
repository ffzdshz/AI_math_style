## Proposition 5.17
**Statement:** If magnitudes be proportional componendo, they will also be proportional separando.

**Step 1.** Write the given proportion componendo as
(A + B):B = (C + D):D,
where A, B and C, D are respectively magnitudes of the same kind. We must prove A:B = C:D, the proportion separando. These are precisely the operations described in D5.14 and D5.15. We take the direct route through the equimultiple criterion D5.5.

**Step 2.** Let m and n be arbitrary positive integers, and let mX denote the magnitude formed from m copies of X (D5.2). Grouping the repeated sums, with equality preserved by repeated addition (CN2), gives
m(A + B) = mA + mB,    (m + n)B = mB + nB,
and
m(C + D) = mC + mD,    (m + n)D = mD + nD.

**Step 3.** Apply D5.5 to the given proportion, taking m-fold multiples of its antecedents and (m + n)-fold multiples of its consequents. Thus m(A + B) exceeds, equals, or falls short of (m + n)B exactly as m(C + D) exceeds, equals, or falls short of (m + n)D. By the identities in Step 2, this compares mA + mB with nB + mB, and mC + mD with nD + mD.

**Step 4.** Subtract mB from both magnitudes in the first comparison, and mD from both magnitudes in the second comparison. Each comparison retains its direction: equality is preserved by CN3; in either unequal case, subtracting the common part leaves the same positive excess, so the whole remains greater than the part (CN3, CN5). Consequently, mA exceeds, equals, or falls short of nB exactly as mC exceeds, equals, or falls short of nD.

**Step 5.** Since m and n were arbitrary, D5.5 gives A:B = C:D. Hence the magnitudes are proportional separando (D5.6, D5.15), as required.
