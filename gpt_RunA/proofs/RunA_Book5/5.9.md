## Proposition 5.9
**Statement:** Magnitudes which have the same ratio to the same are equal to one another; and magnitudes to which the same has the same ratio are equal.

**Step 1.** We use both clauses of 5.8 directly, since they compare ratios with precisely the common terms occurring in the two assertions; no inversion of ratios is needed. A greater ratio cannot be the same as the ratio it exceeds: D5.7 supplies equimultiples for which one antecedent multiple exceeds its corresponding consequent multiple while the other does not, whereas D5.5 requires the corresponding comparisons to agree. Thus, of two ratios which are the same, neither can be greater than the other. [5.8; D5.7; D5.5]

**Step 2.** Let A and B have the same ratio to C. If A were greater than B, then A would have to C a greater ratio than B has to C; if B were greater than A, then B would have to C a greater ratio than A has to C. Both implications follow from the first clause of 5.8, and both contradict the given sameness of ratios by Step 1. Therefore A and B are neither greater nor less than one another, and hence A = B. [5.8; D5.5; D5.7]

**Step 3.** Now let C have the same ratio to A as it has to B. If A were greater than B, then C would have to B a greater ratio than it has to A; if B were greater than A, then C would have to A a greater ratio than it has to B. Both implications follow from the second clause of 5.8, and both contradict the given sameness of ratios by Step 1. Therefore again A = B. [5.8; D5.5; D5.7]

**Step 4.** Consequently, magnitudes which have the same ratio to the same are equal to one another, and magnitudes to which the same has the same ratio are equal. [Steps 2–3]
