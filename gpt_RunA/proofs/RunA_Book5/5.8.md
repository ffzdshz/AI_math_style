## Proposition 5.8
**Statement:** Of unequal magnitudes, the greater has to the same a greater ratio than the less has; and the same has to the less a greater ratio than it has to the greater.

**Step 1.** Let A>B, and let C be the common magnitude, of the same kind as required for the stated ratios (D5.3). Write A=B+E, where E is the positive excess of A over B. Write rX for the sum of r copies of a magnitude X (D5.2). Our chosen route is to construct one strict separation mB<nC<mA; D5.7 will then establish both assertions directly.

**Step 2.** Let F be the lesser of B and E, or either if they are equal. By D5.4, choose a positive integer m such that mF>C. Repeated addition of equal magnitudes, together with the whole–part comparison, gives mB≥mF and mE≥mF; hence mB>C and mE>C (CN2, CN5). Moreover, adding m copies of A=B+E gives mA=mB+mE. Since mE>C, it follows that mA>mB+C (CN2, CN5).

**Step 3.** By D5.4, some multiple of C exceeds mB. In the finite sequence of successive multiples up to such a multiple, let nC be the first that exceeds mB. Since C<mB by Step 2, n is at least two. Thus
mB<nC, while (n−1)C≤mB.
These are successive multiples in the sense of D5.2; their selection is possible by D5.4.

**Step 4.** Adding C to the latter comparison gives
nC≤mB+C
(CN2, CN5). Combining this with Step 2 and the former comparison in Step 3 yields the strict separation
mB<nC<mA
(CN5).

**Step 5.** Compare A:C with B:C. Take the equimultiples mA and mB of the antecedents, and the equimultiples nC and nC of the consequents. By Step 4, mA exceeds nC, whereas mB does not exceed nC. Therefore A has to C a greater ratio than B has to C (D5.7).

**Step 6.** Compare C:B with C:A. Take the equimultiples nC and nC of the antecedents, and the equimultiples mB and mA of the consequents. By Step 4, nC exceeds mB, whereas nC does not exceed mA. Therefore C has to B a greater ratio than C has to A (D5.7). Thus the greater magnitude has to the same a greater ratio than the less has, and the same has to the less a greater ratio than it has to the greater.
