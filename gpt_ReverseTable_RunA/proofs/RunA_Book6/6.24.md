## Proposition 6.24
**Statement:** In any parallelogram the parallelograms about the diameter are similar both to the whole and to one another.

**Step 1.** Let ABCD be a parallelogram, with vertices in order, and let AC be its diameter. Take any point P inside AC. Through P draw FG parallel to AB, meeting AD at F and BC at G, and EH parallel to BC, meeting AB at E and CD at H. The parallelograms about the diameter are AEPF and PGCH. These parallels can be drawn by [1.31].

**Step 2.** The triangles AEP and ABC are equiangular: AE lies along AB, AP lies along AC, and EP is parallel to BC. Likewise, triangles PGC and ABC are equiangular: PG is parallel to AB, GC lies along BC, and PC lies along AC. The required angle equalities follow from the angles made by transversals with parallel lines. [1.29]

**Step 3.** We use these equiangular triangles directly, since the diameter already supplies the triangles needed for the side proportions. By [6.4],
AE : EP = AB : BC,
and
PG : GC = AB : BC.

**Step 4.** Opposite sides of a parallelogram are equal. Thus
AE = PF, EP = FA;
PG = CH, GC = HP;
AB = CD, BC = DA.
Consequently, the first proportion in Step 3 gives
AE : FA = AB : DA,
AE : EP = AB : BC,
PF : EP = CD : BC,
PF : FA = CD : DA.
These are the proportions between the sides about the corresponding vertices A, E, P, F and A, B, C, D. Similarly, the second proportion gives
PG : PH = AB : AD,
PG : GC = AB : BC,
CH : CG = CD : CB,
HC : HP = DC : DA,
which are the required proportions at the corresponding vertices P, G, C, H and A, B, C, D. [1.34]

**Step 5.** The parallelograms AEPF and ABCD are equiangular: their angles at A agree, their angles at E and B agree by parallelism, and their remaining corresponding angles agree because opposite angles of parallelograms are equal. Likewise, PGCH and ABCD are equiangular: their angles at C agree, their angles at G and B agree by parallelism, and equality of opposite angles supplies the other two pairs. [1.29, 1.34] Together with Step 4, the definition of similar rectilineal figures therefore shows that AEPF and PGCH are each similar to ABCD. [D6.1]

**Step 6.** Figures similar to the same rectilineal figure are similar to one another. Hence AEPF and PGCH are also similar to one another. Thus, in any parallelogram, the parallelograms about the diameter are similar both to the whole and to one another. [6.21]
