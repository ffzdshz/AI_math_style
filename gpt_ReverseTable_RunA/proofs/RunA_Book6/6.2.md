## Proposition 6.2
**Statement:** If a straight line be drawn parallel to one of the sides of a triangle, it will cut the sides of the triangle proportionally; and, if the sides of the triangle be cut proportionally, the line joining the points of section will be parallel to the remaining side of the triangle.

**Step 1.** Let ABC be a triangle, with D in the interior of AB and E in the interior of AC. Join DE, BE, and CD. Write [XYZ] for the area of triangle XYZ. We shall prove that DE is parallel to BC if and only if AD:DB = AE:EC. [P1]

**Step 2.** Use the direct area route, since the divided segments are bases of triangles of the same height. Triangles ADE and BDE have their bases AD and DB on AB and the common vertex E; triangles ADE and CDE have their bases AE and EC on AC and the common vertex D. Hence
AD:DB = [ADE]:[BDE],
and
AE:EC = [ADE]:[CDE]. [6.1]

**Step 3.** First suppose DE is parallel to BC. Triangles BDE and CDE stand on the same base DE and lie between the same parallels DE and BC. Therefore [BDE] = [CDE]. [1.37]

**Step 4.** The same magnitude has the same ratio to equal magnitudes, so
[ADE]:[BDE] = [ADE]:[CDE].
Combining this with Step 2, by equality of ratios, gives AD:DB = AE:EC. Thus the parallel cuts the two sides proportionally. [5.7, 5.11]

**Step 5.** Conversely, suppose AD:DB = AE:EC. The two identities in Step 2 and equality of ratios give
[ADE]:[BDE] = [ADE]:[CDE]. [5.11]

**Step 6.** Magnitudes to which the same magnitude has the same ratio are equal. Consequently [BDE] = [CDE]. [5.9]

**Step 7.** Because D and E are interior points of AB and AC, B and C lie on the same side of DE. Thus the equal triangles BDE and CDE stand on the same base DE and on the same side of it. Apply the direct converse for such triangles, rather than constructing an auxiliary parallel: they lie in the same parallels. Their opposite vertices B and C therefore lie on a straight line parallel to DE; that line is BC. Hence DE is parallel to BC. Both assertions are proved. [1.39]
