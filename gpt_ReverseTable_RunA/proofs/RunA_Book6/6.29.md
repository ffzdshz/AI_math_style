## Proposition 6.29
**Statement:** To a given straight line to apply a parallelogram equal to a given rectilineal figure and exceeding by a parallelogrammic figure similar to a given one.

**Step 1.** Let AB be the given straight line, C the given rectilineal figure, and K the parallelogram to which the excess must be similar. Write [F] for the area of a figure F. We use the midpoint-and-complements construction, enlarging a similar parallelogram by the given area. Bisect AB at E, and on EB describe the parallelogram EBFD similar and similarly situated to K. Denote this parallelogram also by S = DFBE, listing its vertices from D. [1.10, 6.18]

**Step 2.** Form a single rectilineal figure T of area [S] + [C] as follows. Produce BF beyond F, and in the exterior angle between FD and this produced ray construct a parallelogram equal to C. It lies on the opposite side of DF from S and shares a boundary segment with S; their union is the required rectilineal figure T. Construct a parallelogram R similar to S and equal to T. Thus [R] = [S] + [C] > [S]. [P2, 1.45, CN2, 6.25, CN5]

**Step 3.** On the rays DF and DE take X and H respectively so that DX and DH equal the corresponding adjacent sides of R, and complete the parallelogram DXGH by parallels. The included angle at D equals the corresponding angle of R, so this parallelogram coincides with R upon superposition. Consequently it is similar and similarly situated to S, and
[DXGH] = [S] + [C].
Moreover, similarity gives DX : DH = DF : DE. If DX were less than or equal to DF, then DH would respectively be less than or equal to DE; DXGH would therefore be contained in S or coincide with S, contrary to its greater area. Hence DX > DF and DH > DE: X lies beyond F, and H beyond E. [P2, 1.3, 1.31, D6.1, CN4, 5.14, CN5]

**Step 4.** The smaller parallelogram DFBE lies within DXGH, is similar and similarly situated to it, and shares its angle at D. Proposition VI.26 applies directly because of this shared-angle arrangement: the two parallelograms are about the same diameter. Therefore B lies on the diameter DG. [6.26]

**Step 5.** Produce AB to meet XG at P. Through B draw a parallel to DH, meeting HG at Q. Through A draw another parallel to DH, meeting HG produced at L. Thus A, E, B, P lie in that order on one straight line, and L, H, Q, G lie in that order on another. The figure APGL is a parallelogram applied along AB and extended beyond B; its excess is precisely the parallelogram BPGQ. [P2, 1.31]

**Step 6.** Since B lies on DG, the parallelograms DFBE and BPGQ are the two parallelograms about the diameter of DXGH. They are therefore similar to one another. Since DFBE is similar to K, BPGQ is also similar to K. [6.24, 6.21]

**Step 7.** The other two parallelograms, EBQH and FXPB, are the complements about that diameter, and hence
[EBQH] = [FXPB].
Also AE = EB, and the parallelograms AEHL and EBQH stand on these equal bases between the same parallels AP and LG. Therefore
[AEHL] = [EBQH] = [FXPB].
[1.43, 1.36, CN1]

**Step 8.** The parallelogram APGL consists of AEHL, EBQH, and BPGQ. The remainder of DXGH after removing S consists of FXPB, EBQH, and BPGQ. Substituting the equal areas AEHL and FXPB gives
[APGL] = [DXGH] − [S] = ([S] + [C]) − [S] = [C].
Thus APGL is equal to the given rectilineal figure C, is applied to the given straight line AB, and exceeds by the parallelogram BPGQ similar to K. The required construction is complete. [CN2, CN3]
