## Proposition 6.31
**Statement:** In right - angled triangles the figure on the side subtending the right angle is equal to the similar and similarly described figures on the sides containing the right angle.

**Step 1.** Let ABC be right-angled at A. Let F, G, and H denote the areas of the similar and similarly described figures on BC, AB, and AC, respectively. We use the altitude to divide the given triangle into two similar triangles. Draw AD perpendicular to BC, meeting the hypotenuse at D. [1.12]

**Step 2.** The triangles ABD and ACD are each similar to ABC. Write U, V, and T for the areas of ABD, ACD, and ABC, respectively. Their corresponding hypotenuses are AB, AC, and BC, and the division by AD gives U + V = T. [6.8]

**Step 3.** By the earlier result specifically concerning similar triangles, U:T is the duplicate ratio of AB:BC, and V:T is the duplicate ratio of AC:BC. For the prescribed similar figures, the polygonal result gives G:F and H:F as those same respective duplicate ratios; if the figures are triangles, the triangle result itself applies. Therefore
U:T = G:F, and V:T = H:F.
[6.19, 6.20, 5.11]

**Step 4.** Alternating these proportions gives
G:U = F:T, and H:V = F:T.
Consequently G:U = H:V. This choice of figure-to-triangle ratios permits the two corresponding pairs to be added directly. [5.16, 5.11]

**Step 5.** Since G:U = H:V, the sum of the antecedents has to the sum of the consequents the same ratio:
(G + H):(U + V) = G:U = F:T.
Using U + V = T, we obtain
(G + H):T = F:T.
[5.12, 5.11]

**Step 6.** Magnitudes having the same ratio to the same magnitude are equal. Hence G + H = F. Thus the figure on the side subtending the right angle is equal to the similar and similarly described figures on the sides containing the right angle, taken together. [5.9]
