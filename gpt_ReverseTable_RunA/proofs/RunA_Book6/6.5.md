## Proposition 6.5
**Statement:** If two triangles have their sides proportional, the triangles will be equiangular and will have those angles equal which the corresponding sides subtend.

**Step 1.** Let triangles ABC and DEF have their corresponding sides AB, BC, CA and DE, EF, FD proportional. Thus, by hypothesis,
AB:BC = DE:EF and BC:CA = EF:FD.
We will construct an auxiliary triangle equiangular with ABC on the side DE, use the forward implication in 6.4, and then compare equal sides by 1.8.

**Step 2.** On the same side of DE as F, construct at D an angle equal to ∠BAC and at E an angle equal to ∠ABC [1.23]. These two angles together are less than two right angles [1.17]. Therefore their other arms meet on that side of DE at a point G [P5]. Hence
∠GDE = ∠BAC and ∠DEG = ∠ABC.

**Step 3.** The angles of each of triangles DEG and ABC sum to two right angles [1.32]. Subtracting the two respective equal angles gives
∠DGE = ∠ACB [CN3].
Thus triangles DEG and ABC are equiangular.

**Step 4.** Apply 6.4, the natural forward-direction result for this equiangular pair. Corresponding sides are proportional, so
DE:EG = AB:BC and EG:GD = BC:CA [6.4].
Comparing these ratios with the hypothesized ratios yields
DE:EG = DE:EF and EG:GD = EF:FD [5.11].

**Step 5.** Since DE has the same ratio to EG and EF, we have EG = EF [5.9]. Substituting this equality into the second ratio equality gives
EF:GD = EF:FD.
Therefore GD = FD, again by 5.9.

**Step 6.** Triangles DEG and DEF now have DE common, EG = EF, and GD = FD. Applying 1.8 with each corresponding pair of sides as the two sides containing the angle gives
∠GDE = ∠FDE,
∠DEG = ∠DEF,
and ∠DGE = ∠DFE [1.8].
This side-equality comparison is appropriate because all three corresponding sides have been established equal.

**Step 7.** Combining these equalities with Steps 2–3 gives
∠BAC = ∠FDE,
∠ABC = ∠DEF,
and ∠ACB = ∠DFE [CN1].
Consequently the original triangles are equiangular: the angles opposite BC and EF are equal, those opposite CA and FD are equal, and those opposite AB and DE are equal. Thus equal angles are precisely those subtended by corresponding sides, as required.
