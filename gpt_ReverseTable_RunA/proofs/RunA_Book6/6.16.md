## Proposition 6.16
**Statement:** If four straight lines be proportional, the rectangle contained by the extremes is equal to the rectangle contained by the means; and, if the rectangle contained by the extremes be equal to the rectangle contained by the means, the four straight lines will be proportional.

**Step 1.** Let the four straight lines, in their given order, be a, b, c, d. Consider the rectangles R and S contained respectively by a, d and by b, c. Thus R and S are rectangular parallelograms with these pairs of adjacent sides [D2.1]. Since all right angles are equal, they are equiangular [P4]. We use 6.14 directly: its two directions concern precisely equality and reciprocal proportionality in equiangular parallelograms.

**Step 2.** Suppose the four straight lines are proportional, so that a:b = c:d [D5.6]. Here a and d are the sides about a right angle of R, while b and c are the sides about a right angle of S. The given proportion compares a side of R with a side of S, and the other side of S with the other side of R; hence these sides are reciprocally proportional [D6.2]. Therefore the equiangular parallelograms R and S are equal [6.14]. That is, the rectangle contained by the extremes a, d equals the rectangle contained by the means b, c [D2.1].

**Step 3.** Conversely, suppose the rectangle contained by a, d equals the rectangle contained by b, c. Then R and S are equal equiangular parallelograms. Their sides about the equal right angles are consequently reciprocally proportional [6.14]. Comparing a with b, this gives a:b = c:d [D6.2]. Thus the four straight lines a, b, c, d are proportional [D5.6]. Both assertions are proved.
