## Proposition 6.19
**Statement:** Similar triangles are to one another in the duplicate ratio of the corresponding sides.

**Step 1.** Let ABC and DEF be similar triangles, with A, B, C corresponding respectively to D, E, F. Write [XYZ] for the area of triangle XYZ. By the definition of similar figures,
∠ABC = ∠DEF and AB:DE = BC:EF. [D6.1]

**Step 2.** We use a third proportional, since this leads directly to the duplicate ratio. Construct a length t such that BC:EF = EF:t. On the ray from B through C, producing BC if necessary, mark G using a circle with centre B and radius t, and join AG. Thus
BC:EF = EF:BG. [6.11, P2, P3, P1]

**Step 3.** The proportions in Steps 1 and 2 give
AB:DE = EF:BG.
Also, since BG is on the ray BC, ∠ABG = ∠ABC = ∠DEF. Hence triangles ABG and DEF have an equal included angle and reciprocally proportional sides about that angle. They are therefore equal:
[ABG] = [DEF]. [5.11, 6.15]

**Step 4.** Triangles ABC and ABG have their bases BC and BG on the same straight line and have the same altitude from A. Consequently,
[ABC]:[ABG] = BC:BG.
Replacing [ABG] by the equal magnitude [DEF] gives
[ABC]:[DEF] = BC:BG. [6.1, 5.7, 5.11]

**Step 5.** Since BC:EF = EF:BG, the three straight lines BC, EF, BG are proportional. Thus BC:BG is the duplicate ratio of BC:EF. By Step 4, the ratio of triangle ABC to triangle DEF is therefore the duplicate ratio of their corresponding sides BC and EF. These were arbitrarily named corresponding sides, so similar triangles are to one another in the duplicate ratio of the corresponding sides. [D5.9]
