## Proposition 6.33
**Statement:** In equal circles angles have the same ratio as the circumferences on which they stand, whether they stand at the centres or at the circumferences.

**Step 1.** Let two equal circles have centres O and P. Let a and b be the respective circumferences (arcs) BC and EF, and let α and β be the central angles standing on them. We first prove α : β = a : b by the equimultiple definition of equal ratios. The natural direction needed is that equal arcs give equal central angles, so we use III.27. [D5.5, 3.27]

**Step 2.** Take any positive integers m and n. Lay m copies of a consecutively around the first circle and n copies of b consecutively around the second. If a succession passes through a complete revolution, retain that revolution as part of the succession rather than identifying the total with its final residual arc. The circles have equal radii. Each copy of a subtends an angle equal to α, and each copy of b an angle equal to β. Consequently the arc totals ma and nb have corresponding angular sums mα and nβ. These are sums of angular magnitudes and need not be single angles between two rays. [D3.1, 3.27, CN2]

**Step 3.** Compare these two arc successions from a common starting radius and in the same direction, superposing their equal circular tracks and retaining the successive revolutions. Subdivide their common initial portion at the endpoints of all constituent copies, adding further divisions if necessary so that each piece is a proper arc. Corresponding pieces coincide and are therefore equal; their central angles are equal by III.27, and the sums of those angles are equal by addition.

If ma = nb, this common portion exhausts both successions, so mα = nβ. If ma > nb, the first succession consists of the common portion followed by a nonzero arc remainder. Its angular sum therefore consists of the equal common angular sum together with a positive remainder, so mα > nβ. If ma < nb, the same argument with the successions interchanged gives mα < nβ. Keeping complete revolutions as separate consecutive portions makes this argument applicable to every m and n, without any restriction to totals smaller than one circumference. [CN4, 3.27, CN2, CN5]

**Step 4.** Thus, for every choice of m and n, mα and nβ alike exceed, equal, or fall short of one another exactly as ma and nb do. By the definition of equal ratios,
α : β = a : b.
This proves the assertion for angles at the centres. [D5.5]

**Step 5.** Now let θ and φ be angles at the circumferences standing respectively on the same arcs a and b. The angle at the centre is double the angle at the circumference on the same arc. Hence α = 2θ and β = 2φ. [3.20]

**Step 6.** Parts have the same ratio as their corresponding equal multiples; therefore
θ : φ = 2θ : 2φ = α : β.
Combining this with Step 4 gives θ : φ = a : b. Thus, in equal circles, angles have the same ratio as the circumferences on which they stand, both at the centres and at the circumferences. [5.15, 5.11]
