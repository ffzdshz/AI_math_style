## Proposition 6.27
**Statement:** Of all the parallelograms applied to the same straight line and deficient by parallelogrammic figures similar and similarly situated to that described on the half of the straight line, that parallelogram is greatest which is applied to the half of the straight line and is similar to the defect.

**Step 1.** Write [R] for the area of a figure R. Bisect the given straight line AB at E, and let H = EBCD be the prescribed parallelogram on EB. Complete Q = AEDF on AE, with F, D, C collinear, by drawing parallels (1.10, 1.31). Opposite sides of parallelograms are equal, so FD = AE = EB = DC, and AF = ED = BC (1.34, CN1). Corresponding angles of Q and H are equal by parallelism; hence Q and H are similar and similarly situated (1.29, D6.1). Moreover, they are on the equal bases AE and EB and between the same parallels, so [Q] = [H] (1.36).

**Step 2.** Let P = AXYZ be any parallelogram applied to AB, deficient by T = XBWY, where X lies between A and B and T is similar and similarly situated to H. Thus BW lies on the ray BC, and EB:BC = XB:BW (D6.1). Consequently, if X lies between E and B, T is contained in H; if X lies between A and E, H is contained in T: the corresponding side BW is respectively shorter or longer than BC (5.14). If X = E, the same proposition gives BW = BC; the figures coincide, and P = Q (CN4). It remains to consider the two strict cases.

**Step 3.** Suppose first that X lies between E and B. The smaller defect T is similar and similarly situated to H and shares its angle at B. Therefore T is about the same diameter as H, so Y lies on BD (6.26). This common-diameter route applies directly to the nested defects and gives equal complements. Let L be the intersection of the side-line YW with ED, and N the intersection of the side-line XY with DC. The complements EXYL and YWCN in H are equal (1.43). Also, AELZ and EBWL are on the equal bases AE and EB and between the same parallels AB and ZW; hence they are equal (1.36).

**Step 4.** The divisions just described give
[P] = [AELZ] + [EXYL],
[H] = [EBWL] + [YWCN] + [LYND].
Substituting the equal areas from Step 3 yields
[H] = [P] + [LYND]
(CN1, CN2). Here LYND is a nondegenerate parallelogram, since X is strictly between E and B.

**Step 5.** Now suppose that X lies between A and E. The smaller defect H is similar and similarly situated to T and shares its angle at B. Therefore H is about the same diameter as T, and D lies on BY (6.26). Let U be the intersection of XY with the line FDC, and V the intersection of the side-line ED with ZW. The complements XEDU and DCWV in T are consequently equal:
[XEDU] = [DCWV]
(1.43).

**Step 6.** Since FD = DC by Step 1, the parallelograms FDVZ and DCWV are on equal bases and between the same parallels FC and ZW. Thus [FDVZ] = [DCWV] (1.36), and therefore [XEDU] = [FDVZ] (CN1). Dividing the figures along their indicated sides now gives
[Q] = [AXUF] + [XEDU]
= [AXUF] + [FDVZ]
= [AXUF] + [FUYZ] + [UDVY]
= [P] + [UDVY]
(CN2). The parallelogram UDVY is nondegenerate, since X is strictly between A and E.

**Step 7.** In the first strict case, Step 4 and [Q] = [H] show that Q equals P together with the positive parallelogram LYND. In the second strict case, Step 6 shows that Q equals P together with the positive parallelogram UDVY. Hence [Q] > [P] in either case, since the whole is greater than the part (CN1, CN5). Equality occurs when X = E, as established in Step 2. Therefore the greatest of all the stated applied parallelograms is Q, which is applied to half of AB and is similar to its defect. This proves the proposition.
