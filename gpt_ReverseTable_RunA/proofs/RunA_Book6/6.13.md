## Proposition 6.13
**Statement:** To two given straight lines to find a mean proportional.

**Step 1.** Let the two given straight lines have lengths a and b. Take AB to be the first given line and produce it beyond B. Describe a circle with centre B and radius equal to the second given line, meeting the produced ray at C. Thus A, B, C lie in this order on one straight line, with AB = a and BC = b. [P2, P3, D1.15]

**Step 2.** Bisect AC at O and describe the circle with centre O and radius OA. Since OA = OC, both A and C lie on this circle, and AC is a diameter. [1.10, P3, D1.15, D1.17]

**Step 3.** At B draw a perpendicular to AC, and produce it to meet either semicircle at D. Join AD and CD. [1.11, P2, P1]

**Step 4.** Since AC is a diameter, angle ADC is right. Therefore DB is the perpendicular from the right angle of triangle ADC to its base AC. Use the altitude-similarity route: VI.8 directly gives the similarity of triangles ABD and DBC, with corresponding vertices A ↔ D, B ↔ B, and D ↔ C. [3.31, 6.8]

**Step 5.** The corresponding sides of these similar triangles are proportional, so AB : BD = BD : BC. Since AB and BC equal the two given straight lines, BD is their mean proportional. Thus the required straight line has been found. [D6.1]
