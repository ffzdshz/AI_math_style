## Proposition 6.23
**Statement:** Equiangular parallelograms have to one another the ratio compounded of the ratios of their sides.

**Step 1.** Let the given equiangular parallelograms be ABCD and AEFG. Place their equal angles at A together, with AB and AE on one ray and AD and AG on the other. Such superposition preserves the figures and their magnitudes. Write P and Q for their respective areas. We must show that P:Q is compounded of AB:AE and AD:AG. [CN4]

**Step 2.** Complete the auxiliary parallelogram AEHD by drawing through E a parallel to AD and through D a parallel to AE, meeting at H. Write R for its area. This mixed-side parallelogram is the direct path: it allows both required comparisons to be made between parallelograms of the same height. [1.31]

**Step 3.** The parallelograms ABCD and AEHD have bases AB and AE on the same straight line, and their opposite sides lie on the same parallel through D. Therefore
P:R = AB:AE.
Likewise, AEHD and AEFG, taken on bases AD and AG, have their opposite sides on the same parallel through E. Therefore
R:Q = AD:AG. [6.1]

**Step 4.** Take any straight-line magnitude x. Find a fourth proportional y to AB, AE, x, and then a fourth proportional z to AD, AG, y. Thus
AB:AE = x:y, and AD:AG = y:z.
Combining these with Step 3 gives
P:R = x:y, and R:Q = y:z. [6.12, 5.11]

**Step 5.** The three areas P, R, Q and the three lengths x, y, z have their successive ratios equal in the same order. Hence, by ordinary ex aequali,
P:Q = x:z. [5.22]

**Step 6.** The ratio x:z is compounded of x:y and y:z, since the sizes of those two ratios multiplied together give the size of x:z. By Step 4 these factors are respectively the ratios AB:AE and AD:AG, and by Step 5 their compounded ratio is P:Q. Therefore the given equiangular parallelograms have to one another the ratio compounded of the ratios of their corresponding sides about the equal angles. [D6.5]
