## Proposition 6.20
**Statement:** Similar polygons are divided into similar triangles, and into triangles equal in multitude and in the same ratio as the wholes, and the polygon has to the polygon a ratio duplicate of that which the corresponding side has to the corresponding  side.

**Step 1.** Let the similar polygons be P = A₁A₂…Aₙ and Q = B₁B₂…Bₙ, with vertices in corresponding order. Their corresponding angles are equal, and the sides about those angles are proportional. By alternation, the ratios of corresponding boundary sides are equal; denote their common ratio by r. Thus AᵢAᵢ₊₁ : BᵢBᵢ₊₁ = r, including the closing sides. [D6.1, 5.16, 5.11]

**Step 2.** Join A₁ to A₃,…,Aₙ₋₁, and B₁ to B₃,…,Bₙ₋₁. These fan dissections divide the polygons into the respective triangles
Tᵢ = A₁AᵢAᵢ₊₁ and Uᵢ = B₁BᵢBᵢ₊₁, for i = 2,…,n−1.
Each dissection therefore contains n−2 triangles. We use this dissection because successive triangle angles can be obtained by subtracting already matched angles from corresponding polygon angles. [P1]

**Step 3.** In T₂ and U₂, the angles at A₂ and B₂ are equal, and A₁A₂ : A₂A₃ = B₁B₂ : B₂B₃. Apply the two-sides-and-included-angle criterion: it is the direct choice because its hypotheses are precisely the given polygon angle and its adjacent sides. The triangles are equiangular, and consequently their corresponding sides are proportional; hence they are similar. In particular, by alternation,
A₁A₃ : B₁B₃ = A₁A₂ : B₁B₂ = r. [D6.1, 6.6, 6.4, 5.16]

**Step 4.** Suppose Tᵢ₋₁ and Uᵢ₋₁ have already been shown similar, with A₁Aᵢ : B₁Bᵢ = r, where 3 ≤ i ≤ n−1. Subtract their equal angles Aᵢ₋₁AᵢA₁ and Bᵢ₋₁BᵢB₁ from the equal polygon angles Aᵢ₋₁AᵢAᵢ₊₁ and Bᵢ₋₁BᵢBᵢ₊₁. The remaining angles A₁AᵢAᵢ₊₁ and B₁BᵢBᵢ₊₁ are equal. Moreover,
A₁Aᵢ : B₁Bᵢ = AᵢAᵢ₊₁ : BᵢBᵢ₊₁,
so, alternately,
A₁Aᵢ : AᵢAᵢ₊₁ = B₁Bᵢ : BᵢBᵢ₊₁.
The same included-angle criterion makes Tᵢ and Uᵢ equiangular, and their sides are therefore proportional. They are similar, and alternation gives A₁Aᵢ₊₁ : B₁Bᵢ₊₁ = r. Repeating this argument proves that every corresponding triangle pair is similar. [D6.1, CN3, 5.11, 5.16, 6.6, 6.4]

**Step 5.** For each i = 3,…,n−1, the successive triangles Tᵢ₋₁ and Tᵢ share the side A₁Aᵢ, while Uᵢ₋₁ and Uᵢ share B₁Bᵢ. Similar triangles are in the duplicate ratio of corresponding sides. Consequently, both Tᵢ₋₁ : Uᵢ₋₁ and Tᵢ : Uᵢ are the duplicate of the very same ratio A₁Aᵢ : B₁Bᵢ, and hence are equal. It follows successively that
T₂ : U₂ = T₃ : U₃ = ⋯ = Tₙ₋₁ : Uₙ₋₁.
For n = 3 there is just one triangle pair. [6.19, 5.11]

**Step 6.** Since these triangle-area ratios are equal, one antecedent is to its consequent as the sum of all antecedents is to the sum of all consequents. The sums are exactly the two polygons. Therefore, for every i,
Tᵢ : Uᵢ = (T₂ + ⋯ + Tₙ₋₁) : (U₂ + ⋯ + Uₙ₋₁) = P : Q.
Thus the corresponding triangles are in the same ratio as the whole polygons. [5.12]

**Step 7.** In particular, P : Q = T₂ : U₂, and the latter is the duplicate ratio of A₁A₂ : B₁B₂. More generally, any chosen boundary side belongs to one of the fan triangles, and its corresponding side belongs to the corresponding triangle. Applying the same similar-triangle theorem to that pair gives the same conclusion for those sides. Hence the similar polygons are divided into similar triangles equal in multitude and in the same ratio as the wholes, and the polygon has to the polygon the duplicate ratio of any corresponding side to its corresponding side. [6.19, 5.11]
