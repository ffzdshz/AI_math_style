## Proposition 6.25
**Statement:** To construct one and the same figure similar to a given rectilineal figure and equal to another given rectilineal figure.

**Step 1.** Let F be the given rectilineal figure to which the required figure must be similar, and G the given rectilineal figure to which it must be equal. Construct squares S and T equal to F and G respectively, and denote their sides by s and t (2.14). We choose this square-reduction route because the similar-polygon area theorem will then give both needed area comparisons directly.

**Step 2.** Choose a side a of F. Construct a fourth proportional x to s, t, a, so that s:t = a:x (6.12).

**Step 3.** On x construct a rectilineal figure H similar and similarly situated to F, with x corresponding to a (6.18).

**Step 4.** The squares S and T are similar: their angles are equal right angles, and their corresponding sides are proportional because each square is equilateral (D1.22, P4, D6.1). By 6.20, S:T is the duplicate ratio of s:t, and F:H is the duplicate ratio of a:x. Since s:t = a:x, these are the same duplicate ratio; hence S:T = F:H (D5.9, 5.11). Here 6.20 is used directly because it applies to both pairs of similar polygons.

**Step 5.** Since S equals F and T equals G, the preceding equality gives F:G = F:H. Magnitudes to which the same magnitude has the same ratio are equal; therefore G equals H (5.9). Thus H is both similar to F and equal to G, as required.
