## Proposition 6.17
**Statement:** If three straight lines be proportional, the rectangle contained by the extremes is equal to the square on the mean; and, if the rectangle contained by the extremes be equal to the square on the mean, the three straight lines will be proportional.

**Step 1.** Let the three straight lines, in order, be A, B, C. The square on B is precisely the rectangle contained by B and B: it is right-angled and all its sides equal B. [D1.22, D2.1] We use 6.16 directly, with B occupying both middle places, because that proposition already provides both required implications. [6.16]

**Step 2.** Suppose A, B, C are proportional; that is, A:B = B:C. [D5.6] Apply 6.16 to the four terms A, B, B, C. The rectangle contained by the extremes A, C equals the rectangle contained by the means B, B, which, by Step 1, is the square on B. Thus the rectangle contained by the extremes equals the square on the mean. [6.16]

**Step 3.** Conversely, suppose the rectangle contained by A, C equals the square on B. By Step 1, it therefore equals the rectangle contained by B, B. The converse of 6.16, applied to A, B, B, C, gives A:B = B:C. Hence A, B, C are proportional. [6.16, D5.6] Both directions are proved.
