## Proposition 6.18
**Statement:** On a given straight line to describe a rectilineal figure similar and similarly situated to a given rectilineal figure.

**Step 1.** Let AB be the given straight line, and let C₁C₂…Cₙ be the given rectilineal figure, with C₁C₂ the side to which AB is to correspond. Draw non-crossing diagonals dividing the figure into triangles, and begin with the triangle having C₁C₂ as a side. We choose to copy these triangles by their angles and then deduce proportionality, rather than prescribe their side lengths. [P1]

**Step 2.** The required triangle construction is as follows. If XYZ is one of the original triangles and xy is the already constructed side corresponding to XY, construct at x and y, on the prescribed side of xy, angles equal respectively to ∠YXZ and ∠XYZ. This is possible by I.23. Their sum is less than two right angles, since the three angles of XYZ equal two right angles. Therefore the two constructed rays meet at a point z. The remaining angle at z equals the angle at Z, by subtracting the equal sums of the other two angles from two right angles. Thus xyz and XYZ are equiangular, with the stated correspondence. [1.23, 1.32, CN5, P5, CN2, CN3]

**Step 3.** Apply this construction first on AB to copy the triangle adjoining C₁C₂. Then, working through the dissection, copy each adjoining triangle on the already constructed side corresponding to their shared diagonal. Put each new triangle on the side of that diagonal corresponding to its position in the original figure. Continue until every triangle has been copied, preserving the incidences and cyclic order of the original dissection. Denote the resulting boundary by A₁A₂…Aₙ, where A₁ = A and A₂ = B. Every original triangle and its copy are equiangular by Step 2. [1.23, 1.32, P5]

**Step 4.** Within any pair of corresponding triangles, let r and s be two original sides and r′ and s′ their copies. Since the triangles are equiangular, VI.4 gives r:s = r′:s′. Alternating gives r:r′ = s:s′. Hence all three sides of each triangle have the same ratio to their corresponding constructed sides. [6.4, 5.16]

**Step 5.** Two adjoining triangles share a diagonal, and their copies share its corresponding diagonal. Consequently the original-to-copy ratios obtained in Step 4 agree for adjoining triangles. Passing through the connected dissection, all corresponding sides therefore have the same ratio, namely C₁C₂:AB. In particular, at every boundary vertex, the two original sides and their copies satisfy
Cᵢ₋₁Cᵢ:Aᵢ₋₁Aᵢ = CᵢCᵢ₊₁:AᵢAᵢ₊₁.
Alternating, the sides about the corresponding boundary angles are proportional. Here the indices are read cyclically. [5.11, 5.16]

**Step 6.** Each angle of the original figure consists of the angles of the triangles meeting there. The corresponding constructed angle consists, in the same order, of the equal angles of their copies. Adding these equal angles shows that all corresponding angles of the two rectilineal figures are equal. [CN2]

**Step 7.** The constructed figure is therefore equiangular with the given figure and has the sides about equal angles proportional; hence the figures are similar. Their corresponding triangles and boundary sides occur in the same cyclic order, with the same side-of-diagonal choices, so they are also similarly situated. Thus, on the given straight line AB, a rectilineal figure similar and similarly situated to the given rectilineal figure has been described. [D6.1]
