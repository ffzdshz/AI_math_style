## Proposition 6.28
**Statement:** To a given straight line to apply a parallelogram equal to a given rectilineal figure and deficient by a parallelogrammic figure similar to a given one: thus the given rectilineal figure must not be greater than the parallelogram described on the half of the straight line and similar to the defect.

**Step 1.** Let AB be the given straight line, C the given rectilineal figure, and D the parallelogram prescribing the defect. Write |F| for the area of a figure F. Bisect AB at E, and on EB describe the parallelogram P = EBFG similar and similarly situated to D. Through A draw a parallel to EG, meeting FG produced at H. The parallelograms AEGH and EBFG have equal bases AE and EB and lie between the same parallels, so their areas are equal. The hypothesis is |C| ≤ |P|. [1.10, 6.18, 1.31, P2, 1.36]

**Step 2.** If |C| = |P|, take AEGH: it equals C and, within ABFH, has the defect EBFG, which is similar to D. Thus suppose |C| < |P|.

Represent the excess |P| − |C| by an actual rectilineal figure as follows. Divide C into triangles by straight joins. Apply these triangles successively as adjacent parallelogrammic strips, beginning on GF and proceeding towards EB, with their lateral sides along GE and FB, produced if necessary. Each successive strip is applied to the opposite side of the preceding strip, in the same angle. Their union U is a parallelogram equal to C. Since |U| < |P|, its opposite side must lie strictly between GF and EB; otherwise U would contain P. Consequently the remaining strip V is a parallelogram satisfying |V| = |P| − |C|. [P1, 1.44, P2, CN2, CN5]

**Step 3.** Construct a parallelogram Q similar to P and equal to V. Thus
|Q| = |P| − |C| < |P|. [6.25, CN5]

Its sides are shorter than the corresponding sides of P. Indeed, similarity makes the two pairs of adjacent sides proportional; if either side of Q were greater than, or equal to, its corresponding side of P, the other would respectively be greater than, or equal to, its corresponding side. With corresponding angles aligned, Q would then contain P properly, or have equal area with it, contrary to |Q| < |P|. In the equal-side case, the equality follows by applying side-angle-side to the triangles formed by adjacent sides and then doubling their areas. [D6.1, 5.14, CN5, 1.4, 1.34]

**Step 4.** On GF and GE cut off GR and GY equal to the corresponding adjacent sides of Q, and complete the parallelogram GRZY by parallels. This is a copy of Q: its adjacent sides and included angle agree with those of Q, so the corresponding triangles, and hence the parallelograms, are equal. Retain Q as the name of this placed parallelogram. [1.3, 1.31, 1.4, 1.34]

It lies inside P, is similar and similarly situated to P, and shares its angle at G. Therefore its opposite vertex Z lies on the diameter GB of P. Here VI.26 is the direct choice because the smaller similar parallelogram has already been constructed at a common corner. [P1, 6.26]

**Step 5.** Produce RZ to meet EB at X. Produce YZ to meet BF at S and AH at T. Thus AXZT and XBSZ are parallelograms whose union is the parallelogram ABST on the whole given line AB. The construction places X between E and B; we take this branch, giving an applied base AX greater than half of AB. [P2, 1.31]

**Step 6.** In P, the parallelograms GRZY and XBSZ are about the diameter GB. Their complements EXZY and RFSZ are therefore equal. [1.43]

Also AEYT and EBSY have equal bases AE and EB and lie between the same parallels AB and TS; hence they are equal. The equal-base theorem is the natural choice here, without any proportional-area calculation. [1.36]

Adding these equalities gives
|AXZT| = |AEYT| + |EXZY|
          = |EBSY| + |RFSZ|
          = |P| − |Q|
          = |C|.
The third expression is precisely the part of P outside Q, and the last equality follows from |Q| = |P| − |C|. [CN2, CN3]

**Step 7.** Since XBSZ is about the diameter of P, it is similar to P; and since P is similar to D, XBSZ is similar to D. Thus AXZT is equal to C and is applied to AB with the required parallelogrammic defect XBSZ. [6.24, 6.21]

Finally, VI.27 shows that any such application cannot exceed the parallelogram on half of AB similar and similarly situated to its defect. Hence the stated non-excess condition is necessary as well. Together with the equality case in Step 2, the construction proves the proposition under exactly its stated restriction. [6.27]
