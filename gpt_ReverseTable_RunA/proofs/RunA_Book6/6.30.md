## Proposition 6.30
**Statement:** To cut a given finite straight line in extreme and mean ratio.

**Step 1.** Let AB be the given finite straight line. Choose the direct rectangle–square construction: by 2.11, cut AB at C so that the rectangle contained by AB and CB equals the square on AC. [2.11]

**Step 2.** The three-line proportionality criterion applies directly to this rectangle–square equality. By 6.17, the lines AB, AC, CB are proportional; hence
AB : AC = AC : CB. [6.17]

**Step 3.** Since AC is a proper part of AB, we have AB > AC. Applying 5.14 to the proportion in Step 2, with AB, AC, AC, CB as the four magnitudes, gives AC > CB. Thus AC is the greater segment and CB the less. [CN5, 5.14]

**Step 4.** Therefore, as the whole AB is to the greater segment AC, so is AC to the lesser segment CB. By the definition of extreme and mean ratio, AB has been cut in extreme and mean ratio at C, as required. [D6.3]
