## Proposition 6.4
**Statement:** In equiangular triangles the sides about the equal angles are proportional, and those are corresponding sides which subtend the equal angles.

**Step 1.** Let the equiangular triangles be ABC and DCE, with ∠BAC = ∠CDE, ∠ABC = ∠DCE, and ∠BCA = ∠CED. Place their bases consecutively on one straight line, with B, C, E in that order, and their vertices A and D on the same side. This placement preserves the triangles’ magnitudes. We use this adjoining-base arrangement so that VI.2 applies directly to divided sides. [P2, CN4]

**Step 2.** Since ∠ABC = ∠DCE, BA is parallel to CD. Since ∠BCA = ∠CED, AC is parallel to ED. Here I.28 is the direct parallelism criterion: the given equalities are equalities of corresponding exterior and interior angles on the straight line BCE. [1.28]

**Step 3.** In triangle ABC, ∠ABC + ∠BCA is less than two right angles. Hence ∠ABC + ∠CED is also less than two right angles. Therefore BA and ED, produced toward A and D, meet at G on the side of BCE containing the triangles. With the parallels established in Step 2 and C between B and E, A lies between B and G, and D between E and G. [1.32, CN5, P2, P5]

**Step 4.** The quadrilateral ACDG is a parallelogram: AC is parallel to DG, and CD is parallel to AG. Its opposite sides are therefore equal:
AG = CD and DG = AC. [1.34]

**Step 5.** In triangle BGE, AC is parallel to GE. Thus
BA:AG = BC:CE.
Replacing AG by its equal CD gives
AB:CD = BC:CE.
Alternately,
AB:BC = CD:CE.
This is the required proportion about the equal angles ABC and DCE. [6.2, 5.7, 5.11, 5.16]

**Step 6.** In triangle EGB, DC is parallel to GB. Thus
ED:DG = EC:CB.
Alternately,
ED:EC = DG:CB.
Replacing DG by AC and reversing the equality gives
AC:BC = DE:CE.
This is the required proportion about the equal angles BCA and CED. Alternating this last proportion also gives
AC:DE = BC:CE. [6.2, 5.16, 5.7, 5.11]

**Step 7.** Step 5 gives AB:CD = BC:CE, and Step 6 gives AC:DE = BC:CE. Ratios equal to the same ratio are equal; hence
AB:CD = AC:DE.
Alternately,
AB:AC = CD:DE,
which is the required proportion about the equal angles BAC and CDE. Together with Steps 5 and 6, this proves proportionality about every pair of equal angles. The corresponding sides are AB and CD, BC and CE, and AC and DE; these respectively subtend the equal angles at C and E, A and D, and B and C. Therefore, in equiangular triangles the sides about the equal angles are proportional, and corresponding sides subtend equal angles. [5.11, 5.16]
