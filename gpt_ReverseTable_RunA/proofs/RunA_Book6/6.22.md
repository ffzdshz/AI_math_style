## Proposition 6.22
**Statement:** If four straight lines be proportional, the rectilineal figures similar and similarly described upon them will also be proportional; and, if the rectilineal figures similar and similarly described upon them be proportional, the straight lines will themselves also be proportional.

**Step 1.** Let a, b, c, d be the four straight lines, and let P, Q, R, S be the rectilineal figures described upon them respectively, with P and Q similar and similarly described, and R and S likewise. Find third proportionals e and f, so that a:b = b:e and c:d = d:f. [6.11]

**Step 2.** We use third proportionals to express the duplicate ratios explicitly. Similar triangles have the duplicate ratio of their corresponding sides by VI.19; for polygons with more sides, VI.20 supplies the corresponding result. Thus, using the earlier triangle result wherever applicable, the definition of duplicate ratio gives
P:Q = a:e and R:S = c:f.
The bases are corresponding sides because the figures are similarly described. [6.19, 6.20, D5.9, 5.11]

**Step 3.** First suppose a:b = c:d. Then b:e = d:f, since these ratios equal a:b and c:d respectively. Applying ex aequali to the sequences a, b, e and c, d, f gives a:e = c:f. Step 2 therefore yields P:Q = R:S. This proves the forward implication. [5.11, 5.22]

**Step 4.** Conversely, suppose P:Q = R:S. Step 2 gives a:e = c:f. Find a fourth proportional t such that c:d = a:t, and then a third proportional u such that a:t = t:u. Consequently a:t = c:d and t:u = d:f; hence, ex aequali, a:u = c:f. Therefore a:e = a:u, and so e = u. [6.12, 6.11, 5.11, 5.22, 5.9]

**Step 5.** We now have a:b = b:e and a:t = t:e, the latter because u = e. If b were greater than t, then a:b would be less than a:t, whereas b:e would be greater than t:e. This contradicts the two displayed equalities of ratios. If b were less than t, the reverse inequalities would give the same contradiction. Thus b = t. [5.7, 5.8]

**Step 6.** Since b = t, we have a:b = a:t; and by construction a:t = c:d. Hence a:b = c:d. Therefore the four straight lines are proportional if and only if the rectilineal figures similar and similarly described upon them are proportional. [5.7, 5.11]
