## Proposition 6.7
**Statement:** If two triangles have one angle equal to one angle, the sides about other angles proportional, and the remaining angles either both less or both not less than a right angle, the triangles will be equiangular and will have those angles equal, the sides about which are proportional.

**Step 1.** Let ABC and DEF satisfy ∠BAC = ∠EDF and AB:BC = DE:EF, with ∠BCA and ∠EFD either both less than a right angle or both not less than a right angle. Suppose ∠ABC and ∠DEF are unequal. Interchanging the triangles if necessary, assume ∠ABC > ∠DEF. Construct inside ∠ABC the ray BG such that ∠ABG = ∠DEF, meeting AC at G. Since the constructed angle is strictly between zero and ∠ABC, G lies between A and C. [1.23]

**Step 2.** In triangles ABG and DEF, ∠BAG = ∠EDF and ∠ABG = ∠DEF. Their angle sums are each two right angles; adding the equal pairs and subtracting them from these equal sums gives ∠AGB = ∠EFD. Thus these triangles are equiangular. [1.32, CN2, CN3]

**Step 3.** Use the equiangular-triangle route, since the auxiliary triangles’ angles are already known to be equal. Their corresponding sides satisfy AB:BG = DE:EF. Together with the given AB:BC = DE:EF, this yields AB:BG = AB:BC, and consequently BG = BC. [6.4, 5.11, 5.9]

**Step 4.** Triangle BGC is therefore isosceles, so ∠BGC = ∠BCG. These two angles together are less than two right angles, and hence each is less than a right angle. Since G lies on AC, ∠BCG is ∠BCA. Thus the original remaining angle ∠BCA is less than a right angle. [1.5, 1.17]

**Step 5.** Since GA and GC are opposite rays, ∠AGB and ∠BGC together equal two right angles. As ∠BGC is less than a right angle, ∠AGB is greater than a right angle. But ∠AGB = ∠EFD by Step 2, so ∠EFD is greater than a right angle. This contradicts either permitted hypothesis: if both remaining angles are less than a right angle, ∠EFD contradicts it; if both are not less than a right angle, ∠BCA contradicts it. Therefore ∠ABC = ∠DEF. [1.13; Steps 2–4]

**Step 6.** Finally, ∠BAC = ∠EDF by hypothesis and ∠ABC = ∠DEF by Step 5. Subtracting these equal angle pairs from the equal triangle angle sums gives ∠BCA = ∠EFD. Hence ABC and DEF are equiangular, and in particular the angles ∠ABC and ∠DEF, about which the given proportional sides lie, are equal, as required. [1.32, CN2, CN3]
