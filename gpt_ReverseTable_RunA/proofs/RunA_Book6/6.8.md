## Proposition 6.8
**Statement:** If in a right-angled triangle a perpendicular be drawn from the right angle to the base, the triangles adjoining the perpendicular are similar both to the whole and to one another.

**Step 1.** Let ABC be a triangle right-angled at A, and let AD be the given perpendicular to the base BC, with D on BC. The angles ADB and ADC are right angles by the definition of perpendicularity, and therefore each equals the right angle BAC. [D1.10, P4]

**Step 2.** Since BD lies along BC, the angle ABD is the same as ABC. Thus triangles ABD and ABC have two angles respectively equal: ABD equals ABC, and ADB equals BAC. The angles of each triangle sum to two right angles; subtracting the equal angles leaves BAD equal to ACB. Hence triangles DBA and ABC are equiangular, with correspondence D ↔ A, B ↔ B, A ↔ C. [1.32, CN3]

**Step 3.** Since CD lies along CB, the angle ACD is the same as ACB. Triangles ACD and ABC therefore also have two angles respectively equal: ACD equals ACB, and ADC equals BAC. Subtracting these equal angles from their equal angle sums gives DAC equal to ABC. Hence triangles DAC and ABC are equiangular, with correspondence D ↔ A, A ↔ B, C ↔ C. [1.32, CN3]

**Step 4.** Apply the theorem on proportional sides in equiangular triangles. This is the direct route because the perpendicular and the shared base angles have already established equiangularity. The corresponding sides of DBA and ABC are proportional, as are those of DAC and ABC. Since their corresponding angles are also equal, each of the two smaller triangles is similar to the whole triangle. [6.4, D6.1]

**Step 5.** The smaller triangles are also equiangular to one another: ADB equals ADC, since both equal BAC; DBA equals DAC, since both equal ABC; and DAB equals DCA, since both equal ACB. Their corresponding sides are therefore proportional, so they are similar to one another. Thus the triangles adjoining the perpendicular are similar both to the whole and to one another, as required. [CN1, 6.4, D6.1]
