## Proposition 6.21
**Statement:** Figures which are similar to the same rectilineal figure are also similar to one another.

**Step 1.** Let the rectilineal figures F and G each be similar to the same rectilineal figure H. Match the angles and sides of F and G through their respective correspondences with H. We take the direct route through the definition of similarity: equality of corresponding angles and proportionality of the sides about them. [D6.1]

**Step 2.** Each angle of F and its corresponding angle of G are equal to the same angle of H, by the assumed similarities. Therefore they are equal to one another. Thus F and G have their corresponding angles severally equal. [D6.1, CN1]

**Step 3.** At any corresponding angles, denote the two incident sides in F by a, a′, those in G by b, b′, and those in H by c, c′, with the sides listed in corresponding order. The assumed similarities give a:a′ = c:c′ and b:b′ = c:c′. Ratios which are the same with the same ratio are the same with one another; hence a:a′ = b:b′. Thus the sides of F and G about each pair of corresponding equal angles are proportional. [D6.1, 5.11]

**Step 4.** Since F and G have their angles severally equal and the sides about the equal angles proportional, they are similar. Therefore figures which are similar to the same rectilineal figure are also similar to one another. [D6.1]
