## Proposition 6.26
**Statement:** If from a parallelogram there be taken away a parallelogram similar and similarly situated to the whole and having a common angle with it, it is about the same diameter with the whole.

**Step 1.** Let ABCD be the whole parallelogram, and let AEFG be the similar and similarly situated parallelogram taken from it, with their common angle at A, E on AB, and G on AD. The corresponding sides are proportional, so
AB : BC = AE : EF. [D6.1]

**Step 2.** Draw the diameter AC. Let it meet EF, produced if necessary, at H. Through H draw HK parallel to AB, meeting AD at K. Then AEHK is a parallelogram about the diameter AC of ABCD. Moreover, H and F lie on the same ray from E, directed into the whole parallelogram. [P1, P2, 1.31]

**Step 3.** Apply 6.24 to the auxiliary parallelogram AEHK: this is the direct route because that parallelogram is already about AC. It is similar to ABCD, and therefore
AB : BC = AE : EH. [6.24, D6.1]

**Step 4.** The two ratios AE : EF and AE : EH are thus equal to the same ratio AB : BC, and hence equal to one another. Since the same magnitude AE has the same ratio to EF and EH, we obtain EF = EH. [5.11, 5.9]

**Step 5.** Since F and H are on the same ray from E, their equality of distance from E forces F = H: otherwise one of EF and EH would be a proper part of the other, contrary to their equality. Thus F lies on AC. Consequently the diameter AF of AEFG lies on the diameter AC of ABCD, so the parallelogram taken away is about the same diameter with the whole. [CN5]
