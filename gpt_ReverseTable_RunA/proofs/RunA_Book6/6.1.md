## Proposition 6.1
**Statement:** Triangles and parallelograms which are under the same height are to one another as their bases.

**Step 1.** Place the figures of the given height between two parallel straight lines ℓ and h, their bases lying on ℓ and their opposite vertices or sides on h. Such placement does not change their magnitudes. Let T and U be any two triangles so placed, with bases b and c. We shall establish their ratio directly by the equimultiple criterion, without assuming that their bases are commensurable. [CN4, D5.5]

**Step 2.** Choose a point O on ℓ and a point A on h. Let m and n be arbitrary positive whole numbers. Starting at O along the same ray of ℓ, lay off m consecutive segments each equal to b, ending at X; independently, lay off n consecutive segments each equal to c, ending at Y. Produce the line and cut off the required segments successively, and join A to all their endpoints. Thus OX = mb and OY = nc. [P2, 1.3, P1]

**Step 3.** Each of the m triangles formed over the successive copies of b is equal to T: its base equals b, and it lies between the same parallels ℓ and h. Likewise, each of the n triangles formed over the copies of c is equal to U. Proposition 1.38 is the natural equal-base result here, since the copied bases need not be the identical segment. The first collection partitions triangle AOX, and the second partitions triangle AOY. Adding equals therefore gives
[AOX] = mT and [AOY] = nU,
where brackets denote the magnitude of a triangle. [1.38, CN2]

**Step 4.** Because OX and OY lie on the same ray:
• If mb > nc, then Y lies strictly between O and X. Triangle AOY is a proper part of triangle AOX, so mT > nU.
• If mb = nc, then X and Y coincide, and the triangles coincide, so mT = nU.
• If mb < nc, then X lies strictly between O and Y. Triangle AOX is a proper part of triangle AOY, so mT < nU.
Thus the corresponding multiples of the triangles and their bases always have the same comparison. [CN5, CN4]

**Step 5.** Since m and n were arbitrary, any equimultiples of T and b alike exceed, equal, or fall short of the corresponding equimultiples of U and c. By the definition of equality of ratios,
T : U = b : c.
This proves the assertion for triangles. [D5.5]

**Step 6.** Now let P and Q be parallelograms of the same height, with bases b and c. On each base form a triangle by joining its endpoints to a vertex on the opposite side of its parallelogram. Call these triangles T and U. Each triangle has the same base and lies between the same parallels as its parallelogram. Consequently,
P = 2T and Q = 2U. [P1, 1.41]

**Step 7.** Parts have the same ratio as equal multiples of them taken in corresponding order; hence P : Q = T : U. By Step 5, T : U = b : c, and therefore P : Q = b : c. Thus triangles, and likewise parallelograms, which are under the same height are to one another as their bases. [5.15, 5.11]
