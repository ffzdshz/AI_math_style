## Proposition 6.14
**Statement:** In equal and equiangular parallelograms the sides about the equal angles are reciprocally proportional; and equiangular parallelograms in which the sides about the equal angles are reciprocally proportional are equal.

**Step 1.** Let the given equiangular parallelograms be OABC and ODEF, with the equal angles AOC and DOF superposed, so that OA and OD lie on one ray, and OC and OF on another. This placement changes neither lengths nor areas (CN4). Denote their areas by p and q, respectively.

**Step 2.** Through D draw a parallel to OC, and through C draw a parallel to OD, meeting at H; thus ODHC is a parallelogram (1.31). Denote its area by r. We use this single auxiliary parallelogram because the common-height comparison of 6.1 applies directly to each original parallelogram and the auxiliary one.

**Step 3.** OABC and ODHC have bases OA and OD on the same straight line and lie between that line and its parallel through C. Hence, by 6.1,
 p:r = OA:OD.
Likewise, ODEF and ODHC, regarded as having bases OF and OC, lie between the straight line OC and its parallel through D. Hence, again by 6.1,
 q:r = OF:OC.

**Step 4.** First suppose the given parallelograms are equal, so p=q. Equal magnitudes have the same ratio to the same magnitude; therefore p:r=q:r (5.7). Combining this with the two ratios in Step 3 gives
 OA:OD = OF:OC
by 5.11. Thus the sides about the equal angles are reciprocally proportional: the first side of the first parallelogram is to the corresponding side of the second as the other side of the second is to the other side of the first (D6.2).

**Step 5.** Conversely, suppose the sides about the equal angles are reciprocally proportional, so that OA:OD=OF:OC (D6.2). The ratios in Step 3 then give p:r=q:r by 5.11. Magnitudes having the same ratio to the same magnitude are equal (5.9); hence p=q, and the given parallelograms are equal. Therefore both assertions are proved.
