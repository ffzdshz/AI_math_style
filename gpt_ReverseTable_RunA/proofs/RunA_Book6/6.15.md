## Proposition 6.15
**Statement:** In equal triangles which have one angle equal to one angle the sides about the equal angles are reciprocally proportional; and those triangles which have one angle equal to one angle, and in which the sides about the equal angles are reciprocally proportional, are equal.

**Step 1.** Let ABC and DEF be triangles with ∠BAC = ∠EDF. Write [XYZ] for the area of triangle XYZ. On the rays AB and AC take points G and H such that AG = DE and AH = DF, producing the straight lines if necessary and cutting off the required lengths; if a required length already equals AB or AC, use the existing endpoint. Join GH and GC. (P2, 1.3, P1.)

**Step 2.** Since AG = DE, AH = DF, and ∠GAH = ∠BAC = ∠EDF, the triangles AGH and DEF are equal by the side-angle-side proposition. Thus [AGH] = [DEF]. (1.4.)

**Step 3.** We use the common auxiliary triangle AGC: the same-height theorem directly converts the required side ratios into ratios of triangle areas. Triangles ABC and AGC have their bases AB and AG on the same straight line and the same vertex C. Triangles AGH and AGC have their bases AH and AC on the same straight line and the same vertex G. Consequently,
[ABC] : [AGC] = AB : AG,
and
[AGH] : [AGC] = AH : AC. (6.1.)

**Step 4.** First suppose [ABC] = [DEF]. By Step 2, [ABC] = [AGH]. (CN1.) Equal magnitudes have the same ratio to the same magnitude, so
[ABC] : [AGC] = [AGH] : [AGC]. (5.7.)
Combining this with Step 3 gives AB : AG = AH : AC. (5.11.) Substituting AG = DE and AH = DF yields
AB : DE = DF : AC.
Thus the sides about the equal angles are reciprocally proportional. (D6.2.)

**Step 5.** Conversely, suppose the sides about the equal angles are reciprocally proportional, so AB : DE = DF : AC. (D6.2.) The constructed equalities give AB : AG = AH : AC. Hence Step 3 implies
[ABC] : [AGC] = [AGH] : [AGC]. (5.11.)
Magnitudes having the same ratio to the same magnitude are equal; therefore [ABC] = [AGH]. (5.9.) Since [AGH] = [DEF] by Step 2, it follows that [ABC] = [DEF]. (CN1.) Thus the triangles are equal, completing both directions.
