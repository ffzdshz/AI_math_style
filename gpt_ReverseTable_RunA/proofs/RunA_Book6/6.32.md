## Proposition 6.32
**Statement:** If two triangles having two sides proportional to two sides be placed together at one angle so that their corresponding sides are also parallel, the remaining sides of the triangles will be in a straight line.

**Step 1.** Let the triangles be ABC and DCE, joined at C in the stipulated adjacent placement, with AB:AC = CD:DE, AB ∥ CD, and AC ∥ DE. In this placement A and D lie on the same side of BC, while B and E lie on opposite sides of CD. We must prove that BC and CE form one straight line. (Hypotheses.)

**Step 2.** Since AB ∥ CD, the transversal AC gives ∠BAC = ∠ACD. Since AC ∥ DE, the transversal CD gives ∠ACD = ∠CDE. Therefore ∠BAC = ∠CDE. [1.29, CN1]

**Step 3.** The sides AB, AC are proportional to CD, DE, and their included angles BAC and CDE are equal. Apply 6.6—the direct route because the equal angles are included by the proportional sides. The triangles are equiangular, with corresponding angles ∠ABC = ∠DCE. [6.6]

**Step 4.** Since AB ∥ CD, the transversal BC makes the interior angles on the same side, ∠ABC and ∠BCD, together equal to two right angles. Adding ∠BCD to the equal angles ∠ABC and ∠DCE therefore gives
∠BCD + ∠DCE = two right angles. [1.29, CN2, CN1]

**Step 5.** At C, the rays CB and CE lie on opposite sides of CD, and the adjacent angles BCD and DCE together equal two right angles. Hence CB and CE are in a straight line with one another. Thus the remaining sides BC and CE of the two triangles form one straight line, as required. [1.14]
