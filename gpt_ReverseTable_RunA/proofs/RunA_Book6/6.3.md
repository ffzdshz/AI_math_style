## Proposition 6.3
**Statement:** If an angle of a triangle be bisected and the straight line cutting the angle cut the base also, the segments of the base will have the same ratio as the remaining sides of the triangle; and, if the segments of the base have the same ratio as the remaining sides of the triangle, the straight line joined from the vertex to the point of section will bisect the angle of the triangle.

**Step 1.** Let ABC be a triangle, and let D lie between B and C on its base. Join AD. We shall prove that AD bisects ∠BAC if and only if BD:DC = AB:AC. [P1]

**Step 2.** Through C draw CE parallel to AD, meeting BA produced beyond A at E. This auxiliary parallel is chosen because 6.2 directly relates the base segments to the segments on BE. [1.31, P2]

**Step 3.** In triangle BCE, the line AD is parallel to CE. Therefore
BD:DC = BA:AE.
Also, the parallels AD and CE give
∠BAD = ∠AEC and ∠DAC = ∠ACE,
by the equality of corresponding and alternate angles, respectively. [6.2, 1.29]

**Step 4.** First suppose that AD bisects ∠BAC, so that ∠BAD = ∠DAC. The angle equalities in Step 3 then give ∠AEC = ∠ACE. Hence, in triangle ACE, the sides opposite these equal angles are equal: AC = AE. [CN1, 1.6]

**Step 5.** Since AE = AC, the same magnitude BA has the same ratio to each: BA:AE = BA:AC. Combining this with Step 3 gives
BD:DC = BA:AC = AB:AC.
Thus the segments of the base have the same ratio as the remaining sides. [5.7, 5.11]

**Step 6.** Conversely, suppose that BD:DC = AB:AC. Step 3 also gives BD:DC = BA:AE; consequently BA:AE = BA:AC. Magnitudes to which the same magnitude has the same ratio are equal, so AE = AC. [5.11, 5.9]

**Step 7.** Triangle ACE is therefore isosceles, and ∠AEC = ∠ACE. Using the angle equalities from Step 3, we obtain ∠BAD = ∠DAC. Thus AD bisects ∠BAC. Both directions of the proposition are proved. [1.5, CN1]
