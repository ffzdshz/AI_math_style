## Proposition 12.16
**Statement:** Given two circles about the same centre, to inscribe in the greater circle an equilateral polygon with an even number of sides which does not touch the lesser circle.

**Step 1.** Let O be the common centre. We use subdivision of equal arcs, followed by equality of the corresponding chord-distances. Inscribe a square ABCD in the greater circle. Its sides are equal, so its four successive minor arcs are equal. [4.6, D1.22, 3.28]

**Step 2.** Let H be the intersection of OA with the lesser circle. Through H draw the straight line ℓ perpendicular to OA; since H is inside the greater circle, ℓ cuts its circumference at two points T and U. Choose the naming so that the short arc from A to T proceeds from A in the direction of B. This arc, except for T, lies in the open half-plane bounded by ℓ which contains A, whereas O lies in the other half-plane. [1.11]

**Step 3.** Bisect each of the four equal arcs, and then bisect each resulting arc again. Repeat this double subdivision as necessary. All arcs remain equal, and each round replaces the first arc starting at A by its first quarter. Thus each round removes more than half of that arc. By 10.1, after finitely many rounds the first arc AP is less than the fixed arc AT; if equality occurs, one further round makes the inequality strict. If the initial arc is already smaller, no subdivision is needed. After n rounds there are 4·4ⁿ equal arcs. [3.30, 10.1]

**Step 4.** Join the successive division points. Equal arcs are subtended by equal chords, so these chords form an equilateral polygon inscribed in the greater circle, with the even number 4·4ⁿ of sides. Since arc AP is less than arc AT and starts in the same direction from A, P lies strictly between A and T on that arc. Consequently the entire side AP lies in the open half-plane bounded by ℓ which contains A. [3.29]

**Step 5.** Bisect AP at M. The straight line OM is perpendicular to AP. Since M and O are on opposite sides of ℓ, OM meets ℓ at a point K between O and M. If K=H, then OK=OH. Otherwise triangle OHK is right-angled at H; its other angles are acute, and therefore its hypotenuse OK is greater than OH. In either case, OM>OK≥OH, so OM>OH. [1.10, 3.3, 1.17, 1.19, CN5]

**Step 6.** Let XY be any side of the polygon, and N its midpoint. Then ON is perpendicular to XY. Apply 3.14 directly, since the polygon’s sides have already been proved equal: their perpendicular distances from O are equal, and hence ON=OM>OH. For any point Z on XY, either Z=N, or the right triangle ONZ gives OZ>ON. Thus every point of every side has distance from O strictly greater than OH, the radius of the lesser circle. No side can therefore meet or touch that circle. The constructed polygon is equilateral, has an even number of sides, is inscribed in the greater circle, and does not touch the lesser circle, as required. [1.10, 3.3, 3.14, D3.4, 1.17, 1.19, D1.15]
