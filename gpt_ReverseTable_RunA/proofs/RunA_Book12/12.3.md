## Proposition 12.3
**Statement:** Any pyramid which has a triangular base is divided into two pyramids equal and similar to one another, similar to the whole and having triangular bases, and into two equal prisms; and the two prisms are greater than the half of the whole pyramid.

**Step 1.** Let ABCD be the given triangular pyramid. Bisect AB, AC, AD, BD, CD, BC at E, F, G, H, I, J respectively, and join the required points [1.10, P1]. In each triangular face, a segment joining two midpoints is parallel to the third side [6.2]. It also equals half that side: the three midpoint parallels form parallelograms whose opposite sides are equal [1.34]. We shall use these midpoint facts throughout.

**Step 2.** Consider the triangular pyramids T₁ = AEFG and T₂ = GHID [D11.12]. Under the correspondences
A, B, C, D ↔ A, E, F, G
and
A, B, C, D ↔ G, H, I, D,
every corresponding edge of either smaller pyramid equals half the corresponding edge of ABCD. Thus their corresponding triangular faces have proportional sides and equal angles [6.5, D6.1]. Each smaller pyramid is therefore similar to the whole [D11.9]. Moreover, the corresponding edges of T₁ and T₂ are equal; their corresponding faces are consequently equal and similar [6.5, 1.4]. Hence T₁ and T₂ are equal and similar solids [D11.10].

**Step 3.** Cut off T₁ and T₂ by the planes EFG and GHI. The quadrilateral Q = FJHG is a parallelogram, since FJ and GH are parallel to AB, while FG and JH are parallel to CD. Cut the remainder by its plane. This produces precisely the following two solids, with disjoint interiors:
• R, whose triangular faces are EFG and BJH, and whose other faces are EBJF, EBHG, and FJHG;
• S, whose triangular faces are FJC and GHI, and whose other faces are FJHG, JCIH, and FCIG.
The midpoint facts show that all the listed quadrilateral faces are parallelograms. They also show that each pair of triangular end faces has equal corresponding sides, so those faces are equal and similar [6.5, 1.4]. Their planes are parallel because two intersecting sides of either end face are respectively parallel to two intersecting sides of the other [11.15]. Thus R and S are prisms [D11.13], and ABCD has been divided into T₁, T₂, R, and S.

**Step 4.** We compare the prisms by completing them on their common parallelogram face Q; that face makes diagonal bisection the direct route. Complete a parallelepiped K₊ on Q by drawing parallel edges equal and directed as JB, and a parallelepiped K₋ on Q by drawing parallel edges equal and directed as JC [1.31]. In the first completion F corresponds to E and J to B; in the second J corresponds to C and H to I.
Since JB and JC are equal and oppositely directed, moving K₊ parallel to itself by the directed segment JC makes it coincide with K₋. Hence K₊ and K₋ are equal [CN4]. The plane EBHG passes through the diagonals EG and BH of two opposite faces of K₊, and cuts off R. The plane FCIG passes through the diagonals FC and GI of two opposite faces of K₋, and cuts off S. Each plane bisects its parallelepiped [11.28]. Therefore R and S, being halves of equal solids, are equal.

**Step 5.** Within R take the triangular pyramid U = EBJH. It is a proper part of R: for example, the parallelogram face EBJF extends beyond its triangular portion EBJ. Every edge of U equals half the corresponding edge of ABCD under A, B, C, D ↔ E, B, J, H, by the midpoint facts of Step 1. Consequently its corresponding faces are equal and similar to those of T₁ [6.5, 1.4], and U equals T₁ [D11.10]. Since the whole is greater than its proper part, R is greater than T₁ [CN5]. As R equals S and T₁ equals T₂, S is likewise greater than T₂.

**Step 6.** The two prisms together therefore exceed the two smaller pyramids together: replacing each smaller pyramid by its equal part within the corresponding prism leaves a proper remainder [CN2, CN5]. But these prisms and pyramids together constitute the whole ABCD. Hence the prisms together are greater than half the whole.
Thus the given triangular pyramid is divided into two equal and similar triangular pyramids, each similar to the whole, and two equal prisms; and the two prisms are greater than half the whole pyramid.
