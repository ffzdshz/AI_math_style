## Proposition 12.15
**Statement:** In equal cones and cylinders the bases are reciprocally proportional to the heights; and those cones and cylinders in which the bases are reciprocally proportional to the heights are equal.

**Step 1.** Let X and Y be two cones, or two cylinders. Denote their base areas by B_X and B_Y and their heights by h_X and h_Y. Construct an auxiliary solid Z of the same kind, with base equal to that of Y and height equal to that of X. We use the equal-height comparison first, followed by the equal-base comparison; this route applies directly to both cones and cylinders. Since X and Z have equal heights,
X : Z = B_X : B_Y. [12.11]

**Step 2.** Since Y and Z have equal bases, and Z has height h_X,
Y : Z = h_Y : h_X. [12.14]

**Step 3.** Suppose X and Y are equal. Equal magnitudes have the same ratio to the same magnitude, so X : Z = Y : Z. [5.7] Combining this with Steps 1 and 2 gives
B_X : B_Y = h_Y : h_X. [5.11]
Thus their bases are reciprocally proportional to their heights.

**Step 4.** Conversely, suppose their bases are reciprocally proportional to their heights, so that
B_X : B_Y = h_Y : h_X.
By Steps 1 and 2 and equality of ratios, X : Z = Y : Z. [5.11] Magnitudes having the same ratio to the same magnitude are equal; hence X = Y. [5.9]

Therefore, in equal cones and likewise in equal cylinders, the bases are reciprocally proportional to the heights; conversely, cones, and likewise cylinders, whose bases are reciprocally proportional to their heights are equal.
