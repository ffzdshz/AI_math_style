## Proposition 12.8
**Statement:** Similar pyramids which have triangular bases are in the triplicate ratio of their corresponding sides.

**Step 1.** Let the similar triangular pyramids be T = ABCD and T′ = A′B′C′D′, with corresponding vertices so labelled. Their corresponding triangular faces are similar; hence their corresponding face angles are equal, and all corresponding edges have one common ratio, say r = AB:A′B′. In particular, AB:A′B′ = AC:A′C′ = AD:A′D′. We use completion to parallelepipeds, for which the triplicate-ratio theorem is already available. [D11.9, D6.1, 11.33]

**Step 2.** Complete a parallelepiped S having AB, AC, AD as its three edges issuing from A. Label its remaining vertices E, F, G, H so that ABEC and DFHG are opposite faces, and AD, BF, CG, EH are parallel and equal. Make the corresponding construction S′ from A′B′, A′C′, A′D′. These completions are obtained by drawing parallels; their opposite face planes are parallel. [1.31, 1.34, 11.15]

**Step 3.** The three parallelogram faces of S meeting at A are respectively similar to those of S′ meeting at A′. Indeed, their adjacent sides are proportional by Step 1, and their included angles are the corresponding angles BAC, BAD, CAD and B′A′C′, B′A′D′, C′A′D′. The remaining angles agree because adjacent angles of a parallelogram are supplementary, and its opposite angles agree; opposite sides also agree. The opposite faces have the same respective shapes. Thus the two solids are bounded by six respectively similar faces and are similar parallelepipeds. [1.29, 1.34, D6.1, D11.9]

**Step 4.** Cut S by the plane through the diagonals BC and FG of its opposite faces ABEC and DFHG. This bisects S, and one half is the triangular prism Π with opposite triangular faces ABC and DFG. Within Π, the triangular sections DBC and DCF partition it into the three tetrahedra ABCD, BCDF, and CDFG. The first of these is T. [11.28]

**Step 5.** The tetrahedra ABCD and BCDF are equal. Regard them as pyramids with common vertex C and bases ABD and BDF: those bases are the equal halves of the parallelogram ABFD, and their heights are the same. Likewise, BCDF and CDFG are equal when regarded as pyramids with common vertex D and bases BCF and CFG, the equal halves of the parallelogram BCGF. In each case the ratio of the pyramids equals the ratio of their equal bases. Here the triangular-base theorem is the first applicable equal-height result; its polygonal extension is unnecessary. Consequently all three tetrahedra are equal to T. [1.34, 12.5]

**Step 6.** Therefore Π is three times T, and S, being twice Π, is six times T. The identical construction and argument give S′ equal to six times T′. Parts have the same ratio as corresponding equal multiples, so T:T′ = S:S′. [5.15]

**Step 7.** Since S and S′ are similar parallelepipeds, S:S′ is the triplicate ratio of AB:A′B′. By Step 6, T:T′ is therefore that same triplicate ratio. Since every pair of corresponding sides of the original pyramids has the ratio AB:A′B′, similar pyramids with triangular bases are in the triplicate ratio of their corresponding sides. [11.33, 5.11]
