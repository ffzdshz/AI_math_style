## Proposition 12.13
**Statement:** If a cylinder be cut by a plane which is parallel to its opposite planes, then, as the cylinder is to the cylinder, so will the axis be to the axis.

**Step 1.** Let the cylinder have axis AB, and let the cutting plane, parallel to its two bases, meet AB at C. Denote the parts on AC and CB by K and L. In any axial plane, the cutting plane meets the generating rectangle in a line parallel to its end sides [11.16]. This divides the generating rectangle into two rectangles [1.29], with equal radial widths [1.34]. Rotating these rectangles about AC and CB gives precisely K and L. Thus the two parts are cylinders with the same radius and with axes AC and CB [D11.21, D11.22, D11.23].

**Step 2.** We use the equimultiple criterion directly, since cylinders of this fixed radius can be compared by placing them on one base [D5.5]. Let m and n be arbitrary positive integers. Place m copies of K end to end on a straight axis, obtaining a cylinder U; similarly place n copies of L end to end, obtaining a cylinder V. The generating rectangles join to form single rectangles of the original radial width, so U and V are cylinders whose axes have lengths m·AC and n·CB [D11.21, D11.22]. Each constituent copy equals its original by coincidence, and adding these equal copies gives U = mK and V = nL [CN4, CN2].

**Step 3.** Place U and V with their initial circular bases coincident and their axes extending along the same ray. If m·AC = n·CB, their generating rectangles, and hence their cylinders, coincide; therefore mK = nL [D11.21, CN4]. If m·AC > n·CB, V is a proper part of U: its generating rectangle is the initial portion of the generating rectangle of U. Hence mK > nL [D11.21, CN5]. Likewise, if m·AC < n·CB, then mK < nL [D11.21, CN5]. Coincidence is the direct justification in the equality case; no cylinder-ratio theorem is needed.

**Step 4.** Consequently, for every m and n, the multiples mK and nL alike exceed, are equal to, or fall short of one another exactly as m·AC and n·CB do. By the definition of equal ratios,
K : L = AC : CB [D5.5].
Thus, as the cylinder is to the cylinder, so is the axis to the axis, as required.
