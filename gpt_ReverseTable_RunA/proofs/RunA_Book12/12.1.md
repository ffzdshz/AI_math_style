## Proposition 12.1
**Statement:** Similar polygons inscribed in circles are to one another as the squares on the diameters.

**Step 1.** Let P and Q be similar polygons inscribed in two circles. Take the direct route through the corresponding triangles supplied by 6.20: choose one pair ABC and A′B′C′, so that they are similar and
P : Q = triangle ABC : triangle A′B′C′.
Every triangle has an acute angle, since its angles sum to two right angles. Label the chosen triangles so that ∠ACB is acute; the corresponding angle ∠A′C′B′ is equal to it and therefore acute. [6.20, 1.32, D6.1]

**Step 2.** Let BD and B′D′ be diameters of the respective circles. Neither AB nor A′B′ is a diameter, since the angles they subtend at C and C′ are acute, whereas an angle in a semicircle is right. Thus ABD and A′B′D′ are nondegenerate triangles. Their angles BAD and B′A′D′ are right, and consequently their angles ADB and A′D′B′ are acute. [D1.17, 3.31, 1.32]

**Step 3.** By the classification of angles in circle segments, the acute angles ACB and ADB both stand in the greater segment cut off by AB. They are therefore angles in the same segment, and
∠ADB = ∠ACB.
Likewise,
∠A′D′B′ = ∠A′C′B′.
Since the original triangles are similar, it follows that ∠ADB = ∠A′D′B′. This remains immediate if D coincides with C, or D′ with C′. [3.31, 3.21, D6.1]

**Step 4.** The triangles ABD and A′B′D′ have equal right angles at A and A′ and equal angles at D and D′. Their remaining angles are equal by the angle sum of a triangle, so they are equiangular. Their corresponding sides are therefore proportional:
AB : A′B′ = BD : B′D′. [1.32, 6.4]

**Step 5.** Use the triangle-area result 6.19 for the selected pair: triangle ABC has to triangle A′B′C′ the duplicate ratio of AB to A′B′. Together with Step 1, this says that P : Q is that duplicate ratio.
Let S and S′ denote the squares on BD and B′D′. Squares are similar, since they are equilateral and right-angled. Hence, by 6.20, S : S′ is the duplicate ratio of BD to B′D′. [6.19, 6.20, D1.22, D6.1]

**Step 6.** Step 4 identifies the two side ratios. Their duplicate ratios are therefore equal: by the definition of duplicate ratio, each compounds two copies of its side ratio, and equality follows ex aequali. Consequently,
P : Q = S : S′.
Thus similar polygons inscribed in circles are to one another as the squares on their diameters. [D5.9, 5.22, 5.11]
