## Proposition 12.6
**Statement:** Pyramids which are of the same height and have polygonal bases are to one another as the bases.

**Step 1.** Let P and Q be pyramids of the same height, with polygonal bases A and B. Draw diagonals dividing A into triangles A₁,…,Aᵣ and B into triangles B₁,…,Bₛ. Joining these divisions to the respective apices divides the pyramids into triangular-based pyramids P₁,…,Pᵣ and Q₁,…,Qₛ. Thus A = A₁ + ⋯ + Aᵣ, B = B₁ + ⋯ + Bₛ, P = P₁ + ⋯ + Pᵣ, and Q = Q₁ + ⋯ + Qₛ. Each smaller pyramid has its parent's apex and base-plane, so all have the same height. [P1, D11.12]

**Step 2.** Choose the direct route through the immediately preceding triangular-base case. For every i and j, that theorem gives
Pᵢ : Qⱼ = Aᵢ : Bⱼ.
The two triangulations need not contain the same number of triangles. [12.5]

**Step 3.** Fix j. Add the antecedents of these proportions while keeping the respective consequents Qⱼ and Bⱼ fixed. Repeated application of V.24, together with the decompositions in Step 1, gives
P : Qⱼ = A : Bⱼ.
This holds for every j. [5.24]

**Step 4.** Reverse each proportion to obtain
Qⱼ : P = Bⱼ : A.
This reversal follows directly from the definition of equal ratios: interchange the two arbitrary equimultipliers and reverse the corresponding comparisons. Now apply V.24 repeatedly, keeping P and A as the respective fixed consequents. Summing over j gives
Q : P = B : A. [D5.5, 5.24]

**Step 5.** Reverse this last proportion, again by the definition of equal ratios, to obtain
P : Q = A : B.
Therefore pyramids which are of the same height and have polygonal bases are to one another as their bases. [D5.5]
