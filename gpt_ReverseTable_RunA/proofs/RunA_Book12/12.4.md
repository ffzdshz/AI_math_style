## Proposition 12.4
**Statement:** If there be two pyramids of the same height which have triangular bases, and cach of them be divided into two pyramids equal to one another and similar to the whole, and into two equal prisms, then, as the base of the one pyramid is to the base of the other pyramid, so will all the prisms in the one pyramid be to all the prisms, being equal in multitude, in the other pyramid.

**Step 1.** Let the pyramids be SABC and S′A′B′C′, with common height h and base-areas B₀ and B₀′. Use the division of XII.3 in each. Explicitly, let L, M, N be the midpoints of AB, BC, CA, and U, V, W the midpoints of SA, SB, SC. The residual pyramids are ALNU and SUVW; one prism P has opposite triangular faces BLM and VUW, and the other prism is Q. Thus P = Q. Use corresponding primed letters in the other pyramid, where P′ = Q′. [12.3]

**Step 2.** The midpoint parallels give UV ∥ AB and UW ∥ AC, so the plane UVW is parallel to ABC. Construct also a parallel plane through S. These three parallel planes cut SA and an altitude proportionally; since SU = UA, the distance between UVW and ABC is h/2, as is the distance from S to UVW. Consequently P and P′ have equal heights h/2. The corresponding residual pyramids also have equal heights h/2. [6.2, 11.15, 1.31, 11.17]

**Step 3.** Write b = [BLM] and b′ = [B′L′M′]. For the base comparison, use the earlier same-altitude triangle theorem twice, rather than a similarity-area argument. Since BL is half BA, [BLM] is half [BAM]; since BM is half BC, [BAM] is half [BAC]. Thus B₀ = 4b, and similarly B₀′ = 4b′. The same argument gives [ALN] = B₀/4 and [A′L′N′] = B₀′/4. Moreover, [UVW] = b and [U′V′W′] = b′, because these are opposite triangular faces of the respective prisms. Therefore
b : b′ = B₀ : B₀′,
and each pair of corresponding residual pyramids likewise has its bases in the ratio B₀ : B₀′. [6.1, D11.13, 5.15]

**Step 4.** Compare P and P′ directly by completing them to parallelepipeds K and K′. Complete each triangular base to a parallelogram having that triangle as one of its diagonal halves, and retain the prism’s lateral edges. The resulting parallelogram bases have areas 2b and 2b′. The diagonal plane of each completed solid bisects it into two equal triangular prisms; hence K = 2P and K′ = 2P′. Both completed solids retain height h/2. [1.31, 1.34, 11.28]

**Step 5.** The equal-height parallelepipeds are proportional to their bases, so K : K′ = 2b : 2b′. Taking the same multiples does not change a ratio. Therefore
P : P′ = K : K′ = b : b′ = B₀ : B₀′.
This establishes the required comparison without assuming a formula for the magnitude of a pyramid. [11.32, 5.15, 5.11]

**Step 6.** Since Q = P and Q′ = P′, we also have Q : Q′ = B₀ : B₀′. Adding the antecedents and the consequents of these equal ratios gives
(P + Q) : (P′ + Q′) = B₀ : B₀′.
Thus the assertion holds for the two prisms of the first division. [12.3, 5.12]

**Step 7.** If the division is continued, divide corresponding residual pyramids in the same manner. By Steps 2–3, each such pair has equal heights and bases in the original ratio B₀ : B₀′. Hence the argument of Steps 4–6 applies to that pair, and its corresponding prisms have this same ratio. Its residual pyramids again have equal corresponding heights and bases in the same ratio. Repeating this argument proves that, after any finite number of corresponding divisions, every corresponding pair of prisms has ratio B₀ : B₀′. [12.3, 5.15, 5.11]

**Step 8.** Finally, the prisms in the two pyramids are equal in multitude and can be paired as above. Since every antecedent prism has to its corresponding consequent prism the ratio B₀ : B₀′, all the antecedents together have to all the consequents together that same ratio. Therefore, as the base of one pyramid is to the base of the other, so are all the prisms in the one to all the prisms, equal in multitude, in the other. [5.12]
