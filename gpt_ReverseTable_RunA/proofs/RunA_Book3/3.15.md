## Proposition 3.15
**Statement:** Of straight lines in a circle the diameter is greatest, and of the rest the nearer to the centre is always greater than the more remote.

**Step 1.** Let O be the centre of the circle, AB any diameter, and CD any chord not passing through O. Join OC and OD. Since AB passes through O, AB = AO + OB. [D1.17, P1]

**Step 2.** All radii are equal, so AO + OB = OC + OD. In triangle COD, OC + OD > CD. Therefore AB > CD. Thus every diameter is greater than every chord not through the centre; moreover, all diameters are equal, each consisting of two radii. [D1.15, CN2, 1.20]

**Step 3.** Now let CD and EF be chords not through O, with CD nearer to O than EF. Bisect CD at H and EF at K, and join OH, OK, and OE. The midpoint-to-perpendicular direction of 3.3 gives OH perpendicular to CD and OK perpendicular to EF. Consequently OH < OK, by the definition of greater distance from the centre. Also CH = HD and EK = KF. [1.10, P1, 3.3, D3.5]

**Step 4.** Write S(XY) for the square on a segment XY. The direct route is to compare the right triangles OHC and OKE, since their perpendicular legs are precisely those defining the distances. By the theorem on the square of the hypotenuse,
S(CH) + S(OH) = S(OC),
S(EK) + S(OK) = S(OE).
Since OC and OE are radii, these two sums are equal. [1.47, D1.15]

**Step 5.** Since OH < OK, the square on OK is greater than the square on OH: by 2.4, the square on a greater segment consists of the square on a part equal to the smaller segment together with additional positive areas. Remove an area equal to S(OH) from each of the equal sums in Step 4. On one side there remains S(CH); on the other there remains S(EK) together with the positive excess of S(OK) over S(OH). Hence S(CH) > S(EK). [2.4, CN3, CN5]

**Step 6.** Therefore CH > EK. Indeed, equality of the segments would give equal squares, while CH < EK would, by 2.4 and the whole–part principle, give S(CH) < S(EK). Since CD consists of two parts each equal to CH, and EF of two parts each equal to EK, it follows that CD > EF. Together with Step 2, this proves that the diameter is greatest, and that of the remaining chords the nearer to the centre is always greater than the more remote. [2.4, CN5, CN2, 3.3]
