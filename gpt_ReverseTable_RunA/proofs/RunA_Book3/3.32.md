## Proposition 3.32
**Statement:** If a straight line touch a circle, and from the point of contact there be drawn across, in the circle, a straight line cutting the circle, the angles which it makes with the tangent will be equal to the angles in the alternate segments of the circle.

**Step 1.** Let the straight line TAU touch the circle at A, with AT and AU opposite rays, and let AB be the straight line drawn from A to another point B of the circle. Find the centre O, join AO, and produce it through O to meet the circle again at C. Then AC is a diameter. We choose this diameter route because the radius–tangent theorem applies directly: AC is perpendicular to TAU. Thus ∠TAC and ∠CAU are right angles. Write R for a right angle. [3.1, P1, P2, D1.17, 3.18, P4]

**Step 2.** If B coincides with C, both angles made by AB with the tangent are right angles. The two segments cut off by AB are semicircles, and every angle in either semicircle is right. Therefore the required equalities hold in this case. Henceforth suppose B differs from C. [3.31, P4]

**Step 3.** Name the tangent rays so that T is on the same side of AC as B. Then AB lies inside ∠TAC, and T and C are on opposite sides of AB. Join BC. Since AC is a diameter, ∠ABC is right. The angles of triangle ABC therefore give
∠BAC + ∠BCA = R.
But the right angle TAC also gives
∠TAB + ∠BAC = R.
Subtracting the common angle BAC, we obtain
∠TAB = ∠BCA. [P1, 3.31, 1.32, P4, CN3]

**Step 4.** Let X be any point, other than A and B, on the circumference of the segment with base AB that contains C, and join AX and BX. Angles in this same segment are equal, so ∠AXB = ∠ACB; for X = C the equality is immediate. Consequently ∠TAB = ∠AXB. Since this segment lies on the opposite side of AB from T, it is the alternate segment for ∠TAB. [P1, 3.21, CN1, D3.8]

**Step 5.** Let Y be any point, other than A and B, on the circumference of the other segment, and join AY and BY. The quadrilateral AYBC is inscribed in the circle, so its opposite angles satisfy
∠AYB + ∠ACB = 2R.
Because AT and AU are opposite rays,
∠TAB + ∠BAU = 2R.
By Step 3, ∠TAB = ∠ACB. Subtracting these equal angles from the equal sums gives
∠BAU = ∠AYB.
This second segment lies opposite U across AB, so it is the alternate segment for ∠BAU. Thus each angle made by the chord with the tangent equals the angles in its alternate segment, as required. [P1, 3.22, 1.13, P4, CN3, D3.8]
