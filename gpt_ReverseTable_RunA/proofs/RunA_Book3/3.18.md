## Proposition 3.18
**Statement:** If a straight line touch a circle, and a straight line be joined from the centre to the point of contact, the straight line so joined will be perpendicular to the tangent.

**Step 1.** Let the straight line ℓ touch the circle at A, let O be its centre, and let OA be joined. The centre O lies inside the circle. Since a tangent, even when produced, does not cut the circle, ℓ contains no interior point of the circle; in particular, O is not on ℓ. [D1.15, D1.16, D3.2]

**Step 2.** Use the perpendicular-foot route. Suppose that OA is not perpendicular to ℓ. From O draw OB perpendicular to ℓ, with B on ℓ. Then B differs from A, and ∠OBA is a right angle. [1.12, D1.10]

**Step 3.** In triangle OAB, the angles OBA and OAB together are less than two right angles. Since ∠OBA is right, ∠OAB is less than ∠OBA. Here the two-angle bound is the natural first result needed; a full triangle angle-sum formula is unnecessary. The greater angle is subtended by the greater side, so OA > OB. [1.17, 1.19]

**Step 4.** But B, being on the tangent, is not inside the circle. Since O is inside, the segment OB meets the circumference at a point E, either before reaching B or at B itself. If E is between O and B, OB > OE because the whole is greater than its part; if E = B, OB = OE. Thus OB ≥ OE. [D3.2, D1.15, D1.16, CN5]

**Step 5.** As OA and OE both join the centre to the circumference, OA = OE. Consequently OB ≥ OA, contradicting OA > OB from Step 3. The supposition is therefore false: OA is perpendicular to the tangent ℓ. Hence the straight line joined from the centre to the point of contact is perpendicular to the tangent. [D1.15, D1.16, D1.10]
