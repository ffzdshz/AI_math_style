## Proposition 3.20
**Statement:** In a circle the angle at the centre is double of the angle at the circumference, when the angles have the same circumference as base.

**Step 1.** Let A, B, C be distinct points on a circle with centre O. Let Γ be the arc AB not containing C, and let Θ be the angle at O standing on Γ. Thus Θ is measured through that same arc, including a reflex central angle if required. We must prove Θ = 2∠ACB. Join OA, OB, and CO, and produce CO through O to meet the circle again at D. This diameter gives the direct route: first compare angles having D as one endpoint, then add or subtract them. [D3.9, P1, P2]

**Step 2.** For either endpoint X = A or X = B, provided X ≠ D, consider triangle COX. Its radii OC and OX are equal, so ∠OCX = ∠CXO. Since CD has the same direction from C as CO, ∠OCX = ∠XCD. Use the exterior-angle clause of 1.32 directly, since OD is already the produced side CO: ∠XOD = ∠OCX + ∠CXO = 2∠XCD. Consequently,
∠AOD = 2∠ACD and ∠BOD = 2∠BCD,
whenever the respective endpoint differs from D. [D1.15, 1.5, 1.32, CN2]

**Step 3.** If D coincides with A, the central angle on Γ is ∠BOD and ∠ACB = ∠BCD. Step 2, with X = B, therefore gives Θ = 2∠ACB. If D coincides with B, the same conclusion follows from Step 2 with X = A. This disposes of the cases in which CD is a side of the circumference angle. [D3.9; Step 2]

**Step 4.** Suppose D lies strictly on Γ. Then CD lies inside ∠ACB, and the central angle on Γ is the sum of the central angles on AD and DB. Hence
∠ACB = ∠ACD + ∠DCB,
Θ = ∠AOD + ∠DOB.
Adding the equalities from Step 2 gives
Θ = 2∠ACD + 2∠DCB = 2∠ACB.
This addition also covers the case in which Θ is reflex. [D3.9, CN2; Step 2]

**Step 5.** Finally, suppose D lies outside Γ and is distinct from A and B. Then A and B lie on the same side of the diameter CD. Interchanging their names if necessary, let CA be the ray nearer CD. The angle at C and the central angle on Γ are now the corresponding differences:
∠ACB = ∠BCD − ∠ACD,
Θ = ∠BOD − ∠AOD.
Subtracting the equalities from Step 2 therefore gives
Θ = 2∠BCD − 2∠ACD = 2∠ACB.
The three positions of D exhaust the possibilities. Thus, in a circle, the angle at the centre is double the angle at the circumference standing on the same circumference. [D3.9, CN3; Step 2]
