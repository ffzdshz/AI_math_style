## Proposition 3.22
**Statement:** The opposite angles of quadrilaterals in circles are equal to two right angles.

**Step 1.** Let ABCD be a quadrilateral inscribed in a circle, with its vertices in that order. Join AC and BD. These diagonals divide the interior angles at their endpoints. [P1]

**Step 2.** Choose the same-segment route, since the diagonals directly supply angles to compare with those of a triangle. The angles BAC and BDC stand on BC in the same segment, and the angles BCA and BDA stand on BA in the same segment. Hence
∠BAC = ∠BDC and ∠BCA = ∠BDA.
Adding these equalities gives
∠BAC + ∠BCA = ∠BDC + ∠BDA = ∠CDA. [3.21, CN2]

**Step 3.** In triangle ABC,
∠ABC + ∠BAC + ∠BCA = two right angles.
Using Step 2, it follows that
∠ABC + ∠CDA = two right angles. [1.32, CN2, CN1]

**Step 4.** Likewise, the angles ABD and ACD stand on AD in the same segment, and the angles ADB and ACB stand on AB in the same segment. Thus
∠ABD = ∠ACD and ∠ADB = ∠ACB.
Adding gives
∠ABD + ∠ADB = ∠ACD + ∠ACB = ∠BCD. [3.21, CN2]

**Step 5.** In triangle ABD,
∠BAD + ∠ABD + ∠ADB = two right angles.
Using Step 4, it follows that
∠BAD + ∠BCD = two right angles.
Therefore each pair of opposite angles of a quadrilateral inscribed in a circle is together equal to two right angles. [1.32, CN2, CN1]
