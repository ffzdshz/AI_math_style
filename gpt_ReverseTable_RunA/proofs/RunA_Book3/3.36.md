## Proposition 3.36
**Statement:** If a point be taken outside a circle and from it there fall on the circle two straight lines, and if one of them cut the circle and the other touch it, the rectangle contained by the whole of the straight line which cuts the circle and the straight line intercepted on it outside between the point and the convex circumference will be equal to the square on the tangent.

**Step 1.** Let P be the external point, let the secant meet the circle first at A and then at B, and let PT touch the circle at T. Find the centre O, and join OP, OA, OB, and OT. The radii satisfy OA = OB = OT. Write XY² for the square on XY and XY·ZW for the rectangle contained by XY and ZW. We must prove PA·PB = PT². [3.1, P1, D1.15]

**Step 2.** The radius OT is perpendicular to the tangent PT. Hence the triangle OTP is right-angled at T, and
OP² = OT² + PT².
[3.18, 1.47]

**Step 3.** We use II.6 directly, since its rectangle is precisely the rectangle contained by the whole secant and its external part. First suppose that the secant passes through O. Then AB is a diameter, and OA = OB, so O bisects AB, with P, A, O, B in that order. Apply II.6 to AB, bisected at O and extended by AP. It gives
PA·PB + OA² = OP².
[D1.17, D1.15, 2.6]

**Step 4.** Now suppose that the secant does not pass through O. Bisect AB at M and join OM. Since the line through the centre bisects the chord AB, it is perpendicular to AB. Thus OMP and OMA are right-angled triangles, with their right angles at M. [1.10, P1, 3.3]

**Step 5.** Apply II.6 to AB, bisected at M and extended by AP. Then
PA·PB + AM² = PM².
Also, applying the theorem on the square of the hypotenuse to OMP and OMA gives
PM² + OM² = OP²,
and
AM² + OM² = OA².
[2.6, 1.47]

**Step 6.** Add OM² to both sides of the first equality in Step 5:
PA·PB + AM² + OM² = PM² + OM².
Using the other two equalities, this becomes
PA·PB + OA² = OP².
Thus this equality holds whether or not the secant passes through the centre. [CN2, CN1]

**Step 7.** Comparing this equality with Step 2 gives
PA·PB + OA² = PT² + OT².
Since OA = OT, their squares are equal. Subtracting these equal squares yields
PA·PB = PT².
Therefore the rectangle contained by the whole secant PB and the external portion PA equals the square on the tangent PT, as required. [D1.15, CN1, CN3]
