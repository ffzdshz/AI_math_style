## Proposition 3.14
**Statement:** In a circle equal straight lines are equally distant from the centre, and those which are equally distant from the centre are equal to one another.

**Step 1.** Let AB and CD be chords of a circle with centre O, and first suppose that neither chord passes through O. Bisect AB at M and CD at N, and join OM, ON, OA, and OC [1.10, P1]. We choose the midpoint construction because the straight lines through O which bisect the chords are immediately perpendicular to them by III.3 [3.3]. Thus OM and ON are precisely the perpendiculars whose equality means that the chords are equally distant from the centre [D3.4].

**Step 2.** The radii OA and OC are equal [D1.15]. Applying I.47 to the right-angled triangles OMA and ONC gives
OA² = OM² + AM²,
OC² = ON² + CN² [1.47].
Consequently,
OM² + AM² = ON² + CN² [CN1].
Here squares denote the areas of the squares on the indicated segments. We use I.47 because this single equality proves both implications by subtraction.

**Step 3.** Suppose AB = CD. Since M and N are midpoints, AM = CN: otherwise doubling the greater half would give a greater chord [CN2, CN5]. Subtracting their equal squares from the equality in Step 2 gives OM² = ON², and hence OM = ON [CN3]. Therefore AB and CD are equally distant from O [D3.4].

**Step 4.** Conversely, suppose AB and CD are equally distant from O. Then OM = ON [D3.4]. Subtracting their equal squares from the equality in Step 2 gives AM² = CN², and hence AM = CN [CN3]. Since MB = AM and ND = CN, adding equal halves gives AB = CD [CN2].

**Step 5.** It remains to include chords through the centre, whose distance from it is zero. Such a chord has length twice a radius [D1.15, CN2]. Any chord XY not through O is shorter: joining OX and OY gives a triangle, and XY < OX + OY, which is twice a radius [P1, 1.20, D1.15]. Thus, if two equal chords include one through O, both must pass through O and have the same zero distance. Conversely, if two equally distant chords include one through O, both have zero distance and therefore both pass through O; each is twice a radius, so they are equal [D1.15, CN1, CN2]. Together with Steps 3 and 4, this proves that equal straight lines in a circle are equally distant from the centre, and those equally distant from the centre are equal to one another.
