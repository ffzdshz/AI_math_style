## Proposition 3.13
**Statement:** A circle does not touch a circle at more points than one, whether it touch it internally or externally.

**Step 1.** Suppose, for contradiction, that two circles touch at two distinct points A and B. Let their centres be O and P. Since touching circles do not have the same centre, O and P are distinct. [D1.15, D1.16, 3.6]

**Step 2.** Take the direct line-of-centres route: draw OP and produce it as necessary. If the contact is internal, apply 3.11 at A and again at B; both points lie on the produced line of centres. If the contact is external, apply 3.12 at each point, with the same result. Thus A, B, O, and P lie on one straight line in either case. [P1, P2, 3.11, 3.12]

**Step 3.** The radii of each circle are equal, so OA = OB and PA = PB. Each centre must lie strictly between A and B: it cannot coincide with a circumference point, since it lies within its circle; and if it lay outside the segment AB, one of its two equal radii would be a proper part of the other, contrary to the whole being greater than the part. [D1.15, CN5]

**Step 4.** Since the centres are distinct and both lie between A and B, interchange their names if necessary so that their order is A, O, P, B. Then AP > AO and OB > PB. Together with AO = OB, these give AP > AO = OB > PB, contradicting PA = PB. Therefore two circles cannot touch at two distinct points. A circle consequently does not touch another circle at more than one point, whether internally or externally. [CN5, D1.15]
