## Proposition 3.9
**Statement:** If a point be taken within a circle, and more than two equal straight lines fall from the point on the circle, the point taken is the centre of the circle.

**Step 1.** Let P be the given point within the circle. Since more than two equal straight lines fall from P upon the circle, choose three of them, PA, PB, PC, with distinct endpoints A, B, C on the circumference. Thus PA = PB = PC. Find the centre O of the circle. [3.1]

**Step 2.** Suppose that P is not O. Join O to P and produce this straight line in both directions to meet the circumference at D and E. Since DE passes through the centre and terminates at the circumference in both directions, it is a diameter; and P, being within the circle, lies on this diameter. [P1, P2, D1.17]

**Step 3.** Apply 3.7 directly: this is the natural route because P is now a point on a diameter other than the centre. That proposition states that only two equal straight lines can fall from such a point upon the circle, one on each side of the least straight line. This contradicts the three equal straight lines PA, PB, PC. Therefore P cannot differ from O, and the given point is the centre of the circle. [3.7]
