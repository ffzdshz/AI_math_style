## Proposition 3.23
**Statement:** On the same straight line there cannot be constructed two similar and unequal segments of circles on the same side.

**Step 1.** Suppose that two similar but unequal circular segments are constructed on the same straight line AB and on the same side of it. Each is bounded by AB and a circular arc. Their circles are distinct and already meet at A and B, so their arcs cannot meet again; consequently one segment lies within the other. Call the outer segment ACB and the inner segment ADB. [D3.6, 3.10]

**Step 2.** Take D on the arc of the inner segment, distinct from A and B. Draw AD and produce it to meet the outer arc at C, so that A, D, C occur in that order. Join DB and CB. [P1, P2]

**Step 3.** The angles ADB and ACB are angles in the two respective segments. Since the segments are similar, these angles are equal:
∠ADB = ∠ACB. [D3.8, D3.11]

**Step 4.** Apply the exterior-angle theorem directly, since the collinearity just constructed makes ∠ADB an exterior angle of triangle BDC. It is therefore greater than the opposite interior angle BCD. But the rays CA and CD coincide, so ∠BCD = ∠ACB. Thus ∠ADB > ∠ACB, contradicting Step 3. Hence on the same straight line there cannot be constructed two similar and unequal segments of circles on the same side. [1.16]
