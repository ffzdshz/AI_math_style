## Proposition 3.28
**Statement:** In equal circles equal straight lines cut off equal circumferences, the greater equal to the greater and the less to the less.

**Step 1.** Let Γ and Γ′ be equal circles, and let AB and DE be equal straight lines joining points on their respective circumferences. The circles have equal radii. Consequently, when their centres are superposed, their complete circumferences coincide and are equal. [D3.1, D1.15, CN4]

**Step 2.** First dispose of the diameter case. If AB is a diameter, then DE equals a diameter of Γ′, since the circles are equal. Therefore DE must itself be a diameter: otherwise it would be shorter than a diameter. The same argument applies with the circles interchanged. Each diameter bisects its circle, and superposing the equal circles with these diameters aligned makes their corresponding semicircumferences coincide. Thus the cut-off circumferences are equal in this case. Henceforth suppose neither chord is a diameter. [D3.1, 3.15, D1.17, CN4]

**Step 3.** Find the centres O and P of Γ and Γ′, and join OA, OB, PD, and PE. Since these are radii of equal circles,
OA = PD and OB = PE.
Also AB = DE by hypothesis. [3.1, P1, D1.15, D3.1]

**Step 4.** The triangles OAB and PDE therefore have their three sides respectively equal. Hence their angles contained by the radii are equal:
∠AOB = ∠DPE.
This central-angle route is direct because the given chord equality and the equality of radii supply exactly the side equalities required by I.8. [1.8]

**Step 5.** The ordinary, non-reflex central angles ∠AOB and ∠DPE stand on the respective lesser circumferences cut off by AB and DE. Equal angles in equal circles stand on equal circumferences; therefore these lesser circumferences are equal. [3.26]

**Step 6.** Subtract these equal lesser circumferences from the equal complete circumferences established in Step 1. The remaining circumferences—the respective greater ones—are equal. Thus equal straight lines in equal circles cut off equal circumferences, the greater equal to the greater and the less to the less. [CN3]
