## Proposition 3.21
**Statement:** In a circle the angles in the same segment are equal to one another.

**Step 1.** Let AB be the base of a segment of a circle, and let P and Q be any two points on its circumference, distinct from A and B. Join PA, PB, QA, and QB. The angles APB and AQB are angles in this same segment. Both have as their circumference-base the same arc AB opposite the segment containing P and Q. [D3.6, D3.8, P1]

**Step 2.** Find the centre O of the circle and join OA and OB. Let Θ denote the angle at the centre corresponding to that common arc AB. We compare the two given angles directly through Θ, choosing the central angle with their common arc as base. [3.1, P1]

**Step 3.** The angle at the centre is double the angle at the circumference when they have the same circumference as base. Applying this first to APB and then to AQB gives Θ = 2∠APB and Θ = 2∠AQB. Thus each given angle is half of the same central angle Θ. [3.20]

**Step 4.** Consequently ∠APB = ∠AQB, since both equal the same half of Θ. As P and Q were arbitrary, the angles in the same segment of a circle are equal to one another. [CN1]
