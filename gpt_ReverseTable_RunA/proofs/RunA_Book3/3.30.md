## Proposition 3.30
**Statement:** To bisect a given circumference.

**Step 1.** Let the given circumference be an arc with endpoints A and B. Join AB, and bisect it at D, so that AD = DB. [P1, 1.10]

**Step 2.** At D draw a perpendicular to AB, and produce it on the side containing the given arc until it meets that arc at C. Join AC and BC. The angles ADC and BDC are right angles and therefore equal. [1.11, P2, P1, P4]

**Step 3.** In triangles ADC and BDC, AD = DB, DC is common, and the included angles ADC and BDC are equal. Hence the corresponding remaining angles DAC and DBC are equal. Since D lies between A and B, these are respectively the angles BAC and ABC. Thus ∠BAC = ∠ABC. [1.4]

**Step 4.** Use the equal-angle route, since the preceding congruence supplies exactly the angles needed. The given circle is equal to itself, and equal angles at its circumference stand on equal circumferences. Now ∠BAC stands on the subarc BC not containing A, while ∠ABC stands on the subarc AC not containing B. These are precisely the two parts into which C divides the given arc. Therefore arc AC equals arc CB, and the given circumference has been bisected at C. [D3.1, 3.26]
