## Proposition 3.33
**Statement:** On a given straight line to describe a segment of a circle admitting an angle equal to a given rectilineal angle.

**Step 1.** Let AB be the given straight line and θ the given rectilineal angle. At A construct a ray AT such that ∠BAT = θ, and bisect AB at M. [1.23, 1.10]

**Step 2.** Draw the straight line ℓ through A perpendicular to AT, and the straight line m through M perpendicular to AB. If θ is right, take O = M; then AO is perpendicular to AT. If θ is not right, choose the ray of ℓ making an acute angle α with AB: when θ is acute, this ray lies on the side of AB opposite T and α is the difference between a right angle and θ; when θ is obtuse, it lies on T’s side and α is the difference between θ and a right angle. These angle relations follow from the perpendicular construction and the supplementary-angle relation. [1.11, D1.10, 1.13]

**Step 3.** In the non-right case, consider ℓ and m cut by the transversal AM, on the side containing the chosen ray of ℓ. Their interior angles are α and a right angle, and hence together are less than two right angles. Therefore ℓ and m, produced if necessary, meet on that side; call their intersection O. Thus in every case O is constructed and OA is perpendicular to AT. [P2, P5]

**Step 4.** We have OA = OB. Indeed, when O = M this follows from the bisection of AB. Otherwise join OB. In triangles AMO and BMO, AM = BM, MO is common, and ∠AMO and ∠BMO are equal right angles. Therefore their bases OA and OB are equal. [P1, P4, 1.4]

**Step 5.** Describe the circle with centre O and radius OA. Since OB = OA, it passes through both A and B. The line OA, produced through the circle, is a diameter line; AT is perpendicular to it at A. Consequently the straight line containing AT falls outside the circle except at A and therefore touches the circle there. [P3, D1.15, P2, D1.17, 3.16, D3.2]

**Step 6.** Take the segment bounded by AB and the arc lying on the side of AB opposite T. Let C be any point of this arc other than A and B, and join CA and CB. The tangent–chord theorem is the direct choice here, because the construction has supplied a tangent making the prescribed angle with the chord. It gives
∠ACB = ∠BAT = θ.
By definition, ∠ACB is an angle in the chosen segment. Thus the segment admits an angle equal to the given rectilineal angle, including when that angle is right. The required segment has therefore been described on AB. [D3.6, P1, 3.32, CN1, D3.8]
