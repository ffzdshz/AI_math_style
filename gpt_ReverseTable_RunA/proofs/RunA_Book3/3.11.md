## Proposition 3.11
**Statement:** If two circles touch one another internally, and their centres be taken, the straight line joining their centres, if it be also produced, will fall on the point of contact of the circles.

**Step 1.** Let the circles touch internally at T, and let O be the centre of the larger circle and P the centre of the smaller. Thus T belongs to both circumferences, and the smaller circle is contained in the larger. The centres O and P are distinct. [D3.3, 3.6]

**Step 2.** Draw OP, OT, and PT. Produce OP beyond P, meeting the smaller circumference at U and the larger circumference at V. Since P is inside the smaller circle, and that circle is contained in the larger, U lies between P and V or coincides with V. Consequently OU ≤ OV, by the whole being greater than its part when U and V are distinct. [P1, P2, D1.15, D1.16, CN5]

**Step 3.** As radii of their respective circles, PT = PU and OT = OV. Adding OP to the first equality gives OP + PT = OP + PU = OU. [D1.15, D1.16, CN2]

**Step 4.** Suppose T does not lie on the straight line OP. Then OPT is a triangle. Use the direct triangle-inequality route, since its sides are precisely the two radii to T and the segment joining the centres: OP + PT > OT. By Step 3, this says OU > OT. But Step 2 gives OU ≤ OV, and Step 3 gives OV = OT—a contradiction. [1.20]

**Step 5.** Therefore O, P, and T lie on one straight line. Hence the straight line joining the centres, produced as necessary, passes through the point of contact of the circles. [P2]
