## Proposition 3.1
**Statement:** To find the centre of a given circle.

**Step 1.** Choose two distinct points A and B on the given circumference and join AB. Bisect AB at D, and draw the straight line ℓ perpendicular to AB at D. We use the perpendicular-bisector route to locate the centre. (P1, 1.10, 1.11)

**Step 2.** Let O denote the centre whose existence is included in the definition of the given circle; its position is not assumed known. Join OA and OB. Since A and B lie on the circumference, OA = OB. (D1.15, D1.16, P1)

**Step 3.** The only point on the straight line AB equidistant from A and B is D. Indeed, for a point outside the segment AB, one of its distances to the endpoints properly contains the other. For a point within AB but different from D, one distance is smaller than one half of AB and the other is greater than the other, equal half. Thus in either case the distances are unequal. Consequently, if O lies on AB, then O = D and hence O lies on ℓ. (CN1, CN5)

**Step 4.** Suppose instead that O does not lie on AB, and join OD. In triangles ADO and BDO, AD = BD, DO is common, and AO = BO. Proposition 1.8 applies directly because all three corresponding side equalities are available; it gives ∠ADO = ∠BDO. These are adjacent angles on the straight line AB, so each is right. Therefore OD is perpendicular to AB. (P1, CN4, 1.8, D1.10)

**Step 5.** Take the ray DX of ℓ on the same side of AB as O. Both ∠ADX and ∠ADO are right, so they are equal. If DX and DO were distinct rays, one of these angles would be a proper part of the other, contradicting that the whole is greater than the part. Thus DO lies on ℓ. Together with Step 3, this proves that the centre O lies on the constructed line ℓ in every case. (P4, CN5)

**Step 6.** Extend ℓ to meet the circumference at E and F, and bisect EF at G. Since ℓ passes through the interior point O, the points E and F lie on opposite sides of O; moreover, OE = OF by the definition of the circle. The midpoint argument of Step 3, applied to EF, therefore gives O = G. Hence the constructed point G is the centre of the given circle, as required. (P2, 1.10, D1.15, CN1, CN5, D1.16)
