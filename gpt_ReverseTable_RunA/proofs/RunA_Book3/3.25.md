## Proposition 3.25
**Statement:** Given a segment of a circle, to describe the complete circle of which it is a segment.

**Step 1.** Let AB be the straight boundary of the given segment, and choose a third point C on its curved boundary. Denote by Γ the circle of which it is a segment; this names the existing circle without assuming that it has been drawn. The points A, B, C are not collinear: otherwise the middle point would lie on the circumference and also within the chord joining the outermost two, contrary to III.2. [D3.6, 3.2]

**Step 2.** Join AC. Bisect AB at M and AC at N. At M draw the straight line ℓ perpendicular to AB, and at N draw the straight line m perpendicular to AC, producing these lines as necessary. This construction uses only the given segment. [P1, 1.10, 1.11, P2]

**Step 3.** The lines ℓ and m are distinct. For, if they coincided, the triangle AMN would have right angles at both M and N. This contradicts the fact that any two angles of a triangle are together less than two right angles. [D1.10, 1.17]

**Step 4.** Let S denote the centre of Γ. We use the direct chord-bisection criterion III.3 to locate S, rather than divide the construction into cases according to the size of the segment. If S is not on the straight line AB, join SM. Since SM passes through the centre and bisects the chord AB, III.3 makes it perpendicular to AB; hence S lies on ℓ. If S is on the straight line AB, the equal radii SA and SB force S to be the midpoint M: S cannot be beyond either endpoint, since then one radius would be a proper part of the other. Thus S lies on ℓ in this case also. Applying the same reasoning to AC and its midpoint N shows that S lies on m. [D1.15, D1.16, P1, 3.3, CN5]

**Step 5.** Produce ℓ and m to meet at O. Their intersection exists because both contain S, and it is unique because the lines are distinct. Consequently O is S, the centre of Γ. Thus the centre has been obtained by constructions on the given segment alone. [P2; Steps 3–4]

**Step 6.** With centre O and distance OA describe a circle. Since O is the centre of Γ and A lies on Γ, this is precisely the complete circle whose segment was given. Therefore the required circle has been described. [P3, D1.15, D1.16]
