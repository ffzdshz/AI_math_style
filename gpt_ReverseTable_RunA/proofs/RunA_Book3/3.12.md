## Proposition 3.12
**Statement:** If two circles touch one another externally, the straight line joining their centres will pass through the point of contact.

**Step 1.** Let the two circles touch externally at C, and let their centres be A and B. The centres are distinct by 3.6. Draw AB, AC, and BC (P1).

**Step 2.** Let D be the point where the segment AB leaves the circle with centre A, and E the point where it enters the circle with centre B. Since the contact is external, the circles have no overlapping interiors: they meet without cutting one another (D3.3 and the external-contact hypothesis). Thus the order along AB is A, D, E, B, allowing D and E to coincide; the opposite order of D and E would give a segment inside both circles. Consequently,
AD + EB ≤ AB,
with equality if D = E, and strict inequality otherwise, since AB then contains the additional segment DE (CN5).

**Step 3.** Radii of the same circle are equal, so AC = AD and BC = BE (D1.15). Adding these equalities gives AC + BC = AD + BE (CN2). Hence
AC + BC ≤ AB.

**Step 4.** Suppose C were not on the straight line AB. Then ACB would be a triangle. We use the triangle inequality directly, since it compares precisely these three segments: AC + BC > AB (1.20). This contradicts Step 3. Therefore the straight line joining the centres A and B passes through the point of contact C, as required.
