## Proposition 3.29
**Statement:** In equal circles equal circumferences are subtended by equal straight lines.

**Step 1.** Let two equal circles have equal arcs AB and CD, subtended by the straight lines AB and CD. If these arcs are semicircles, their chords are diameters, and therefore AB = CD by the definition of equal circles. Thus it remains to consider the non-semicircular case. [D1.18, D3.1]

**Step 2.** Find the centres O and P of the respective circles, and join OA, OB, PC, and PD, as well as the chords AB and CD. [3.1, P1]

**Step 3.** Radii of a circle are equal, and equal circles have equal radii. Consequently OA = PC and OB = PD. [D1.15, D3.1]

**Step 4.** Since the arcs AB and CD are equal, the angles they subtend at the centres are equal; hence the included angles ∠AOB and ∠CPD are equal. We use the centre-angle form of III.27 because it directly supplies the included angles for the two pairs of equal radii. [3.27]

**Step 5.** The triangles AOB and CPD therefore have two sides respectively equal and their included angles equal. By I.4 their bases are equal: AB = CD. Thus, in equal circles, equal circumferences are subtended by equal straight lines. [1.4]
