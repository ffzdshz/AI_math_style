## Proposition 3.31
**Statement:** In a circle the angle in the semicircle is right, that in a greater segment less than a right angle, and that in a less segment greater than a right angle; and further the angle of the greater segment is greater than a right angle, and the angle of the less segment less than a right angle.

**Step 1.** Let O be the centre of a circle, BC a diameter, and A any point on one of its semicircumferences, distinct from B and C. Join AB, AC, and AO, and produce BA through A to F. Thus O lies between B and C. [D1.17, D1.18, P1, P2]

**Step 2.** Use the equal radii and the isosceles triangles supplied directly by this construction. Since OA = OB, ∠OAB = ∠ABO = ∠ABC. Since OA = OC, ∠OAC = ∠ACO = ∠ACB. Adding, and observing that AO divides ∠BAC, gives
∠BAC = ∠ABC + ∠ACB.
[D1.15, 1.5, CN2]

**Step 3.** By the exterior-angle theorem in triangle ABC,
∠FAC = ∠ABC + ∠ACB.
Consequently ∠BAC = ∠FAC. These are adjacent angles made by AC standing on the straight line BAF, so each is right. Since A was arbitrary, every angle in a semicircle is right. Write R for a right angle; all right angles are equal. [1.32, CN1, D1.10, D3.8, P4]

**Step 4.** Consider the two segments with base AC, and choose D arbitrarily on the arc AC not containing B. The segment ABC is greater than a semicircle: it contains the entire semicircle on BC opposite A, together with an additional part. The complementary segment ADC is a proper part of the semicircle on BC containing A, and is therefore less than a semicircle. This configuration covers any non-diametral chord: name its endpoints A and C and take B diametrically opposite C. [D1.17, D1.18, D3.6, 3.2, CN5]

**Step 5.** Reusing the triangle-angle sum in 1.32, the angles of triangle ABC total two right angles. Subtracting the right angle BAC gives
∠ABC + ∠ACB = R.
Hence ∠ABC < R. This is an angle in the greater segment ABC, and every other angle in that segment equals it. Thus every angle in a segment greater than a semicircle is less than a right angle. [1.32, CN3, CN5, D3.8, 3.21]

**Step 6.** The quadrilateral ABCD is inscribed in the circle, so its opposite angles satisfy
∠ABC + ∠ADC = 2R.
Since ∠ABC < R, its supplement ∠ADC is greater than R. Because D was arbitrary on the lesser arc, every angle in a segment less than a semicircle is greater than a right angle. [3.22, CN3, CN5, D3.8]

**Step 7.** Finally distinguish the angle *of* a segment—the angle between its base and its circumference—from an angle *in* it. At A, on the greater-segment side of AC, the chord AB lies strictly inside the circle and between AC and the bounding circumference. Hence the angle of the greater segment ABC strictly contains ∠BAC, which is right, and is therefore greater than a right angle. On the lesser-segment side of AC, the bounding arc AD lies between AC and the outward continuation AF of BA. Consequently the angle of the lesser segment ADC is a proper part of ∠FAC, which is right, and is therefore less than a right angle. Either endpoint of the base could have been designated A, so the comparisons hold at either endpoint. [D3.7, 3.2, CN5]

Therefore the angle in a semicircle is right; the angle in a greater segment is less than a right angle; the angle in a lesser segment is greater than a right angle; and the angles of the greater and lesser segments are respectively greater and less than a right angle.
