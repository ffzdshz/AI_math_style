## Proposition 3.17
**Statement:** From a given point to draw a straight line touching a given circle.

**Step 1.** Let Γ be the given circle and A the given point, outside Γ or on its circumference. These are the admissible cases: a line through an interior point cuts the circle and therefore cannot touch it. Find the centre O of Γ. [D3.2, 3.1]

**Step 2.** First suppose A is outside Γ. Join OA, and let B be its intersection with Γ between O and A. Describe the auxiliary circle Ω with centre O and radius OA. [P1, P3]

**Step 3.** At B draw a perpendicular to OA, and produce it to meet Ω at D, choosing either side of OA. Join OD, let E be its intersection with Γ between O and D, and join AE. [1.11, P2, P1]

**Step 4.** Compare triangles OBD and OEA. We have OB = OE, since both are radii of Γ, and OD = OA, since both are radii of Ω. Moreover, ∠BOD = ∠EOA: OB and OA lie along the same ray, as do OE and OD, so these angles coincide. Thus side–angle–side is the direct choice, since the two pairs of sides and their included angles are already equal. By I.4, ∠OBD = ∠OEA. But ∠OBD is right by construction; therefore AE is perpendicular to OE. [D1.15, CN4, 1.4, 1.11]

**Step 5.** Produce EO through O to the opposite circumference of Γ, obtaining a diameter with extremity E. The line AE is perpendicular to this diameter at E. By III.16 it falls outside Γ apart from E. It therefore meets Γ without cutting it, even when produced, and so touches Γ at E. Thus AE is the required straight line from the exterior point A. [P2, D1.17, 3.16, D3.2]

**Step 6.** If instead A lies on Γ, join OA and produce it through O to form a diameter with extremity A. At A draw a straight line perpendicular to that diameter. By III.16 this line falls outside Γ apart from A, and hence touches Γ at A. Therefore, in either admissible case, a straight line touching the given circle has been drawn from the given point. [P1, P2, D1.17, 1.11, 3.16, D3.2]
