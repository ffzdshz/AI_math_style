## Proposition 3.5
**Statement:** If two circles cut one another, they will not have the same centre.

**Step 1.** Let two circles cut one another at A, and suppose, for a contradiction, that they have the same centre O. We use the defining equality of radii, since the common point A immediately relates the radii of the two circles. By the definitions of circle and centre, O lies inside both circles. Since the circles cut one another, choose a point B on the circumference of the first circle strictly inside the second. [D1.15, D1.16]

**Step 2.** Join OA and OB, and produce OB to meet the circumference of the second circle at C beyond B. Thus O, B, C lie on one straight line, with B between O and C. [P1, P2]

**Step 3.** Since O is the centre of the first circle and A and B lie on its circumference, OA = OB. Since O is also the centre of the second circle and A and C lie on its circumference, OA = OC. Therefore OB = OC, because things equal to the same thing are equal to one another. [D1.15, D1.16, CN1]

**Step 4.** But OB is a proper part of OC, so OC is greater than OB, contradicting their equality. Hence the two circles cannot have the same centre. Therefore, if two circles cut one another, they will not have the same centre. [CN5]
