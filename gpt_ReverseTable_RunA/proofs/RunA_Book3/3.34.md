## Proposition 3.34
**Statement:** From a given circle to cut off a segment admitting an angle equal to a given rectilineal angle.

**Step 1.** Let the given circle be Γ and the given rectilineal angle be α. Choose a point A on Γ and draw a tangent AT at A. We use the tangent–chord route because it transfers the prescribed angle directly to a segment of the given circle. [3.17]

**Step 2.** At A, on the side of the tangent containing the circle, construct an angle TAB equal to α, letting its other side meet Γ again at B. Thus AB is a chord and ∠TAB = α. [1.23]

**Step 3.** Take the segment S bounded by AB and the arc AB lying on the opposite side of AB from T; this is the alternate segment corresponding to ∠TAB. Let P be any point of that arc other than A and B, and join PA and PB. Then ∠APB is an angle in S. [D3.6, P1, D3.8]

**Step 4.** Since AT touches Γ and AB is drawn from the point of contact across the circle, the angle between AT and AB equals the angle in the alternate segment: ∠APB = ∠TAB. But ∠TAB = α, so ∠APB = α. Since P was arbitrary, S admits an angle equal to the given angle. Thus the chord AB cuts off the required segment from the given circle. [3.32, CN1]
