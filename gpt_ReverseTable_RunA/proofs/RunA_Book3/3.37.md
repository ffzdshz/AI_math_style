## Proposition 3.37
**Statement:** If a point be taken outside a circle and from the point there fall on the circle two straight lines, if one of them cut the circle, and the other fall on it, and if further the rectangle contained by the whole of the straight line which cuts the circle and the straight line intercepted on it outside between the point and the convex circumference be equal to the square on the straight line which falls on the circle, the straight line which falls on it will touch the circle.

**Step 1.** Let P be outside the circle, let the secant meet it first at A and then at B, and let C be the point where the other straight line meets the circle. Thus, by hypothesis, the rectangle contained by PB and PA equals the square on PC. Draw a tangent PT to the circle, touching it at T [3.17]. We choose this direct route because the known tangent allows the given rectangle to be evaluated by 3.36.

**Step 2.** The rectangle contained by PB and PA equals the square on PT [3.36]. Consequently the squares on PC and PT are equal [CN1], and hence PC = PT. Indeed, if their sides were unequal, fitting the squares together at a corner along corresponding sides would make the smaller square a proper part of the larger, contradicting their equality [D1.22, CN5].

**Step 3.** Find the centre O and join OP, OC, and OT [3.1, P1]. The radii OC and OT are equal [D1.15]. Since PT touches the circle at T, OT is perpendicular to PT; thus angle OTP is right [3.18].

**Step 4.** The lengths OC, CP, and PO equal respectively OT, TP, and PO. Since the latter are the sides of the nondegenerate right triangle OTP, they satisfy the strict triangle inequalities [1.20]; therefore O, C, and P are not collinear. Compare triangles OCP and OTP by side–side–side, the natural choice because all three corresponding side equalities are now known. Their sides OC and CP equal OT and TP, and their base OP is common. Hence angle OCP equals angle OTP [1.8], and is therefore right.

**Step 5.** Extend CO through O to form the diameter having C as an extremity [P2]. The straight line PC is at right angles to this diameter at C [D1.10]. Therefore it falls outside the circle [3.16]. Since it meets the circle at C but does not cut it when produced, it touches the circle [D3.2]. Thus the straight line which falls on the circle is a tangent, as required.
