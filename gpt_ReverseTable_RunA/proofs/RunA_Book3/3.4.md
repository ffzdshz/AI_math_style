## Proposition 3.4
**Statement:** If in a circle two straight lines cut one another which are not through the centre, they do not bisect one another.

**Step 1.** Let the chords AB and CD intersect at E, and suppose neither passes through the centre. Assume, for a contradiction, that they bisect one another, so AE = EB and CE = ED. Find the centre O of the circle. Since neither chord passes through O, we have O ≠ E. [3.1]

**Step 2.** Join OE. Choose the endpoint names A and C so that they lie on the same side of the straight line OE. Interchanging the names of the two chords if necessary, let EC lie strictly inside angle OEA. This is possible because the chords are distinct and neither lies on OE. [P1]

**Step 3.** Use the forward implication of 3.3, since the assumed bisections are precisely its hypothesis. The straight line OE passes through the centre and bisects AB, which does not pass through the centre; therefore angle OEA is a right angle. Likewise, because OE bisects CD, angle OEC is a right angle. [3.3]

**Step 4.** All right angles are equal, so angle OEA equals angle OEC. But EC lies strictly inside angle OEA, making angle OEC a proper part of angle OEA; hence angle OEA is greater than angle OEC. This is a contradiction. Therefore two straight lines intersecting in a circle, neither passing through its centre, do not bisect one another. [P4, CN5]
