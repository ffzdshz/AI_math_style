## Proposition 3.19
**Statement:** If a straight line touch a circle, and from the point of contact a straight line be drawn at right angles to the tangent, the centre of the circle will be on the straight line so drawn.

**Step 1.** Let the straight line t touch the circle at A, and let ℓ be the straight line drawn through A at right angles to t. Let O be the centre of the circle, and join AO. [P1]

**Step 2.** Apply III.18 directly: it concerns precisely the given tangent and the radius to its point of contact. Thus AO is perpendicular to t, and the angles it makes with t are right angles. [3.18, D1.10]

**Step 3.** Suppose that O does not lie on ℓ. Choose a ray AX of ℓ on the same side of t as O, producing ℓ through A if necessary, and choose a ray AT along t. Since AX and AO are distinct rays in the same half-plane bounded by t, one of the angles TAX and TAO is a proper part of the other. Therefore these angles are unequal, since the whole is greater than the part. [P2, CN5]

**Step 4.** But angle TAX is right by hypothesis, and angle TAO is right by Step 2. All right angles are equal, contradicting Step 3. Therefore O lies on ℓ: the centre is on the straight line drawn at right angles to the tangent from the point of contact. [P4]
