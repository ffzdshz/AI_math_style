## Proposition 3.3
**Statement:** If in a circle a straight line through the centre bisect a straight line not through the centre, it also cuts it at right angles; and if it cut it at right angles, it also bisects it.

**Step 1.** Let O be the centre of the circle, AB a chord not passing through O, and the given straight line through O meet AB at D, between A and B. Join OA and OB [P1]. By the definition of a circle and its centre, OA = OB [D1.15, D1.16]. Since O is not on AB, the triangles ODA and ODB are nondegenerate.

**Step 2.** First suppose that AD = DB. In triangles ADO and BDO, AD = BD, DO is common, and AO = BO. The three side equalities give the direct application of I.8: ∠ADO = ∠BDO [1.8]. These are adjacent angles made by OD standing on the straight line AB. Hence each is right, and the line through O cuts AB at right angles [D1.10].

**Step 3.** Conversely, suppose that the line through O cuts AB at right angles. Then ∠ODA = ∠ODB, since all right angles are equal [P4]. Also, OA = OB, so the base angles ∠OAB and ∠OBA of triangle OAB are equal [1.5]. Because D lies between A and B, these are respectively ∠OAD and ∠OBD. Thus ∠OAD = ∠OBD.

**Step 4.** Apply I.26 directly to triangles OAD and OBD: the angles at A and B are equal, the right angles at D are equal, and OA = OB are the sides opposite those equal right angles. This uses the angle and side equalities already available, without any auxiliary construction. Therefore AD = DB [1.26]. Thus the line through the centre bisects AB. Both implications are proved.
