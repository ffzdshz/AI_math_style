## Proposition 9.12
**Statement:** If as many numbers as we please beginning from an unit be in continued proportion, by however many prime numbers the last is measured, the next to the unit will also be measured by the same.

**Step 1.** Write the given continued proportion as \(1,A_1,A_2,\ldots,A_n\), where \(A_1\) is next to the unit. We take the direct successive-product route, because the common ratio determines each term from its predecessor. For every \(k\geq2\),
\[
1:A_1=A_{k-1}:A_k.
\]
Since a number consists of units, the unit measures \(A_1\) as many times as there are units in \(A_1\). By the definition of numerical proportion, \(A_{k-1}\) measures \(A_k\) that same number of times. Thus, by the definition of multiplication,
\[
A_k=A_1A_{k-1}.
\]
[D7.2, D7.20, D7.15]

**Step 2.** Let \(p\) be any prime number measuring the last term \(A_n\). If \(n=1\), it already measures the number next to the unit. Otherwise, since \(A_n=A_1A_{n-1}\), VII.30 shows that \(p\) measures either \(A_1\) or \(A_{n-1}\). If it measures \(A_1\), the assertion follows. If not, apply the same theorem to the preceding product, and continue backwards. This finite process must reach \(A_2=A_1A_1\), where VII.30 necessarily gives that \(p\) measures \(A_1\). Hence every prime number measuring the last term also measures the number next to the unit. Therefore, by however many prime numbers the last is measured, the next to the unit is measured by the same. [7.30]
