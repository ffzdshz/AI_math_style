## Proposition 9.31
**Statement:** If an odd number be prime to any number, it will also be prime to the double of it.

**Step 1.** Let A be an odd number prime to a number B. Since A is odd, it cannot be divided into two equal parts. Therefore 2 does not measure A: otherwise A would be twice some number, and its two copies would give two equal parts. [D7.7, D7.15]

**Step 2.** Since 2 consists of two units, its only measures are the unit and 2 itself. As 2 does not measure A, the unit alone is a common measure of A and 2. Thus A and 2 are prime to one another. [D7.2, D7.12]

**Step 3.** Choose the direct product route of VII.24, because both factors B and 2 are now known to be prime to the same number A. By that proposition, their product is also prime to A. This product is B added to itself, hence the double of B. Since being prime to one another is symmetric, A is prime to the double of B, as required. [7.24, D7.15, D7.12]
