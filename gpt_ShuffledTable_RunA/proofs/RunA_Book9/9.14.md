## Proposition 9.14
**Statement:** If a number be the least that is measured by prime numbers, it will not be measured by any other prime number except those originally measuring it.

**Step 1.** Let N be the least number measured by the given primes p₁, …, pᵣ. Suppose that another prime q, distinct from every pᵢ, measures N. Distinct primes cannot measure one another, since a prime has no numerical measure other than itself. Consequently N ≠ q, for otherwise p₁ would measure q. [D7.11]

**Step 2.** Since q measures N and is less than N, there is a number M such that N = qM. This product consists of as many copies of M as there are units in q. Since q is a number, there is more than one such copy; hence M < N. [D7.5, D7.15, D7.2, CN5]

**Step 3.** Use the prime-divisor-of-a-product theorem directly, since the factorization N = qM already supplies its hypotheses. Each original prime pᵢ measures qM, so it measures either q or M. But pᵢ cannot measure the distinct prime q. Therefore every pᵢ measures M. [7.30, D7.11]

**Step 4.** By Steps 2–3, M is a number less than N measured by all the original primes, contradicting the stipulated leastness of N. Thus N is not measured by any prime except those originally measuring it.
