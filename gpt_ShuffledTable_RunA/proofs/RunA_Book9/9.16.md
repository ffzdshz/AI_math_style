## Proposition 9.16
**Statement:** If two numbers be prime to one another, the second will not be to any other number as the first is to the second.

**Step 1.** Let A and B be numbers prime to one another; thus their only common measure is the unit. Suppose, for a contradiction, that there is a number C such that A:B = B:C. [D7.12]

**Step 2.** Choose the least-numbers route, since it applies directly to the given coprimality. By 7.21, A and B are the least numbers among those having the same ratio as A:B. [7.21]

**Step 3.** The least numbers having a given ratio measure the corresponding numbers having that ratio. Applying this to A:B = B:C, we obtain that A measures B (and B measures C). [7.20]

**Step 4.** Since A also measures itself, A is a common measure of A and B. But A is a number, a multitude of units, not the unit; this contradicts their being prime to one another. Therefore no number C can satisfy B:C = A:B: the second number is not to any other number as the first is to the second. [D7.2, D7.12]
