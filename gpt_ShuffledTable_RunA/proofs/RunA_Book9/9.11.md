## Proposition 9.11
**Statement:** If as many numbers as we please beginning from an unit be in continued proportion, the less measures the greater according to some one of the numbers which have place among the proportional numbers.

**Step 1.** Let the given continued proportion be
\(1,A_1,A_2,\ldots,A_n\), where \(1\) denotes the unit. Since \(A_1\) is a multitude of units and every consecutive pair has the ratio \(1:A_1\), the terms increase. Choose any two of the numbers, the less \(A_i\) and the greater \(A_j\), so that \(i<j\), and put \(d=j-i\). Then \(1\le d\le n-1\), so both \(A_d\) and \(A_{d+1}\) occur in the given progression. [D7.2, D7.3, D7.20]

**Step 2.** By continued proportion,
\[
1:A_1=A_d:A_{d+1}.
\]
The unit measures \(A_1\) according to \(A_1\); hence \(A_d\) measures \(A_{d+1}\) the same number of times. Apply VII.15, choosing this alternation rule because it explicitly concerns a unit: the unit measures \(A_d\) the same number of times that \(A_1\) measures \(A_{d+1}\). Therefore \(A_1\) measures \(A_{d+1}\) according to \(A_d\). [D7.2, D7.3, D7.20, 7.15]

**Step 3.** Consider the two blocks of numbers
\[
A_1,A_2,\ldots,A_{d+1}
\quad\text{and}\quad
A_i,A_{i+1},\ldots,A_j.
\]
They have equal length, and their corresponding consecutive ratios are equal by the given continued proportion. Thus VII.14 applies directly to these blocks and gives, ex aequali,
\[
A_1:A_{d+1}=A_i:A_j.
\]
[7.14]

**Step 4.** Since \(A_1\) measures \(A_{d+1}\) according to \(A_d\), equality of these ratios means that \(A_i\) likewise measures \(A_j\) according to \(A_d\). This is one of the numbers in the given progression. If the less term chosen is the initial unit itself, it measures any greater term according to that very term. Consequently, in every case the less measures the greater according to one of the numbers having a place among the proportional numbers, as required. [D7.20, D7.3, D7.2]
