## Proposition 9.25
**Statement:** If from an even number an odd number be subtracted, the remainder will be odd.

**Step 1.** Let A be an even number, B the odd number subtracted from it, and C the remainder; thus A = B + C. By the definitions of even and odd, it suffices to show that C is not even. [D7.6, D7.7]

**Step 2.** Use the preceding subtraction proposition, 9.24, since it applies directly to the same whole with its remainder subtracted. Suppose C were even. Then subtracting C from the even number A would leave an even number. But what remains is B, which is odd—a contradiction. [9.24, D7.6, D7.7]

**Step 3.** Therefore C is not even: it is not divisible into two equal parts, and hence is odd. Thus, if an odd number is subtracted from an even number, the remainder is odd. [D7.6, D7.7]
