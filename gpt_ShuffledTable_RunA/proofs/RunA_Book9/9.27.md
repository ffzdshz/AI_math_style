## Proposition 9.27
**Statement:** If from an odd number an even number be subtracted, the remainder will be odd.

**Step 1.** Let A be the given odd number, B the even number subtracted from it, and R the remainder, so that A = B + R. By the definitions, B is divisible into two equal parts, whereas A is not. [D7.6, D7.7]

**Step 2.** Use IX.21, since adding B back to the remainder recovers A. If R were even, then B and R would both be even, and their sum A would be even by IX.21. Thus A would be divisible into two equal parts, contrary to its being odd. [9.21, D7.6, D7.7]

**Step 3.** Therefore R is not divisible into two equal parts and hence is odd. Consequently, if an even number is subtracted from an odd number, the remainder is odd. [D7.6, D7.7]
