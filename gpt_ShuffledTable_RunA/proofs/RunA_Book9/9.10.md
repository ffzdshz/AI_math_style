## Proposition 9.10
**Statement:** If as many numbers as we please beginning from an unit be in continued proportion, and the number after the unit be not square, neither will any other be square except the third from the unit and all those which leave out one.

**Step 1.** Write the given progression as A₁, A₂, …, Aₙ, where A₁ is the unit and A₂ is not square. By IX.8, A₃, A₅, A₇, and all subsequent terms in alternate positions are square. It remains to exclude squares in the even positions. [9.8]

**Step 2.** Use VIII.22 on reversed consecutive triples: this is the direct route because it transfers squareness two positions backward without constructing any auxiliary numbers. For every j ≥ 4, continued proportion gives
Aⱼ₋₂ : Aⱼ₋₁ = Aⱼ₋₁ : Aⱼ.
By the definition of numerical proportionality, reversing these ratios gives
Aⱼ : Aⱼ₋₁ = Aⱼ₋₁ : Aⱼ₋₂.
Thus Aⱼ, Aⱼ₋₁, Aⱼ₋₂ are also in continued proportion. If Aⱼ is square, VIII.22 therefore makes Aⱼ₋₂ square. [D7.20, 8.22]

**Step 3.** Suppose an even-positioned term A₂ᵣ, with r ≥ 2, were square. Repeatedly applying Step 2 would make A₂ᵣ₋₂, A₂ᵣ₋₄, and eventually A₂ square, contrary to the hypothesis. Hence no even-positioned term is square; A₂ itself is already excluded by hypothesis. Together with Step 1, this proves that among the numbers after the unit, precisely the third from the unit and those successively leaving out one are square. [8.22, 9.8]
