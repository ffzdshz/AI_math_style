## Proposition 9.5
**Statement:** If a cube number by multiplying any number make a cube number, the multiplied number will also be cube.

**Step 1.** Let the cube number A, by multiplying the number B, produce the cube number C. Let a and c be the respective sides of these cubes. Thus A = a³, C = c³, and AB = C. [D7.19]

**Step 2.** Since AB = C, A measures C. We use the direct side-divisibility theorem, since the hypothesis already supplies this measurement: because the cube A measures the cube C, its side a measures the side c. Let d be the number of times that a measures c; then c = ad. [D7.15, 8.15]

**Step 3.** Regrouping the repeated sums defining multiplication and interchanging factors gives
C = c³ = (ad)(ad)(ad) = (aaa)(ddd) = A·d³.
Consequently AB = A·d³, since both products equal C. [D7.15, 7.16, CN1]

**Step 4.** Hence B = d³. Indeed, if either B or d³ were greater, its A-fold sum would contain the other's A-fold sum as a proper part and would therefore be greater, contrary to their equality. Thus B is the product of three equal numbers d, and so is a cube. Therefore, if a cube number multiplying a number produces a cube, the multiplied number is also a cube. [D7.15, CN5, D7.19]
