## Proposition 9.30
**Statement:** If an odd number measure an even number, it will also measure the half of it.

**Step 1.** Let the odd number a measure the even number b. Thus b consists of some whole number q of copies of a. Since an odd number cannot equal an even number, more than one copy occurs, so q is a number. In the language of multiplication, q multiplied by a produces b. [D7.3, D7.15, D7.6, D7.7]

**Step 2.** We use the odd-product criterion, since it applies directly to this multiplier q. If q were odd, then q multiplied by the odd number a would produce an odd number, by 9.29. But its product b is even. Therefore q is not odd and hence is even. [9.29, D7.6, D7.7]

**Step 3.** Since q is even, divide its multitude of units into two equal parts. Accordingly, separate the q copies of a making up b into two groups with equally many copies. The sums of these groups are equal, because they are sums of equally many equal numbers; together they constitute b. Each group therefore sums to the half of b. [D7.6, D7.15, CN2]

**Step 4.** Each group consists of a whole number of copies of a, so a measures its sum (with one copy allowed). Consequently a measures the half of b. Therefore, if an odd number measures an even number, it also measures the half of it. [D7.3, D7.15]
