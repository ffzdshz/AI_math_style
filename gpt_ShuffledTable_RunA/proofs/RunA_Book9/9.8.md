## Proposition 9.8
**Statement:** If as many numbers as we please beginning from an unit be in continued proportion, the third from the unit will be square, as will also those which successively leave out one; the fourth will be cube, as will also all those which leave out two; and the seventh will be at once cube and square, as will also those which leave out five.

**Step 1.** Write the given progression as u, a₁, a₂, …, aₙ, where u is the unit; thus aⱼ occupies position j+1. All assertions below concern terms that exist. We choose the direct route of comparing equally spaced blocks and then applying the definitions of square and cube, since these comparisons supply the required equal factors. [D7.1; 7.14; D7.18; D7.19]

**Step 2.** Put a=a₁. Continued proportion gives u:a = aⱼ:aⱼ₊₁. Hence aⱼ measures aⱼ₊₁ as many times as the unit measures a. By VII.15, alternately, a measures aⱼ₊₁ as many times as the unit measures aⱼ. Consequently,
a:aⱼ₊₁ = u:aⱼ.
This holds for every j≥1 for which aⱼ₊₁ exists. [D7.20; 7.15]

**Step 3.** Let m≥1 and suppose a₂ₘ exists. Compare the two blocks of numbers
a₁, a₂, …, aₘ₊₁ and aₘ, aₘ₊₁, …, a₂ₘ.
Their corresponding successive ratios are equal. Therefore, ex aequali,
aₘ:a₂ₘ = a₁:aₘ₊₁ = u:aₘ,
where the last equality is Step 2. If a₃ₘ also exists, comparing the first block with a₂ₘ, a₂ₘ₊₁, …, a₃ₘ similarly gives
a₂ₘ:a₃ₘ = a₁:aₘ₊₁ = u:aₘ.
These applications involve blocks consisting entirely of numbers, not the unit. [7.14]

**Step 4.** From u:aₘ = aₘ:a₂ₘ, the number aₘ measures a₂ₘ as many times as the unit measures aₘ. Thus a₂ₘ is produced by multiplying aₘ by itself, and is square. Likewise, from u:aₘ = a₂ₘ:a₃ₘ, the number a₃ₘ is produced by multiplying that square by aₘ, and is therefore cube. As m ranges over the positive integers, the square terms occupy positions 3, 5, 7, …, successively leaving out one; the cube terms occupy positions 4, 7, 10, …, successively leaving out two. [D7.20; D7.15; D7.18; D7.19]

**Step 5.** For every positive integer k with a₆ₖ present, Step 4 with m=3k shows that a₆ₖ is square, while Step 4 with m=2k shows that the same term is cube. These terms occupy positions 7, 13, 19, …, successively leaving out five. Hence the seventh term is both square and cube, as is every term obtained thereafter by leaving out five. Together with Step 4, this proves every assertion. [D7.18; D7.19]
