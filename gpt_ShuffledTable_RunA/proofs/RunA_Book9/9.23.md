## Proposition 9.23
**Statement:** If as many odd numbers as we please be added together, and their multitude be odd, the whole will also be odd.

**Step 1.** If there is only one summand, the whole is that given odd number. Otherwise, set aside one odd summand, calling it A. Since the multitude of summands is odd, the multitude remaining after removing one is even. [D7.7]

**Step 2.** Let E be the sum of the remaining odd numbers. By IX.22, E is even. This is the direct route: setting aside one summand reduces the question to the already established even-multitude case. [9.22]

**Step 3.** Since A is odd, it differs by a unit from an even number B. The sum E + B is even, because it is a sum of even numbers. But the required whole E + A differs by that same unit from E + B; therefore it is odd. Thus the sum of any odd multitude of odd numbers is odd. [D7.7, 9.21]
