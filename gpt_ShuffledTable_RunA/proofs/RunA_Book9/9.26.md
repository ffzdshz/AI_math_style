## Proposition 9.26
**Statement:** If from an odd number an odd number be subtracted, the remainder will be even.

**Step 1.** Let A and B be odd numbers, with A greater than B, and let R be the remainder when B is subtracted from A. Thus A = B + R. By the definition of an odd number, write A = E + 1 and B = F + 1, where E and F are even. We choose this direct reduction because removing the common unit turns the given subtraction into an even-from-even subtraction. [D7.7]

**Step 2.** Substituting gives E + 1 = F + 1 + R. Subtracting the same unit from these equal quantities gives E = F + R. Hence R is also the remainder when F is subtracted from E. [CN3]

**Step 3.** Since E and F are even, the remainder when F is subtracted from E is even. Therefore R is even: if an odd number is subtracted from an odd number, the remainder is even. [9.24]
