## Proposition 9.3
**Statement:** If a cube number by multiplying itself make some number, the product will be cube.

**Step 1.** Let A be the given cube. By the definition of a cube, there is a number a such that, on putting b = a·a, we have A = a·b. [D7.19] We use the four-term cube criterion, since it allows the given cube A to be the first term of a continued proportion. [8.23]

**Step 2.** Multiplication of a and b by a produces b and A respectively. Therefore
 a:b = b:A. [7.17]

**Step 3.** Put D = b·b, E = b·A, and F = A·A. Commutativity gives b·a = A and A·b = E. [7.16] Multiplying a and b by b, multiplying b and A by b, and multiplying b and A by A, respectively, gives
 A:D = a:b,
 D:E = b:A,
 E:F = b:A. [7.17]
Together with Step 2, these equalities show that A:D = D:E = E:F. Thus A, D, E, F are four numbers in continued proportion.

**Step 4.** Since the first number A is a cube, the fourth number F is also a cube. [8.23] But F = A·A. Therefore a cube number multiplied by itself produces a cube, as required.
