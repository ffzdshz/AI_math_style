## Proposition 9.29
**Statement:** If an odd number by multiplying an odd number make some number, the product will be odd.

**Step 1.** Let A and B be odd numbers, and let A by multiplying B produce C. By the definition of multiplication, C is the sum of as many copies of B as there are units in A. We choose this direct repeated-addition route because it immediately expresses the product as a sum of odd numbers. [D7.15]

**Step 2.** Each summand B is odd, and their multitude A is odd. Therefore their sum C is odd, since a sum of odd numbers whose multitude is odd is itself odd. Thus an odd number multiplying an odd number produces an odd number. [9.23]
