## Proposition 9.21
**Statement:** If as many even numbers as we please be added together, the whole is even.

**Step 1.** Let A₁, …, Aₙ be any finite collection of even numbers. We take the direct route through the definition of even. Divide each Aᵢ into two equal parts Bᵢ and Cᵢ, so that Aᵢ = Bᵢ + Cᵢ and Bᵢ = Cᵢ. [D7.6]

**Step 2.** Add all the parts Bᵢ together, and separately add all the parts Cᵢ together. Since each Bᵢ equals its corresponding Cᵢ, repeated addition of equals to equals gives B₁ + ⋯ + Bₙ = C₁ + ⋯ + Cₙ. [CN2]

**Step 3.** The whole A₁ + ⋯ + Aₙ consists precisely of these two equal collections of parts. It is therefore divisible into two equal parts and hence is even. Thus, if as many even numbers as we please are added together, the whole is even. [D7.6]
