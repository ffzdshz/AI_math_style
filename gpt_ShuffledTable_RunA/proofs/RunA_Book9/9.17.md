## Proposition 9.17
**Statement:** If there be as many numbers as we please in continued proportion, and the extremes of them be prime to one another, the last will not be to any other number as the first to the second.

**Step 1.** Let A₁, A₂, …, Aₙ be numbers in continued proportion, with A₁ and Aₙ prime to one another. Suppose, contrary to the claim, that a number X satisfies Aₙ:X = A₁:A₂. Then A₁, A₂, …, Aₙ, X are in continued proportion. [D7.20]

**Step 2.** We compare the original progression with its one-place shift, since this brings the given coprime extremes directly into the argument. The two lists A₁, …, Aₙ and A₂, …, Aₙ, X have equal corresponding successive ratios. Therefore, ex aequali,
A₁:Aₙ = A₂:X. [7.14]

**Step 3.** Since A₁ and Aₙ are prime to one another, they are the least numbers having their ratio. Consequently, in the proportion just obtained, A₁ measures A₂ and Aₙ measures X the same number of times. In particular, write A₂ = kA₁, where k is a positive integral multiplicity. [7.21, 7.20]

**Step 4.** For every i < n, continued proportion gives A₁:A₂ = Aᵢ:Aᵢ₊₁. By the definition of proportional numbers, Aᵢ₊₁ = kAᵢ. Repeatedly substituting yields Aₙ = kⁿ⁻¹A₁. Thus A₁ measures Aₙ. [D7.20]

**Step 5.** Hence A₁ measures both extremes. But A₁ is a number, not a unit, whereas numbers prime to one another have only a unit as a common measure. This contradicts the hypothesis. Therefore the last number cannot be to any other number as the first is to the second. [D7.2, D7.12]
