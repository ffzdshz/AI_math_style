## Proposition 9.35
**Statement:** If as many numbers as we please be in continued proportion, and there be subtracted from the second and the last numbers equal to the first, then, as the excess of the second is to the first, so will the excess of the last be to all those before it.

**Step 1.** Let a₁, a₂, …, aₙ be the given numbers in continued proportion, and let d = a₂ − a₁ and e = aₙ − a₁ be the stated excesses. Put S = a₁ + ⋯ + aₙ₋₁ and T = a₂ + ⋯ + aₙ. We choose the numerical sum-of-antecedents theorem directly, since the adjacent pairs already have equal ratios. From a₁:a₂ = a₂:a₃ = ⋯ = aₙ₋₁:aₙ, it gives a₁:a₂ = S:T. [7.12]

**Step 2.** Alternating this proportion gives a₁:S = a₂:T. [7.13]

**Step 3.** The sums T and S contain the same intermediate terms a₂, …, aₙ₋₁. Cancelling these common terms gives T − S = aₙ − a₁ = e. (For two given numbers, this identity is immediate.) [CN3]

**Step 4.** In the proportion a₂:T = a₁:S, subtract a₁ from the whole a₂ and S from the whole T. Since the subtracted numbers have the same ratio as the wholes, the remainders have that ratio also. Thus (a₂ − a₁):(T − S) = a₁:S, or d:e = a₁:S. [7.11]

**Step 5.** Alternating once more gives d:a₁ = e:S. Therefore, as the excess of the second number over the first is to the first, so is the excess of the last over the first to the sum of all the numbers before the last, as required. [7.13]
