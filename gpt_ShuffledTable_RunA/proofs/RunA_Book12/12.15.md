## Proposition 12.15
**Statement:** In equal cones and cylinders the bases are reciprocally proportional to the heights; and those cones and cylinders in which the bases are reciprocally proportional to the heights are equal.

**Step 1.** Let S₁ and S₂ be two cones or two cylinders, with bases B₁, B₂ and heights h₁, h₂. Construct an auxiliary solid T of the same kind, with base equal to B₁ and height h₂. We choose this comparison because the equal-height and equal-base propositions apply directly, without cutting the given solids. [12.11, 12.14]

**Step 2.** Since T and S₂ have the same height, and T and S₁ have equal bases,
T : S₂ = B₁ : B₂,
and
T : S₁ = h₂ : h₁.
[12.11, 12.14]

**Step 3.** Suppose S₁ and S₂ are equal. The same magnitude T has the same ratio to these equal magnitudes, so T : S₂ = T : S₁. Combining this with Step 2 gives B₁ : B₂ = h₂ : h₁. Thus the bases are reciprocally proportional to the heights. [5.7, 5.11]

**Step 4.** Conversely, suppose B₁ : B₂ = h₂ : h₁. By Step 2 and equality of ratios, T : S₂ = T : S₁. Magnitudes to which the same magnitude has the same ratio are equal; hence S₂ = S₁. Therefore, for cones as well as for cylinders, equality of the solids is equivalent to reciprocal proportionality of their bases and heights. [5.11, 5.9]
