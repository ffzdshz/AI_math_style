## Proposition 12.13
**Statement:** If a cylinder be cut by a plane which is parallel to its opposite planes, then, as the cylinder is to the cylinder, so will the axis be to the axis.

**Step 1.** Let the parallel cutting plane meet the axis AC of the given cylinder at B. Denote the cylinder between A and B by U, and that between B and C by V; put a = AB and b = BC. In a plane through the axis, the traces of the cutting plane and the end planes are parallel (11.16). They therefore divide the generating rectangle into two rectangles of the same width (1.34). By the definitions of cylinder, axis, and bases, U and V are cylinders with axes a and b and with circular bases of the same radius (D11.21, D11.22, D11.23). These bases are equal, since they coincide when superposed (CN4).

**Step 2.** We use the equimultiple criterion for equality of ratios directly (D5.5). Let m and n be arbitrary positive integers. Using the same circular base and the same axial ray, form cylinders P and Q whose axial lengths are respectively ma and nb. Divide the generating rectangle of P into m successive rectangles of axial length a, and that of Q into n successive rectangles of axial length b. Rotation gives corresponding partitions of P and Q into cylinders, all having bases equal to the original base (D11.21, D11.23, CN4).

**Step 3.** Each of the m pieces of P has the same height and an equal base as U. By 12.11, its ratio to U is the ratio of their equal bases; taking onefold multiples in D5.5 shows that the piece equals U. Likewise, each of the n pieces of Q equals V. Hence, by addition of equals, P = mU and Q = nV (CN2). The equal-height theorem 12.11 is the direct choice here because precisely these equal-height comparisons are required.

**Step 4.** Compare P and Q in their common position, with the same base and axial ray. If ma > nb, the generating rectangle of Q is a proper part of that of P, so Q is a proper part of P (D11.21), and therefore mU > nV (CN5). If ma = nb, their generating rectangles and the cylinders they generate coincide, so mU = nV (D11.21, CN4). If ma < nb, the reverse containment gives mU < nV (D11.21, CN5).

**Step 5.** Thus, for every pair of positive integers m and n, the multiples mU and nV have exactly the same comparison as ma and nb. By D5.5, U : V = a : b = AB : BC. Therefore, as one of the two cylinders is to the other, so is its axis to the other's axis, as required.
