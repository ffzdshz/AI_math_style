## Proposition 12.8
**Statement:** Similar pyramids which have triangular bases are in the triplicate ratio of their corresponding sides.

**Step 1.** Let the similar triangular pyramids be P = ABCD and P′ = A′B′C′D′, with vertices named in corresponding order. Their corresponding triangular faces are similar; hence the corresponding angles between AB, AC, AD and A′B′, A′C′, A′D′ are equal, and
AB : A′B′ = AC : A′C′ = AD : A′D′.
We use completion to parallelepipeds: XII.7 will give the same fixed multiple of each pyramid, and XI.33 then supplies the required triplicate ratio directly. [D11.9, D6.1]

**Step 2.** By drawing parallels, complete the parallelepiped Q having AB, AC, AD as its three edges at A. Name its base parallelogram ABEC and its opposite face DFHG, so that AD, BF, EH, CG are parallel edges. Likewise construct Q′ from A′B′, A′C′, A′D′, using corresponding primed names. The opposite faces of these solids are equal and parallelogrammic. [1.31, 11.24]

**Step 3.** The three faces of Q meeting at A are similar respectively to the three faces of Q′ meeting at A′. Indeed, their corresponding adjacent sides are proportional by Step 1, and their included angles are equal. Their remaining angles are equal because adjacent angles of a parallelogram sum to two right angles and opposite angles are equal; their remaining sides are proportional because opposite sides are equal. Thus these corresponding parallelograms satisfy the definition of similar rectilineal figures. The opposite faces correspond similarly, since they have sides parallel and equal to those of the respective faces already considered. Consequently Q and Q′ are contained by six corresponding similar faces and are similar solids. [1.29, 1.34, 11.24, D6.1, D11.9]

**Step 4.** Cut Q by the plane BCFG, which passes through the diagonals BC and FG of its opposite faces ABEC and DFHG. This bisects Q, and one half is the triangular prism T with opposite bases ABC and DFG. The corresponding cut of Q′ gives the triangular prism T′ with bases A′B′C′ and D′F′G′. Therefore Q is twice T, and Q′ is twice T′. [11.28]

**Step 5.** Apply the three-pyramid division of XII.7 to T, drawing the dividing planes BCD and CDF. Its three equal triangular pyramids are ABCD, BCDF, and CDFG. The first is precisely P; hence T is three times P. The corresponding division of T′ shows that T′ is three times P′. Together with Step 4, this gives Q as six times P and Q′ as six times P′. [12.7]

**Step 6.** Parts have the same ratio as their corresponding equimultiples, so
P : P′ = Q : Q′.
But Q and Q′ are similar parallelepipedal solids, and therefore Q : Q′ is the triplicate ratio of AB : A′B′. The equality of ratios transfers this conclusion to P : P′. Since AB and A′B′ are corresponding sides of the given pyramids, and all corresponding side-pairs have the same ratio by their similar faces, the pyramids are in the triplicate ratio of their corresponding sides. [5.15, 11.33, 5.11, D6.1]

Therefore similar pyramids which have triangular bases are in the triplicate ratio of their corresponding sides.
