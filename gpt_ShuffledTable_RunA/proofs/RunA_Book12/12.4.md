## Proposition 12.4
**Statement:** If there be two pyramids of the same height which have triangular bases, and cach of them be divided into two pyramids equal to one another and similar to the whole, and into two equal prisms, then, as the base of the one pyramid is to the base of the other pyramid, so will all the prisms in the one pyramid be to all the prisms, being equal in multitude, in the other pyramid.

**Step 1.** Let the pyramids be DABC and D′A′B′C′, with triangular bases ABC and A′B′C′ and the same height h. Write [XYZ] for the area of a triangle. Make the midpoint division of XII.3 in each pyramid. In the first, let E, F, G be the midpoints of AB, BC, CA, and H, K, L the midpoints of DA, DB, DC; use corresponding primed letters in the second. The two smaller pyramids are DHKL and HAEG. One of the prisms, P, has opposite triangular faces EBF and HKL; call the other prism Q. Similarly define P′ and Q′. The joining lines are parallel to the corresponding edges, and XII.3 gives P = Q and P′ = Q′. [6.2, 12.3]

**Step 2.** Since HK and HL are respectively parallel to AB and AC, the plane HKL is parallel to the plane ABC. Because H bisects DA, the parallel planes through D, through HKL, and through ABC also bisect a perpendicular measuring the height. Thus the distance between HKL and ABC is h/2, and the distance from D to HKL is also h/2. The same holds in the primed pyramid. Consequently P and P′ have the same height h/2; moreover, all four smaller pyramids have height h/2 relative to their indicated triangular bases. [6.2, 11.15, 11.17]

**Step 3.** Triangles ABC and EBC have the same altitude to AB, while AB is double EB. Triangles EBC and EBF have the same altitude to BC, while BC is double BF. Hence
[ABC] = 2[EBC] = 4[EBF].
Likewise [A′B′C′] = 4[E′B′F′]. Therefore
[EBF] : [E′B′F′] = [ABC] : [A′B′C′].
The same two applications of the triangle-area theorem give [AEG] = [ABC]/4 and [A′E′G′] = [A′B′C′]/4. Also, the opposite triangular faces of a prism are equal, so [HKL] = [EBF] and [H′K′L′] = [E′B′F′]. Thus every smaller pyramid has a base equal to one quarter of its parent's base. [6.1, 5.15, D11.13]

**Step 4.** We take the direct route through parallelepipeds: diagonal bisection is the natural first bridge from these triangular prisms to the equal-height theorem for parallelepipeds. Complete P and P′ to parallelepipeds U and U′, respectively, so that each prism is cut off by the plane through diagonals of opposite faces. Their parallelogram bases are double EBF and E′B′F′, and U = 2P, U′ = 2P′. Their heights remain h/2. Therefore
U : U′ = (2[EBF]) : (2[E′B′F′]).
Removing the same multiples from both ratios and using Step 3 gives
P : P′ = U : U′ = [EBF] : [E′B′F′] = [ABC] : [A′B′C′].
This uses only the equal-height theorem for parallelepipeds, not a volume theorem for pyramids. [1.34, 11.28, 11.32, 5.15, 5.11]

**Step 5.** Since Q = P and Q′ = P′, the ratio Q : Q′ is also [ABC] : [A′B′C′]. Adding the corresponding antecedents and consequents therefore yields
(P + Q) : (P′ + Q′) = [ABC] : [A′B′C′].
This proves the assertion for the two prisms obtained in the first division. [12.3, 5.12]

**Step 6.** If the division is continued, pair DHKL with D′H′K′L′ and HAEG with H′A′E′G′. By Steps 2 and 3, each pair consists of pyramids of equal height whose bases have the original ratio [ABC] : [A′B′C′]. Apply the same division and the argument of Steps 1–5 to each pair. The newly obtained corresponding prisms therefore have that same ratio. At every subsequent division, corresponding heights are again halved and corresponding base areas quartered, so the argument continues for any finite number of corresponding subdivisions. [12.3, 5.15; Steps 2–5]

**Step 7.** Denote all the prisms so obtained, equal in multitude, by R₁, …, Rₙ in the first pyramid and R′₁, …, R′ₙ in the second, pairing corresponding prisms. For every i,
Rᵢ : R′ᵢ = [ABC] : [A′B′C′].
Hence the theorem on sums of proportional magnitudes gives
[ABC] : [A′B′C′] = (R₁ + ··· + Rₙ) : (R′₁ + ··· + R′ₙ).
Thus, as the base of one pyramid is to the base of the other, so are all the prisms in the first to all the prisms, equal in multitude, in the second. [5.12]
