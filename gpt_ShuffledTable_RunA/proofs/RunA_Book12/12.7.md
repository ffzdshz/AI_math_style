## Proposition 12.7
**Statement:** Any prism which has a triangular base is divided into three pyramids equal to one another which have triangular bases.

**Step 1.** Let the given prism have opposite triangular bases ABC and DEF, with corresponding vertices A,D; B,E; and C,F. Its lateral faces ABED, BCFE, and CADF are parallelograms. [D11.13]

**Step 2.** Draw the face diagonals AF, BF, and AE, and cut the prism by the planes ABF and AEF. These planes exist because every triangle lies in a plane. The section ABF separates the triangular pyramid ABCF from the pyramid with vertex F and parallelogram base ABED. The section AEF then divides this remaining pyramid along the diagonal AE of its base into the triangular pyramids ABEF and ADEF. Thus the three pyramids
T₁ = ABCF, T₂ = ABEF, and T₃ = ADEF
fill the prism and have disjoint interiors. [P1, 11.2, D11.12]

**Step 3.** The diagonal BF bisects the parallelogram BCFE, so the triangles BCF and BEF are equal. Regard T₁ and T₂ as pyramids with these respective bases and common vertex A. Their bases lie in the same plane, so their heights are equal. Use the triangular-base proportionality theorem directly, since the comparison bases are triangles: T₁:T₂ = BCF:BEF. Since the bases are equal, the definition of equal ratios, taking all the multiples to be one, gives T₁ = T₂. [1.34, 12.5, D5.5]

**Step 4.** Likewise, the diagonal AE bisects the parallelogram ABED, so the triangles ABE and ADE are equal. Regard T₂ and T₃ as pyramids with these respective bases and common vertex F. Again their bases lie in one plane and their heights are equal. Hence T₂:T₃ = ABE:ADE, and therefore T₂ = T₃. [1.34, 12.5, D5.5]

**Step 5.** Consequently T₁, T₂, and T₃ are equal to one another. By the division in Step 2, they constitute the entire given prism, and each has a triangular base. Therefore any prism with a triangular base is divided into three equal pyramids having triangular bases. [CN1, D11.12]
