## Proposition 12.9
**Statement:** In equal pyramids which have triangular bases the bases are reciprocally proportional to the heights; and those pyramids in which the bases are reciprocally proportional to the heights are equal.

**Step 1.** Let the two triangular pyramids be P = DABC and Q = D′A′B′C′. Write b and c for their respective base areas, and h and k for their respective heights. We use parallelepiped completion, since XI.34 already supplies both directions of the required reciprocal-proportion statement for parallelepipeds.

By drawing parallels, complete a parallelepiped K on the three edges AB, AC, AD, and similarly complete L on A′B′, A′C′, A′D′. Their heights are h and k. Let their base areas be B and C. Each triangular base is half its completed parallelogram, so B = 2b and C = 2c. [1.31, 1.34; route: 11.34]

**Step 2.** Cut K through the diagonals of its base and opposite face. By XI.28 this produces two equal triangular prisms. Take the prism whose lower face is ABC and whose upper face is DEF, with AD, BE, CF as its lateral edges. The trisection of XII.7 consists of the pyramids ABCD, BCDE, CDEF, which are equal. Its first pyramid is precisely P. Thus this prism is three times P, and K is six times P. The identical construction for Q gives L six times Q. Hence
K = 6P and L = 6Q. [11.28, 12.7]

**Step 3.** Parts have the same ratio as their corresponding equimultiples. Consequently,
B:C = 2b:2c = b:c,
and
K:L = 6P:6Q = P:Q. [5.15]

**Step 4.** First suppose that P and Q are equal. Their sixfold multiples K and L are equal, by repeated addition of equals. XI.34 therefore gives
B:C = k:h.
Together with Step 3, this yields
b:c = k:h.
Thus the bases of the equal pyramids are reciprocally proportional to their heights. [CN2, 11.34, 5.11]

**Step 5.** Conversely, suppose that the bases are reciprocally proportional to the heights, so that
b:c = k:h.
Step 3 then gives B:C = k:h. By the converse part of XI.34, K and L are equal; hence 6P = 6Q. More explicitly, equality of these multiples and V.15 give
P:Q = 6P:6Q = 6Q:6Q = Q:Q.
Since P and Q thus have the same ratio to Q, they are equal by V.9. Therefore pyramids with triangular bases are equal whenever their bases are reciprocally proportional to their heights. Both assertions are proved. [5.11, 11.34, 5.7, 5.15, 5.9]
