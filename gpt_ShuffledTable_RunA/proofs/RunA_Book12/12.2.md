## Proposition 12.2
**Statement:** Circles are to one another as the squares on the diameters.

**Step 1.** Let C and D denote the areas of the two circles, and S and T the squares on their respective diameters. We use the directly available polygon ratio in XII.1, exhaust the circles by X.1, and verify the equimultiple criterion D5.5. This route requires no auxiliary fourth proportional involving a circle. [12.1, 10.1, D5.5]

**Step 2.** Inscribe a square in each circle. Its consecutive sides cut off equal minor arcs. Repeatedly bisect every arc between consecutive vertices and join consecutive division points. Denote the resulting polygonal areas at matching stages by P_j and Q_j. [4.6, 3.28, 3.30]

At each stage, the polygons have the same number of equal arcs. Equal arcs give equal central angles and equal chords; hence the central angles in the two circles are equal subdivisions of four right angles. The triangles formed by the centre and consecutive vertices are isosceles, so their corresponding base angles, and consequently the corresponding polygon angles, are equal. Within either polygon all sides are equal, so corresponding sides of the two polygons have one ratio. Thus the polygons are similar. They lie inside their respective circles, and XII.1 gives
P_j : Q_j = S : T.
[3.27, 3.29, D1.15, 1.5, 1.32, D6.1, 3.2, 12.1]

**Step 3.** Consider one circular segment remaining outside either polygon. Let AB be its chord, M the midpoint of its minor arc, and O the centre. The chords AM and BM are equal, as are the radii OA and OB. Applying I.8 to triangles OAM and OBM shows that OM bisects angle AMB. Since triangle AMB is isosceles, I.5 and I.32 show that the two adjacent angles where OM meets AB are equal; thus OM is perpendicular to AB. [3.29, D1.15, 1.8, 1.5, 1.32, D1.10]

Through M draw the parallel to AB, and through A and B draw parallels to OM. These lines and AB bound a rectangle R. The line through M is perpendicular to the radius OM and therefore lies outside the circle. Since the arc AB is no greater than a quadrant, its circular segment is properly contained in R. But R is double triangle AMB, having the same base and lying between the same parallels. Consequently, triangle AMB is greater than half the circular segment. [1.31, 3.16, CN5, 1.41]

**Step 4.** Passing from one polygonal stage to the next adds precisely these triangles in all the remaining segments. Hence more than half the total remainder is removed at every stage. Thus each of C − P_j and D − Q_j can be made less than any prescribed positive area, by X.1. The same holds for any fixed positive integral multiple of either remainder, since multiplying preserves the more-than-half removal. [CN2, CN3, 10.1]

**Step 5.** Let m and n be arbitrary positive integers. Suppose first that mC > nD, and put g = mC − nD. By Step 4 choose a matching stage with
m(C − P_j) < g.
Subtracting gives mP_j > nD. Since Q_j is properly inside D, we obtain
mP_j > nD > nQ_j.
The equality P_j : Q_j = S : T and D5.5 therefore imply
mS > nT.
[CN2, CN3, CN5, D5.5]

**Step 6.** Suppose instead that mC < nD. Choose a matching stage with
n(D − Q_j) < nD − mC.
Then nQ_j > mC > mP_j. Applying D5.5 to P_j : Q_j = S : T gives
mS < nT.
Thus both strict comparisons between multiples of the circles imply the corresponding strict comparisons between multiples of the diameter-squares. [CN2, CN3, CN5, D5.5]

**Step 7.** Finally suppose that mC = nD. If mS > nT, put δ = mS − nT. By the magnitude condition D5.4, choose a positive integer r such that rδ > T. Then
(rm)S > (rn + 1)T.
But the assumed equality of the circle-multiples gives
(rm)C = (rn)D < (rn + 1)D.
Step 6, applied to the integers rm and rn + 1, gives the contradictory inequality
(rm)S < (rn + 1)T.
Thus mS > nT is impossible. Exchanging the two circles and their corresponding squares excludes mS < nT in exactly the same way. Therefore mS = nT. [D5.4, CN2, CN3, CN5]

**Step 8.** For every pair of positive integers m and n, the multiples mC and nD respectively exceed, equal, or fall short of one another exactly as mS and nT do. By D5.5,
C : D = S : T.
Therefore circles are to one another as the squares on their diameters. [D5.5]
