## Proposition 12.16
**Statement:** Given two circles about the same centre, to inscribe in the greater circle an equilateral polygon with an even number of sides which does not touch the lesser circle.

**Step 1.** Let O be the common centre, and let r and R be the radii of the lesser and greater circles, respectively, so r<R. Choose B on the lesser circle. Through B draw a perpendicular to OB, and extend it to meet the greater circle at C and D. Thus the chord CD has perpendicular distance OB=r from O. Put φ=∠COD, taking the interior angle of triangle COD. We shall construct equal central angles smaller than φ and compare their chords directly. [D1.15, P1, P2, 1.11]

**Step 2.** Draw two perpendicular diameters of the greater circle. They divide the angles about O into four equal right angles. Bisect every sector angle, and then bisect each resulting angle again. Repeat these paired bisections simultaneously in all sectors. After k rounds there are 4·4^k equal sectors. [P2, 1.11, P4, 1.9]

**Step 3.** A paired bisection replaces each sector angle by one quarter of itself: the amount removed is three quarters, greater than its half. Hence X.1 guarantees a finite stage at which the common sector angle θ is less than φ. If the initial angle is already smaller, no refinement is needed; if equality occurs, one further round makes it smaller. Paired bisection is chosen so that X.1 applies in its stated, strictly-more-than-half form. [1.9, 10.1]

**Step 4.** Let V₁,…,Vₙ be the successive points where the resulting rays meet the greater circle, with n=4·4^k, and join consecutive points, including Vₙ to V₁. Every triangle OVᵢVᵢ₊₁ has two sides equal to R and included angle θ. Therefore I.4 makes all their bases equal. This uses the directly available triangle-congruence proposition, without requiring an arc comparison. The resulting polygon is equilateral, has an even number of sides, and is inscribed in the greater circle because all its vertices lie on that circumference. [P1, D1.15, 1.4, D4.3]

**Step 5.** For any side VᵢVᵢ₊₁, compare triangles COD and OVᵢVᵢ₊₁. Their two radial sides are respectively equal, while φ>θ. Consequently CD>VᵢVᵢ₊₁ by I.24. [D1.15, 1.24]

**Step 6.** Draw OH perpendicular to the straight line containing this arbitrary side. If OH<OB, that side would be nearer the centre than CD and therefore greater than CD by III.15, contrary to Step 5. If OH=OB, the two chords would be equally distant from the centre and therefore equal by III.14, again contrary to Step 5. Thus OH>OB=r. [1.12, D3.4, D3.5, 3.15, 3.14]

**Step 7.** Let X be any point of this side. If X=H, then OX=OH>r. Otherwise triangle OHX is right-angled at H; by I.17 its other angles are less than that right angle, and by I.19 its hypotenuse OX is greater than OH. Thus in every case OX>r. Since every point of the lesser circumference is at distance r from O, no side meets that circumference or enters the lesser circle. Therefore the constructed polygon is an equilateral polygon with an even number of sides, inscribed in the greater circle and not touching the lesser circle, as required. [1.17, 1.19, D1.15]
