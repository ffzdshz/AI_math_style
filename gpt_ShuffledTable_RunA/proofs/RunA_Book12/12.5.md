## Proposition 12.5
**Statement:** Pyramids which are of the same height and have triangular bases are to one another as the bases.

**Step 1.** Let P and Q be pyramids of the same height with triangular bases A and B. We use the immediately preceding prism-proportionality result, 12.4, together with exhaustion; this route does not assume the existence of a fourth proportional solid. Apply 12.3 to both pyramids, and repeatedly apply the same subdivision to every remaining pyramid. At stage j, write S_j and T_j for the respective sums of all prisms removed, and R_j and U_j for the respective sums of the remaining pyramids. Thus P = S_j + R_j and Q = T_j + U_j. Each refinement removes more than half of every remaining pyramid, and hence more than half of their sum. [12.3, CN2]

**Step 2.** Carry out matching stages of subdivision in the two pyramids. The prism collections then have equal numbers of corresponding pieces, so 12.4 gives
A : B = S_j : T_j
at every stage. Consequently, for any positive integers m and n, the comparison of mA with nB is the same as that of mS_j with nT_j. [12.4, D5.5]

**Step 3.** For any fixed positive integer m and any positive solid magnitude E, refinement eventually gives mR_j < E. Indeed, at every refinement more than half of the current remainder is removed; the same is true of its m-fold multiple. Proposition 10.1 therefore applies. Likewise, any fixed multiple of U_j can be made smaller than any prescribed positive solid magnitude. [12.3, 10.1, CN2]

**Step 4.** Let m and n be arbitrary positive integers. Suppose first that mP > nQ, and put E = mP − nQ. Choose a stage with mR_j < E. Then
mS_j = mP − mR_j > nQ > nT_j,
where the last inequality holds because T_j is a proper part of Q. Step 2 therefore gives mA > nB.

Similarly, if mP < nQ, choose a stage with nU_j < nQ − mP. Then
nT_j = nQ − nU_j > mP > mS_j,
so Step 2 gives mA < nB. Thus either strict comparison between arbitrary multiples of the pyramids implies the corresponding strict comparison between the same multiples of their bases. [10.1, D5.5, CN2, CN5]

**Step 5.** Suppose now that mP = nQ. We show that mA = nB. If instead mA > nB, let D = mA − nB. By the Archimedean condition for magnitudes having a ratio, choose a positive integer k such that kD > B. Adding knB gives
kmA > (kn + 1)B.
But mP = nQ gives
kmP = knQ < (kn + 1)Q.
Applying Step 4 with multipliers km and kn + 1 yields kmA < (kn + 1)B, a contradiction. If mA < nB, the identical argument with P and Q, A and B, and m and n interchanged gives a contradiction. Therefore mA = nB. [D5.4, CN2, CN5; Step 4]

**Step 6.** For every pair of positive integers m and n, the multiples mP and nQ therefore exceed, equal, or fall short of one another exactly as mA and nB do. By the definition of equal ratios,
P : Q = A : B.
Hence pyramids of the same height having triangular bases are to one another as their bases. [D5.5]
