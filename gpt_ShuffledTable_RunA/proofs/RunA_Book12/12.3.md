## Proposition 12.3
**Statement:** Any pyramid which has a triangular base is divided into two pyramids equal and similar to one another, similar to the whole and having triangular bases, and into two equal prisms; and the two prisms are greater than the half of the whole pyramid.

**Step 1.** Let ABCD be the given pyramid, with triangular base ABC and vertex D. Bisect AB, AC, AD, BC, BD, CD at E, F, G, H, K, L respectively, and join the requisite points. A segment joining the midpoints of two sides of any face is parallel to the third side and equal to its half: the parallelism follows from VI.2, and the equality from I.29 and VI.4. [1.10, P1, 6.2, 1.29, 6.4]

**Step 2.** Consider the four corner pyramids, writing their vertices in correspondence with A, B, C, D:
T_A = AEFG, T_B = EBHK, T_C = FHCL, and T_D = GKLD.
By Step 1, every edge of each is half the corresponding edge of ABCD. Consequently, their corresponding triangular faces are similar to those of ABCD, by VI.5 and the definition of similar rectilineal figures. Moreover, corresponding faces of the four corner pyramids have equal corresponding sides and equal included angles, and hence are equal by I.4. Thus the four corner pyramids are equal and similar to one another, and each is similar to ABCD. In particular, T_A and T_D have the equality and similarity required in the proposition. [6.5, D6.1, 1.4, D11.9, D11.10]

**Step 3.** We have FH parallel to GK, both being parallel to AB, and FG parallel to HK, both being parallel to CD. Thus FHKG is a planar parallelogram. Also, EFG is parallel to BHK, and FHC is parallel to GKL, since in each pair two intersecting sides are respectively parallel. [11.9, 11.15]

Cut the pyramid by the planes EFG and GKL, removing T_A and T_D, and cut the remaining solid by the plane FHKG. The resulting two solids are:
• P, with triangular ends EFG and BHK and side faces EBHF, EBKG, and FHKG;
• Q, with triangular ends FHC and GKL and side faces FHKG, HCLK, and CFGL.
Their triangular ends are equal and similar by Step 2, and parallel as just shown; their other faces are parallelograms by the midpoint relations. Therefore P and Q are prisms. These cuts partition ABCD, without overlapping interiors, into exactly T_A, T_D, P, and Q. [D11.12, D11.13]

**Step 4.** Regard the parallelogram EBHF as the base of P, and the triangle FHC as the base of Q. Both bases lie in the plane ABC. The plane GKL is parallel to ABC, because GK is parallel to AB and GL to AC. The opposite edge GK of P and the opposite triangular face GKL of Q lie in that same parallel plane. Hence the two prisms, with these choices of base, have the same height. [11.15]

**Step 5.** The diagonal BF bisects the parallelogram EBHF, so that this parallelogram is double the triangle BHF. The triangles BHF and CHF have equal bases BH and HC and lie between the same parallels BC and EF; therefore they are equal. Consequently, the parallelogram EBHF is double the triangle FHC. [1.34, 1.38, CN2]

**Step 6.** Use XI.39 directly: this is the natural comparison here because the prisms have equal heights, one chosen base is a parallelogram, and the other is a triangle. By Steps 4–5, the parallelogram base is double the triangular base. Therefore P and Q are equal. [11.39]

**Step 7.** The comparison pyramid T_B = EBHK is a proper part of P: its base is the triangular end BHK and its vertex E is one vertex of the opposite triangular end EFG. Thus P is greater than T_B. Since T_B equals T_A, P is greater than T_A. Since Q equals P and T_D equals T_A, Q is likewise greater than T_D. Hence the two prisms together are greater than the two remaining pyramids together. [CN5, CN2]

But the whole pyramid is precisely the sum of these two prisms and these two pyramids. Therefore the two prisms together are greater than half the whole pyramid. We have thus divided ABCD into two equal and similar triangular-based pyramids, each similar to ABCD, and two equal prisms whose sum is greater than half of ABCD, as required.
