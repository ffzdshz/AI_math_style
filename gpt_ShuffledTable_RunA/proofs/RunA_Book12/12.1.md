## Proposition 12.1
**Statement:** Similar polygons inscribed in circles are to one another as the squares on the diameters.

**Step 1.** Let P and Q be similar polygons inscribed in two circles, and let A, B, C and A′, B′, C′ be corresponding triples of consecutive vertices. By the definition of similar figures, ∠ABC = ∠A′B′C′ and AB:BC = A′B′:B′C′. Hence triangles ABC and A′B′C′ are equiangular. We will compare a corresponding boundary side with a diameter, then apply the duplicate-ratio conclusion for similar polygons directly. [D6.1, 6.6]

**Step 2.** At least one of the angles at A and C is acute, since the three angles of triangle ABC equal two right angles. Reversing the names A and C, and likewise A′ and C′, if necessary, we may suppose that ∠ACB is acute. Its corresponding angle ∠A′C′B′ is equal and therefore also acute. The lines AB and A′B′ remain corresponding sides of the polygons. This choice avoids a supplementary-angle case in the circle comparison. [1.32, 6.6]

**Step 3.** Draw diameters AD and A′D′. Since ∠ACB is acute, AB is not a diameter, and C lies on the greater segment cut off by AB. Likewise C′ lies on the greater segment cut off by A′B′. The angles ABD and A′B′D′ are right, being angles in semicircles; consequently the angles ADB and A′D′B′ are acute. Thus D lies on the same greater segment as C, and D′ on the same greater segment as C′. [D1.17, 3.31, 1.32]

**Step 4.** Angles in the same segment are equal, so ∠ADB = ∠ACB and ∠A′D′B′ = ∠A′C′B′. Therefore ∠ADB = ∠A′D′B′. Together with the right angles at B and B′, this makes triangles ABD and A′B′D′ equiangular. Their corresponding sides are proportional, giving AB:AD = A′B′:A′D′; alternately,
AB:A′B′ = AD:A′D′. [3.21, 1.32, 6.4, 5.16]

**Step 5.** Since P and Q are similar polygons and AB and A′B′ are corresponding sides, P:Q is the duplicate ratio of AB:A′B′. Here we use the duplicate-ratio clause directly, without summing triangular pieces. [6.20]

**Step 6.** Construct squares S and S′ on AD and A′D′. Squares are equilateral and right-angled, hence are similar figures. Applying the same polygon theorem to these squares, S:S′ is the duplicate ratio of AD:A′D′. [1.46, D1.22, D6.1, 6.20]

**Step 7.** By Step 4 the two underlying ratios are equal, so their duplicate ratios are equal: a duplicate ratio is the ratio of the extremes of three proportional magnitudes, and equality of the two successive ratios gives equality of the extreme ratios ex aequali. Thus Steps 5 and 6 yield P:Q = S:S′. Therefore similar polygons inscribed in circles are to one another as the squares on the diameters. [D5.9, 5.22, 5.11]
