## Proposition 12.14
**Statement:** Cones and cylinders which are on equal bases are to one another as their heights.

**Step 1.** Let C and D be cylinders on equal bases B and B′, with heights h and k. Their axes have lengths h and k by the rotational definition of a cylinder and its axis [D11.21, D11.22]. We first compare cylinders by a parallel cut, which directly handles unequal heights without requiring similarity; afterward we pass to cones by their relation to cylinders.

**Step 2.** If h = k, then C:D = B:B′ by the equal-height theorem [12.11]. Since B = B′, the definition of equal ratios, applied with both multiples equal to one, gives C = D. Ratios of equal magnitudes are the same, so C:D = h:k [D5.5].

**Step 3.** Suppose the heights are unequal, and label the cylinders so that h > k. Cut off from the axis of C a segment of length k [1.3], and cut C there by a plane parallel to its bases. Let E be the cylindrical part of height k and F the remaining cylindrical part, of height h − k. The rotational definition shows that these parts are cylinders with the same base as C, and their axes have the stated lengths [D11.21, D11.22]. Thus C = F + E.

**Step 4.** By the theorem on a cylinder cut parallel to its bases,
F:E = (h − k):k [12.13].
Consequently, by composition,
(F + E):E = ((h − k) + k):k,
and therefore C:E = h:k [5.18].

**Step 5.** The cylinders E and D have equal heights and equal bases. Hence E:D = B:B′ [12.11], and therefore E = D by the definition of equal ratios [D5.5]. Replacing E by its equal D gives C:D = C:E [5.7]; thus C:D = h:k [5.11]. Reversing both ratios preserves their equality by the same definition [D5.5], so the result also holds with the opposite labeling. Together with Step 2, this proves the assertion for all cylinders on equal bases.

**Step 6.** Now let P and Q be cones on equal bases, with heights h and k. Take cylinders C and D respectively on those same bases and with those same heights. Each cone is one-third of its corresponding cylinder [12.10]. Parts have the same ratio as their corresponding equal multiples, so P:Q = C:D [5.15]. The cylinder result gives C:D = h:k; hence P:Q = h:k [5.11]. Therefore cones on equal bases, and likewise cylinders on equal bases, are to one another as their heights.
