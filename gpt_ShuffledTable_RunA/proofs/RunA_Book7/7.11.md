## Proposition 7.11
**Statement:** If, as whole is to whole, so is a number subtracted to a number subtracted, the remainder will also be to the remainder as whole to whole.

**Step 1.** Let A and B be the whole numbers, and let C and D be the numbers subtracted from them, respectively, with A:B = C:D. Put R = A−C and S = B−D, the positive remainders. We work directly with the numerical definition of proportion: the first number is the same multiple, the same part, or the same parts of the second as the third is of the fourth. This makes the earlier numerical subtraction propositions the direct route. [D7.20]

**Step 2.** If A = B, the given proportion gives C = D. Subtracting equals from equals gives R = S. Thus R:S = A:B, both being the ratio of equal numbers. Henceforth suppose A ≠ B. [D7.20, CN3]

**Step 3.** Suppose A is the same part of B that C is of D. Apply 7.7 to these wholes and the numbers subtracted: R is the same part of S that A is of B. Consequently R:S = A:B. We choose 7.7 here because it is the first subtraction proposition and applies directly to the single-part case. [7.7, D7.20]

**Step 4.** Suppose A is the same parts of B that C is of D. Proposition 7.8 gives that R is the same parts of S that A is of B. Therefore R:S = A:B. This is the corresponding direct subtraction result for the parts case. [7.8, D7.20]

**Step 5.** Finally, suppose A is the same multiple of B that C is of D. By the definitions of part and multiple, B is the same part of A that D is of C. Apply 7.7 with B and A as the wholes and D and C as the respective subtractions: S is the same part of R that B is of A. Hence R is the same multiple of S that A is of B, so again R:S = A:B. These cases exhaust the numerical definition of the given proportion. Thus the remainder is to the remainder as whole to whole. [D7.3, D7.5, 7.7, D7.20]
