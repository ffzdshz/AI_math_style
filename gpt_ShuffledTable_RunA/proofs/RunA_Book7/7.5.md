## Proposition 7.5
**Statement:** If a number be a part of a number, and another be the same part of another, the sum will also be the same part of the sum that the one is of the one.

**Step 1.** Let A be a part of B, and let C be the same part of D. We use equal-group decomposition directly, since the hypothesis already supplies the same measuring count. Thus B consists of k copies of A, and D consists of k copies of C, for the same count k, with k at least two. [D7.3]

**Step 2.** Pair each copy of A in B with one corresponding copy of C in D. Each resulting group equals A + C, since equals added to equals give equal wholes. There are exactly k such groups. [CN2]

**Step 3.** These groups contain precisely all the units of B and D, each once; hence together they constitute B + D. Consequently A + C measures B + D exactly k times. Since there are at least two groups, A + C is less than B + D, and therefore is a part of it. Its measuring count is the same as that of A in B, so A + C is the same part of B + D that A is of B, as required. [D7.2, CN5, D7.3]
