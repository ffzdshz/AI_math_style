## Proposition 7.24
**Statement:** If two numbers be prime to any number, their product also will be prime to the same.

**Step 1.** Let A and B each be prime to C, and let P be the number produced when A multiplies B. Thus P consists of A copies of B, so B measures P. We must show that P and C have only the unit as a common measure. [D7.15, D7.12]

**Step 2.** Suppose instead that a number D greater than the unit measures both P and C. Since C and B are prime to one another and D measures C, D and B are prime to one another. [D7.12, 7.23]

**Step 3.** D cannot equal P: otherwise B would measure D as well as itself, contradicting the relative primality of D and B, since B is a number, not the unit. Hence D is less than P. Because D measures P, P is a multiple of D; therefore there is a number E such that E multiplying D produces P. [D7.2, D7.12, D7.5, D7.15]

**Step 4.** The product of E and D equals the product of A and B, both being P. The equal-products direction of 7.19 therefore gives
E : A = B : D. [7.19]

**Step 5.** We use the least-terms route, since the relative primality already established supplies its hypothesis: B and D are the least numbers having their ratio. These least numbers measure the corresponding terms of any equal ratio. Applying this to B : D = E : A, we obtain that D measures A. [7.21, 7.20]

**Step 6.** Thus D, which is greater than the unit, measures both A and C. This contradicts the hypothesis that A and C are prime to one another. Consequently P and C have no common measure except the unit, and the product of A and B is prime to C, as required. [D7.12]
