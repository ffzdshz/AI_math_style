## Proposition 7.14
**Statement:** If there be as many numbers as we please, and others equal to them in multitude, which taken two and two are in the same ratio, they will also be in the same ratio ex aequali.

**Step 1.** Let A₁, …, Aₙ and B₁, …, Bₙ be the two lists of numbers, equal in multitude, with
Aᵢ : Aᵢ₊₁ = Bᵢ : Bᵢ₊₁
for each i = 1, …, n−1. To establish their proportionality ex aequali, we must show A₁ : Aₙ = B₁ : Bₙ, taking the extreme terms and removing the intermediate terms. [D5.17]

**Step 2.** Choose the numerical alternation theorem, since it turns the given proportions directly into a chain with matching intermediate ratios. Applying it to each given proportion gives
Aᵢ : Bᵢ = Aᵢ₊₁ : Bᵢ₊₁
for every i = 1, …, n−1. [7.13]

**Step 3.** Ratios which are the same with the same ratio are the same with one another. Applying this successively along the finite chain obtained in Step 2 yields
A₁ : B₁ = Aₙ : Bₙ. [5.11]

**Step 4.** Alternating this last proportion gives
A₁ : Aₙ = B₁ : Bₙ.
Thus the two lists are in the same ratio ex aequali, as required. [7.13; D5.17]
