## Proposition 7.36
**Statement:** Given three numbers, to find the least number which they measure.

**Step 1.** Let the three given numbers be A, B, and C. Choose the direct route of applying the earlier two-number construction successively. By 7.34, find D, the least number measured by A and B. Apply 7.34 again to find L, the least number measured by D and C. [7.34]

**Step 2.** The numbers A and B measure D, and D measures L. Thus L consists of whole copies of D; replacing each such copy by its whole copies of A shows that A measures L, and replacing it by its whole copies of B shows that B measures L. Also C measures L by construction. Hence all three given numbers measure L. [D7.5, 7.34]

**Step 3.** Let N be any number measured by A, B, and C. Since A and B measure N, their least common measured number D also measures N, by 7.35. Therefore D and C both measure N. But L was constructed as the least number measured by D and C, so N cannot be less than L. Consequently L is the least number measured by A, B, and C, and the required number has been found. [7.35, 7.34]
