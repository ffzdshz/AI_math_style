## Proposition 7.13
**Statement:** If four numbers be proportional, they will also be proportional alternately.

**Step 1.** Let A, B, C, D be proportional, so that A:B = C:D. By D7.20, A is the same multiple, the same part, or the same parts of B that C is of D. We take the direct route through the part-alternation result 7.9, using 7.10 only for the distinct same-parts case and reducing the multiple case to the same-part case.

**Step 2.** Suppose A is the same part of B that C is of D. By 7.9, alternately, whatever part or parts A is of C, B is the same part or parts of D. Thus A:C = B:D by D7.20.

**Step 3.** Suppose A is the same parts of B that C is of D. By 7.10, alternately, whatever parts or part A is of C, B is the same parts or part of D. Again A:C = B:D by D7.20.

**Step 4.** Suppose A is the same multiple of B that C is of D. Then B measures A the same number of times that D measures C; hence B is the same part of A that D is of C (D7.5, D7.3). Apply 7.9 to B, A, D, C: whatever part or parts B is of D, A is the same part or parts of C. Therefore A:C = B:D by D7.20.

**Step 5.** All alternatives in D7.20 have been covered. Consequently, if A:B = C:D, then A:C = B:D: the four numbers are proportional alternately.
