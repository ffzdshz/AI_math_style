## Proposition 7.34
**Statement:** Given two numbers, to find the least number which they measure.

**Step 1.** Let the given numbers be A and B. If they are equal, take L = A. If either measures the other, take L to be the latter. In either case both measure L, and no smaller number can be measured by the larger given number. These cases are therefore settled. Henceforth suppose that neither given number measures the other. [D7.3, D7.15, CN5]

**Step 2.** By 7.33, find the least numbers C and D having the same ratio as A and B, so that C:D = A:B. We choose this least-ratio construction because it directly supplies the multipliers needed below. [7.33]

**Step 3.** Let D multiply A to produce L. Since C:D = A:B, the products CB and DA are equal. Thus L = DA = CB, and consequently both A and B measure L. [7.19, D7.15]

**Step 4.** Let N be any number measured by both A and B. Write N = XA = YB, where X and Y count the respective repetitions. Neither count is a unit: otherwise N would equal one of the given numbers and the other would measure it, contrary to Step 1. Thus X and Y are numbers. Since YB = XA, the converse of 7.19 gives Y:X = A:B. Hence C and D are the least numbers in the same ratio as Y and X. By 7.20, D measures X; in particular, X is not less than D. [D7.15, 7.19, 7.20]

**Step 5.** Now N consists of X repetitions of A, while L consists of D repetitions of A. If X = D, then N = L; if X > D, the repetitions forming L are a proper part of those forming N, so N > L. Therefore every number measured by both A and B is at least L. Since both measure L by Step 3, L is the least number which they measure, and it has been constructed. [D7.15, CN5]
