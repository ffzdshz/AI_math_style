## Proposition 7.7
**Statement:** If a number be that part of a number, which a number subtracted is of a number subtracted, the remainder will also be the same part of the remainder that the whole is of the whole.

**Step 1.** Let A be a part of B, and let C, subtracted from A, be the same part of D, subtracted from B. Thus C < A and D < B. By the meaning of a part, B consists of a certain number k of equal copies of A, while D consists of exactly k equal copies of C. We take the direct route of subtracting corresponding equal pieces, since the hypothesis already supplies this common number of repetitions. [D7.3]

**Step 2.** From each of the k copies of A composing B, subtract a copy of C. Since equal numbers are subtracted from equal numbers, each remainder equals A − C. Hence the pieces left are k equal copies of A − C. [CN3]

**Step 3.** The pieces removed are k copies of C. By repeated addition of equals, their total equals D, which likewise consists of k copies of C. Consequently, subtracting their total from B leaves the same remainder as subtracting D from B, namely B − D. Thus B − D consists of exactly k equal copies of A − C. [CN2, CN3]

**Step 4.** Therefore A − C measures B − D exactly as many times as A measures B. By the definition of a part, the remainder A − C is the same part of the remainder B − D that the whole A is of the whole B. This proves the proposition. [D7.3]
