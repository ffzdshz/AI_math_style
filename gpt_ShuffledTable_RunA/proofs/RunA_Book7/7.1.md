## Proposition 7.1
**Statement:** Two unequal numbers being set out, and the less being continually subtracted in turn from the greater, if the number which is left never measures the one before it until an unit is left, the original numbers will be prime to one another.

**Step 1.** Let A>B be the given numbers, and perform the successive subtractions specified in the hypothesis until a unit is left. We follow an arbitrary common measure through these individual subtractions: it suffices to show that such a measure can only be the unit. [D7.12]

**Step 2.** Let c be any common measure of A and B. At any subtraction, suppose c measures both the greater quantity X and the lesser quantity Y. Then X and Y consist of whole copies of c. From the copies constituting X, remove exactly as many copies as constitute Y. Since equal quantities have been subtracted from equal quantities, the remaining copies constitute X−Y. Thus c measures the positive remainder as well as Y. [D7.5, CN3]

**Step 3.** Starting with A and B, apply this reasoning at every successive subtraction. Retaining the lesser quantity and replacing the greater by the remainder therefore preserves c as a common measure; interchanging their roles does not change this. Consequently c measures every remainder reached, including the final unit. [D7.5, CN3]

**Step 4.** A measure of the unit cannot exceed the unit: it is either the whole unit or a part of it. But any number, being a multitude composed of units, is greater than a single unit. Hence c cannot be a number greater than the unit and must itself be the unit. [D7.1, D7.2, CN5]

**Step 5.** The unit measures both original numbers, since each is composed of units, and the preceding steps show that they have no other common measure. Therefore A and B are prime to one another. [D7.2, D7.12]
