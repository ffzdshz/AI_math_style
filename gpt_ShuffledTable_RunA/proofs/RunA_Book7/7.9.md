## Proposition 7.9
**Statement:** If a number be a part of a number, and another be the same part of another, alternately also, whatever part or parts the first is of the third, the same part, or the same parts, will the second also be of the fourth.

**Step 1.** Let A be the same part of B that C is of D. By the meaning of a part, B consists of some number n of copies of A, and D consists of the same number n of copies of C. Thus
B = A + ⋯ + A, D = C + ⋯ + C,
with n summands in each sum. We use the direct additive route, since the hypothesis supplies equally many corresponding summands. [D7.3]

**Step 2.** Suppose first that A is a part of C. By VII.5, the sum of two copies of A is the same part of the sum of two copies of C. Apply VII.5 again to these sums and one further pair A, C, and continue through the n pairs. At each addition the sums remain in the same part-relation as A to C. Consequently B is the same part of D that A is of C. [7.5]

**Step 3.** Suppose instead that A is parts of C, that is, A is less than C but does not measure it. By VII.6, the sum of two copies of A is the same parts of the sum of two copies of C. Applying VII.6 successively with each further pair A, C preserves these same parts through all n pairs. Consequently B is the same parts of D that A is of C. [D7.4, 7.6]

**Step 4.** Therefore, alternately, whatever part or parts the first number A is of the third C, the same part or parts the second B is of the fourth D, as required. [7.5, 7.6]
