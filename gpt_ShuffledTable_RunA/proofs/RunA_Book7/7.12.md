## Proposition 7.12
**Statement:** If there be as many numbers as we please in proportion, then, as one of the antecedents is to one of the consequents, so are all the antecedents to all the consequents.

**Step 1.** Let a₁, …, aₙ be the antecedents and b₁, …, bₙ their corresponding consequents, with a₁:b₁ = ⋯ = aₙ:bₙ. Put A = a₁ + ⋯ + aₙ and B = b₁ + ⋯ + bₙ. By the definition of numerical proportion, the antecedents are respectively the same multiple, the same part, or the same parts of their consequents. We take the direct numerical route, using the addition results VII.5 and VII.6, whose hypotheses match these defining relations. [D7.20]

**Step 2.** Suppose each aᵢ is the same part of bᵢ. By VII.5, a₁ + a₂ is that same part of b₁ + b₂. Applying VII.5 again to these sums and the next pair, and continuing through the finitely many pairs, shows that A is that same part of B. [7.5]

**Step 3.** Suppose instead that each aᵢ is the same parts of bᵢ. By VII.6, a₁ + a₂ is those same parts of b₁ + b₂. Repeating the application with each succeeding pair shows that A is those same parts of B. [7.6]

**Step 4.** Suppose each aᵢ is the same multiple of bᵢ. If the multiple is greater than one, each bᵢ is consequently the same part of aᵢ. Repeated application of VII.5 therefore makes B that same part of A; hence A is the same multiple of B that each aᵢ is of bᵢ. If the common multiple is one, corresponding numbers are equal, and repeated addition of equals gives A = B. [D7.3, D7.5, 7.5, CN2]

**Step 5.** Thus, in every case, A has to B the same defining numerical relation that any antecedent aᵢ has to its corresponding consequent bᵢ. By the definition of proportion, aᵢ:bᵢ = A:B. Therefore, as one antecedent is to its corresponding consequent, so are all the antecedents together to all the consequents together. [D7.20]
