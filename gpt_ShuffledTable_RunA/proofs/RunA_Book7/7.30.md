## Proposition 7.30
**Statement:** If two numbers by multiplying one another make some number, and any prime number measure the product, it will also measure one of the original numbers.

**Step 1.** Let A and B multiply one another to produce C, and let the prime number P measure C. If P measures A, the conclusion already holds. Suppose therefore that P does not measure A. Then P is prime to A. [7.29]

**Step 2.** Suppose, for a contradiction, that P does not measure B either. Then P is also prime to B. Thus both A and B are prime to P. [7.29; D7.12]

**Step 3.** We choose the product-coprimality route because it applies directly to these two coprimality statements: since A and B are each prime to P, their product C is prime to P. [7.24]

**Step 4.** But P measures C by hypothesis and measures itself once. Hence C and P have the common measure P, which is a number, not a unit. This contradicts their being prime to one another, for such numbers have only a unit as a common measure. Therefore P must measure B when it does not measure A. Consequently, any prime number measuring the product measures at least one of the original numbers. [D7.2; D7.12]
