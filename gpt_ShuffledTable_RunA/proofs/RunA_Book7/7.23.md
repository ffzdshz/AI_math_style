## Proposition 7.23
**Statement:** If two number be prime to one another, the number which measures the one of them will be prime to the remaining number.

**Step 1.** Let A and B be numbers prime to one another, and let C measure A. We use the defining common-measure criterion directly: the unit alone measures both A and B. It therefore suffices to show that no number other than the unit measures both C and B. [D7.12]

**Step 2.** Suppose a number D other than the unit measures both C and B. Since C measures A, A consists of equal copies of C; since D measures C, each such copy consists of equal copies of D. Replacing each copy of C by its copies of D expresses A as repeated addition of D. Thus D measures A, while by supposition it also measures B. [D7.5, D7.15]

**Step 3.** This contradicts the fact that the unit alone is a common measure of A and B. Consequently, no number other than the unit measures both C and B, so C and B are prime to one another. Hence a number which measures either of two numbers prime to one another is prime to the remaining number. [D7.12]
