## Proposition 7.35
**Statement:** If two numbers measure any number, the least number measured by them will also measure the same.

**Step 1.** Let A and B measure N, and let L be the least number measured by both A and B, supplied by 7.34. We use repeated subtraction: the leastness of L will exclude a nonzero remainder. [7.34]

**Step 2.** Subtract L from N whenever the quantity remaining is at least L. At every subtraction, any nonempty remainder is still measured by both A and B. Indeed, the quantity before subtraction consists of whole copies of A, and L also consists of whole copies of A; removing the latter copies leaves whole copies of A. The same argument applies to B. [D7.15, CN3]

**Step 3.** This process must stop: N consists of finitely many units, and each subtraction removes a positive number of them. Thus eventually either nothing remains, or a positive remainder R less than L remains. [D7.2, CN5]

**Step 4.** A positive remainder R is impossible. By Step 2, both A and B would measure R, contrary to L being the least number measured by them, since R < L. Hence nothing remains. [7.34]

**Step 5.** Therefore N is exactly a whole number of copies of L. Consequently L measures N: the least number measured by A and B also measures the number measured by them both. [D7.15, D7.5]
