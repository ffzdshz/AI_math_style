## Proposition 7.22
**Statement:** The least numbers of those which have the same ratio with them are prime to one another.

**Step 1.** Let A and B be the least numbers among those having their ratio. Suppose that they are not prime to one another. Then they have a common measure d other than the unit; thus d > 1. [D7.12, D7.2]

**Step 2.** Let a and b be the respective numbers of times that d measures A and B. Multiplication therefore gives A = da and B = db. We use this common measure directly; there is no need to find a greatest common measure. [D7.15]

**Step 3.** Since the same number d multiplies a and b to produce A and B, the products have the ratio of the numbers multiplied. Hence A:B = a:b. This is the direct common-multiplier route, for which 7.17 applies immediately. [7.17]

**Step 4.** Since d > 1, A consists of more than one copy of a, and B of more than one copy of b. Consequently a < A and b < B, because the whole is greater than the part. This contradicts the assumption that A and B are the least numbers having their ratio. Thus they have no common measure other than the unit and are therefore prime to one another. [D7.15, CN5, D7.12]
