## Proposition 7.37
**Statement:** If a number be measured by any number, the number which is measured will have a part called by the same name as the measuring number.

**Step 1.** Let the number M measure the number N. A part called by the same name as M means an Mth part: one of as many equal parts as there are units in M. If N=M, a unit is already such a part, since N then consists of exactly as many units as M. We may therefore suppose N>M. [D7.1, D7.2]

**Step 2.** Let Q be the number of repetitions of M which make up N. Since N>M, this count is a number, not merely a unit. Thus Q multiplying M produces N, by the definition of multiplication. [D7.2, D7.15]

**Step 3.** Let M multiplying Q produce P. By 7.16, the products obtained by multiplying these two numbers in opposite orders are equal; hence P=N. This direct interchange of the factors is the natural route, because it makes M the count of the equal parts. [D7.15, 7.16]

**Step 4.** Consequently, N consists of Q repeated as many times as there are units in M. Therefore Q measures N and, since M contains more than one unit, Q is less than N. Thus Q is a part of N, and its repetition count shows that it is precisely the Mth part. Hence the measured number has a part called by the same name as the measuring number. [D7.2, D7.3, D7.15]
