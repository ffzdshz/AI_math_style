## Proposition 7.27
**Statement:** If two numbers be prime to one another, and each by multiplying itself make a certain number, the products will be prime to one another; and, if the original numbers by multiplying the products make certain numbers, the latter will also be prime to one another [and this is always the case with the extremes].

**Step 1.** Let a and b be prime to one another. We use VII.24 directly, since its rule for multiplying numbers prime to a fixed number applies successively to the repeated factors here. Applying it to a and a, each prime to b, shows that a² is prime to b. [7.24]

**Step 2.** Consequently b is prime to a². Applying VII.24 to b and b, with a² held fixed, shows that b² is prime to a². Thus the products obtained by multiplying each original number by itself are prime to one another. [7.24]

**Step 3.** Both a² and a are prime to b, so VII.24 shows that their product a³ is prime to b. Now b and b are each prime to a³, so b² is prime to a³. Finally, b² and b are each prime to a³, so their product b³ is prime to a³. Therefore the products obtained by multiplying the original numbers into their respective squares are also prime to one another. [7.24]

**Step 4.** The same reasoning continues without limit. For any positive integer n, repeated applications of VII.24, multiplying by a at each stage while holding b fixed, show that aⁿ is prime to b. Holding aⁿ fixed instead, repeated applications of VII.24 to successive products of b show that bⁿ is prime to aⁿ. Hence the squares, the cubes, and every subsequent pair of corresponding powers—the extremes at each continuation—are prime to one another. [7.24]
