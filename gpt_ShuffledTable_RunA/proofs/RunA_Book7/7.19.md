## Proposition 7.19
**Statement:** If four numbers be proportional, the number produced from the first and fourth will be equal to the number produced from the second and third; and, if the number produced from the first and fourth be equal to that produced from the second and third, the four numbers will be proportional.

**Step 1.** Let A, B, C, D be the four numbers, in order. Form E by A multiplying D, F by B multiplying C, and G by B multiplying D. Thus E = AD, F = BC, and G = BD. [D7.15]

**Step 2.** Since A and B multiply the same number D, their products satisfy E:G = A:B. [7.18] Since B multiplies C and D, its products satisfy F:G = C:D. [7.17] We use the common comparison product G because these two propositions apply directly in the stated factor order, without interchanging factors.

**Step 3.** Suppose A:B = C:D. The ratios in Step 2 then give E:G = F:G, since ratios equal to the same ratio are equal to one another. [5.11] Numbers having the same ratio to the same number are equal; hence E = F, or AD = BC. [5.9]

**Step 4.** Conversely, suppose AD = BC, so E = F. Substituting this equality into the ratios of Step 2, A:B and C:D both equal E:G. Therefore A:B = C:D. [5.11] Thus the four numbers are proportional if and only if the product of the first and fourth equals the product of the second and third.
