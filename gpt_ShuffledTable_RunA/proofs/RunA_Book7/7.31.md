## Proposition 7.31
**Statement:** Any composite number is measured by some prime number.

**Step 1.** Let A be a composite number. Then A has a numerical measure smaller than itself. A numerical measure is a number—a multitude of units—not the unit alone. [D7.13, D7.3, D7.2]

**Step 2.** Take the direct least-measure route: among the numerical measures smaller than A, choose the least, P. This choice is possible because the collection is nonempty by Step 1 and finite: A is a finite multitude of units, so only finitely many numbers are smaller than A. [D7.2, D7.3]

**Step 3.** If P were not prime, it would have a numerical measure Q smaller than itself. Since Q measures P, P consists of repeated copies of Q; since P measures A, A consists of repeated copies of P. Replacing each copy of P by its copies of Q therefore expresses A as repeated copies of Q. Thus Q measures A. But Q is a number smaller than P, contradicting the choice of P as the least numerical measure of A. [D7.11, D7.3, D7.15]

**Step 4.** Consequently P is prime, and by its choice P measures A. Therefore any composite number is measured by some prime number. [D7.11]
