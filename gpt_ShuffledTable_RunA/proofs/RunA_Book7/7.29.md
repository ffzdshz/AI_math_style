## Proposition 7.29
**Statement:** Any prime number is prime to any number which it does not measure.

**Step 1.** Let P be a prime number and N a number which P does not measure. We take the direct definitional route: it suffices to show that the unit alone is their common measure. [D7.12]

**Step 2.** Any common measure of P and N must measure P. Since P is prime, no number less than P measures it; hence any common measure other than the unit would have to be P itself. But P does not measure N by hypothesis. Therefore there is no common measure of P and N other than the unit. [D7.11]

**Step 3.** The unit measures both P and N, since each number is a multitude composed of units. Consequently the unit alone is their common measure, so P and N are prime to one another. Thus any prime number is prime to any number which it does not measure. [D7.2, D7.12]
