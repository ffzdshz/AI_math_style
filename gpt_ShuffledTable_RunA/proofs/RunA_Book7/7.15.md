## Proposition 7.15
**Statement:** If an unit measure any number, and another number measure any other number the same number of times, alternately also, the unit will measure the third number the same number of times that the second measures the fourth.

**Step 1.** Let u be the unit, and let B, C, D be the second, third, and fourth terms respectively. Suppose that u measures B as many times as C measures D. We use direct decomposition into units, rather than an alternation theorem for four numbers, since the first term here is a unit. [D7.1, D7.2]

**Step 2.** Denote the common number of measurements by n. Thus B consists of n units, while D consists of n copies of C. Let C consist of m units; consequently u measures C m times. View the units of D as n rows, one for each copy of C, with m units in each row. This merely displays the given decomposition of D into copies of C and of each copy into units. [D7.2, D7.3]

**Step 3.** Collect the first unit from every row into one group, the second unit from every row into another, and continue through the m positions. Each group consists of n units and therefore equals B: its units equal the corresponding units of B, so their sums are equal by repeated addition of equals. There are exactly m such groups, and they exhaust the units of D without omission or repetition. [D7.2, CN2]

**Step 4.** Hence D is composed of exactly m copies of B, so B measures D m times. But u also measures C m times. Therefore, alternately, the unit measures the third number the same number of times that the second measures the fourth, as required. [D7.2, D7.3]
