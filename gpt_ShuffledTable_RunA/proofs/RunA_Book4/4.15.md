## Proposition 4.15
**Statement:** In a given circle to inscribe an equilateral and equiangular hexagon.

**Step 1.** Construct an equilateral triangle by I.1. Applying I.5 to two pairs of its equal sides shows that its three angles are equal. By IV.2, inscribe in the given circle a triangle ACE equiangular with this triangle. Thus the angles of ACE are equal. [1.1, 1.5, CN1, 4.2]

**Step 2.** Consider the three intervening arcs AC, CE, and EA, each chosen not to contain the remaining vertex. They are subtended respectively by the equal angles AEC, CAE, and ECA; hence they are equal by III.26. We take this equal-arc route because III.26 applies directly to the angle equality already obtained, without first establishing equality of the triangle’s chords. [3.26]

**Step 3.** Bisect arc AC at B, arc CE at D, and arc EA at F by III.30. The points therefore occur around the circle in the order A, B, C, D, E, F, and the six successive arcs AB, BC, CD, DE, EF, FA are equal. Indeed, each is a half of one of the three equal arcs; unequal halves would have unequal doubles, contrary to the equality of those whole arcs. This is the elementary consequence of addition and whole–part comparison. Join AB, BC, CD, DE, EF, and FA. [3.30, CN1, CN2, CN5, P1]

**Step 4.** Equal arcs in the same circle are subtended by equal straight lines, by III.29. Consequently
AB = BC = CD = DE = EF = FA,
so ABCDEF is equilateral. [3.29]

**Step 5.** Each interior angle of ABCDEF stands on the arc between its two neighbouring vertices that does not contain its own vertex. Each such arc consists of four of the equal successive arcs from Step 3: for example, ∠FAB stands on arc BCDEF, while ∠ABC stands on arc CDEFA. Thus all six intercepted arcs are equal, by addition of equals. By III.27, the six interior angles standing on them are equal. Hence ABCDEF is equiangular. [CN2, 3.27]

**Step 6.** All six vertices lie on the given circumference, so ABCDEF is inscribed in the given circle. Together with Steps 4 and 5, this proves that the constructed figure is an inscribed equilateral and equiangular hexagon, as required. [D4.3]
