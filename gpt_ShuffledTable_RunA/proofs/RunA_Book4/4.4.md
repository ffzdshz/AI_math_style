## Proposition 4.4
**Statement:** In a given triangle to inscribe a circle.

**Step 1.** Let ABC be the given triangle. We use the direct construction by two internal angle bisectors. Bisect ∠ABC and ∠BCA. Their angles with BC have sum less than two right angles, so the bisectors meet on the side of BC containing A. Call their intersection D. Since D lies within both internal angles, it lies inside triangle ABC. [1.9, 1.17, P5]

**Step 2.** Produce the side lines as necessary, and draw DE perpendicular to AB, DF perpendicular to BC, and DG perpendicular to CA, with E, F, G on the respective lines. Each half-angle at B and C is acute. Consequently E lies on ray BA, F lies on both rays BC and CB, and G lies on ray CA: a foot on an opposite ray would give a right triangle another angle greater than a right angle, contrary to 1.17. [P2, 1.12, 1.17]

**Step 3.** In triangles BDE and BDF, ∠EBD = ∠DBF because BD bisects ∠ABC; the angles at E and F are equal, being right angles; and BD is common. Thus DE = DF by the two-angle-and-side criterion. This is the natural criterion here, since the two equal angles and the common side are already available. [1.9, P4, 1.26]

**Step 4.** Similarly, in triangles CDF and CDG, ∠FCD = ∠DCG because CD bisects ∠BCA; the angles at F and G are equal right angles; and CD is common. Hence DF = DG. Together with Step 3, this gives DE = DF = DG. [1.9, P4, 1.26, CN1]

**Step 5.** Describe the circle with centre D and radius DE. Since DF and DG equal DE, this circle passes through E, F, and G. [P3, D1.15]

**Step 6.** Produce the radii through D to form diameters. At E, F, and G respectively, the lines AB, BC, and CA are perpendicular to these diameters at their extremities. Therefore each line falls outside the circle apart from its point of contact, and so touches the circle. The circle lies on the side of each tangent containing its centre D. Since D is inside ABC, the circle is contained within the triangle; in particular, its contacts E, F, and G lie on the side segments themselves. Thus the circle touches every side of ABC and is inscribed in the given triangle, as required. [P2, 3.16, D3.2, D4.5]
