## Proposition 4.6
**Statement:** In a given circle to inscribe a square.

**Step 1.** Let the given circle have centre O, found by Proposition 3.1. Choose A on its circumference, join AO, and produce AO through O to meet the circumference again at C. Thus AC is a diameter. [3.1, P1, P2, D1.17]

**Step 2.** At O construct OB perpendicular to AC, with B on the circumference, and produce BO through O to meet the circumference again at D. Thus BD is also a diameter. The angles AOB and BOC are right by the definition of perpendicularity; their vertically opposite angles COD and DOA are likewise right. Hence all four central angles are equal. [1.11, P2, D1.17, D1.10, 1.15, P4]

**Step 3.** Join AB, BC, CD, and DA. Since O is the centre, OA, OB, OC, and OD are equal radii. [P1, D1.15, D1.16]

**Step 4.** Use side–angle–side directly: the construction already supplies equal radii and equal included angles. Comparing triangle AOB with each of triangles BOC, COD, and DOA, Proposition 1.4 gives AB = BC, AB = CD, and AB = DA. Consequently ABCD is equilateral. [1.4, CN1]

**Step 5.** Because AC is a diameter, the angles ABC and ADC are angles in semicircles and therefore right. Because BD is a diameter, the angles BAD and BCD are likewise right. Thus ABCD is right-angled. [3.31]

**Step 6.** The quadrilateral ABCD is both equilateral and right-angled, so it is a square. Its four vertices lie on the circumference of the given circle, so it is inscribed in that circle. Therefore a square has been inscribed in the given circle, as required. [D1.22, D4.3]
