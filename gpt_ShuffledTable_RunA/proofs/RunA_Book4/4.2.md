## Proposition 4.2
**Statement:** In a given circle to inscribe a triangle equiangular with a given triangle.

**Step 1.** Let Ω be the given circle and DEF the given triangle. We use the tangent–chord construction to obtain two of the required angles directly. Choose A on Ω and draw the tangent XAY at A, with A between X and Y. [3.17]

**Step 2.** The angles of triangle DEF sum to two right angles, so ∠DEF + ∠DFE is less than two right angles. We use the angle-sum theorem here because it will also determine the third angle below. [1.32] On the side of XAY containing the circle, construct rays AB and AC such that
∠YAB = ∠DFE and ∠XAC = ∠DEF. [1.23]
Produce these rays to meet the circumference again at B and C, respectively. [P2] Since the two prescribed angles together are less than two right angles, the rays occur in the order AY, AB, AC, AX within that half-plane; in particular, B and C are distinct. [1.13, 1.32] Join BC. [P1]

**Step 3.** The angle between the tangent AY and chord AB equals the angle in the alternate segment, namely ∠ACB. Consequently,
∠ACB = ∠YAB = ∠DFE. [3.32, CN1]

**Step 4.** Likewise, the angle between the tangent AX and chord AC equals the angle in the alternate segment, namely ∠ABC. Consequently,
∠ABC = ∠XAC = ∠DEF. [3.32, CN1]

**Step 5.** The angles of each of triangles ABC and DEF sum to two right angles. [1.32] The two angle equalities just established give equal sums; subtracting those equal sums from the equal totals yields
∠BAC = ∠EDF. [CN2, CN3]
Thus ABC is equiangular with DEF. Since its three vertices A, B, and C lie on the circumference of Ω, it is inscribed in Ω. [D4.3] Therefore, in the given circle a triangle equiangular with the given triangle has been inscribed.
