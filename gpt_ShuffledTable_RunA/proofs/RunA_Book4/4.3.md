## Proposition 4.3
**Statement:** About a given circle to circumscribe a triangle equiangular with a given triangle.

**Step 1.** Let the given circle be Γ and the given triangle be ABC. Write α, β, γ for its angles at A, B, C, and let R denote a right angle. By the angle-sum theorem, α + β + γ = 2R. Produce sides at A and B to obtain exterior angles α′ = 2R − α and β′ = 2R − β. Each of α′ and β′ is positive and less than 2R. [P2, P4, 1.13, 1.32, CN3]

**Step 2.** Find the centre O of Γ, choose a point P on its circumference, and join OP. At O construct a ray OQ making angle α′ with OP, and, on the opposite side of OP, a ray OS making angle β′ with OP; let Q and S be their intersections with Γ. The three successive sectors between OP, OQ, OS have angles
∠POQ = 2R − α,
∠QOS = 4R − α′ − β′ = α + β = 2R − γ,
∠SOP = 2R − β,
where the middle sector does not contain OP. Thus all three sectors are positive and less than two right angles. [3.1, P1, P2, 1.23, 1.13, 1.32, CN2, CN3]

**Step 3.** At P, Q, S erect straight lines p, q, s perpendicular to OP, OQ, OS respectively. Extend the radii through O to diameters. Each perpendicular falls outside Γ and meets it at its prescribed point, so each is a tangent. This is the direct choice of construction: the contact points are already fixed, and the perpendicular-to-diameter result applies immediately. [P2, 1.11, 3.16, D3.2]

**Step 4.** Verify that the required intersections exist. Consider in turn the consecutive pairs (P,Q), (Q,S), and (S,P), writing U,V for the pair and θ for its central sector angle. Join UV. Since OU = OV, the base angles of triangle OUV are equal; call each b. Its angle sum gives 2b + θ = 2R, so b < R. On the side of UV opposite O, the angles made by UV with the tangents at U and V are each R − b. Their sum is therefore 2R − 2b = θ < 2R. Consequently those tangents meet on that side of UV. Denote p ∩ q by X, p ∩ s by Y, and q ∩ s by Z. [P1, D1.15, 1.5, 1.32, CN2, CN3, P5]

**Step 5.** At each contact point, the other two contact radii lie on opposite sides of the line through that point and O, because each of the three consecutive central sectors is less than 2R. In the construction of Step 4, the tangent intersection for U,V lies on the ray of the tangent at U directed toward the side of OU containing V. Hence X and Y lie on opposite rays of p from P; X and Z lie on opposite rays of q from Q; and Y and Z lie on opposite rays of s from S. Thus P lies between X and Y, Q between X and Z, and S between Y and Z. The actual sides of triangle XYZ therefore touch Γ at P, Q, S respectively, so XYZ is circumscribed about Γ. [P5, 3.16, D3.2, D4.4]

**Step 6.** In each tangent triangle considered in Step 4, the two angles at the contact points have sum θ. Its angle at the tangent intersection is consequently 2R − θ. Applying this to triangles XPQ, YPS, and ZQS, and using the betweenness established in Step 5, gives
∠YXZ = 2R − ∠POQ = α,
∠XYZ = 2R − ∠SOP = β,
∠XZY = 2R − ∠QOS = γ.
Thus XYZ is equiangular with ABC and is circumscribed about the given circle, as required. [1.32, CN3, D4.4]
