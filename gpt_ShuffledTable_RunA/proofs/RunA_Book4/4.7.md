## Proposition 4.7
**Statement:** About a given circle to circumscribe a square.

**Step 1.** Let the given circle have centre O, found by III.1. Draw a diameter AC, and through O draw its perpendicular, meeting the circle at B and D, so that A, B, C, D occur successively around the circle. The four angles at O are right angles, and OA = OB = OC = OD. [3.1, P1, P2, 1.11, D1.10, D1.17, D1.15]

**Step 2.** At A and C draw straight lines perpendicular to AC, and at B and D draw straight lines perpendicular to BD; denote these lines by a, b, c, d respectively. We choose these perpendiculars because III.16 directly shows that each lies outside the circle except at its endpoint of contact. Thus a, b, c, d touch the circle at A, B, C, D respectively. [1.11, 3.16, D3.2]

**Step 3.** Let consecutive tangents meet at E = a ∩ b, F = b ∩ c, G = c ∩ d, and H = d ∩ a. These intersections exist on the required sides: for example, join AB. Since triangle OAB is right-angled at O, its angles at A and B are each less than a right angle. Consequently the angles made by AB with a and b on the side of AB opposite O are each less than a right angle, being the complements of those triangle angles. The fifth postulate therefore makes a and b meet on that side. The same argument applies to BC, CD, and DA. Thus A lies between H and E, B between E and F, C between F and G, and D between G and H. [P1, P2, 1.17, D1.10, P5]

**Step 4.** The lines a and c are each parallel to BD, since they and BD are perpendicular to AC. Likewise b and d are each parallel to AC. Hence OAEB, OBFC, OCGD, and ODHA are parallelograms. [P4, 1.28]

**Step 5.** Opposite sides and opposite angles of these parallelograms are equal. Their angles at O are right angles, so the angles of EFGH at E, F, G, H are all right angles. Moreover, every one of the segments AE, EB, BF, FC, CG, GD, DH, HA equals a radius of the given circle. Since all those radii are equal,
AE = EB = BF = FC = CG = GD = DH = HA. [1.34, D1.15, CN1]

**Step 6.** Since the contact points lie between the corresponding vertices,
EF = EB + BF, FG = FC + CG, GH = GD + DH, and HE = HA + AE.
Adding equal segments gives EF = FG = GH = HE. Therefore EFGH is both equilateral and right-angled, and hence is a square. Its four sides touch the given circle at B, C, D, A respectively, so it is circumscribed about that circle. Thus about the given circle a square has been circumscribed, as required. [CN2, D1.22, D4.4]
