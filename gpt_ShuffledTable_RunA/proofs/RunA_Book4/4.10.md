## Proposition 4.10
**Statement:** To construct an isosceles triangle having each of the angles at the base double of the remaining one.

**Step 1.** Take a straight line AB and cut it at C so that the rectangle contained by AB and BC equals the square on AC. Thus A, C, B occur in this order, and AC < AB. [2.11, CN5]

**Step 2.** Describe a circle with centre A and radius AB. Since AC is less than the radius, and therefore less than the diameter, fit into this circle a chord BD equal to AC, taking B as one endpoint. Join AD and CD. The radii AB and AD are equal, whereas BD = AC < AB; in particular, A, C, D are not collinear. [P3, D1.15, D1.17, CN5, 4.1, P1]

**Step 3.** Circumscribe a circle about triangle ACD. The point B is outside this circle, and the secant from B meets it first at C and then at A. By the construction,
rectangle AB, BC = square AC = square BD.
Consequently BD touches this circle at D. This tangent criterion is the direct route: the rectangle–square equality already supplies precisely its hypothesis. [4.5, CN1, 3.37]

**Step 4.** Apply the tangent–chord theorem to the tangent DB and chord DC. The angle between them equals the angle in the alternate segment:
∠BDC = ∠DAC.
Since A, C, B are collinear, ∠DAC = ∠DAB. [3.32]

**Step 5.** The angle BCD is an exterior angle of triangle ACD, so
∠BCD = ∠CAD + ∠ADC.
Replacing ∠CAD by its equal ∠BDC gives
∠BCD = ∠BDC + ∠ADC = ∠BDA,
because DC lies inside angle BDA. Also, AB = AD, so the base angles of triangle ABD satisfy ∠DBA = ∠BDA. Since ray BC is ray BA, it follows that ∠DBC = ∠BCD. Therefore, in triangle BCD, the opposite sides DC and DB are equal. [1.32, CN2, 1.5, CN1, 1.6]

**Step 6.** Now DC = DB = AC. Hence triangle ACD is isosceles, and ∠ADC = ∠CAD. Both ∠CAD and, by Step 4, ∠BDC equal ∠BAD. Therefore
∠BDA = ∠BDC + ∠CDA = 2∠BAD.
The other base angle ∠ABD equals ∠BDA, and is consequently also double ∠BAD. [CN1, 1.5, CN2]

**Step 7.** Thus triangle ABD has AB = AD and BD < AB, so it is isosceles; each of its base angles ABD and BDA is double its remaining angle BAD. The required triangle has been constructed. [D1.20; Steps 2 and 6]
