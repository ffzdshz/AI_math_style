## Proposition 4.12
**Statement:** About a given circle to circumscribe an equilateral and equiangular pentagon.

**Step 1.** In the given circle inscribe an equilateral and equiangular pentagon ABCDE, with its vertices in circular order. We use these vertices as the points of contact and construct the required figure from their tangents; this is the direct route supplied by the inscribed pentagon. [4.11]

**Step 2.** Join AC, BD, CE, DA, and EB. Consider the consecutive-vertex triangles ABC, BCD, CDE, DEA, and EAB. Their corresponding pairs of sides are equal, and their included angles are equal because ABCDE is equilateral and equiangular. Therefore their corresponding angles are equal by I.4—the natural comparison, since two sides and the included angle are already given equal. Each triangle is also isosceles, so its two base angles are equal. Consequently all these base angles have one common value, α. Since the two base angles of a triangle together are less than two right angles, α is acute. [P1, 1.4, 1.5, CN1, 1.17]

**Step 3.** Draw tangents to the circle at A, B, C, D, and E, extending them as necessary. For the tangents at A and B, take the rays lying on the side of AB opposite C. By the tangent–chord theorem, each makes with AB an angle equal to ∠ACB, namely α. Their two interior angles on that side therefore total less than two right angles, so the rays meet; call their intersection P. In precisely the same way, the tangents at B and C meet at Q outside BC, those at C and D at R outside CD, those at D and E at S outside DE, and those at E and A at T outside EA. Thus every triangle PAB, QBC, RCD, SDE, and TEA has both base angles equal to α. [3.17, P2, 3.32, P5]

**Step 4.** The contact points lie between the appropriate intersections. For example, P, B, and Q lie on the tangent at B. The chord rays BA and BC lie on the same side of that tangent, because it does not cut the circle. Moreover, ∠PBA = ∠QBC = α. If BP and BQ were the same ray, these equal angles would have distinct other rays on the same side, making one a proper part of the other, which is impossible. Hence BP and BQ are opposite rays, and B lies between P and Q. Likewise C lies between Q and R, D between R and S, E between S and T, and A between T and P. The five tangent segments therefore form the pentagon PQRST, with the respective contact points B, C, D, E, A on its sides. [D3.2, 3.32, CN5]

**Step 5.** The triangles PAB, QBC, RCD, SDE, and TEA have two corresponding angles equal and the sides between those angles equal, since AB = BC = CD = DE = EA. By I.26 their corresponding remaining sides and their apex angles are equal. Thus
PA = QB = RC = SD = TE,
PB = QC = RD = SE = TA,
and
∠APB = ∠BQC = ∠CRD = ∠DSE = ∠ETA. [1.26]

**Step 6.** Since the contact points are between consecutive vertices,
PQ = PB + BQ, QR = QC + CR, RS = RD + DS, ST = SE + ET, and TP = TA + AP.
Adding the corresponding equal segments from Step 5 gives PQ = QR = RS = ST = TP. The interior angles of PQRST are exactly the five equal apex angles established in Step 5. Hence PQRST is equilateral and equiangular. Each of its sides touches the given circle at its stated contact point, so it is circumscribed about that circle. Therefore an equilateral and equiangular pentagon has been circumscribed about the given circle, as required. [CN2, 1.26, D4.4]
