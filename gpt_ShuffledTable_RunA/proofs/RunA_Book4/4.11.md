## Proposition 4.11
**Statement:** In a given circle to inscribe an equilateral and equiangular pentagon.

**Step 1.** Let the given circle be Γ. Construct an isosceles triangle whose two base angles are each double its vertex angle, and inscribe in Γ a triangle ACD equiangular with it, with A corresponding to the vertex. Thus ∠ACD = ∠CDA = 2∠CAD. This angle-ratio construction is the direct route, since the prescribed angles can be transferred immediately to the given circle. [4.10, 4.2]

**Step 2.** Bisect ∠ACD and ∠CDA. Produce their bisectors to meet Γ again at E and B respectively: E lies on the arc DA not containing C, and B on the arc AC not containing D. The points therefore occur around Γ in the order A, B, C, D, E. Join AB, BC, DE, and EA; together with CD these form pentagon ABCDE. [1.9, P2, P1]

**Step 3.** Put α = ∠CAD. Since each angle bisected in Step 2 is double α, each of its halves equals α. Consequently
∠CAD = ∠ACE = ∠ECD = ∠CDB = ∠BDA.
[4.10, 4.2, 1.9, CN1]

**Step 4.** These five equal angles stand respectively on the arcs CD, AE, ED, CB, and BA, taking in each case the arc not containing the angle's vertex. Equal angles at the circumference stand on equal arcs. Hence the five successive arcs AB, BC, CD, DE, and EA are equal. We pass from angles to arcs first because equal angles are what the construction supplies directly. [3.26]

**Step 5.** Equal arcs are subtended by equal straight lines. Therefore AB = BC = CD = DE = EA, so pentagon ABCDE is equilateral. [3.29]

**Step 6.** Each interior angle of the pentagon stands on the arc between its adjacent vertices that does not contain its own vertex. Each such arc consists of three of the five equal successive arcs established in Step 4. These five three-part arcs are equal, because adding equals to equals gives equal wholes. Angles at the circumference standing on equal arcs are equal; hence ∠EAB, ∠ABC, ∠BCD, ∠CDE, and ∠DEA are all equal. The pentagon is therefore equiangular. [CN2, 3.27]

**Step 7.** All five vertices lie on Γ, so ABCDE is inscribed in the given circle. By Steps 5 and 6 it is both equilateral and equiangular. Thus the required pentagon has been constructed. [D4.3]
