## Proposition 4.8
**Statement:** In a given square to inscribe a circle.

**Step 1.** Let ABCD be the given square. We use its diagonals as angle bisectors and construct equal perpendiculars to its sides. Join AC and BD, meeting inside the square at O. Since the sides of the square are equal, triangles ABC and ADC have their three sides respectively equal; hence AC bisects the angles at A and C. Similarly, comparison of triangles ABD and CBD shows that BD bisects the angles at B and D. Thus each angle between a side and the segment from its endpoint to O is half a right angle, and therefore less than a right angle. [D1.22, P1, 1.8, CN5]

**Step 2.** From O draw perpendiculars OE, OF, OG, OH to the straight lines AB, BC, CD, DA respectively. Their feet lie on the sides themselves, not on their extensions. Indeed, consider any side UV and its perpendicular foot T. If T lay beyond U, then in triangle OUT the exterior angle OUV would, by 1.16, be greater than the right angle OTU; but Step 1 shows that OUV is less than a right angle. The case where T lies beyond V is identical. Nor can T coincide with either endpoint, since the angle there between the side and the segment to O is not right. Hence E, F, G, H lie strictly within their respective sides. [1.12, D1.10, 1.16, P4]

**Step 3.** Triangles AEO and AHO have equal right angles at E and H, equal angles at A, and the common side AO opposite their right angles. Therefore OE = OH by 1.26. Likewise, comparison of triangles BEO and BFO gives OE = OF, and comparison of triangles CFO and CGO gives OF = OG. Consequently
OE = OF = OG = OH.
Here 1.26 applies directly because the known data are two equal angles and the common side opposite a right angle. [P4, 1.26, CN1]

**Step 4.** Describe the circle with centre O and distance OE. By the equalities just proved, its circumference passes through E, F, G, and H. [P3, D1.15]

**Step 5.** Extend each radius through O to form its diameter. The line AB is perpendicular at E to the diameter through E, so it falls outside the circle by 3.16; since it meets the circle at E, it touches it there. The same argument shows that BC, CD, and DA touch the circle at F, G, and H respectively. The circle therefore touches every side of the square, at points on those sides, and is inscribed in ABCD. Thus a circle has been inscribed in the given square, as required. [P2, 3.16, D3.2, D4.5]
