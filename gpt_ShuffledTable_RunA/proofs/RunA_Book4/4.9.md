## Proposition 4.9
**Statement:** About a given square to circumscribe a circle.

**Step 1.** Let ABCD be the given square, with its vertices in order. Its four sides are equal and its four angles are right angles. Join AC and BD, and let their intersection be E. [D1.22, P1]

**Step 2.** Use side–angle–side congruence (1.4), since the equal sides and right angles of the square supply its hypotheses directly. All right angles are equal. Comparing the following pairs of triangles therefore gives:
- ABC and BAD: AB = BA, BC = AD, and ∠ABC = ∠BAD; hence ∠BAC = ∠ABD.
- BCD and CBA: BC = CB, CD = BA, and ∠BCD = ∠CBA; hence ∠CBD = ∠BCA.
- CDA and DCB: CD = DC, DA = CB, and ∠CDA = ∠DCB; hence ∠DCA = ∠CDB. [D1.22, P4, 1.4]

**Step 3.** Since E lies on both diagonals, these angle equalities give, respectively,
∠BAE = ∠ABE, ∠CBE = ∠BCE, and ∠DCE = ∠CDE.
In each triangle, the sides opposite equal angles are equal. Thus EA = EB in triangle ABE, EB = EC in triangle BCE, and EC = ED in triangle CDE. Consequently EA = EB = EC = ED. [1.6, CN1]

**Step 4.** Describe the circle with centre E and radius EA. Because EB, EC, and ED are also equal to this radius, its circumference passes through A, B, C, and D. It is therefore circumscribed about the square ABCD, as required. [P3, D1.15, D4.6]
