## Proposition 4.1
**Statement:** Into a given circle to fit a straight line equal to a given straight line which is not greater than the diameter of the circle.

**Step 1.** Let Γ be the given circle and let ℓ be the given straight line, not greater than its diameter. Find the centre O of Γ. Choose A on its circumference, join AO, and produce AO to meet the circumference again at B. Thus AB is a diameter. [3.1, P1, P2, D1.17]

**Step 2.** If ℓ equals AB, then AB itself is the required straight line: its extremities lie on Γ. Otherwise ℓ is less than AB; cut off AD from AB, with D between A and B, so that AD equals ℓ. [D4.7, 1.3]

**Step 3.** Describe an auxiliary circle with centre A and radius AD. This endpoint-centred construction gives the required length directly as a radius. A is inside the auxiliary circle, while B is outside it because AB is greater than AD; hence the semicircumference of Γ from A to B crosses the auxiliary circumference at a point C. [P3, D1.15]

**Step 4.** Join AC. Since C and D lie on the auxiliary circle with centre A, AC equals AD. But AD equals ℓ; therefore AC equals ℓ. [P1, D1.15, CN1]

**Step 5.** Both A and C lie on the circumference of Γ, so AC is fitted into Γ. Thus in either case a straight line equal to the given straight line has been fitted into the given circle, as required. [D4.7]
