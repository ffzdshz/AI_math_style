## Proposition 4.16
**Statement:** In a given circle to inscribe a fifteen-angled figure which shall be both equilateral and equiangular.

**Step 1.** Construct an equilateral triangle by I.1. Its angles are equal by I.5. In the given circle inscribe a triangle equiangular with this triangle by IV.2; its sides are equal by I.6. Call one vertex A, and let C be the next vertex in a chosen direction around the circle. [1.1, 1.5, 4.2, 1.6]

**Step 2.** Inscribe an equilateral and equiangular pentagon in the same circle, taking A as one vertex, and let B be its next vertex in the same direction. Equal chords cut off equal corresponding arcs; hence the consecutive arcs of the triangle are thirds of the circumference, and those of the pentagon are fifths. Thus, writing c for the whole circumference, arc AC is c/3 and arc AB is c/5, and B lies between A and C on this arc. [4.11, 3.28]

**Step 3.** Subtracting arc AB from arc AC gives
arc BC = c/3 − c/5 = 2c/15.
Bisect arc BC at D. Each of arcs BD and DC is therefore c/15. This is the direct triangle–pentagon route: subtract their successive arcs and bisect the remainder to obtain the required fifteenth of the circumference. [CN3, 3.30]

**Step 4.** Join BD. As a chord of the given circle, BD is not greater than its diameter. Starting with V₀ = B and V₁ = D, repeatedly fit a chord equal to BD from the latest vertex, proceeding in the same direction around the circle. This fitting is possible by IV.1. By III.28 every corresponding minor arc is equal to arc BD, hence is c/15. Consequently fifteen such arcs make the whole circumference: the fifteenth chord ends at V₀, while the preceding fourteen endpoints are distinct. Join successive vertices to obtain the inscribed fifteen-sided figure V₀V₁…V₁₄. [P1, 3.15, 4.1, 3.28, CN2]

**Step 5.** Every side of this figure was constructed equal to BD. Its fifteen sides are therefore equal to one another, so the figure is equilateral. [4.1, CN1]

**Step 6.** At any vertex Vᵢ, the interior angle stands on the arc joining its two neighbouring vertices and not containing Vᵢ. This arc consists of thirteen of the equal successive arcs. All fifteen such intercepted arcs are therefore equal. By III.27, the angles standing on them are equal. This proposition applies directly because the angles stand on equal arcs, rather than necessarily in the same segment. Hence the figure is equiangular as well as equilateral. [CN2, 3.27]

Therefore an equilateral and equiangular fifteen-angled figure has been inscribed in the given circle, as required.
