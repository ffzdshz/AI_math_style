## Proposition 5.8
**Statement:** Of unequal magnitudes, the greater has to the same a greater ratio than the less has; and the same has to the less a greater ratio than it has to the greater.

**Step 1.** Let A and B be unequal magnitudes, with A greater than B, and let C be the common magnitude to which they are compared. Write A = B + D, where D is the positive excess. For a positive integer r, write rX for r copies of X. Repeated addition gives rA = rB + rD. We take the direct route through the definition of greater ratio: a single strict separation of multiples will establish both assertions without any theorem about inversion. [D5.2, CN2, D5.7]

**Step 2.** Choose a positive integer m such that both mD > C and mB > C. Such a choice is possible because some multiple of D exceeds C and some multiple of B exceeds C; taking the larger of the two multipliers preserves both inequalities, since further copies only enlarge a magnitude. [D5.4, CN5]

**Step 3.** Some multiple of C exceeds mB. In the finite succession C, 2C, and so on up to such a multiple, let nC be the first that exceeds mB. Since C < mB, we have n ≥ 2, and the preceding multiple satisfies (n−1)C ≤ mB. Adding C therefore gives
nC ≤ mB + C.
But C < mD, so
mB + C < mB + mD = mA.
Consequently,
mB < nC < mA.
Here addition preserves the inequalities because the larger whole contains the smaller whole together with a positive excess. [D5.4, D5.2, CN2, CN5]

**Step 4.** Compare A:C with B:C. Take the equimultiples mA and mB of the antecedents, and nC and nC of the consequents. By Step 3, mA exceeds nC, whereas mB does not exceed nC. Thus A has to C a greater ratio than B has to C. [D5.7]

**Step 5.** Compare C:B with C:A. Take the equimultiples nC and nC of the antecedents, and mB and mA of the consequents. By Step 3, nC exceeds mB, whereas nC does not exceed mA. Thus C has to B a greater ratio than C has to A. Therefore, of unequal magnitudes, the greater has to the same a greater ratio than the less has, and the same has to the less a greater ratio than it has to the greater. [D5.7]
