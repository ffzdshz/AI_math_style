## Proposition 5.7
**Statement:** Equal magnitudes have to the same the same ratio, as also has the same to equal magnitudes.

**Step 1.** Let A and B be equal magnitudes, and let C be a magnitude of the same kind. We use the equimultiple definition of equal ratios directly for both assertions, rather than deducing the second by inversion. [D5.5]

**Step 2.** Let m and n be arbitrary positive integers, with mX denoting m copies of a magnitude X added together. Since A = B, repeated addition of equals to equals gives mA = mB and nA = nB. [D5.2, CN2]

**Step 3.** Because mA = mB, these magnitudes alike exceed, are alike equal to, or alike fall short of nC. Thus arbitrary equimultiples mA and mB of A and B have matching comparisons with the corresponding equimultiples nC and nC of C and C. By the definition of equal ratios, A:C = B:C. [D5.5]

**Step 4.** Likewise, because nA = nB, the magnitude mC exceeds both, equals both, or falls short of both. Thus arbitrary equimultiples mC and mC of C and C have matching comparisons with the corresponding equimultiples nA and nB of A and B. By the same definition, C:A = C:B. Therefore equal magnitudes have to the same magnitude the same ratio, and the same magnitude has to equal magnitudes the same ratio. [D5.5]
