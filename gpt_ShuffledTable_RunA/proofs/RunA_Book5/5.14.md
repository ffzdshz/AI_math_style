## Proposition 5.14
**Statement:** If a first magnitude have to a second the same ratio as a third has to a fourth, and the first be greater than the third, the second will also be greater than the fourth; if equal, equal; and if less, less.

**Step 1.** Let A, B, C, D be magnitudes such that A:B = C:D. We choose the direct route of comparing ratios with a common first magnitude: the second clauses of 5.10 and 5.9 then compare their second magnitudes.

**Step 2.** Suppose A > C. By 5.8, A:B > C:B. Since C:D = A:B, proposition 5.13 gives C:D > C:B. Thus the same magnitude C has a greater ratio to D than to B; by the second clause of 5.10, D < B. Hence B > D. [5.8, 5.13, 5.10]

**Step 3.** Suppose A = C. By 5.7, A:B = C:B. Together with the hypothesis A:B = C:D, proposition 5.11 gives C:B = C:D. Since the same magnitude C has the same ratio to B and D, the second clause of 5.9 gives B = D. [5.7, 5.11, 5.9]

**Step 4.** Suppose A < C. By 5.8, C:D > A:D. Since A:B = C:D, proposition 5.13 gives A:B > A:D. Thus the same magnitude A has a greater ratio to B than to D; by the second clause of 5.10, B < D. [5.8, 5.13, 5.10]

**Step 5.** Therefore, when A:B = C:D, if A is greater than C, B is greater than D; if A equals C, B equals D; and if A is less than C, B is less than D, as required.
