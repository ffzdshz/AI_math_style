## Proposition 5.22
**Statement:** If there be any number of magnitudes whatever, and others equal to them in multitude, which taken two and two together are in the same ratio, they will also be in the same ratio ex aequali.

**Step 1.** Let A₁,…,Aₙ and B₁,…,Bₙ be two lists of magnitudes such that Aᵢ:Aᵢ₊₁ = Bᵢ:Bᵢ₊₁ for every successive pair. We must show A₁:Aₙ = B₁:Bₙ, which is precisely the ratio ex aequali. [D5.17]

**Step 2.** First take three magnitudes in each list, with A₁:A₂ = B₁:B₂ and A₂:A₃ = B₂:B₃. Let r and s be arbitrary positive integers. Applying 5.4 to each given proportion gives
rA₁:sA₂ = rB₁:sB₂,
and
sA₂:sA₃ = sB₂:sB₃. [5.4]

**Step 3.** Apply 5.20 to the triples rA₁, sA₂, sA₃ and rB₁, sB₂, sB₃. This ordered comparison is the direct route because the two proportions retain corresponding order. If rA₁ is greater than sA₃, then rB₁ is greater than sB₃; if equal, equal; and if less, less. [5.20]

**Step 4.** Since r and s were arbitrary, every pair of equimultiples of A₁ and B₁ alike exceeds, equals, or falls short of the corresponding equimultiples of A₃ and B₃. Therefore A₁:A₃ = B₁:B₃ by the definition of equal ratios. This establishes the three-term case. [D5.5]

**Step 5.** For any finite number of terms, repeat this same three-term argument. The equality A₁:A₂ = B₁:B₂ is given. Whenever A₁:Aₖ = B₁:Bₖ has been established and k < n, combine it with the given equality Aₖ:Aₖ₊₁ = Bₖ:Bₖ₊₁. Steps 2–4, applied to A₁, Aₖ, Aₖ₊₁ and B₁, Bₖ, Bₖ₊₁, give A₁:Aₖ₊₁ = B₁:Bₖ₊₁. Thus successive removal of the intermediate terms yields A₁:Aₙ = B₁:Bₙ. Hence the magnitudes are in the same ratio ex aequali, as required. [5.4, 5.20, D5.5, D5.17]
