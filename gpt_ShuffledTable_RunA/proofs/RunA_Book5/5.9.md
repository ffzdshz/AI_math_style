## Proposition 5.9
**Statement:** Magnitudes which have the same ratio to the same are equal to one another; and magnitudes to which the same has the same ratio are equal.

**Step 1.** We use Proposition 5.8 directly, since it compares exactly the ratios arising from unequal magnitudes. A greater ratio cannot also be the same ratio: Definition D5.7 supplies equimultiples for which one corresponding multiple exceeds the other while the other pair does not, whereas Definition D5.5 requires the comparisons to agree for every choice of equimultiples. [5.8, D5.5, D5.7]

**Step 2.** Let A and B have the same ratio to C. Suppose A and B are unequal; interchanging their names if necessary, let A be greater than B. By the first assertion of Proposition 5.8, A has to C a greater ratio than B has to C. This contradicts their having the same ratio, by Step 1. Hence A equals B. Thus magnitudes which have the same ratio to the same are equal to one another. [5.8, D5.5, D5.7]

**Step 3.** Let C have the same ratio to A as it has to B. Suppose A and B are unequal; again let A be the greater. By the second assertion of Proposition 5.8, C has to B a greater ratio than it has to A. This contradicts the assumed sameness of those ratios, by Step 1. Hence A equals B. Thus magnitudes to which the same has the same ratio are equal, completing both assertions. [5.8, D5.5, D5.7]
