## Proposition 5.16
**Statement:** If four magnitudes be proportional, they will also be proportional alternately.

**Step 1.** Let A, B, C, D be the given magnitudes, with A:B = C:D. By the definition of alternate ratio, we must prove A:C = B:D. [D5.12]

**Step 2.** We take the direct route through the equimultiple criterion for equality of ratios. Let E, F be arbitrary equimultiples of A, B respectively, and let G, H be arbitrary equimultiples of C, D respectively, with the second multiplier chosen independently of the first. By 5.15, A:B = E:F and C:D = G:H. [D5.5, 5.15]

**Step 3.** Combining these equalities with the hypothesis A:B = C:D, we obtain E:F = G:H, by applying the equality of ratios having the same ratio. [5.11]

**Step 4.** Apply 5.14 directly, since it transfers precisely the three comparisons needed: if E exceeds G, then F exceeds H; if E equals G, then F equals H; and if E falls short of G, then F falls short of H. [5.14]

**Step 5.** Here E, F are arbitrary equimultiples of the first and third magnitudes in the ordered list A, C, B, D, while G, H are arbitrary equimultiples of its second and fourth magnitudes. Step 4 therefore establishes the defining criterion for A:C = B:D. Thus the four magnitudes are proportional alternately, as required. [D5.5, D5.12]
