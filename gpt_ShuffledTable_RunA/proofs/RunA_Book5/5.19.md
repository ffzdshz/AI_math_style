## Proposition 5.19
**Statement:** If, as a whole is to a whole, so is a part subtracted to a part subtracted, the remainder will also be to the remainder as whole to whole.

**Step 1.** Let the wholes be A and B, the subtracted parts C and D, and the remainders R and S, so that A=C+R and B=D+S. The hypothesis is A:B=C:D. Use alternation first, since it puts each whole in ratio to its own part and thus makes separation applicable. By Proposition 5.16,
A:C=B:D.

**Step 2.** Substituting the decompositions of the wholes gives
(C+R):C=(D+S):D.
By separation, Proposition 5.17,
R:C=S:D.

**Step 3.** Applying alternation again, by Proposition 5.16,
R:S=C:D.

**Step 4.** Both R:S and A:B are therefore equal to the ratio C:D. By Proposition 5.11,
R:S=A:B.
Thus the remainder is to the remainder as the whole is to the whole, as required.
