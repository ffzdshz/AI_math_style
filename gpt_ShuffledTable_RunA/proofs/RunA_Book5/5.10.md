## Proposition 5.10
**Statement:** Of magnitudes which have a ratio to the same, that which has a greater ratio is greater; and that to which the same has a greater ratio is less.

**Step 1.** We use the defining equimultiple criterion directly. Suppose A has to C a greater ratio than B has to C. By D5.7, there are positive whole numbers m and n such that mA exceeds nC, while mB does not exceed nC. Consequently mA exceeds mB. Here mX denotes m copies of the magnitude X. [D5.7]

**Step 2.** If A equalled B, their m-fold multiples would be equal by repeated addition of equals, contradicting Step 1. If A were less than B, each copy of A could be taken as a proper part of the corresponding copy of B; thus mA would be a proper part of mB, and would be less than it, again contradicting Step 1. Therefore A is greater than B. [CN2, CN5]

**Step 3.** Now suppose C has to A a greater ratio than it has to B. By D5.7, there are positive whole numbers m and n such that mC exceeds nA, while mC does not exceed nB. Consequently nA is less than nB. [D5.7]

**Step 4.** If A equalled B, their n-fold multiples would be equal by repeated addition of equals, contradicting Step 3. If A were greater than B, each copy of B could be taken as a proper part of the corresponding copy of A; thus nB would be a proper part of nA and would be less than it, again contradicting Step 3. Therefore A is less than B. Hence, of magnitudes having a ratio to the same magnitude, that which has the greater ratio is greater; and that to which the same magnitude has the greater ratio is less. [CN2, CN5]
