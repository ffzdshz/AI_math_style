## Proposition 5.12
**Statement:** If any number of magnitudes be proportional, as one of the antecedents is to one of the consequents, so will all the antecedents be to all the consequents.

**Step 1.** Let the proportional magnitudes be A₁, B₁; …; Aᵣ, Bᵣ, and put S = A₁ + ⋯ + Aᵣ and T = B₁ + ⋯ + Bᵣ. Fix any index j. By the meaning of proportionality, Aⱼ:Bⱼ = Aᵢ:Bᵢ for every i. We use the defining equimultiple test directly, rather than an intervening ratio theorem. [D5.6]

**Step 2.** Take arbitrary positive integral multiplicities m and n. The magnitude mS consists of m copies of S, hence of m copies of each Aᵢ; grouping these equal parts gives mS = mA₁ + ⋯ + mAᵣ. Likewise, nT = nB₁ + ⋯ + nBᵣ. These identities follow from the meaning of a multiple and repeated addition of equals. [D5.2, CN2]

**Step 3.** Since Aⱼ:Bⱼ = Aᵢ:Bᵢ, the definition of equal ratios gives, for every i: if mAⱼ exceeds nBⱼ, then mAᵢ exceeds nBᵢ; if mAⱼ equals nBⱼ, then mAᵢ equals nBᵢ; and if mAⱼ falls short of nBⱼ, then mAᵢ falls short of nBᵢ. [D5.5]

**Step 4.** In the equality case, addition of equals yields mS = nT. In the exceeding case, each mAᵢ contains a part equal to nBᵢ together with a positive remainder; therefore mS contains a part equal to nT together with the sum of those remainders, and mS > nT. Reversing the roles gives mS < nT in the falling-short case. Thus mAⱼ and mS alike exceed, equal, or fall short of nBⱼ and nT respectively. [CN2, CN5]

**Step 5.** Since m and n were arbitrary, the defining equimultiple test proves Aⱼ:Bⱼ = S:T. As j was arbitrary, any antecedent is to its corresponding consequent as the sum of all the antecedents is to the sum of all the consequents. This is the required conclusion. [D5.5]
