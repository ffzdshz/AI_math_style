## Proposition 5.2
**Statement:** If a first magnitude be the same multiple of a second that a third is of a fourth, and a fifth also be the same multiple of the second that a sixth is of the fourth, the sum of the first and fifth will also be the same multiple of the second that the sum of the third and sixth is of the fourth.

**Step 1.** Denote the first, second, third, fourth, fifth, and sixth magnitudes by A, B, C, D, E, and F, respectively. We take the direct route of counting measuring parts. Since A is the same multiple of B that C is of D, A consists of some number m of parts each equal to B, while C consists of the same number m of parts each equal to D. [D5.2]

**Step 2.** Similarly, since E is the same multiple of B that F is of D, E consists of some number n of parts each equal to B, while F consists of the same number n of parts each equal to D. The number n need not equal m. [D5.2]

**Step 3.** Add the two groups of measuring parts for A and E, and likewise those for C and F. By addition of equals, A + E equals the combined group of m parts and n parts each equal to B; C + F equals the combined group of m parts and n parts each equal to D. Thus both combined groups contain the same number of measuring parts, namely m + n. [CN2]

**Step 4.** Consequently B measures A + E exactly as many times as D measures C + F. Therefore the sum of the first and fifth is the same multiple of the second that the sum of the third and sixth is of the fourth, as required. [D5.2]
