## Proposition 5.25
**Statement:** If four magnitudes be proportional, the greatest and the least are greater than the remaining two.

**Step 1.** Label the proportional magnitudes so that A:B = C:D, with A the greatest and D the least. Thus A>B and A>C. From A:B = C:D and A>C, Proposition 5.14 gives B>D. Let E and F be the positive remainders defined by A=C+E and B=D+F. [5.14]

**Step 2.** The wholes A,B have the same ratio as the subtracted parts C,D. Hence Proposition 5.19 gives E:F = A:B. This whole-and-remainder route applies directly to the given proportion, without a preliminary alternation. [5.19]

**Step 3.** Alternating A:B = E:F gives A:E = B:F. Since A>B, Proposition 5.14 now gives E>F. [5.16, 5.14]

**Step 4.** Write E=F+G, where G is positive. Adding equals to equals and regrouping gives
A+D = C+E+D = C+F+G+D = B+C+G.
Thus A+D contains B+C as a proper part, and is greater than it. Therefore the greatest and the least together are greater than the remaining two together. [CN2, CN5]
