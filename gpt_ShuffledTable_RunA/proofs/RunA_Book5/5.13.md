## Proposition 5.13
**Statement:** If a first magnitude have to a second the same ratio as a third to a fourth, and the third have to the fourth a greater ratio than a fifth has to a sixth, the first will also have to the second a greater ratio than the fifth to the sixth.

**Step 1.** Let the six magnitudes be A, B, C, D, E, F, with A:B = C:D and C:D > E:F. We take the direct route through the equimultiple definitions, since they allow the comparison witnessing the greater ratio to be transferred. By the definition of greater ratio, there are positive integers m and n such that mC exceeds nD, while mE does not exceed nF. [D5.7]

**Step 2.** Take the same m-fold multiples of A and C and the same n-fold multiples of B and D. Since A:B = C:D, these multiples must alike exceed, alike equal, or alike fall short of their corresponding multiples. Because mC exceeds nD, mA therefore exceeds nB. [D5.5]

**Step 3.** Thus, for these same multipliers, mA exceeds nB while mE does not exceed nF. By the definition of greater ratio, A:B > E:F. Therefore the first magnitude has to the second a greater ratio than the fifth has to the sixth, as required. [D5.7]
