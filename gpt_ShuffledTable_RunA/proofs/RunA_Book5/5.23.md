## Proposition 5.23
**Statement:** If there be three magnitudes, and others equal to them in multitude, which taken two and two together are in the same ratio, and the proportion of them be perturbed, they will also be in the same ratio ex aequali.

**Step 1.** Let the two triples be A, B, C and D, E, F, with the perturbed proportions A:B = E:F and B:C = D:E. We must prove A:C = D:F, the ratio ex aequali. [D5.18; D5.17]

**Step 2.** Choose the direct equimultiple route, retaining the perturbed arrangement so that its comparison theorem applies. Let m and n be arbitrary positive integers. Take the equimultiples mA, mB, mD, and independently the equimultiples nC, nE, nF. By the definition of equal ratios, it suffices to show that mA and mD alike exceed, equal, or fall short of nC and nF respectively. [D5.5]

**Step 3.** Magnitudes have the same ratio as their corresponding equimultiples, so mA:mB = A:B and nE:nF = E:F. Since A:B = E:F, equality of ratios with the same ratio gives mA:mB = nE:nF. [5.15; 5.11]

**Step 4.** From B:C = D:E, taking m-fold multiples of the antecedents and n-fold multiples of the consequents gives mB:nC = mD:nE. [5.4]

**Step 5.** Thus the triples mA, mB, nC and mD, nE, nF are in perturbed proportion. Applying the comparison theorem for perturbed triples: if mA is greater than nC, then mD is greater than nF; if equal, equal; and if less, less. [D5.18; 5.21]

**Step 6.** Since m and n were arbitrary, every pair of equimultiples of A and D has the same respective comparison with every pair of equimultiples of C and F. Therefore A:C = D:F by the definition of equal ratios. Hence the given magnitudes are in the same ratio ex aequali, as required. [D5.5; D5.17]
