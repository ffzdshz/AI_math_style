## Proposition 5.1
**Statement:** If a magnitude be the same multiple of a magnitude that a part subtracted is of a part subtracted, the remainder will also be the same multiple of the remainder that the whole is of the whole.

**Step 1.** Let A and B be the whole magnitudes, and let C and D be the parts subtracted from them respectively. Put R=A−C and S=B−D. By hypothesis and the definition of a multiple, there is one and the same positive whole number n such that A consists of n copies of B and C consists of n copies of D. We use these equal numbers of copies directly, without introducing ratios. [D5.2]

**Step 2.** Each copy of B consists of a copy of D together with a remainder equal to S: subtracting equal copies of D from equal copies of B leaves equal remainders. Thus, in the collection of n copies of B, separate off one copy of D from each. The removed portions together equal C, and the remaining portions together equal a magnitude T consisting of n copies of S, by repeated addition of equals. [CN3, CN2]

**Step 3.** The original collection equals A, and its removed portions equal C. Subtracting these equal removed magnitudes from the equal original magnitudes therefore gives T=A−C=R. [CN3]

**Step 4.** Since T consists of n copies of S and R=T, the remainder R is the same multiple of the remainder S that A is of B. Hence the remainder is the same multiple of the remainder that the whole is of the whole, as required. [D5.2]
