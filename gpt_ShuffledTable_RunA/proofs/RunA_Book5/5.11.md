## Proposition 5.11
**Statement:** Ratios which are the same with the same ratio are also the same with one another.

**Step 1.** Let A:B and C:D each be the same ratio as E:F. We use the defining equimultiple criterion directly, since it gives precisely the condition to be established, without an auxiliary theorem. Choose arbitrary positive integers m and n, taking m-fold multiples of A, C, E and n-fold multiples of B, D, F. [D5.5]

**Step 2.** Since A:B is the same ratio as E:F, the multiples mA and mE alike exceed, are alike equal to, or alike fall short of nB and nF respectively. Thus the comparison of mA with nB agrees with the comparison of mE with nF. [D5.5]

**Step 3.** Since C:D is also the same ratio as E:F, the comparison of mC with nD likewise agrees with the comparison of mE with nF. Consequently, if mA exceeds nB, then mC exceeds nD; if mA equals nB, then mC equals nD; and if mA falls short of nB, then mC falls short of nD. [D5.5]

**Step 4.** Since m and n were arbitrary, equimultiples of A and C always alike exceed, equal, or fall short of the corresponding equimultiples of B and D. Therefore A:B is the same ratio as C:D. Hence ratios which are the same with the same ratio are also the same with one another. [D5.5]
