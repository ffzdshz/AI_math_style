## Proposition 5.17
**Statement:** If magnitudes be proportional componendo, they will also be proportional separando.

**Step 1.** Let X = A + B and Y = C + D, and suppose X : B = Y : D. By the definitions of composition and separation, we must prove A : B = C : D. We take the direct route through the defining equimultiple criterion. [D5.14, D5.15, D5.5]

**Step 2.** Let m and n be arbitrary positive integers. Take the equimultiples mX, mY, and subtract their respective parts mB, mD. The remainders are mA and mC: the whole and the subtracted part are respectively the same multiples of X and B, and of Y and D. Here we use 5.1, the earliest applicable subtraction-of-multiples result in the table. [5.1]

**Step 3.** The sums mB + nB and mD + nD are equimultiples of B and D. Since X : B = Y : D, the equimultiples mX and mY therefore alike exceed, are alike equal to, or alike fall short of mB + nB and mD + nD, respectively. [5.2, D5.5]

**Step 4.** In the first comparison subtract mB from both magnitudes; in the second subtract mD from both magnitudes. Subtracting a common part preserves equality and the direction of inequality: equal remainders follow by subtraction of equals, while in an unequal comparison the excess remains, so the larger remainder is a whole containing the smaller as a part. Consequently mA and mC alike exceed, are alike equal to, or alike fall short of nB and nD, respectively. [CN3, CN5]

**Step 5.** Since m and n were arbitrary, the defining criterion for equality of ratios gives A : B = C : D. Thus magnitudes proportional componendo are also proportional separando. [D5.5, D5.15]
