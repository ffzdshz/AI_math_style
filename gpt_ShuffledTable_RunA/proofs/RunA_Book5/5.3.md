## Proposition 5.3
**Statement:** If a first magnitude be the same multiple of a second that a third is of a fourth, and if equimultiples be taken of the first and third, then also ex aequali the magnitudes taken will be equimultiples respectively, the one of the second and the other of the fourth.

**Step 1.** Let A be the same multiple of B that C is of D, and let E and F be equimultiples of A and C respectively. By the meaning of multiple, E consists of some number n of successive copies of A, and F consists of the same number n of successive copies of C. Denote these copies by A₁, …, Aₙ and C₁, …, Cₙ. [D5.2]

**Step 2.** Use successive addition, since the given equimultiples consist of equally many corresponding copies. The first pair A₁, C₁ are the same multiples of B, D respectively, by hypothesis. Whenever the sums A₁ + ⋯ + Aₖ and C₁ + ⋯ + Cₖ are the same multiples of B and D respectively, the next pair Aₖ₊₁, Cₖ₊₁ are also the same multiples of B and D respectively. Proposition V.2 therefore shows that the enlarged sums A₁ + ⋯ + Aₖ₊₁ and C₁ + ⋯ + Cₖ₊₁ are the same multiples of B and D respectively. [5.2]

**Step 3.** Repeat this addition until all n copies have been included. The resulting sums are E and F; hence E is the same multiple of B that F is of D. Thus, ex aequali, the magnitudes taken are equimultiples respectively of the second and fourth magnitudes, as required. [5.2, D5.2]
