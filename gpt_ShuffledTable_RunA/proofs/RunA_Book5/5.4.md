## Proposition 5.4
**Statement:** If a first magnitude have to a second the same ratio as a third to a fourth, any equimultiples whatever of the first and third will also have the same ratio to any equimultiples whatever of the second and fourth respectively, taken in corresponding order.

**Step 1.** Let A have to B the same ratio as C has to D. Let E and G be any equimultiples of A and C respectively, and F and H any equimultiples of B and D respectively. We use the direct defining test for equality of ratios: test arbitrary equimultiples of E, G against arbitrary equimultiples of F, H. This is the natural route because the hypothesis supplies precisely such comparisons for multiples of A, C and B, D. [D5.5]

**Step 2.** Take arbitrary equimultiples K and M of E and G respectively, and arbitrary equimultiples L and N of F and H respectively. Since E and G are equimultiples of A and C, Proposition 5.3 shows that K and M are equimultiples of A and C. Likewise, since F and H are equimultiples of B and D, the same proposition shows that L and N are equimultiples of B and D. [5.3]

**Step 3.** Since A has to B the same ratio as C has to D, these equimultiples K and M alike exceed, are alike equal to, or alike fall short of L and N respectively. Thus K exceeds L exactly when M exceeds N, K equals L exactly when M equals N, and K falls short of L exactly when M falls short of N. [D5.5]

**Step 4.** The equimultiples K, M and L, N were arbitrary. Therefore E has to F the same ratio as G has to H. Since E, G and F, H were themselves any equimultiples of the original corresponding magnitudes, the required proposition follows. [D5.5]
