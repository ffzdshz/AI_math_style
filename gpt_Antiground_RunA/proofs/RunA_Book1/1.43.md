## Proposition 1.43
**Statement:** In any parallelogram the complements of the parallelograms about the diameter are equal to one another.

**Step 1.** Let ABCD be the given parallelogram with diameter AC. Label the given subdivision so that K lies on AC; E, F, G, H lie respectively on AB, BC, CD, DA; and E, K, G and H, K, F are respectively collinear. The parallelograms about the diameter are AEKH and KFCG; their complements are EBFK and HKGD. Write [X] for the area of figure X. We choose the diameter-bisection clause of **1.34**, since it applies directly to the given parallelogram and both parallelograms about its diameter. Applied to ABCD, it gives
[ABC] = [ADC].

**Step 2.** Since AK is a diameter of parallelogram AEKH, and KC is a diameter of parallelogram KFCG, their respective triangular halves are equal:
[AEK] = [AKH], and [KFC] = [KCG]. (**1.34**)

**Step 3.** Adding these equalities gives
[AEK] + [KFC] = [AKH] + [KCG]. (**CN2**)

**Step 4.** In triangle ABC, removing triangles AEK and KFC leaves exactly the complement EBFK. In triangle ADC, removing triangles AKH and KCG leaves exactly the complement HKGD. Therefore, subtracting the equal sums of Step 3 from the equal areas of Step 1 gives
[EBFK] = [HKGD]. (**CN3**)
Thus the complements of the parallelograms about the diameter are equal to one another.
