## Proposition 1.18
**Statement:** In any triangle the greater side subtends the greater angle.

**Step 1.** Let ABC be any triangle with AC > AB. We must show that ∠ABC > ∠BCA. Cut off AD from AC equal to AB, with D strictly between A and C [1.3]. Join BD [P1].

**Step 2.** In triangle ABD, AB = AD by construction. Therefore its base angles are equal: ∠ABD = ∠BDA [1.5].

**Step 3.** In triangle BDC, the side CD is produced through D to A. Thus ∠BDA is an exterior angle, and ∠BDA > ∠BCD [1.16]. This is the direct path choice: the constructed exterior angle makes 1.16 immediately applicable. Since D lies on CA, the rays CD and CA coincide, so ∠BCD = ∠BCA [CN4].

**Step 4.** Since D lies strictly between A and C, BD divides ∠ABC, and ∠ABD is a proper part of ∠ABC. Hence ∠ABC > ∠ABD [CN5].

**Step 5.** Combining Steps 2–4 gives ∠ABC > ∠ABD = ∠BDA > ∠BCD = ∠BCA. Thus the angle opposite the greater side AC is greater than the angle opposite the lesser side AB. Since the triangle and the unequal sides were arbitrary, in any triangle the greater side subtends the greater angle.
