## Proposition 1.30
**Statement:** Straight lines parallel to the same straight line are also parallel to one another.

**Step 1.** Let a and b be distinct straight lines in the same plane, each parallel to the straight line c. We must prove that a is parallel to b. (Hypothesis.)

**Step 2.** Draw a straight transversal t cutting a, b, and c at distinct points A, B, and C, and produce it beyond these intersections. At each intersection, select the angle between the ray of t pointing in one fixed direction and the ray of the intersected line lying on one fixed side of t. Denote these corresponding angles by α, β, and γ respectively. [P1, P2]

**Step 3.** Since a is parallel to c, the corresponding angles α and γ are equal. Likewise, since b is parallel to c, the corresponding angles β and γ are equal. In each application, these are the exterior angle and the interior opposite angle on the same side of the transversal. [1.29]

**Step 4.** Therefore α = β, since each equals γ. [CN1]

**Step 5.** Thus t makes an exterior angle equal to the interior opposite angle on the same side when it cuts a and b. Consequently, a is parallel to b. The corresponding-angle criterion is the direct path here: the angles already obtained have precisely the configuration required by its first clause, so no conversion to alternate angles is needed. Hence straight lines parallel to the same straight line are parallel to one another. [1.28]
