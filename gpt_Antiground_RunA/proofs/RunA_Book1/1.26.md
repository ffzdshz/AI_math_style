## Proposition 1.26
**Statement:** If two triangles have the two angles equal to two angles respectively, and one side equal to one side, namely, either the side adjoining the equal angles, or that subtending one of the equal angles, they will also have the remaining sides equal tothe remaining sides and the remaining angle to the remaining angle.

**Step 1.** Let triangles ABC and DEF satisfy ∠ABC = ∠DEF and ∠ACB = ∠DFE. The given equal sides are either BC = EF, adjoining the equal angles, or a corresponding pair opposite one of them. In the latter case, by interchanging B with C and E with F if necessary, we may take AB = DE. These exhaust the hypotheses. Path choice: in each case establish a second pair of equal sides, then apply 1.4, which directly uses a given included angle. [Hypotheses; 1.4.]

**Step 2.** First suppose BC = EF. If AB and DE are unequal, interchange the triangles if necessary so that AB > DE. Cut off BG = DE from BA, with G strictly between B and A, and join GC. [1.3; P1.]

**Step 3.** Since BG and BA have the same direction from B, ∠GBC = ∠ABC = ∠DEF. Thus triangles GBC and DEF have BG = DE, BC = EF, and their included angles equal. Therefore ∠GCB = ∠DFE by 1.4. Together with the given equality ∠DFE = ∠ACB, this gives ∠GCB = ∠ACB. [CN4; hypotheses; 1.4; CN1.]

**Step 4.** But G lies strictly between A and B, so CG lies inside ∠ACB: ∠GCB is a proper part of ∠ACB. Hence ∠ACB > ∠GCB, contradicting Step 3. Consequently AB = DE; the opposite inequality is excluded by the same argument with the triangles interchanged. Applying 1.4 to ABC and DEF, using AB = DE, BC = EF, and ∠ABC = ∠DEF, now gives AC = DF and ∠BAC = ∠EDF. This completes the case of the adjoining side. [CN5; Step 3; 1.4.]

**Step 5.** Now suppose AB = DE, the sides opposite the given equal angles at C and F. If BC and EF are unequal, interchange the triangles if necessary so that BC > EF. Cut off BH = EF from BC, with H strictly between B and C, and join AH. [Hypotheses; 1.3; P1.]

**Step 6.** Since BH and BC have the same direction from B, ∠ABH = ∠ABC = ∠DEF. Thus triangles ABH and DEF have AB = DE, BH = EF, and their included angles equal. By 1.4, ∠AHB = ∠DFE; hence ∠AHB = ∠ACB by the given equality at C and F. [CN4; hypotheses; 1.4; CN1.]

**Step 7.** In triangle AHC, the side CH is extended beyond H to B. Therefore its exterior angle ∠AHB is greater than its interior opposite angle ∠ACH. Since CH and CB have the same direction from C, ∠ACH = ∠ACB. Thus ∠AHB > ∠ACB, contradicting Step 6. Consequently BC = EF; again, the opposite inequality is excluded by interchanging the triangles. [1.16; CN4; Step 6.]

**Step 8.** With AB = DE, BC = EF, and ∠ABC = ∠DEF, proposition 1.4 gives AC = DF and ∠BAC = ∠EDF. Therefore, whether the given equal sides adjoin the equal angles or subtend corresponding equal angles, the remaining sides are respectively equal and the remaining angles are equal, as required. [1.4; Steps 4 and 7.]
