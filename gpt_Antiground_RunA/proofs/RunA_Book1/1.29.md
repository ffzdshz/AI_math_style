## Proposition 1.29
**Statement:** A straight line falling on parallel straight lines makes the alternate angles equal to one another, the exterior angle equal to the interior and opposite angle, and the interior angles on the same side equal to two right angles.

**Step 1.** Let the parallel straight lines AB and CD be cut by a transversal at G and H. Label points so that A,G,B; C,H,D; and E,G,H,F occur in those orders, with A and C on one side of the transversal and B and D on the other. Since AB and CD are parallel, they do not meet when produced indefinitely in either direction. [D1.23]

**Step 2.** Write 2R for two right angles. The adjacent-angle theorem gives
∠AGH + ∠BGH = 2R,
∠CHG + ∠GHD = 2R.
The two right-angle totals are equal because all right angles are equal. [1.13, P4]
Path choice: use these adjacent-angle equalities directly with P5, since they supply precisely its two-right-angle threshold without any auxiliary construction.

**Step 3.** Suppose ∠AGH > ∠GHD. Adding the common angle ∠BGH preserves the positive excess, so
∠GHD + ∠BGH < ∠AGH + ∠BGH = 2R. [Step 2; CN2, CN5]
Thus the interior angles on the side containing B and D are less than two right angles. By P5, AB and CD, produced indefinitely, would meet on that side, contrary to their being parallel. Hence ∠AGH cannot exceed ∠GHD. [P5, D1.23]

**Step 4.** Suppose instead ∠AGH < ∠GHD. Adding the common angle ∠CHG gives
∠AGH + ∠CHG < ∠GHD + ∠CHG = 2R. [Step 2; CN2, CN5]
Now P5 would make AB and CD meet on the side containing A and C, again contradicting parallelism. Neither strict inequality is possible; therefore
∠AGH = ∠GHD. [P5, D1.23; Step 3]

**Step 5.** The two sums in Step 2 are equal. Subtracting the equal angles ∠AGH and ∠GHD established in Step 4 leaves
∠BGH = ∠CHG.
Thus both pairs of alternate angles are equal. [Steps 2 and 4; CN1, CN3]

**Step 6.** Vertical angles are equal. Combining their equalities with Steps 4–5 gives
∠EGB = ∠AGH = ∠GHD,
∠EGA = ∠BGH = ∠CHG,
∠FHD = ∠CHG = ∠BGH,
∠FHC = ∠GHD = ∠AGH.
Consequently, each exterior angle equals the interior and opposite angle at the other intersection on the same side of the transversal. [1.15, CN1; Steps 4–5]

**Step 7.** Adding equal angles to the equalities already obtained gives
∠AGH + ∠CHG = ∠AGH + ∠BGH = 2R,
∠BGH + ∠GHD = ∠BGH + ∠AGH = 2R.
These are the interior-angle sums on the two respective sides of the transversal. Hence the interior angles on either same side equal two right angles; together with Steps 4–6, this proves all three assertions. [Steps 2, 4–6; CN2, CN1]
