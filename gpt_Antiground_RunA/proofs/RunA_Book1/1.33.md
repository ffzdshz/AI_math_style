## Proposition 1.33
**Statement:** The straight lines joining equal and parallel straight lines (at the extremities which are) in the same directions (respectively) are themselves also equal and parallel.

**Step 1.** Let AB and CD be equal and parallel straight lines, labeled so that A and C, and B and D, are the corresponding extremities in the same directions. Join AC and BD, and draw the auxiliary straight line BC (P1). We shall prove that AC and BD are equal and parallel.

**Step 2.** Since AB is parallel to CD and BC falls upon them, the alternate angles ∠ABC and ∠DCB are equal (1.29). The stipulated correspondence of extremities places these angles in alternate positions.

**Step 3.** In triangles ABC and DCB, AB = DC by hypothesis, BC = CB because it is the same straight line (CN4), and ∠ABC = ∠DCB by Step 2. Thus the two sides and their included angle are respectively equal. Applying 1.4—the direct side–angle–side route—gives AC = DB and ∠ACB = ∠DBC.

**Step 4.** The equal angles ∠ACB and ∠DBC are alternate angles made by BC falling upon AC and BD. Therefore AC is parallel to BD (1.27). This criterion applies directly to the angle equality already obtained. Together with AC = BD from Step 3, this proves that the straight lines joining the corresponding extremities are themselves equal and parallel.
