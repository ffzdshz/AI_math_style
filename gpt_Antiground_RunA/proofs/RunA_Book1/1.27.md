## Proposition 1.27
**Statement:** If a straight line falling on two straight lines make the alternate angles equal to one another, the straight lines will be parallel to one another.

**Step 1.** Let the transversal meet the two coplanar straight lines AB and CD at E and F respectively. Name points on the lines, producing them if necessary, so that A,E,B and C,F,D occur in these orders, with A and C on one side of EF and B and D on the other. The given equality of alternate angles is ∠AEF = ∠EFD. [Hypothesis; P2]

**Step 2.** Produce both straight lines in both directions. We choose the exterior-angle inequality as the direct route: any alleged meeting forms a triangle in which one of the given equal angles is exterior and the other is interior and opposite. [P2; 1.16]

**Step 3.** Suppose the lines meet at G on the side of EF containing B and D. In triangle EFG, ∠AEF is the exterior angle at E, and ∠EFD is the interior opposite angle at F, since FG and FD are the same ray. Therefore ∠AEF > ∠EFD, contradicting their given equality. Thus the lines cannot meet on this side. [1.16; Step 1]

**Step 4.** Suppose instead that the lines meet at H on the side of EF containing A and C. In triangle EFH, ∠EFD is the exterior angle at F, and ∠AEF is the interior opposite angle at E, since EH and EA are the same ray. Therefore ∠EFD > ∠AEF, again contradicting their given equality. Thus the lines cannot meet on this side either. [1.16; Step 1]

**Step 5.** The two straight lines therefore do not meet in either direction when produced indefinitely. Since they lie in the same plane, they are parallel to one another. [Steps 3–4; D1.23]
