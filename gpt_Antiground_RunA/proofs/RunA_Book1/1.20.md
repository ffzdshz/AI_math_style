## Proposition 1.20
**Statement:** In any triangle two sides taken together in any manner are greater than the remaining one.

**Step 1.** Let ABC be any triangle. We first prove AB + AC > BC, choosing an isosceles extension to obtain an angle comparison. Produce BA beyond A to E with AE > AC (P2), and cut off AD = AC from AE (1.3). Thus B, A, D lie in that order on one straight line. Join CD (P1).

**Step 2.** Since AD = AC, the base angles of triangle ACD are equal: ∠ACD = ∠CDA (1.5). Since the rays DA and DB coincide, ∠CDA = ∠CDB (CN4).

**Step 3.** Because A lies between B and D, ray CA lies inside ∠BCD. Therefore ∠ACD is a proper part of ∠BCD, so ∠BCD > ∠ACD (CN5). Substituting the equal angles from Step 2 gives ∠BCD > ∠CDB.

**Step 4.** In triangle BCD, the greater angle ∠BCD is subtended by BD, while ∠CDB is subtended by BC. Consequently BD > BC (1.19). This angle-to-side proposition applies directly to the comparison obtained in Step 3.

**Step 5.** Since A lies between B and D, BD is BA and AD taken together. Adding BA to the equality AD = AC gives BA + AD = BA + AC (CN2). Thus BD = BA + AC, and Step 4 yields AB + AC > BC.

**Step 6.** Apply Steps 1–5 with (A, B, C) replaced first by (B, C, A) and then by (C, A, B). These give BA + BC > AC and CA + CB > AB, respectively. Together with Step 5, these establish that any two sides of the triangle taken together are greater than the remaining side.
