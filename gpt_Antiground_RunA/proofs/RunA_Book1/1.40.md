## Proposition 1.40
**Statement:** Equal triangles which are on equal bases and on the same side are also in the same parallels.

**Step 1.** Let the equal triangles ABC and DEF stand on equal bases BC and EF lying on the same straight line ℓ, with their vertices A and D on the same side of ℓ. Thus BC = EF and triangle ABC equals triangle DEF. [Hypotheses]

**Step 2.** Through A draw a straight line m parallel to ℓ, and join AE and AF. [1.31, P1] If A = D, the original triangles already lie between the same parallels ℓ and m. Otherwise, proceed with the auxiliary triangle AEF; the chosen route is to reduce the problem to the same-base converse, 1.39.

**Step 3.** Triangles ABC and AEF stand on the equal bases BC and EF and lie between the same parallels ℓ and m. Therefore triangle ABC equals triangle AEF. [1.38] This is the direct equal-base theorem; the same-base theorem 1.37 is not needed.

**Step 4.** Triangle AEF and triangle DEF are each equal to triangle ABC. Hence triangle AEF equals triangle DEF. [Step 1, Step 3, CN1]

**Step 5.** The equal triangles AEF and DEF stand on the same base EF and on the same side of it. Therefore the straight line AD is parallel to EF, and hence to its containing straight line ℓ. [1.39] Thus the original triangles ABC and DEF lie between the same parallels ℓ and AD, as required.
