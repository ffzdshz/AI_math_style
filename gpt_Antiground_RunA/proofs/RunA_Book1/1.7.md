## Proposition 1.7
**Statement:** Given two straight lines constructed on a straight line (from its extremities) and meeting in a point, there cannot be constructed on the same straight line (from its extremities), and on the same side of it, two other straight lines meeting in another point and equal to the former two respectively, namely each to that which has the same extremity with it.

**Step 1.** Let AC and BC meet at C on one side of the straight line AB. Suppose, contrary to the proposition, that AD and BD meet at a distinct point D on that same side, with AC = AD and BC = BD. We shall derive a contradiction from these hypotheses.

**Step 2.** The points A, C, D cannot be collinear: since C and D are on the same side of AB, they would lie on the same ray from A, making one of AC and AD a proper part of the other, contrary to their equality (CN5). Likewise, B, C, D cannot be collinear (CN5). Join CD (P1). Thus ACD and BCD are nondegenerate triangles.

**Step 3.** Interchange the names C and D if necessary so that ray AD lies strictly inside angle BAC. This is possible because C and D are on the same side of AB and are not collinear with A (Steps 1–2). There are now two positional cases. If D is on the opposite side of BC from A, the four points have convex boundary order A, C, D, B. If D is on the same side of BC as A, it lies strictly inside triangle ABC. The boundary case D on BC was excluded in Step 2.

**Step 4.** Path choice: apply the isosceles-triangle theorem directly, since the hypotheses already supply the requisite equal sides. From AC = AD and BC = BD, respectively, obtain
α = ∠ACD = ∠CDA,
β = ∠BCD = ∠CDB.
These are precisely the base-angle equalities of 1.5.

**Step 5.** In the first case of Step 3, ray CB lies strictly inside angle ACD, while ray DA lies strictly inside angle CDB. Consequently, ∠ACD > ∠BCD and ∠CDB > ∠CDA, because each whole angle is greater than its proper part (CN5). By the equalities in Step 4, these inequalities say both α > β and β > α, a contradiction. Thus the first case is impossible.

**Step 6.** In the second case, D lies strictly inside triangle ABC. Produce AC beyond C to E, and AD beyond D to F (P2). Applying the exterior-angle clause of 1.5 to triangle ACD gives
γ = ∠ECD = ∠CDF.

**Step 7.** In this interior configuration, ray CB lies strictly inside angle ECD, while ray DF lies strictly inside angle CDB (Steps 3 and 6). Hence ∠ECD > ∠BCD and ∠CDB > ∠CDF (CN5). Using the equalities of Steps 4 and 6, these say both γ > β and β > γ, again a contradiction. Thus the second case is also impossible.

**Step 8.** The two noncollinear arrangements of Step 3 are impossible by Steps 5 and 7, and all collinear boundary arrangements were excluded in Step 2. Therefore no distinct point D on the same side of AB as C can satisfy both AD = AC and BD = BC. This proves the proposition.
