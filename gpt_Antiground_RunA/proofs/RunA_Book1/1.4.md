## Proposition 1.4
**Statement:** If two triangles have the two sides equal to two sides respectively, and have the angles contained by the equal straight lines equal, they will also have the base equal to the base, the triangle will be equal to the triangle, and the remaining angles will be equal to the remaining angles respectively, namely those which the equal sides subtend.

**Step 1.** Let ABC and DEF be triangles such that AB = DE, AC = DF, and ∠BAC = ∠EDF. We shall prove BC = EF, equality of the triangles, and equality of the corresponding remaining angles. The direct path is superposition and comparison by coincidence, rather than an auxiliary construction. [Hypotheses; CN4]

**Step 2.** Apply triangle ABC to triangle DEF, placing A on D and the direction AB along the direction DE, with C on the same side of DE as F. Then B coincides with E. For, since AB = DE, B cannot fall either short of E or beyond E: in either case one of the aligned segments would be a proper part of the other, contradicting their equality. [Hypothesis AB = DE; CN5; coincidence comparison under CN4]

**Step 3.** The direction AC now coincides with the direction DF. Indeed, the angles BAC and EDF have their vertices and first sides coincident, and their second sides lie on the same side of the common first side. If those second sides had different directions, one of the angles would be a proper part of the other. This contradicts the given equality of the angles. [Hypothesis ∠BAC = ∠EDF; D1.8; D1.9; CN5]

**Step 4.** Consequently C coincides with F. Both lie in the same direction from the coincident points A and D, and AC = DF. If their endpoints differed, one segment would be a proper part of the other, again contradicting equality. Thus the sides AC and DF coincide, as do AB and DE. [Hypothesis AC = DF; CN5]

**Step 5.** Since B coincides with E and C with F, the straight base BC coincides with the straight base EF: they are the straight segments between the same two endpoints. Hence BC = EF. [D1.4; CN4]

**Step 6.** All three sides of triangle ABC therefore coincide respectively with the three sides of triangle DEF. The triangular figures contained by these boundaries consequently coincide, so triangle ABC equals triangle DEF. [D1.14; D1.19; CN4]

**Step 7.** The sides forming ∠ABC coincide respectively with those forming ∠DEF, and the sides forming ∠ACB coincide respectively with those forming ∠DFE. Thus ∠ABC = ∠DEF and ∠ACB = ∠DFE. The first pair is opposite the equal sides AC and DF; the second pair is opposite the equal sides AB and DE. Together with Steps 5 and 6, these are precisely the required conclusions. [D1.8; D1.9; CN4]
