## Proposition 1.31
**Statement:** Through a given point to draw a straight line parallel to a given straight line.

**Step 1.** Let BC be the given straight line and A the given point in its plane, outside BC. This is the necessary configuration: if A lay on BC, every line through A would meet BC and therefore could not be parallel to it (D1.23). Join AB (P1).

**Step 2.** On the straight line AB, at A, construct the angle BAE equal to the angle ABC, choosing E on the opposite side of AB from C (1.23).

**Step 3.** Produce EA through A to F, so that E, A, F lie on one straight line (P2).

**Step 4.** The straight line AB, falling on EF and BC, makes the alternate angles BAE and ABC equal by Step 2. Therefore EF is parallel to BC (1.27). This criterion is the natural first choice because the construction gives equal alternate angles directly, without conversion to another angle condition. Since EF passes through A by Step 3, the required parallel through the given point has been drawn.
