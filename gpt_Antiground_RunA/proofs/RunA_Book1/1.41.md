## Proposition 1.41
**Statement:** If a parallelogram have the same base with a triangle and be in the same parallels, the parallelogram is double of the triangle.

**Step 1.** Let ABCD be the given parallelogram and ABE the given triangle, with common base AB and lying between the same parallels AB and CD. Thus E lies on the straight line CD. Join AC. [Hypothesis; P1]

**Step 2.** The diagonal AC bisects the parallelogram ABCD into the two equal triangles ABC and ACD. These two triangles together constitute the whole parallelogram. [1.34]

**Step 3.** Triangles ABC and ABE have the same base AB and lie between the same parallels AB and CD; hence they are equal. The same-base theorem is the direct choice here, rather than its equal-base generalization. Since triangle ACD equals triangle ABC by Step 2, triangle ACD also equals triangle ABE. [1.37; CN1]

**Step 4.** Adding the equalities ABC = ABE and ACD = ABE, the two triangles constituting parallelogram ABCD together equal triangle ABE taken twice. Therefore the parallelogram is double the triangle, as required. [CN2; Step 2]
