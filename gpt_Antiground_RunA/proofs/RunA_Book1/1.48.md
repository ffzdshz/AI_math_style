## Proposition 1.48
**Statement:** If in a triangle the square on one of the sides be equal to the squares on the remaining two sides of the triangle, the angle contained by the remaining two sides of the triangle is right.

**Step 1.** Let ABC be a triangle such that the square on BC equals the squares on AB and AC together. Write S(XY) for the area of the square on XY; squares needed below may be constructed by 1.46 and are equilateral and right-angled by D1.22. Thus the hypothesis is S(BC) = S(AB) + S(AC). Our route is to compare ABC with a constructed right triangle, using 1.47 and then the three-side criterion 1.8.

**Step 2.** At A draw a straight line perpendicular to AC by 1.11. This is the appropriate construction because A lies on the given straight line. Produce the perpendicular far enough and cut off AD equal to AB (P2, 1.3). Join DC (P1). The angle DAC is right by the definition of perpendicularity (D1.10).

**Step 3.** Since AD = AB, the squares on AD and AB are equal: their corresponding sides are equal and their angles are right, so they coincide when corresponding vertices and adjacent sides are superposed (D1.22, P4, CN4). Hence S(AD) = S(AB). Adding S(AC) to both gives
S(AD) + S(AC) = S(AB) + S(AC) (CN2).

**Step 4.** Apply 1.47 to the right-angled triangle DAC. It gives
S(DC) = S(AD) + S(AC).
Together with Step 3 and the hypothesis in Step 1, this yields S(DC) = S(BC) (CN1).

**Step 5.** Therefore DC = BC. Indeed, if these sides differed, place their squares with one vertex in common and their two adjacent sides along the same two perpendicular rays. Such alignment is possible because their angles are right (D1.22, P4). The square on the shorter side would then be a proper part of the square on the longer side, since each square has all its sides equal (D1.22). The whole would therefore be greater than the part (CN5), contradicting the equality established in Step 4. Thus the sides cannot differ.

**Step 6.** In triangles BAC and DAC, BA = DA by construction, AC is common (CN4), and BC = DC by Step 5. Consequently ∠BAC = ∠DAC by 1.8. This is the direct comparison criterion because all three corresponding sides are now known to be equal. Since ∠DAC is right by Step 2, ∠BAC is right also (D1.10). Hence the angle contained by the remaining two sides AB and AC is right, as required.
