## Proposition 1.17
**Statement:** In any triangle two angles taken together in any manner are less than two right angles.

**Step 1.** Let ABC be any triangle, and choose any two of its angles, labelling their vertices B and C. Produce AB beyond B to D, so that A, B, D lie on one straight line. [P2]

**Step 2.** The exterior angle CBD is greater than the interior and opposite angle BCA. [1.16] This is the direct path: the extension naturally supplies an exterior angle to which the previously proved exterior-angle inequality applies.

**Step 3.** Since BC stands on the straight line ABD, the adjacent angles ABC and CBD together equal two right angles. [1.13]

**Step 4.** Let E denote the nonzero excess of angle CBD over angle BCA established in Step 2. Thus ∠CBD = ∠BCA + E. Adding ∠ABC to both sides gives
∠ABC + ∠CBD = (∠ABC + ∠BCA) + E. [CN2]
Consequently, ∠ABC + ∠BCA is a proper part of ∠ABC + ∠CBD and is therefore less than it. [CN5] By Step 3, the latter sum equals two right angles. Hence ∠ABC + ∠BCA is less than two right angles. [Steps 2–3]

**Step 5.** The triangle and the selected pair of angles were arbitrary. Therefore, in any triangle, two angles taken together in any manner are less than two right angles. [Steps 1–4]
