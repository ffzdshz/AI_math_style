## Proposition 1.16
**Statement:** In any triangle, if one of the sides be produced, the exterior angle is greater than either of the interior and opposite angles.

**Step 1.** Let ABC be a triangle, and produce BC beyond C to D. We must show that ∠ACD is greater than both ∠BAC and ∠ABC. We use midpoint constructions: these provide two equal sides and equal included vertical angles, making the side–angle–side theorem 1.4 the natural congruence criterion. [P2]

**Step 2.** Bisect AC at E. Join BE, produce it beyond E, and cut off EF equal to EB on the produced part, first extending that part sufficiently. Join CF. Thus AE = EC, BE = EF, and B, E, F occur in that order. [1.10, P1, P2, 1.3]

**Step 3.** The angles ∠AEB and ∠CEF are vertical angles and therefore equal. Hence triangles AEB and CEF have two sides respectively equal and their included angles equal. Consequently, ∠BAE = ∠ECF. [1.15, 1.4]

**Step 4.** Since E lies between A and C, ∠BAE coincides with ∠BAC, and ∠ECF coincides with ∠ACF. Therefore ∠BAC = ∠ACF. [CN4, CN1; Step 3]

**Step 5.** The point order in Step 2 places F on the side of AC opposite B and on the same side of BC as A. Thus CF lies strictly inside the exterior angle ∠ACD. Consequently, ∠ACF is a proper part of ∠ACD, so ∠ACD > ∠ACF = ∠BAC. [CN5; Steps 1–4]

**Step 6.** Now produce AC beyond C to G. Bisect BC at H, join AH, and produce AH beyond H. On the produced part, extended sufficiently, cut off HK equal to HA, and join CK. Thus BH = HC, AH = HK, and A, H, K occur in that order. [P2, 1.10, P1, 1.3]

**Step 7.** The vertical angles ∠BHA and ∠CHK are equal. Therefore triangles BHA and CHK have two sides respectively equal and their included angles equal, giving ∠HBA = ∠HCK. Since H lies between B and C, these angles coincide respectively with ∠ABC and ∠BCK. Hence ∠ABC = ∠BCK. [1.15, 1.4, CN4, CN1]

**Step 8.** The point order in Step 6 places K on the side of BC opposite A and on the same side of AC as B. Thus CK lies strictly inside ∠BCG. Consequently, ∠BCK is a proper part of ∠BCG, and ∠BCG > ∠BCK = ∠ABC. [CN5; Steps 6–7]

**Step 9.** Since BC is produced to D and AC is produced to G, ∠BCG and ∠ACD are vertical angles and are equal. Step 8 therefore gives ∠ACD > ∠ABC, while Step 5 gives ∠ACD > ∠BAC. Thus the exterior angle is greater than either interior and opposite angle. [1.15; Steps 5 and 8]
