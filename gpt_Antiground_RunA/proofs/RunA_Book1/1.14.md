## Proposition 1.14
**Statement:** If with any straight line, and at a point on it, two straight lines not lying on the same side make the adjacent angles equal to two right angles, the two straight lines will be in a straight line with one another.

**Step 1.** Let B be the given point, and let BA be the common side of the adjacent angles. Let BC and BD lie on opposite sides of the straight line containing BA, with ∠CBA + ∠ABD equal to two right angles. We must show that BC and BD are in one straight line. [Hypothesis]

**Step 2.** Produce CB through B to E in a straight line. Thus BE lies on the opposite side of the line containing BA from BC, and hence on the same side as BD. [P2; hypothesis]

**Step 3.** Since BA stands on the straight line CBE, ∠CBA + ∠ABE equals two right angles. This is the direct route: 1.13 already supplies the adjacent-angle sum for the constructed straight continuation. [1.13]

**Step 4.** All right angles are equal, so the sums of two right angles in Step 1 and Step 3 are equal. Therefore
∠CBA + ∠ABD = ∠CBA + ∠ABE.
[P4; CN2; CN1]

**Step 5.** Subtract the common angle ∠CBA from these equal sums. The remaining angles are equal:
∠ABD = ∠ABE.
[CN3]

**Step 6.** If BD and BE were distinct rays, then, since they lie on the same side of the line containing BA, one would lie inside the angle formed by BA and the other. Thus one of ∠ABD and ∠ABE would be a proper part of the other, and the whole would be greater than the part. This contradicts Step 5. Hence BD and BE coincide in direction. [Step 2; CN5; Step 5]

**Step 7.** Since BE is the straight continuation of CB and BD coincides with BE, BC and BD are in one straight line with one another, as required. [P2; Steps 2 and 6]
