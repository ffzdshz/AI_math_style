## Proposition 1.37
**Statement:** Triangles which are on the same base and in the same parallels are equal to one another.

**Step 1.** Let triangles ABC and DBC have the same base BC, and let their vertices A and D lie on a straight line ℓ parallel to BC, as given. We shall prove that the triangles are equal in area.

**Step 2.** Through B draw a straight line parallel to CA and another parallel to CD (1.31), producing the lines as necessary (P2). Let them meet ℓ at E and F respectively. These intersections exist: if either drawn line did not meet ℓ, it would be parallel to ℓ (D1.23); then 1.30 would make CA or CD parallel to ℓ, contrary to its meeting ℓ at A or D (D1.23).

**Step 3.** The quadrilaterals EBCA and FBCD are parallelograms: EB is parallel to CA and FB to CD by construction, while EA and FD lie on ℓ and are therefore parallel to BC. Both parallelograms are on the same base BC and between the same parallels BC and ℓ, so they are equal (1.35). This is the direct path: the earlier same-base proposition applies immediately, without passing to an equal-base reformulation.

**Step 4.** BA is a diameter of parallelogram EBCA, and BD is a diameter of parallelogram FBCD. A diameter bisects a parallelogrammic area (1.34). Consequently EBCA consists of two triangles each equal to ABC, and FBCD consists of two triangles each equal to DBC.

**Step 5.** The halves of these equal areas are equal. Indeed, if either triangle were greater than the other, two copies of the lesser would together be a proper part of two copies of the greater; addition of equals and the fact that the whole is greater than the part would then make their doubles unequal (CN2, CN5), contradicting Step 3. Therefore triangle ABC equals triangle DBC. Thus triangles on the same base and in the same parallels are equal to one another.
