## Proposition 1.10
**Statement:** To bisect a given finite straight line.

**Step 1.** Let AB be the given finite straight line. Construct the equilateral triangle ABC on AB by Proposition 1.1. Thus CA = CB by the definition of an equilateral triangle. [1.1, D1.20]

**Step 2.** Bisect the angle ACB by Proposition 1.9, and produce its internal bisecting straight line as necessary to meet AB at D, between A and B. Then ∠ACD = ∠DCB. This chooses angle bisection as the route to the required segment bisection. [1.9, P2]

**Step 3.** The side CD is common to triangles ACD and BCD, and hence equals itself. Together with Steps 1–2, we therefore have CA = CB, CD = CD, and the included angles ∠ACD = ∠BCD. [CN4; Steps 1–2]

**Step 4.** Apply Proposition 1.4 to triangles ACD and BCD: their bases AD and BD are equal. The side–angle–side criterion is the natural choice because precisely its hypotheses have already been established. Since D lies between A and B, the given finite straight line AB has been divided into the two equal parts AD and DB, as required. [1.4; Step 2]
