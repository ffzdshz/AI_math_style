## Proposition 1.34
**Statement:** In parallelogrammic areas the opposite sides and angles are equal to one another, and the diameter bisects the areas.

**Step 1.** Let ABCD be a parallelogrammic area, with its vertices in boundary order, so that AB is parallel to CD and BC is parallel to AD. Join the opposite vertices A and C, drawing the diameter AC. [P1]

**Step 2.** Since AC falls on the parallel lines AB and CD, the alternate angles BAC and DCA are equal. Since AC also falls on the parallel lines BC and AD, the alternate angles BCA and DAC are equal. [1.29] Moreover, AC equals CA, being the same segment. [CN4]

**Step 3.** Apply 1.26 to triangles ABC and CDA: their angles BAC and BCA equal DCA and DAC respectively, and their included side AC is common. Therefore AB = CD, BC = DA, and ∠ABC = ∠CDA. Thus the opposite sides and one pair of opposite angles are equal. The direct path is 1.26 because the two angle equalities and the included side equality are already available. [1.26]

**Step 4.** Add the equal angles from Step 2. The angles DAC and CAB together form ∠DAB, while the respectively equal angles BCA and ACD together form ∠BCD. Hence ∠DAB = ∠BCD. Thus the other pair of opposite angles is also equal. [CN2]

**Step 5.** In triangles BAC and DCA, we have BA = DC by Step 3, AC = CA by Step 2, and the included angles BAC and DCA equal by Step 2. Therefore the triangles are equal in area. Here we use 1.4 because its conclusion explicitly includes equality of the triangles, whereas the stated conclusion of 1.26 supplies sides and angles. [1.4] The diameter AC divides ABCD into precisely these two triangular regions, so it bisects the area. Since either diameter can be designated AC by naming the vertices accordingly, the conclusion holds for either diameter. Consequently, in parallelogrammic areas the opposite sides and angles are equal, and the diameter bisects the areas.
