## Proposition 1.47
**Statement:** In right-angled triangles the square on the side subtending the right angle is equal to the squares on the sides containing the right angle.

**Step 1.** Let ABC be a triangle whose angle BAC is right. Construct externally the squares ABFG, ACKH, and BCDE, with their vertices named in cyclic order (1.46). Their sides are equal and their angles are right (D1.22); their opposite sides are parallel (1.28). Write [F] for the area of a figure F.

**Step 2.** The adjacent angles GAB and BAC are right, and AG and AC lie on opposite sides of AB. Therefore G, A, C lie on one straight line (1.14). Likewise, the adjacent right angles CAH and CAB show that H, A, B lie on one straight line (1.14). Consequently C lies on the line AG, which is parallel to BF, and B lies on the line AH, which is parallel to CK.

**Step 3.** Through A draw a line ℓ parallel to BE, producing it as necessary (1.31; P2). Let it meet BC at M and ED at L. These intersections exist: otherwise BC or ED would be parallel to ℓ and hence to BE, although each meets BE (D1.23; 1.30). Since BE is parallel to CD, ℓ is also parallel to CD (1.30). Its angles with BC are right, by the corresponding-angle equality for parallels (1.29).

**Step 4.** Both angles ABC and ACB are less than a right angle, since each, together with the right angle BAC, is less than two right angles (1.17). Hence M lies strictly between B and C. Indeed, if M lay beyond B, angle ABC would be an exterior angle of the right triangle ABM and would exceed its right angle at M (1.16); if M lay beyond C, the same contradiction would follow for angle ACB in triangle ACM (1.16). An endpoint M would make one of those acute angles right. Since ℓ passes through the interior point M of BC and cannot cross either parallel side BE or CD, its intersection L with ED lies between E and D (D1.23). Thus ℓ divides square BCDE into the two parallelograms BMLE and MCDL, whose interiors do not overlap.

**Step 5.** Join AE, CF, AD, and BK (P1). At B, the external construction of the squares gives
∠ABE = ∠ABC + ∠CBE,
and
∠FBC = ∠FBA + ∠ABC.
The angles CBE and FBA are right and therefore equal (D1.22; P4). Adding the common angle ABC gives ∠ABE = ∠FBC (CN2). Also AB = FB and BE = BC, by the respective squares (D1.22). Therefore triangles ABE and FBC are equal (1.4). This is the direct congruence choice: the two corresponding sides and their included angle are already known.

**Step 6.** Parallelogram BMLE and triangle ABE have the same base BE and lie between the same parallels BE and ℓ. Hence [BMLE] is double [ABE] (1.41). Square ABFG and triangle FBC have the same base BF and lie between the same parallels BF and AG, since C lies on AG. Hence [ABFG] is double [FBC] (1.41). Equal triangles have equal doubles (CN2), so Step 5 yields
[BMLE] = [ABFG] (CN1).
The area-path choice is to apply 1.41 directly to these common-base, common-parallel pairs, rather than introduce further dissections or area constructions.

**Step 7.** At C, the external construction similarly gives
∠ACD = ∠ACB + ∠BCD,
and
∠KCB = ∠KCA + ∠ACB.
The angles BCD and KCA are right and therefore equal (D1.22; P4), so ∠ACD = ∠KCB (CN2). Also AC = KC and CD = CB (D1.22). Thus triangles ACD and KCB are equal by the same two-side-and-included-angle criterion (1.4).

**Step 8.** Parallelogram MCDL and triangle ACD have the same base CD and lie between the same parallels CD and ℓ. Therefore [MCDL] is double [ACD] (1.41). Square ACKH and triangle KCB have the same base CK and lie between the same parallels CK and AH, since B lies on AH. Therefore [ACKH] is double [KCB] (1.41). Using Step 7 and adding equals to equals gives
[MCDL] = [ACKH] (CN2; CN1).

**Step 9.** Add the equalities obtained in Steps 6 and 8 (CN2). Because BMLE and MCDL exactly partition square BCDE, the result is
[BCDE] = [ABFG] + [ACKH].
Thus the square on BC, the side subtending the right angle, is equal to the squares on AB and AC, the sides containing the right angle, taken together.
