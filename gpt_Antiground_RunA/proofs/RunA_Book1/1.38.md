## Proposition 1.38
**Statement:** Triangles which are on equal bases and in the same parallels are equal to one another.

**Step 1.** Let triangles ABC and DEF have equal bases BC and EF lying on a straight line ℓ, and let their vertices A and D lie on a straight line m parallel to ℓ. We must prove the triangles equal in area. (Hypotheses.)

**Step 2.** Through C draw a straight line parallel to BA, and through F draw a straight line parallel to ED; produce these to meet m at G and H respectively. Then BCGA and EFHD are parallelograms: BC is parallel to AG and CG to BA, while EF is parallel to DH and FH to ED. (1.31, P2; the given parallelism.)

**Step 3.** The parallelograms BCGA and EFHD are on the equal bases BC and EF and between the same parallels ℓ and m. Therefore they are equal in area. This is the direct path: the equal-base parallelogram theorem applies immediately to the constructed figures. (1.36.)

**Step 4.** The diagonal AC bisects parallelogram BCGA, and the diagonal DF bisects parallelogram EFHD. Consequently, BCGA consists of two triangles each equal to ABC, and EFHD consists of two triangles each equal to DEF. (1.34.)

**Step 5.** Thus twice the area of ABC equals twice the area of DEF. (Steps 3–4, CN1.) To justify passing to their halves, suppose either triangle had greater area than the other. Its area would then equal the smaller area together with a nonzero remainder. Adding this equality to itself would express its double as the smaller double together with two such remainders (CN2), making it strictly greater than the smaller double (CN5), contrary to their equality. Hence triangle ABC equals triangle DEF in area. Therefore triangles on equal bases and in the same parallels are equal to one another.
