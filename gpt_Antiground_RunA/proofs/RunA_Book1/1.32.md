## Proposition 1.32
**Statement:** In any triangle, if one of the sides be produced, the exterior angle is equal to the two interior and opposite angles, and the three interior angles of the triangle are equal to two right angles.

**Step 1.** Let ABC be any triangle, and produce BC beyond C to D. The exterior angle is ∠ACD, and its interior and opposite angles are ∠BAC and ∠ABC. [P2]

**Step 2.** Through C draw a straight line parallel to AB, taking its ray CE inside the exterior angle ∠ACD. Path choice: use the available parallel construction directly, rather than reconstructing it from elementary constructions. [1.31]

**Step 3.** Since AC falls on the parallel straight lines AB and CE, the alternate angles are equal: ∠BAC = ∠ACE. [1.29]

**Step 4.** Since BD falls on the same parallel straight lines, the exterior angle ∠ECD equals the interior and opposite angle ∠ABC. Here the exterior-angle clause applies directly, so no auxiliary angle conversion is needed. [1.29]

**Step 5.** Add the equalities in Steps 3 and 4. Since CE divides ∠ACD into the adjacent angles ∠ACE and ∠ECD, we obtain ∠BAC + ∠ABC = ∠ACE + ∠ECD = ∠ACD. Thus the exterior angle equals the two interior and opposite angles together. [CN2]

**Step 6.** Since AC stands on the straight line BCD, the adjacent angles ∠BCA and ∠ACD together equal two right angles. [1.13]

**Step 7.** Add ∠BCA to both sides of the equality in Step 5. Then ∠BAC + ∠ABC + ∠BCA = ∠ACD + ∠BCA, and the latter sum equals two right angles by Step 6. Hence the three interior angles of the triangle equal two right angles. Both assertions are proved. [CN2, CN1]
