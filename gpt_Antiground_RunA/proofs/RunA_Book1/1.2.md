## Proposition 1.2
**Statement:** To place at a given point (as an extremity) a straight line equal to a given straight line.

**Step 1.** Let A be the given point and BC the given straight line, labeling its endpoints so that B is distinct from A. Draw AB (P1).

**Step 2.** Construct an equilateral triangle DAB on AB (1.1). Then DA = DB (D1.20). This preceding construction supplies equal parts to subtract; either choice of side of AB for the triangle will serve.

**Step 3.** Produce DB beyond B (P2). Describe the circle with centre B and distance BC, and let it meet this produced straight line at G beyond B (P3). Thus D, B, G lie in that order, and BG = BC because they are radii of the same circle (D1.15).

**Step 4.** Since DB is a proper part of DG, DG > DB (CN5). Together with DA = DB from Step 2, this gives DG > DA.

**Step 5.** Describe the circle with centre D and distance DG (P3). Produce DA beyond A to meet this circle at L (P2); the intersection is beyond A because DG > DA, as established in Step 4. Thus D, A, L lie in that order, and DL = DG because they are radii of this circle (D1.15).

**Step 6.** From the equal wholes DL and DG subtract the equal parts DA and DB. The respective remainders are AL and BG, so AL = BG (CN3).

**Step 7.** Since AL = BG and BC = BG, we have AL = BC (CN1). Therefore AL is a straight line equal to the given straight line BC and has the given point A as an extremity, as required.
