## Proposition 1.25
**Statement:** If two triangles have the two sides equal to two sides respectively, but have the base greater than the base, they will also have the one of the angles contained by the equal straight lines greater than the other.

**Step 1.** Let ABC and DEF be triangles with AB = DE, AC = DF, and BC > EF (hypotheses). We must prove that ∠BAC > ∠EDF. We exclude equality and the reverse inequality directly, without an auxiliary construction.

**Step 2.** If ∠BAC = ∠EDF, then the two pairs of equal sides and their equal included angles give BC = EF by **1.4**. This contradicts BC > EF from Step 1. Thus ∠BAC is not equal to ∠EDF. Here 1.4 is the natural choice because its side–angle–side hypotheses hold directly.

**Step 3.** If ∠BAC < ∠EDF, apply **1.24** with triangle DEF first and triangle ABC second. Since DE = AB and DF = AC, the greater included angle ∠EDF would give EF > BC. This contradicts BC > EF from Step 1. Thus ∠BAC is not less than ∠EDF.

**Step 4.** Since ∠BAC is neither equal to nor less than ∠EDF, it must be greater than ∠EDF (elimination of the other two comparisons, Steps 2–3). Therefore the greater base is opposite the greater angle contained by the respectively equal sides, as required.
