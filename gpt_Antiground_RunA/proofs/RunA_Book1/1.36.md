## Proposition 1.36
**Statement:** Parallelograms which are on equal bases and in the same parallels are equal to one another.

**Step 1.** Let ABCD and EFGH be parallelograms on equal bases AB and EF, lying between the same parallel straight lines l and m: AB and EF lie on l, while DC and HG lie on m. Label the endpoints so that AB and HG run in the same direction along these parallels. Thus AB = EF. (Hypotheses; choice of notation.)

**Step 2.** In parallelogram EFGH, the opposite sides HG and EF are equal. Hence AB = HG, since both equal EF. Also AB is parallel to HG because their containing lines are l and m. (1.34; CN1; the given parallels.)

**Step 3.** Join AH and BG. Since AB and HG are equal and parallel, the straight lines AH and BG joining their corresponding endpoints are parallel. Consequently ABGH is a parallelogram. (P1; 1.33.) Path choice: 1.33 directly supplies the bridging parallelogram, reducing the desired comparison to the established same-base case.

**Step 4.** Parallelograms ABCD and ABGH are on the same base AB and between the same parallels l and m. Therefore ABCD and ABGH are equal in area. (1.35.)

**Step 5.** Parallelograms ABGH and EFGH are on the same base HG and between the same parallels m and l. Therefore ABGH and EFGH are equal in area. (1.35.)

**Step 6.** Since ABCD and EFGH are each equal in area to ABGH, they are equal to one another. Therefore parallelograms on equal bases and in the same parallels are equal to one another. (CN1.)
