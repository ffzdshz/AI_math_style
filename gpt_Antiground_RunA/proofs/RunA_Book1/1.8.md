## Proposition 1.8
**Statement:** If two triangles have the two sides equal to two sides respectively, and have also the base equal to the base, they will also have the angles equal which are contained by the equal straight lines.

**Step 1.** Let ABC and DEF be triangles with AB = DE, AC = DF, and BC = EF. We must prove ∠BAC = ∠EDF. (Hypotheses.) The direct route is to superpose the equal bases and invoke the same-side uniqueness supplied by 1.7, rather than rederive that uniqueness.

**Step 2.** Superpose triangle DEF upon the plane of ABC, placing E on B and EF along BC, with D on the same side of BC as A. Since EF = BC, F coincides with C. Denote the position of D by X. The placed sides BX and CX coincide with DE and DF respectively, so BX = DE and CX = DF. (Hypothesis BC = EF; CN4.)

**Step 3.** Since BX = DE and AB = DE, we have BX = AB. Likewise, since CX = DF and AC = DF, we have CX = AC. (Hypotheses; CN1.)

**Step 4.** If X were distinct from A, the pairs BA, CA and BX, CX would stand on the same base BC, meet at distinct points on the same side of it, and be respectively equal at the same extremities. This is impossible by 1.7. Therefore X coincides with A. (1.7.)

**Step 5.** Thus, under this superposition, DE coincides with AB and DF with AC. Consequently ∠EDF coincides with ∠BAC, and coincident angles are equal. Hence ∠BAC = ∠EDF, as required. (CN4.)
