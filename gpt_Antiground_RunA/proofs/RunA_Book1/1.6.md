## Proposition 1.6
**Statement:** If in a triangle two angles be equal to one another, the sides which subtend the equal angles will also be equal to one another.

**Step 1.** Let ABC be a triangle with ∠ABC = ∠ACB. We must prove AB = AC, the sides opposite these equal angles. [Hypothesis]

**Step 2.** Suppose instead that AB and AC are unequal. By interchanging B and C if necessary, assume AB > AC; this interchange preserves the hypothesis. Cut off BD from BA equal to AC, with D strictly between B and A. [1.3]

**Step 3.** Join DC. Since BD lies along BA in the same direction from B, ∠DBC is the same angle as ∠ABC. Consequently ∠DBC = ∠ACB. [P1; D1.8; hypothesis; CN1]

**Step 4.** Compare triangles DBC and ACB. We have BD = AC by construction, BC = CB because it is the common side, and ∠DBC = ∠ACB by Step 3. Therefore triangle DBC equals triangle ACB. The path chosen is the direct side–angle–side application of 1.4, using its equality-of-triangles conclusion. [CN4; 1.4]

**Step 5.** Because D lies strictly between A and B, DC divides triangle ABC into triangles ADC and DBC. Thus triangle DBC is a proper part of triangle ABC, and triangle ABC is greater than triangle DBC. [Construction in Steps 2–3; CN5]

**Step 6.** Triangle ACB is triangle ABC itself. Hence Step 4 makes the whole triangle equal to its proper part, contradicting Step 5. The supposition that AB and AC are unequal is therefore impossible, so AB = AC. Thus the sides subtending the equal angles are equal. [Steps 4–5; CN5]
