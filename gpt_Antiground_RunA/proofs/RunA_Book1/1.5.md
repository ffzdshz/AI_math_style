## Proposition 1.5
**Statement:** In isosceles triangles the angles at the base are equal to one another, and, if the equal straight lines be produced further, the angles under the base will be equal to one another.

**Step 1.** Let ABC be an isosceles triangle with AB = AC and base BC (D1.20). Path choice: construct equal extended sides using 1.3, then apply the available side-angle-side theorem 1.4 twice; this establishes both assertions without requiring a theorem about supplementary angles.

**Step 2.** Produce AB beyond B to F, and produce AC beyond C to E far enough that AE > AF (P2). Cut off AG from AE equal to AF (1.3). Since AF > AB = AC, we have AG > AC, so G lies beyond C. Here the strict inequalities follow from the whole being greater than its part (CN5), together with the stated equalities. Thus the points have orders A, B, F and A, C, G.

**Step 3.** Join FC and GB (P1). Since AF and AB have the same direction from A, as do AG and AC, the angles FAC and GAB are the same angle and hence equal (CN4).

**Step 4.** In triangles AFC and AGB, AF = AG by construction, AC = AB by hypothesis, and ∠FAC = ∠GAB by Step 3. Therefore side-angle-side gives FC = GB, ∠AFC = ∠AGB, and ∠ACF = ∠ABG (1.4).

**Step 5.** Subtract AB = AC from AF = AG. Because B lies between A and F, and C between A and G, the remaining segments satisfy BF = CG (CN3).

**Step 6.** The rays FB and FA coincide, and the rays GC and GA coincide. Consequently ∠BFC = ∠AFC and ∠CGB = ∠AGB (CN4). Together with Step 4, these equalities give ∠BFC = ∠CGB (CN1).

**Step 7.** In triangles BFC and CGB, BF = CG by Step 5, FC = GB by Step 4, and their included angles BFC and CGB are equal by Step 6. Hence ∠FBC = ∠GCB and ∠BCF = ∠CBG (1.4). The first equality is precisely the equality of the angles under the base, since BF and CG are the continuations of AB and AC.

**Step 8.** By the indicated point orders, ∠ACF consists of ∠ACB together with ∠BCF, while ∠ABG consists of ∠ABC together with ∠CBG. The whole angles are equal by Step 4, and the latter parts are equal by Step 7. Subtracting these equal parts gives ∠ACB = ∠ABC (CN3). Thus the angles at the base are equal, and the angles under the base, when the equal sides are produced, are also equal.
