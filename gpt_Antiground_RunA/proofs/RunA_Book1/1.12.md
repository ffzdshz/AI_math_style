## Proposition 1.12
**Statement:** To a given infinite straight line, from a given point which is not on it, to draw a perpendicular straight line.

**Step 1.** Let ℓ be the given infinite straight line and C the given point not on it. Choose A on ℓ, join CA, and produce CA beyond A to D. (P1, P2.)

**Step 2.** Describe the circle with centre C and radius CD. (P3.) Since CA is a proper part of CD, CA < CD; hence A lies inside the circle. The infinite straight line ℓ through A therefore cuts the circumference on either side of A; call these intersections E and F. (CN5, D1.15.)

**Step 3.** Bisect EF at M, so that EM = MF and M lies between E and F. (1.10.) Join CE, CF, and CM. (P1.) The chosen route is to compare the two triangles formed by joining C to the midpoint of EF.

**Step 4.** Since E and F lie on the circle with centre C, CE = CF. (D1.15.) Also EM = MF by the bisection, and CM = CM because it is common to both triangles. (1.10, CN4.)

**Step 5.** In triangles EMC and FMC, the sides EM and MC equal FM and MC respectively, and the bases EC and FC are equal. Therefore ∠EMC = ∠FMC. (1.8.) This is the direct choice of proposition: all three corresponding side equalities are already available, without first needing an angle equality.

**Step 6.** Since E, M, and F lie in that order on ℓ, these equal angles are the adjacent angles made by CM standing on ℓ. Consequently each is a right angle, and CM is perpendicular to ℓ. Thus the required perpendicular has been drawn from the given point C to the given infinite straight line. (D1.10.)
