## Proposition 1.19
**Statement:** In any triangle the greater angle is subtended by the greater side.

**Step 1.** Let ABC be any triangle in which ∠ABC > ∠BCA (hypothesis). We must prove AC > AB. Our path is to exclude equality and the reverse inequality using the previously proved side-to-angle results, without an auxiliary construction.

**Step 2.** If AC = AB, then the angles at the base BC are equal: ∠ABC = ∠BCA, by **1.5**. This contradicts the hypothesis in Step 1. Therefore AC ≠ AB. Proposition 1.5 is the direct choice here because the assumed equality concerns sides and yields equality of the opposite angles.

**Step 3.** If AC < AB, then the greater side AB subtends the greater angle, so ∠BCA > ∠ABC, by **1.18**. This again contradicts the hypothesis in Step 1. Therefore AC is not less than AB.

**Step 4.** Since AC is neither equal to AB nor less than AB (Steps 2–3), the remaining comparison is AC > AB. Thus the greater angle ∠ABC is subtended by the greater side AC. Since the triangle and the pair of unequal angles were arbitrary, in any triangle the greater angle is subtended by the greater side.
