## Proposition 1.21
**Statement:** If on one of the sides of a triangle, from its extremities, there be constructed two straight lines meeting within the triangle, the straight lines so constructed will be less than the remaining two sides of the triangle, but will contain a greaterangle.

**Step 1.** Let ABC be the given triangle, and let BD and CD be the straight lines drawn from the extremities of BC to a point D strictly inside the triangle. We must prove BD + DC < BA + AC and ∠BDC > ∠BAC. [Hypothesis]

**Step 2.** Produce BD beyond D to meet AC at E. Thus B, D, E occur in that order, and E lies between A and C, since D is inside triangle ABC. [P2; interior-point hypothesis] Path choice: this single extension allows direct application of the triangle inequality (1.20) to the lengths and the exterior-angle inequality (1.16) to the angles.

**Step 3.** In triangle ABE, AB + AE > BE. [1.20] Add EC to both sides. Equal addition preserves the strict comparison, since the greater magnitude retains its excess. As AE + EC = AC, we obtain
AB + AC > BE + EC. [CN2, CN5]

**Step 4.** In triangle DEC, DE + EC > DC. [1.20] Add BD to both sides; since BD + DE = BE, this gives
BE + EC > BD + DC. [CN2, CN5]
Together with Step 3, this proves AB + AC > BD + DC. [Steps 3–4]

**Step 5.** In triangle DEC, ED is produced through D to B. Hence the exterior angle BDC is greater than the interior opposite angle DEC. [1.16] The rays ED and EB coincide, so ∠DEC = ∠BEC. [CN4] Therefore ∠BDC > ∠BEC.

**Step 6.** In triangle ABE, AE is produced through E to C. Hence the exterior angle BEC is greater than the interior opposite angle BAE. [1.16] The rays AE and AC coincide, so ∠BAE = ∠BAC. [CN4] Therefore ∠BEC > ∠BAC.

**Step 7.** By Steps 5–6, ∠BDC > ∠BAC; and by Step 4, BD + DC < BA + AC. Thus the two straight lines meeting within the triangle are together less than the remaining two sides, but contain a greater angle, as required. [Steps 4–6]
