## Proposition 1.44
**Statement:** To a given straight line to apply, in a given rectilineal angle, a parallelogram equal to a given triangle.

**Step 1.** Let AB be the given straight line, T the given triangle, and α the given rectilineal angle. Construct a parallelogram PQRS equal to T, with ∠QPS = α (1.42). The path chosen is to obtain the required area and angle from 1.42, then use equal complements (1.43) to obtain the prescribed base. We first explicitly copy the auxiliary parallelogram into a convenient position.

**Step 2.** Produce AB beyond B (P2). At B construct a ray making with this continuation an angle equal to ∠QPS (1.23). On the continuation of AB mark E so that BE = PQ, and on the other ray mark G so that BG = PS, producing the rays sufficiently and cutting off the required lengths (P2, 1.3). Thus A, B, E occur in that order, and ∠EBG = ∠QPS = α (CN1).

**Step 3.** Through E draw a parallel to BG, and through G draw a parallel to BE; let them meet at F (1.31). They must meet: otherwise they would be parallel, and 1.30 would imply that BE and BG were parallel, although they meet at B (D1.23). Thus BEFG is a parallelogram.

**Step 4.** Join QS and EG (P1). The triangles PQS and BEG have PQ = BE, PS = BG, and ∠QPS = ∠EBG. These are exactly two sides and their included angle, so 1.4 gives equality of the triangles. The diagonals QS and EG bisect their respective parallelograms (1.34); adding the equal halves therefore gives PQRS = BEFG (CN2). Consequently BEFG equals T (CN1).

**Step 5.** Through A draw a parallel to BG, meeting FG produced beyond G at H (1.31, P2). The intersection exists because a parallel to BG cannot also be parallel to FG, which is parallel to BE (1.30, D1.23). Since A, B, E occur in that order and AH, BG, EF are parallel, H, G, F occur in that order. In particular, AH ∥ BG and HG ∥ AB, so ABGH is a parallelogram (1.30 and the construction).

**Step 6.** Join HB and produce it beyond B to a point X; also produce FE beyond E to a point Y (P1, P2). Ray BG lies strictly inside ∠EBH, so ∠EBG < ∠EBH (CN5). Moreover,
∠EBX + ∠EBH = two right angles (1.13),
and ∠BEY = ∠EBG, because BG ∥ FE (1.29).
It follows that ∠EBX + ∠BEY is less than two right angles (CN5). These are the interior angles on the same side of transversal BE. Hence the rays BX and EY meet at a point K (P5). Thus H, B, K occur in that order, as do F, E, K.

**Step 7.** Through K draw a parallel to BE, meeting AH produced beyond A at L and GB produced beyond B at M (1.31, P2). These intersections exist by 1.30 and D1.23, since the two directions are those of the intersecting lines BE and BG. We have HF ∥ LK, and HL ∥ FK because both are parallel to BG (1.30). Therefore HFKL is a parallelogram. Also AB ∥ LM and AL ∥ BM, so ABML is a parallelogram.

**Step 8.** In the parallelogram HFKL, the diagonal HK passes through B. The lines ABE and GBM through B are parallel to its respective sides. The parallelograms H G B A and B E K M are therefore the two parallelograms about this diagonal; their complements are BEFG and ABML. These are exactly the figures to which 1.43 applies, so BEFG = ABML. Since BEFG equals T, ABML also equals T (CN1).

**Step 9.** The rays BA and BE are opposite, as are BM and BG. Hence ∠ABM and ∠EBG are vertically opposite angles and are equal (1.15). Therefore ∠ABM = α (CN1). Thus ABML is a parallelogram applied to the given straight line AB, equal to the given triangle T, and having an angle equal to the given angle α, as required.
