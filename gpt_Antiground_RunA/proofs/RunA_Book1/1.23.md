## Proposition 1.23
**Statement:** On a given straight line and at a point on it to construct a rectilineal angle equal to a given rectilineal angle.

**Step 1.** Let A be the given point on the given straight line ℓ, and let the given rectilineal angle have vertex C. Choose points D and E, distinct from C, on its two respective sides. These sides do not lie in one straight line, so C, D, E are noncollinear (D1.8, D1.9). Join DE, forming triangle CDE (P1).

**Step 2.** In triangle CDE, any two sides together are greater than the remaining side. Thus CD + CE > DE, CD + DE > CE, and CE + DE > CD (1.20).

**Step 3.** Apply the three-side triangle construction of 1.22, taking A as its initial vertex and ℓ as the supporting line of its first side. Construct triangle AFG such that F lies on ℓ and AF = CD, AG = CE, and FG = DE (1.22). The inequalities in Step 2 supply all the required hypotheses. This uses the established triangle construction directly rather than repeating its underlying construction.

**Step 4.** The triangles AFG and CDE have AF = CD and AG = CE, and their bases FG and DE are equal. Therefore their included angles ∠FAG and ∠DCE are equal (1.8). This is the direct applicable angle-equality criterion because the construction supplies three corresponding side equalities. Since AF lies on ℓ and A is the prescribed point, ∠FAG is the required rectilineal angle on the given straight line at that point (D1.9).
