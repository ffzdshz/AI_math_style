## Proposition 1.35
**Statement:** Parallelograms which are on the same base and in the same parallels are equal to one another.

**Step 1.** Let ABCD and EBCF be the given parallelograms, named in boundary order, on the common base BC, with their opposite sides AD and EF on the other parallel. Orient that parallel so that A precedes D and E precedes F. By 1.34, AD = BC and EF = BC; therefore AD = EF by CN1. Also AB = DC by 1.34.

**Step 2.** If A coincides with E, then D coincides with F: otherwise one of the equal segments AD and EF would be a proper part of the other, contrary to CN5. The parallelograms then coincide and are equal by CN4. Otherwise, interchange the names of the two parallelograms if necessary so that A precedes E. Then D precedes F, for if F coincided with D or preceded D, EF would be a proper part of AD, again contradicting AD = EF by CN5. Thus D and E both lie strictly between A and F, although their order relative to one another is unrestricted.

**Step 3.** Join AF by P1, forming the outer quadrilateral ABCF. The whole segment AF consists both of AD together with DF and of AE together with EF. Subtracting the equal segments AD and EF from this same whole gives DF = AE by CN3. We will compare the parallelograms by removing equal triangles from the common outer quadrilateral; this works whether their upper sides overlap, meet, or are separated.

**Step 4.** Since AB is parallel to DC, the transversal AF makes the exterior angle CDF equal to the interior and opposite angle BAE on the same side, by 1.29. Hence ∠BAE = ∠CDF.

**Step 5.** In triangles ABE and DCF, AB = DC, AE = DF, and the included angles BAE and CDF are equal. Therefore triangle ABE is equal to triangle DCF by 1.4. This side–angle–side comparison is the direct choice because precisely those data have already been established.

**Step 6.** Within the quadrilateral ABCF, removing triangle DCF leaves exactly parallelogram ABCD, while removing triangle ABE leaves exactly parallelogram EBCF. Since the removed triangles are equal, the remainders are equal by CN3. Therefore parallelograms which are on the same base and in the same parallels are equal to one another.
