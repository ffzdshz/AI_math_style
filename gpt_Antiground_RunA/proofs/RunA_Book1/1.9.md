## Proposition 1.9
**Statement:** To bisect a given rectilineal angle.

**Step 1.** Let BAC be the given rectilineal angle. Interchange the names B and C if necessary so that AB is not greater than AC. We must draw a straight line from A dividing this angle into two equal angles. [D1.9]

**Step 2.** If AB = AC, take E = C. Otherwise, cut off AE from AC equal to AB. Thus, in either case, E lies on the side AC and AB = AE. [1.3]

**Step 3.** Join BE and construct the equilateral triangle BEF on it, choosing F on the side of BE opposite A. In particular, BF = EF. The opposite-side choice ensures that the auxiliary vertex is distinct from A and places it beyond BE rather than toward A. [P1, 1.1, D1.20]

**Step 4.** Join AF. In triangles BAF and EAF, the sides BA and EA are equal by Step 2, and AF is common to both triangles, hence equal to itself. Their bases BF and EF are equal by Step 3. [P1, CN4]

**Step 5.** Therefore the angles contained by BA, AF and by EA, AF are equal: ∠BAF = ∠EAF. We use the three-side criterion directly because the construction has supplied exactly its required side equalities. [1.8]

**Step 6.** With F chosen across BE from A, the joining line AF passes inside the angle BAE. Since AE lies along AC, the angles EAF and FAC coincide and are equal. Thus ∠BAF = ∠FAC, and AF divides the given angle BAC into two equal angles. The given rectilineal angle has therefore been bisected. [CN4, CN1, D1.9]
