## Proposition 1.11
**Statement:** To draw a straight line at right angles to a given straight line from a given point on it.

**Step 1.** Let C be the given point on the given straight line. Choose a point D distinct from C on that line. Produce the line on the opposite side of C to a point G far enough that CG > CD. Thus C lies between D and G. [P2]

**Step 2.** From CG cut off CE equal to CD, with E on CG. Then D, C, E lie on the given straight line, C lies between D and E, and CD = CE. [1.3]

**Step 3.** On DE construct an equilateral triangle DFE, whose vertex F is off the given line. In particular, DF = EF. [1.1, D1.20]

**Step 4.** Join C to F. The side CF is common to triangles DCF and ECF, so CF = CF. [P1, CN4]

**Step 5.** In triangles DCF and ECF, the sides DC and CF equal EC and CF respectively, and the bases DF and EF are equal. Therefore ∠DCF = ∠ECF. The path choice is to apply 1.8 directly: the three side equalities are already established, and it yields precisely the required angles at C without first proving another angle equality. [1.8]

**Step 6.** Since D, C, E are collinear with C between D and E, these are the adjacent angles made by CF standing on the given straight line. They are equal, so each is a right angle and CF is perpendicular to that line. Thus CF is the required straight line drawn at right angles from the given point C. [D1.10]
