## Proposition 1.28
**Statement:** If a straight line falling on two straight lines make the exterior angle equal to the interior and opposite angle on the same side, or the interior angles on the same side equal to two right angles, the straight lines will be parallel to one another.

**Step 1.** Let the transversal meet the two coplanar straight lines AB and CD at E and F respectively. Label the points so that A, E, B and C, F, D occur in those orders, with B and D on the same side of the transversal. Produce FE beyond E to G. [P2] The given alternatives are then ∠GEB = ∠EFD, or ∠BEF + ∠EFD equals two right angles. [Hypotheses]

**Step 2.** First suppose that the exterior angle ∠GEB equals the interior and opposite angle ∠EFD. Since AB and GF intersect at E, their vertical angles ∠AEF and ∠GEB are equal. [1.15] Consequently ∠AEF = ∠EFD. [Hypothesis, CN1]

**Step 3.** The angles ∠AEF and ∠EFD are alternate angles made by the transversal EF with AB and CD. Therefore AB is parallel to CD. [1.27] This is the direct path: the vertical-angle equality immediately supplies the hypothesis of the previously established alternate-angle criterion.

**Step 4.** Now suppose instead that ∠BEF + ∠EFD equals two right angles. [Hypothesis] Because EA and EB are opposite rays, ∠AEF + ∠BEF also equals two right angles. [1.13] All right angles are equal, so any two pairs of right angles have equal sums. [P4, CN2] Hence
∠AEF + ∠BEF = ∠BEF + ∠EFD. [CN1]

**Step 5.** Subtract the common angle ∠BEF from these equal sums. The remaining angles are equal:
∠AEF = ∠EFD. [CN3]

**Step 6.** Again these are alternate angles, so AB is parallel to CD. [1.27] Thus either of the two stated conditions makes the straight lines parallel to one another.
