## Proposition 1.45
**Statement:** To construct, in a given rectilineal angle, a parallelogram equal to a given rectilineal figure.

**Step 1.** Let F be the given rectilineal figure and θ the given rectilineal angle. Divide F into finitely many triangles T₁, …, Tₙ with disjoint interiors. For completeness, this division can be made even when F is nonconvex: draw parallel lines through its vertices, choosing their direction parallel to none of its sides (D1.19, 1.31). Within each open strip between successive lines there is no boundary vertex; consequently each piece is bounded by two unbroken boundary segments and portions of the strip boundaries, and its closure is a triangle or a convex quadrilateral. Divide every such quadrilateral by a diagonal (P1). These triangles together constitute F. If F is already a triangle, take F itself as T₁.

**Step 2.** Construct a parallelogram ABCD equal to T₁, with ∠DAB = θ (1.42). This is the natural initial construction: 1.42 suffices because no side of the first parallelogram is prescribed.

**Step 3.** Suppose that ABCD has been constructed equal to T₁, …, Tₖ together, with ∠DAB = θ, where k < n. Apply to BC, on the side opposite ABCD, a parallelogram BEFC equal to Tₖ₊₁, making ∠CBE equal to ∠BCD (1.44). Here 1.44 is used because the common side BC is already prescribed; the chosen angle will make the adjoining sides continue straight.

**Step 4.** Since AB is parallel to DC, the angles ∠ABC and ∠BCD together equal two right angles (1.29). Since ∠CBE = ∠BCD, the angles ∠ABC and ∠CBE also together equal two right angles (CN2, CN1). The rays BA and BE lie on opposite sides of BC by the construction. Therefore A, B, E lie on one straight line, with B between A and E (1.14).

**Step 5.** Since BE is parallel to CF, the angles ∠CBE and ∠BCF together equal two right angles (1.29). Substituting the equal angle ∠BCD for ∠CBE shows that ∠BCD and ∠BCF together equal two right angles (CN2, CN1). The rays CD and CF lie on opposite sides of BC. Therefore D, C, F lie on one straight line, with C between D and F (1.14).

**Step 6.** The straight lines AE and DF are consequently parallel, since they are the same straight lines as AB and DC. Also AD and EF are each parallel to BC, and therefore are parallel to one another (1.30). Thus AEFD is a parallelogram. Its angle ∠DAE coincides with ∠DAB, so ∠DAE = θ (CN4, CN1).

**Step 7.** The parallelograms ABCD and BEFC have disjoint interiors, being on opposite sides of their common side BC. By the straight-line continuations established in Steps 4–5, their union is exactly AEFD. Since ABCD equals T₁, …, Tₖ together and BEFC equals Tₖ₊₁, their whole AEFD equals T₁, …, Tₖ₊₁ together (CN2).

**Step 8.** Regard AEFD as the new ABCD and repeat Steps 3–7 for each remaining triangle. Each repetition preserves the angle θ and adds a parallelogram equal to the next triangle (1.44, CN2). After finitely many repetitions, the resulting parallelogram equals all the triangles together, and hence equals F by Step 1 (CN1). It has an angle equal to θ by Step 6. Therefore a parallelogram equal to the given rectilineal figure has been constructed in the given rectilineal angle, as required.
