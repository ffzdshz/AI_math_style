## Proposition 1.46
**Statement:** On a given straight line to describe a square.

**Step 1.** Let AB be the given finite straight line. Draw AX at right angles to AB at A (1.11). Produce AX until it is longer than AB (P2), and cut off AD equal to AB (1.3). Thus AD = AB and ∠DAB is right.

**Step 2.** Through B draw a straight line parallel to AD (1.31). On its ray lying on the same side of AB as D, take BC equal to AD, producing the ray sufficiently and cutting off the required length (P2, 1.3). Thus AD and BC are equal, parallel, and directed toward the same side of AB.

**Step 3.** Join D to C (P1). Since AD and BC are equal and parallel, the lines AB and DC joining their corresponding extremities are themselves equal and parallel (1.33). This is the direct path choice: 1.33 applies immediately to the equal parallel sides just constructed and supplies the remaining pair of sides.

**Step 4.** We have AB = AD, AD = BC, and AB = DC. Therefore all four sides of quadrilateral ABCD are equal (CN1).

**Step 5.** Since AD is parallel to BC, the transversal AB makes ∠DAB and ∠ABC together equal to two right angles (1.29). Since ∠DAB is right, ∠ABC is right (P4, CN3). Likewise, since AB is parallel to DC, the transversal AD makes ∠DAB and ∠ADC together equal to two right angles (1.29); hence ∠ADC is right (P4, CN3).

**Step 6.** Since AB is parallel to DC, the transversal BC makes ∠ABC and ∠BCD together equal to two right angles (1.29). Since ∠ABC is right, ∠BCD is also right (P4, CN3). Thus all four angles of ABCD are right.

**Step 7.** The quadrilateral ABCD is both equilateral and right-angled, and is therefore a square (D1.22). Hence a square has been described on the given straight line AB.
