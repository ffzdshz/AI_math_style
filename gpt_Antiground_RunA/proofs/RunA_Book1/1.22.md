## Proposition 1.22
**Statement:** Out of three straight lines, which are equal to three given straight lines, to construct a triangle: thus it is necessary that two of the straight lines taken together in any manner should be greater than the remaining one.

**Step 1.** The stated condition is necessary: in any triangle, two sides taken together in any manner are greater than the remaining side, by **1.20**. For the construction, let the given lengths be a, b, c, with a greatest, and suppose a+b>c, a+c>b, and b+c>a.

**Step 2.** Take the given line of length a itself as AB. If b<a, cut off AD=b from AB by **1.3**; if b=a, take D=B. Thus D lies on AB and AD=b. This is the direct path: retain a given line as the base and use **1.3**, rather than transfer that base to a new position.

**Step 3.** Describe the circle Γ with centre A and distance AD, and the circle Δ with centre B and distance c (**P3**). Produce BA beyond A to the opposite endpoint E of the diameter of Γ through D (**P2**, **D1.17**). Then AE=AD=b, since both are radii of Γ (**D1.15**).

**Step 4.** Because D lies between A and B, or coincides with B, we have BD=a−b; because A lies between B and E, we have BE=a+b (**CN2**, **CN3**). The assumed inequality b+c>a gives BD<c, while a+b>c gives BE>c. Hence D is inside Δ and E is outside Δ; if D=B, D is its centre and therefore inside it (**D1.15**).

**Step 5.** Consider either semicircular circumference of Γ from D to E (**D1.18**). It passes from a point inside Δ to a point outside Δ, and therefore meets the circumference bounding Δ. Choose such a meeting point C. Thus C lies on both circles (**D1.15**). Neither D nor E lies on Δ, by the strict inequalities in Step 4. The only points of Γ on its diameter-line DE are D and E, since a radius on either ray from A has the fixed length AD (**D1.15**, **CN5**). Consequently C does not lie on AB.

**Step 6.** Join AC and BC (**P1**). Since C belongs to Γ, AC=AD=b; since C belongs to Δ, BC=c (**D1.15**, **CN1**). Also AB=a by its selection in Step 2.

**Step 7.** The three noncollinear points A, B, C therefore determine the triangle ABC, bounded by AB, BC, and CA (**D1.19**). Its sides equal the three given straight lines respectively. Thus the required triangle has been constructed whenever every two given lines together exceed the remaining one; Step 1 establishes the necessity of that condition.
