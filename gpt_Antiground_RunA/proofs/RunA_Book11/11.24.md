## Proposition 11.24
**Statement:** If a solid be contained by parallel planes, the opposite planes in it are equal and parallelogrammic.

**Step 1.** Choose an arbitrary pair of opposite faces of the solid and name them ABCD and EFGH, with corresponding vertices A, E; B, F; C, G; and D, H. The other opposite pairs are ABFE and DCGH, and ADHE and BCGF. By hypothesis, the planes of each opposite pair are parallel. Their common sections, which form the edges, are straight lines [11.3].

**Step 2.** The plane ABCD cuts the parallel planes ABFE and DCGH in AB and DC; therefore AB is parallel to DC. It also cuts the parallel planes ADHE and BCGF in AD and BC; therefore AD is parallel to BC [11.16]. Thus ABCD is a parallelogram. The same argument applies to every face: each cuts the other two pairs of parallel bounding planes, so both pairs of its opposite sides are parallel [11.16]. Hence all six faces are parallelograms.

**Step 3.** In the parallelogram ABFE, opposite sides AB and EF are equal; in the parallelogram ADHE, opposite sides AD and EH are equal [1.34]. Consequently, AB = EF and AD = EH. Moreover, AB is parallel to EF, and AD is parallel to EH, by Step 2.

**Step 4.** The intersecting straight lines AB and AD are respectively parallel, in corresponding directions, to the intersecting straight lines EF and EH in the other plane. Therefore angle BAD equals angle FEH [11.10]. This supplies the included-angle equality directly from the parallel sides.

**Step 5.** Join BD and FH in their respective faces [P1]. The triangles BAD and FEH have BA = FE, AD = EH, and the included angle BAD equal to the included angle FEH. Therefore these triangles are equal [1.4]. Side–angle–side is the direct choice here because precisely these two side equalities and their included-angle equality have already been established.

**Step 6.** The diagonal BD bisects the parallelogram ABCD, and the diagonal FH bisects the parallelogram EFGH [1.34]. Thus ABCD and EFGH are respectively doubles of the equal triangles BAD and FEH, and are therefore equal [CN2]. Since the opposite pair was chosen arbitrarily, every pair of opposite faces is equal. Together with Step 2, this proves that the opposite faces of the solid are equal and parallelogrammic.
