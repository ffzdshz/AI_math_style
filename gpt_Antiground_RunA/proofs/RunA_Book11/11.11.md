## Proposition 11.11
**Statement:** From a given elevated point to draw a straight line perpendicular to a given plane.

**Step 1.** Let A be the given point outside the given plane Π. Take a straight line ℓ in Π and a point B on ℓ. Join AB [P1]. The intersecting lines AB and ℓ lie in one plane, which we call Σ [11.2].

**Step 2.** In Σ draw AD perpendicular to ℓ, with D on ℓ [1.12]. In Π draw the straight line m through D perpendicular to ℓ [1.11]. Since A is outside Π, AD and m are distinct; being intersecting straight lines, they lie in a plane Q [11.2].

**Step 3.** The line ℓ is perpendicular to both AD and m at their common point D. Therefore ℓ is perpendicular to their plane Q [11.4].

**Step 4.** Since A is outside Π and m lies in Π, A is not on m. In Q draw AF perpendicular to m, with F on m [1.12]. Thus F belongs to Π. We shall show that AF is the required perpendicular.

**Step 5.** If F=D, let n be ℓ; then n lies in Π and is perpendicular to Q by Step 3. If F≠D, draw through F in Π the line n parallel to ℓ [1.31]. Since ℓ is perpendicular to Q, n is also perpendicular to Q [11.8]. This uses the direct perpendicularity-transfer theorem rather than a separate comparison of angles. In either case, m and n are distinct: in the first case they are perpendicular by Step 2; in the second, m meets ℓ whereas n, being parallel to ℓ, does not [D1.23].

**Step 6.** The line AF lies in Q and meets n at F. Since n is perpendicular to Q, it makes right angles with AF [D11.3]. Also AF is perpendicular to m by Step 4. Hence AF is perpendicular to the two distinct lines m and n at their common point F, and therefore is perpendicular to their plane Π [11.4]. Thus from the given elevated point A we have drawn AF perpendicular to the given plane Π, as required.
