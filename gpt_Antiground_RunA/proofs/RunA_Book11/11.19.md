## Proposition 11.19
**Statement:** If two planes which cut one another be at right angles to any plane, their common section will also be at right angles to the same plane.

**Step 1.** Let the distinct planes α and β cut one another, and let each be at right angles to the plane π. Denote their common section by ℓ, and their respective common sections with π by a and b. The sections a and b exist by the hypothesis of perpendicular planes (D11.4), and all three common sections are straight lines (11.3).

**Step 2.** Choose a point X on ℓ. In α draw a straight line m through X perpendicular to a: use 1.11 if X lies on a, and 1.12 otherwise. Likewise, in β draw a straight line n through X perpendicular to b, using 1.11 if X lies on b and 1.12 otherwise. Thus m lies in α and n lies in β, and both pass through X (1.11, 1.12).

**Step 3.** Since α is perpendicular to π, the line m, drawn in α perpendicular to their common section a, is perpendicular to π. Likewise, since β is perpendicular to π, the line n is perpendicular to π (D11.4).

**Step 4.** If m and n were distinct, they would be parallel, since both are perpendicular to the same plane π (11.6). But both pass through X, whereas parallel straight lines do not meet (D1.23). Therefore m and n are the same straight line. This parallelism criterion is the direct choice here because it does not require X to lie in π.

**Step 5.** This coincident line lies in both α and β, so it is their common straight-line section ℓ (11.3). Since it is perpendicular to π by Step 3 (D11.4), ℓ is perpendicular to π. Therefore the common section of the two given planes is at right angles to the same plane, as required.
