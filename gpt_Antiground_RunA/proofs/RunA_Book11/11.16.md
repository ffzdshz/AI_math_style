## Proposition 11.16
**Statement:** If two parallel planes be cut by any plane, their common sections are parallel.

**Step 1.** Let the parallel planes α and β be cut by a plane γ, and denote their common sections with γ by ℓ and m, respectively. By 11.3, ℓ and m are straight lines; both lie in γ.

**Step 2.** By 11.1, the straight line ℓ remains in α and γ however far it is produced in either direction. Likewise, m remains in β and γ however far it is produced in either direction.

**Step 3.** If ℓ and m met at any point P, including upon production, Step 2 would place P in both α and β. Those planes would therefore meet, contrary to their being parallel (D11.8). Hence ℓ and m do not meet in either direction.

**Step 4.** The straight lines ℓ and m lie in the same plane γ and, when produced indefinitely in both directions, do not meet. Therefore they are parallel by D1.23. This direct defining criterion is the natural route, since coplanarity and non-intersection have already been established. Thus the common sections of the two parallel planes with the cutting plane are parallel.
