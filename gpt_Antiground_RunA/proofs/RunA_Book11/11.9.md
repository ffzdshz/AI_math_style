## Proposition 11.9
**Statement:** Straight lines which are parallel to the same straight line and are not in the same plane with it are also parallel to one another.

**Step 1.** Let a and b each be parallel to c, and suppose that no single plane contains all three lines. By the definition of parallel straight lines, a and c lie in a plane α, while b and c lie in a plane β. These planes are distinct by hypothesis. Choose a point O on c. [D1.23]

**Step 2.** In α draw a straight line x through O perpendicular to c, and in β draw a straight line y through O perpendicular to c. [1.11] The common section of α and β is c. Hence x and y are distinct: if they coincided, their common line would lie in that section and thus coincide with c, contrary to their construction as perpendiculars to c. [11.3]

**Step 3.** Since x and y intersect at O, they lie in a plane σ. [11.2] The line c is perpendicular to both x and y at their common point O; therefore c is perpendicular to σ. [11.4]

**Step 4.** Since a is parallel to c and c is perpendicular to σ, a is also perpendicular to σ. Likewise, since b is parallel to c, b is perpendicular to σ. [11.8]

**Step 5.** The straight lines a and b are perpendicular to the same plane σ; therefore they are parallel to one another. [11.6] This is the natural concluding criterion: the common perpendicular plane allows 11.6 to establish parallelism without first assuming that a and b are coplanar. Thus the required proposition is proved.
