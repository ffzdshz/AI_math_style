## Proposition 11.39
**Statement:** If there be two prisms of equal height, and one have a parallelogram as base and the other a triangle, and if the parallelogram be double of the triangle, the prisms will be equal.

**Step 1.** Use the side-base convention for the first prism: its parallelogram base is a lateral face of a triangular-ended prism. Let this prism be P, with supporting base ABCD, triangular ends ABE and DCF, and opposite edge EF. Let the other prism Q have triangular base KLM and opposite triangular face K′L′M′. Their common height is h, and ABCD is double triangle KLM. The remaining faces of each prism are parallelograms. (Hypothesis; D11.13.)

**Step 2.** Complete ABE and DCF to parallelograms ABGE and DCHF by drawing the requisite parallels. This completes P to a parallelepiped R with base ABCD and opposite face EGHF. Since EG is parallel to AB and EF is parallel to AD, the plane EGHF is parallel to ABCD. It contains the opposite edge EF of P, so R has the same height h as P. (1.31; 11.15; D6.4.)

**Step 3.** The plane BCEF passes through BE and CF, which are diagonals of the opposite faces ABGE and DCHF of R. It therefore bisects R. One of its two parts is precisely P; hence R is double P. (11.28.)

**Step 4.** Similarly, complete KLM to the parallelogram KLNM, whose diagonal is LM, and complete K′L′M′ correspondingly to K′L′N′M′. These completions give a parallelepiped S containing Q. Its base and opposite face remain in the two original triangular-face planes, so its height is h. Moreover, the diagonal LM bisects KLNM; consequently KLNM is double triangle KLM. (1.31; D11.13; D6.4; 1.34.)

**Step 5.** The plane through LM and L′M′ passes through diagonals of the opposite faces KLNM and K′L′N′M′ of S. It therefore bisects S, and one of the resulting parts is Q. Thus S is double Q. (11.28.)

**Step 6.** By hypothesis ABCD is double triangle KLM, and by Step 4 so is KLNM. Hence the bases ABCD and KLNM are equal. The parallelepipeds R and S also have the same height h, so they are equal. This is the direct equal-base, equal-height route; no general proportionality theorem for solids is required. (CN1; 11.31.)

**Step 7.** Since R is double P and S is double Q, the ratios R:P and S:Q are equal: for any positive integers m and n, the comparison of mR with nP is determined by the comparison of 2m with n, exactly as is the comparison of mS with nQ. Since R=S, one and the same magnitude therefore has the same ratio to P and Q. It follows that P=Q. Thus the two prisms are equal. (D5.5; 5.9.)
