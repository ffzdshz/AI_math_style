## Proposition 11.38
**Statement:** If the sides of the opposite planes of a cube be bisected, and planes be carried through the points of section, the common section of the planes and the diameter of the cube bisect one another.

**Step 1.** Let ABCD and EFGH be the opposite square faces of the cube, with AE, BF, CG, DH joining corresponding vertices. Let K, L, M, N bisect AB, BC, CD, DA respectively, and let K′, L′, M′, N′ bisect EF, FG, GH, HE respectively. Denote the cutting planes through K, M, K′, M′ and through L, N, L′, N′ by π and σ. Join AC, EG, and the cube diameter AG. We shall work in the diagonal plane containing these lines, reducing the conclusion to a comparison of plane triangles. [D11.25, P1]

**Step 2.** The opposite sides of each square face are equal and parallel. Thus KB and MC are equal and parallel, and 1.33 gives KM ∥ BC. Likewise BL and AN are equal and parallel, so 1.33 gives LN ∥ AB. Here the equalities of the half-sides follow because halves of equal magnitudes are equal. The same argument applies to the upper face. [D1.22, 1.28, CN2, CN5, 1.33]

**Step 3.** Let KM meet AC at O. In triangle ABC, because KO ∥ BC, we have AK:KB = AO:OC. Since AK = KB, it follows that AO = OC. Similarly, in triangle CBA, the line LN, being parallel to BA and passing through the midpoint L of CB, meets CA at its midpoint. Consequently LN also passes through O, since a segment has only one midpoint. Applying the identical argument in EFGH, the lines K′M′ and L′N′ meet at a point O′ satisfying EO′ = O′G. [6.2, D5.5, CN5]

**Step 4.** The traces of π on the two opposite faces are KM and K′M′, while those of σ are LN and L′N′. Hence both cutting planes contain O and O′. Their common section is therefore the straight line OO′, whose portion within the cube is the segment between these opposite faces. [11.3]

**Step 5.** The square lateral faces give AE ∥ BF and BF ∥ CG; therefore AE ∥ CG. They also give AE = AB = BC = CG. The parallel edges AE and CG lie in one plane, and their joining lines AC and EG lie in that plane. Since AE and CG are equal and parallel, 1.33 gives AC ∥ EG and AC = EG. Thus ACGE is a parallelogram containing AG, O, and O′. [D1.22, 1.28, 11.9, CN1, 11.7, 1.33]

**Step 6.** In this parallelogram, O lies inside AC and O′ lies inside EG, on opposite sides of the diagonal AG. Hence OO′ meets AG at an interior point X. Since AO and GO′ are halves of the equal segments AC and EG, we have AO = GO′. Moreover, AC ∥ EG gives ∠OAX = ∠O′GX and ∠AOX = ∠GO′X, by alternate angles with the transversals AG and OO′ respectively. [Step 5, CN2, CN5, 1.29]

**Step 7.** The triangles AOX and GO′X therefore have two corresponding angles equal and the intervening sides AO and GO′ equal. By 1.26—the congruence criterion directly supplied by these parallel-line angle equalities—we obtain AX = XG and OX = XO′. Thus the diameter AG and the common section OO′ of the cutting planes bisect one another, as required. [1.26]
