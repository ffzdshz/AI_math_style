## Proposition 11.33
**Statement:** Similar parallelepipedal solids are to one another in the triplicate ratio of their corresponding sides.

**Step 1.** Let S and T be similar parallelepipedal solids. Let OA, OB, OC be the three edges of S issuing from one vertex, and let a, b, c denote the lengths of the corresponding edges of T. Their corresponding faces are similar, so
OA : a = OB : b = OC : c.
This follows by applying the proportionality of corresponding sides to the faces meeting at the chosen vertices. (D11.9, D6.1, 5.11.)

**Step 2.** On the ray OA, produced if necessary, take OX = a. (P2, 1.3.) Apply 11.27 on OX, using the already given solid angle of S at O, to construct a parallelepiped Q similar and similarly situated to S. Its other edges at O lie along the rays OB and OC; call them OY and OZ. Thus
OA : OX = OB : OY = OC : OZ.
Since OX = a, comparison with Step 1 gives OB : OY = OB : b and OC : OZ = OC : c. Consequently OY = b and OZ = c. (11.27, D6.1, 5.11, 5.9.)

**Step 3.** Corresponding faces of Q and T are similar, since each is similar to the corresponding face of S. (6.21.) For the three pairs of faces meeting at the chosen vertices, corresponding sides are equal by Step 2. These faces are therefore equal: by 6.20 their ratio is the duplicate ratio of equal sides, which is the ratio of equality. (6.20, D5.9, 5.9.) The other three pairs of faces are also equal, since opposite faces of each parallelepiped are equal. (11.24, CN1.) Hence Q and T are equal and similar solids; in particular, Q = T. (D11.10.)

**Step 4.** Complete two intermediate parallelepipeds: U with edges OX, OB, OC at O, and V with edges OX, OY, OC at O. The necessary parallels can be drawn by 1.31, and the opposite face planes are parallel by 11.15. Write [R,W] for the parallelogram with adjacent sides OR and OW. Our path is to change one edge at a time: each successive pair will have the same height, so 11.32 applies directly.

**Step 5.** Regard S and U as standing on the bases [A,B] and [X,B] in the plane OAB. Their opposite faces lie in the same parallel plane through C, so their heights are equal. (D6.4.) Therefore
S : U = [A,B] : [X,B]. (11.32.)
The two base parallelograms themselves have equal heights when OA and OX are taken as their bases: their opposite sides lie on the same parallel through B. Hence
[A,B] : [X,B] = OA : OX,
and consequently S : U = OA : OX. (6.1, 5.11.)

**Step 6.** Similarly, U and V stand on [X,B] and [X,Y] in the plane OAB, with their opposite faces in the same parallel plane through C. They therefore have equal heights. Their base parallelograms have equal heights when OB and OY are taken as bases, since their opposite sides lie on the same parallel through X. Thus
U : V = [X,B] : [X,Y] = OB : OY.
(D6.4, 11.32, 6.1, 5.11.)

**Step 7.** Now regard V and Q as standing on [Y,C] and [Y,Z] in the plane OBC. Their opposite faces lie in the same parallel plane through X, so these solids also have equal heights. The two base parallelograms have equal heights when OC and OZ are taken as bases, since their opposite sides lie on the same parallel through Y. Consequently
V : Q = [Y,C] : [Y,Z] = OC : OZ.
(D6.4, 11.32, 6.1, 5.11.)

**Step 8.** Combining Steps 2 and 5–7 gives
S : U = U : V = V : Q = OA : OX. (5.11.)
Thus S, U, V, Q are four continuously proportional magnitudes. By the definition of triplicate ratio, S : Q is the triplicate ratio of OA : OX. (D5.10.) Since Q = T and OX = a, substitution of equals gives that S : T is the triplicate ratio of OA : a. (5.7.) The chosen edges were corresponding edges of the given solids. Therefore similar parallelepipedal solids are to one another in the triplicate ratio of their corresponding sides.
