## Proposition 11.25
**Statement:** If a parallelepipedal solid be cut by a plane which is parallel to the opposite planes, then, as the base is to the base, so will the solid be to the solid.

**Step 1.** Label the opposite faces of the given parallelepiped ABCD and A′B′C′D′, with corresponding vertices joined. Let the cutting plane be parallel to ADD′A′ and BCC′B′, meeting AB, DC, A′B′, D′C′ at E, F, E′, F′ respectively. Denote the base parallelograms AEFD and EBCF by B₁ and B₂, and the solids standing on them by S₁ and S₂. The sections are parallel to the corresponding face edges, and the two portions have parallelogrammic faces, by 11.16 and 11.24. We must prove B₁:B₂ = S₁:S₂.

**Step 2.** Path choice: use the equimultiple criterion D5.5 directly, constructing arbitrary equimultiples of both bases and solids within the same four lateral planes. Let m and n be arbitrary positive integers. Produce AB, DC, A′B′, D′C′ as necessary (P2). Starting at A on the ray AB, lay off m consecutive segments each equal to AE, ending at M; separately, starting again at A, lay off n consecutive segments each equal to EB, ending at N (1.3). Through every division point X draw, in the planes ABCD and ABB′A′, lines parallel to AD and AA′ respectively (1.31). These intersecting lines lie in a plane by 11.2, and that plane is parallel to ADD′A′ by 11.15. Use these planes as sections of the continued solid. Let U and V be the whole solids from ADD′A′ to the sections through M and N, and let R and T be their respective bases in the plane ABCD.

**Step 3.** Each newly bounded portion is contained by three pairs of parallel planes, so its faces are parallelograms (11.24). The edges of its transverse sections are parallel to the corresponding edges of ADD′A′ (11.16). Applying 1.34 to the lateral parallelograms between the initial section and any new section shows that every transverse edge has the corresponding fixed length AD or AA′. Applying 1.34 within each cell shows that its four longitudinal edges have the length of its segment on AB. Consequently, every cell in the first subdivision has corresponding edge lengths equal to those of S₁, and every cell in the second has corresponding edge lengths equal to those of S₂ (CN1).

**Step 4.** Compare a cell with its corresponding original solid S₁ or S₂. Corresponding lateral faces are parallelograms on equal bases and between the same parallel longitudinal lines; therefore they are equal by 1.36. This equality theorem is sufficient here because the bases being compared are equal. Corresponding end faces are equal by 11.24 applied to the portion between their section planes, or by CN4 when they coincide. Corresponding face angles are equal by 1.29 for faces in the same lateral plane and by 11.10 for end faces in distinct parallel planes. Together with Step 3, these angle and side equalities make the corresponding faces similar (D6.1). Thus the six corresponding faces are equal and similar, and the cell and its original solid are equal by D11.10. Their corresponding bases are equal as well. Hence the first subdivision consists of m cells equal to S₁ with bases equal to B₁, and the second of n cells equal to S₂ with bases equal to B₂.

**Step 5.** Adding these equal cells and their equal bases gives
R = mB₁,  U = mS₁,  T = nB₂,  V = nS₂,
where each multiple denotes repeated addition (CN2; D5.2). Thus R and U are corresponding equimultiples of B₁ and S₁, while T and V are corresponding equimultiples of B₂ and S₂.

**Step 6.** Both rows begin at the same end face and occupy the same four lateral planes. If M lies farther along the ray AB than N, then R contains T as a proper part, and U contains V as a proper part; hence R > T and U > V (CN5). If M and N coincide, the bases coincide and the solids coincide, so R = T and U = V (CN4). If M lies nearer A than N, the containments are reversed, so R < T and U < V (CN5). These cases exhaust the possible positions of M and N.

**Step 7.** Therefore, for every choice of positive integers m and n, mB₁ and nB₂ have exactly the same comparison as mS₁ and nS₂. By D5.5, B₁:B₂ = S₁:S₂. Thus, as the base is to the base, so is the solid to the solid.
