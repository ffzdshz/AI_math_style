## Proposition 11.13
**Statement:** From the same point two straight lines cannot be set up at right angles to the same plane on the same side.

**Step 1.** Suppose, for contradiction, that two distinct rays OA and OB are set up from the same point O perpendicular to the same plane Π and on the same side of it. Their supporting straight lines are distinct: otherwise the rays would either coincide or lie on opposite sides of Π. Since both straight lines are perpendicular to Π, they are parallel by **11.6**. This is the direct path: apply the established parallelism theorem without constructing an auxiliary plane.

**Step 2.** By **D1.23**, parallel straight lines do not meet, even when produced indefinitely. But the supporting lines of OA and OB both contain O, a contradiction. Therefore, from the same point two distinct straight lines cannot be set up at right angles to the same plane on the same side.
