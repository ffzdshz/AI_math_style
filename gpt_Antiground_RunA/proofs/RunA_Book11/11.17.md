## Proposition 11.17
**Statement:** If two straight lines be cut by parallel planes, they will be cut in the same ratios.

**Step 1.** Let α, β, γ be three parallel planes, with β between α and γ. These planes do not meet one another [D11.8]. Let them cut the first straight line at A, B, C, respectively, and the second at D, E, F, respectively. Thus B lies between A and C, and E between D and F. We must prove AB:BC = DE:EF.

**Step 2.** First suppose that A ≠ D and C ≠ F. Join AF, AD, and CF [P1]. The triangles ACF and ADF each lie in a plane [11.2]. Let AF meet the middle plane β at X, between A and F. The chosen route is to obtain parallel sections in these two triangle planes and then use proportional division directly, rather than establish similarity.

**Step 3.** In the plane of triangle ACF, the sections with β and γ are the straight lines BX and CF [11.3]. Since β and γ are parallel, BX ∥ CF [11.16]. Similarly, in the plane of triangle ADF, the sections with α and β are AD and EX [11.3]; hence AD ∥ EX [11.16].

**Step 4.** In triangle ACF, since BX is parallel to CF, proportional division gives
AB:BC = AX:XF [6.2].
In triangle FDA, since EX is parallel to DA, proportional division gives
FE:ED = FX:XA [6.2].
Reversing both ratios gives DE:EF = AX:XF. Such reversal preserves equality of ratios because the defining comparisons of equimultiples are reversed together [D5.5].

**Step 5.** The ratios AB:BC and DE:EF are therefore each equal to AX:XF. Consequently,
AB:BC = DE:EF [5.11].

**Step 6.** The cases of a common outer intersection require only one triangle. If A = D, form triangle ACF [P1, 11.2]. Its middle-plane section BE is parallel to CF [11.3, 11.16], so AB:BC = AE:EF = DE:EF [6.2]. If C = F, form triangle CAD [P1, 11.2]. Its middle-plane section BE is parallel to AD [11.3, 11.16], so CB:BA = CE:ED [6.2]; reversing both ratios gives AB:BC = ED:CE = DE:EF [D5.5]. If the two straight lines coincide, their corresponding segments coincide, and the required ratios are identical [D5.5]. Thus in every case the corresponding intercepted segments are proportional. Since the three parallel planes were arbitrary, two straight lines cut by parallel planes are cut in the same ratios.
