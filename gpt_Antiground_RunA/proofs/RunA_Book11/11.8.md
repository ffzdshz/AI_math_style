## Proposition 11.8
**Statement:** If two straight lines be parallel, and one of them be at right angles to any plane, the remaining one will also be at right angles to the same plane.

**Step 1.** Let l and m be parallel straight lines, and let l be perpendicular to the plane Π at A. The parallel lines lie in a common plane Σ [D1.23]. This plane is distinct from Π because it contains l; their common section is therefore a straight line t through A [11.3]. Since t lies in Π, l is perpendicular to t [D11.3].

**Step 2.** The line m meets t. For otherwise m would be parallel to t in Σ [D1.23]; since l is also parallel to m, l would be parallel to t [1.30], contradicting their meeting at A. Let B be the intersection of m and t. Then B differs from A [D1.23], and m is perpendicular to t by the equality of corresponding angles made by a transversal with parallel lines [1.29]. Moreover, m is not contained in Π: otherwise it would lie in the common section t, contrary to its being perpendicular to t [11.3].

**Step 3.** In Π draw s through B perpendicular to t [1.11]. Since m is not contained in Π, m and s are distinct intersecting lines; let Λ be a plane containing them [11.2]. The line t is perpendicular to both m and s at B, so t is perpendicular to Λ [11.4].

**Step 4.** In Λ draw n through B perpendicular to s [1.11]. Because t is perpendicular to Λ and n lies in Λ through B, t is perpendicular to n [D11.3]. Thus n is perpendicular to both s and t at their intersection B. Since s and t lie in Π, n is perpendicular to Π [11.4]. This is the chosen two-line route: use 11.4 to obtain a normal at B, rather than verify perpendicularity to every line of Π separately.

**Step 5.** Now l and n are perpendicular to the same plane Π, so the directly applicable proposition 11.6 gives l parallel to n [11.6]. Let Ω be their common plane [D1.23]. Both Ω and Σ contain the entire line l and also the point B, which is not on l. Consequently Ω and Σ coincide: if they were distinct, their common section would be a single straight line, which could not contain both l and a point outside l [11.3]. Hence n and m both lie in Σ.

**Step 6.** If n and m were distinct, then, being in Σ and both parallel to l, they would be parallel to one another [1.30]. But they meet at B, contrary to the definition of parallel lines [D1.23]. Therefore n and m coincide. Since n is perpendicular to Π by Step 4, m is perpendicular to Π, as required.
