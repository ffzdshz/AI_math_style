## Proposition 11.34
**Statement:** In equal parallelepipedal solids the bases are reciprocally proportional to the heights; and those parallelepipedal solids in which the bases are reciprocally proportional to the heights are equal.

**Step 1.** Let S and T be parallelepipedal solids with bases A and B and perpendicular heights h and k, respectively. We must prove that S = T if and only if A : B = k : h. Construct a rectangle R equal in area to B, using 1.45 with a right angle, and denote its adjacent side lengths by u and v. [1.45]

**Step 2.** On R construct rectangular parallelepipeds U and V of heights h and k, respectively. For this, erect a perpendicular to the plane of R at a vertex, produce it as necessary, mark off the prescribed heights, and complete the solids by parallels. The edges parallel to the erected perpendicular are also perpendicular to the base plane, and the opposite face planes are parallel. [11.12, P2, 1.3, 1.31, 11.8, 11.15]

**Step 3.** Since T and V have equal bases B and R and the same height k, they are equal: T = V. [11.31] Path choice: use the equal-base, equal-height theorem for this replacement, and then use the equal-height ratio theorem for the comparisons below. The rectangular construction allows side faces to serve as bases, converting the comparison of heights into a comparison of areas.

**Step 4.** The solids S and U have the same height h. Therefore their ratio is the ratio of their bases. Since R = B,
S : U = A : R = A : B. [11.32, 5.7]

**Step 5.** Let Fₖ and Fₕ be the rectangular side faces of V and U erected on the same side of R of length u. Viewed as parallelograms with bases of lengths k and h, these faces have the same height u. Hence
Fₖ : Fₕ = k : h. [6.1]
Now regard Fₖ and Fₕ as the bases of V and U. In each solid the edge of length v is perpendicular to the two intersecting edge directions in the chosen base, and therefore perpendicular to its plane. Thus both solids have height v relative to these bases. [11.4] Applying the equal-height ratio theorem gives
V : U = Fₖ : Fₕ = k : h. [11.32, 5.11]

**Step 6.** Suppose first that S = T. Since T = V by Step 3, S = V. [CN1] Equal magnitudes have the same ratio to U, so S : U = V : U. [5.7] Combining this equality with Steps 4 and 5 yields
A : B = k : h.
Thus equal parallelepipedal solids have their bases reciprocally proportional to their heights. [5.11]

**Step 7.** Conversely, suppose that A : B = k : h. Steps 4 and 5 then give S : U = V : U. [5.11] Magnitudes having the same ratio to the same magnitude are equal, so S = V. [5.9] Since V = T by Step 3, S = T. [CN1] Therefore parallelepipedal solids whose bases are reciprocally proportional to their heights are equal, completing both directions.
