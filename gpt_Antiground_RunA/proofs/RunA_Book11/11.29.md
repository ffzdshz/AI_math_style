## Proposition 11.29
**Statement:** Parallelepipedal solids which are on the same base and of the same height, and in which the extremities of the sides which stand up are on the same straight lines, are equal to one another.

**Step 1.** Let the two solids be S = ABCD–EFGH and T = ABCD–KLMN, with common base ABCD and standing edges AE, BF, CG, DH and AK, BL, CM, DN respectively. Label the vertices so that E, F, K, L lie on one straight line ℓ, and H, G, N, M on another straight line m, as the hypothesis permits. All their faces are parallelograms, and opposite faces are equal (11.24); their opposite edges are equal and parallel (1.34). Thus ℓ and m are parallel. The top vertices are coplanar; likewise A, B, E, F, K, L are coplanar, and D, C, H, G, N, M are coplanar (11.7).

**Step 2.** The joining edges EH, FG, KN, LM are equal and parallel, each being equal and parallel to AD (1.34, 11.9). If E = K, the corresponding upper vertices therefore coincide, and S = T by CN4. Otherwise, interchange the names of the solids if necessary so that K lies beyond E in the direction from E to F. Since EF = KL, the points E, F, L and E, K, L occur in these respective orders. Subtracting EF = KL from the common segment EL gives EK = FL (CN3). The corresponding orders on m are H, G, M and H, N, M; subtracting HG = NM from HM similarly gives HN = GM (1.34, CN3). No assumption is made about whether F precedes or follows K.

**Step 3.** Join E to L and H to M (P1), and consider the enclosing solid R bounded by the faces ABCD, ELMH, ABLE, DCMH, ADHE, and BCML. Its boundary gives two dissections (D11.2). Cutting along BCGF leaves S and the end-solid V whose triangular faces are BFL and CGM. Cutting along ADNK leaves T and the end-solid U whose triangular faces are AEK and DHN. Indeed, on the front face, BF divides ABLE into ABFE and BFL, while AK divides it into ABLK and AEK; the corresponding divisions occur on the opposite face. Thus R consists of S together with V, and also of T together with U, with disjoint interiors in each dissection. We use this common enclosure so that overlapping and nonoverlapping positions of the two upper faces require no separate cases.

**Step 4.** The triangular faces AEK and BFL are equal and similar: AE = BF and AK = BL by 1.34, while EK = FL by Step 2, so 1.8 applies. Likewise, DH = CG, DN = CM, and HN = GM, so DHN and CGM are equal and similar by 1.8 and D6.1. Side–side–side is the direct congruence criterion here because all three corresponding side equalities are already available.

**Step 5.** Two pairs of the remaining faces of U and V are
AEHD and BFGC, and AKND and BLMC.
The first pair consists of opposite faces of S, and the second of opposite faces of T; hence each pair is equal (11.24). Their corresponding sides are equal by 1.34, and their corresponding angles are equal because the sides containing them are respectively parallel (11.10). Therefore these face pairs are also similar (D6.1).

**Step 6.** The last faces are EKNH and FLMG. They are parallelograms: EK and HN lie on the parallel lines ℓ and m, as do FL and GM, while EH ∥ KN and FG ∥ LM by Step 2. Their bases EK and FL are equal, and they lie between the same parallels ℓ and m. Consequently they are equal by 1.36, which applies directly to these equal, though not necessarily identical, bases. Moreover, EH = FG by 1.34, their other corresponding sides are equal by 1.34 and Step 2, and their corresponding angles are equal by 1.29. Hence they are similar as well (D6.1).

**Step 7.** The five boundary faces of U are therefore respectively equal and similar to the five boundary faces of V, with the correspondence A ↔ B, E ↔ F, K ↔ L, D ↔ C, H ↔ G, N ↔ M. Thus U and V are equal and similar solids (D11.10), and in particular U = V.

**Step 8.** From the same enclosing solid R, subtract the equal end-solids U and V. The respective remainders are T and S by Step 3; therefore T = S (CN3). Hence parallelepipedal solids on the same base and of the same height, whose standing edges terminate on the same straight lines, are equal to one another.
