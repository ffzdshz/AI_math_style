## Proposition 11.1
**Statement:** A part of a straight line cannot be in the plane of reference and a part in a plane more elevated.

**Step 1.** Choose the direct straight-production route. Suppose, for a contradiction, that a straight line ABC has its part AB in a plane Π, while its continuation BC rises out of Π. Here A, B, C occur in that order, and ABC is straight throughout, hence lies evenly with its points. [D1.4; supposition for contradiction.]

**Step 2.** In the plane Π, produce AB continuously in a straight line through B to D, taking the production BD longer than BC. This is straight production carried out in the given plane. [P2; D1.7.]

**Step 3.** BC and BD are both straight continuations of the same segment AB beyond B. They cannot diverge: a continuation departing from the straight course already fixed by the points of AB would no longer lie evenly with those points. Thus straight continuations cannot separate after a common segment, and BC coincides with the initial part of BD of its own length. [D1.4.]

**Step 4.** Since BD lies in Π, its coincident part BC also lies in Π, contrary to the supposition that BC rises out of Π. Therefore a part of a straight line cannot be in the plane of reference and another part in a plane more elevated. [Steps 1–3.]
