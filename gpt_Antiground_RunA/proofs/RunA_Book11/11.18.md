## Proposition 11.18
**Statement:** If a straight line be at right angles to any plane, all the planes through it will also be at right angles to the same plane.

**Step 1.** Let the straight line l be perpendicular to the plane α at O, and let β be any plane containing l. The common section of α and β is a straight line s through O (11.3). We take the direct route of verifying that every straight line in β perpendicular to s is perpendicular to α, as required by D11.4.

**Step 2.** Since s lies in α and meets l at O, the given perpendicularity of l to α implies that l is perpendicular to s (D11.3).

**Step 3.** Let X be any point of s different from O, and let m be any straight line in β through X perpendicular to s. The lines l and m are coplanar, and the transversal s makes equal corresponding right angles with them (P4). Hence m is parallel to l (1.28). This parallelism criterion applies directly because the corresponding angles are right angles. Since l is perpendicular to α, its parallel m is also perpendicular to α (11.8).

**Step 4.** At O, any straight line m in β perpendicular to s must coincide with l. Indeed, choose a ray of m and a ray of l on opposite sides of s. With a fixed ray of s from O, they make adjacent angles whose sum is two right angles. Therefore those two rays lie in a straight line, so m and l coincide (1.14). Consequently m is perpendicular to α by the hypothesis on l.

**Step 5.** Thus every straight line drawn in β at right angles to the common section s is perpendicular to α. Therefore β is at right angles to α (D11.4). Since β was an arbitrary plane through l, all planes through l are at right angles to α, as required.
