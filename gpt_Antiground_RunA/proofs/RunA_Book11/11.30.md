## Proposition 11.30
**Statement:** Parallelepipedal solids which are on the same base and of the same height, and in which the extremities of the sides which stand up are not on the same straight lines, are equal to one another.

**Step 1.** Let the given parallelepipedal solids be S = ABCD–EFGH and T = ABCD–KLMN, with standing edges AE, BF, CG, DH and AK, BL, CM, DN, respectively. Their faces are parallelograms (11.24). Let α be the plane of their common base ABCD. Their opposite faces lie in a common plane β parallel to α: these faces are parallel to the base and, by the equal-height hypothesis, are at the same perpendicular distance above it (D6.4).

**Step 2.** We choose to construct one intermediate parallelepiped and apply 11.29 twice. This directly uses the preceding equality theorem rather than introducing a subdivision or a proportional comparison. The intersections of the side-face planes with the parallel planes α and β give EF ∥ AB, GH ∥ CD, KN ∥ AD, and LM ∥ BC (11.16). Since ABCD is a parallelogram, transitivity of parallels gives EF ∥ GH ∥ AB and KN ∥ LM ∥ AD (1.30; 11.9).

**Step 3.** Produce the upper edges as necessary and define
X = EF ∩ KN, Y = EF ∩ LM, Z = GH ∩ LM, and W = GH ∩ KN (P2).
These intersections exist. Indeed, all four lines lie in β; the first pair has the direction of AB and the second pair the direction of AD. Since AB and AD meet, transitivity of parallels shows that no line of the first pair can be parallel to, or coincide with, a line of the second pair (1.30; 11.9). Coplanar lines which are not parallel meet when sufficiently produced (D1.23). Thus XYZW is a parallelogram, its opposite sides lying on EF and GH, and on KN and LM.

**Step 4.** Form the solid U bounded by α and β, by the two side-face planes ABEF and DCGH of S, and by the two side-face planes ADNK and BCML of T. These six planes occur in three parallel pairs. Their traces on α bound ABCD, and their traces on β bound XYZW. Their remaining intersections are the straight standing edges AX, BY, CZ, and DW (11.3). Hence U is a parallelepipedal solid, with parallelogrammic faces (11.24), on the same base ABCD and of the same height as S and T, since it lies between α and β (D6.4).

**Step 5.** Compare S and U. They have the same base and height. The upper extremities E, F, X, Y lie on the same straight line EF, while G, H, Z, W lie on the same straight line GH. Consequently S = U by 11.29.

**Step 6.** Compare U and T. Again they have the same base and height. The upper extremities X, W, K, N lie on the same straight line KN, while Y, Z, L, M lie on the same straight line LM. Consequently U = T by 11.29.

**Step 7.** Since S and T are equal to the same intermediate solid U, they are equal to one another (CN1). Therefore parallelepipedal solids on the same base and of the same height are equal in the stated case.
