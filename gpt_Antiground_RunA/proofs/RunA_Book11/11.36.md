## Proposition 11.36
**Statement:** If three straight lines be proportional, the parallelepipedal solid formed out of the three is equal to the parallelepipedal solid on the mean which is equilateral, but equiangular with the aforesaid solid.

**Step 1.** Let the three given lengths be a, b, c, with a:b = b:c, expressing their proportionality (D5.6). Let S be the parallelepiped with coinitial edges OA = a, OB = c, OC = b. Let T be the equilateral parallelepiped equiangular with S, with corresponding edges O′A′ = O′B′ = O′C′ = b. Choose the faces through OA, OB and O′A′, O′B′ as bases. This choice places the extreme lengths in the first base and leaves equal standing edges in the two solids.

**Step 2.** The chosen bases are equiangular by hypothesis, and
OA:O′A′ = O′B′:OB,
since a:b = b:c. Their sides about corresponding equal angles are therefore reciprocally proportional. Hence the two bases are equal by 6.14.

**Step 3.** Suppose first that either standing edge, OC or O′C′, is perpendicular to its base plane. Its angles with both base edges are then right (D11.3). By equiangularity, the corresponding angles in the other solid are also right; consequently its standing edge is likewise perpendicular to its base plane (11.4). In this case the heights are the standing edges themselves, both of length b, and are therefore equal.

**Step 4.** In the remaining case neither standing edge is perpendicular to its base. Draw CH and C′H′ perpendicular to the respective base planes (11.11), and join OH and O′H′ (P1). Here H differs from O and H′ differs from O′, since otherwise the corresponding standing edge would be perpendicular to its base. The base angles AOB and A′O′B′ are equal, and the elevated edges OC and O′C′ make equal angles with the corresponding base edges, by the assumed equiangularity. Therefore 11.35 gives
∠COH = ∠C′O′H′.

**Step 5.** The angles CHO and C′H′O′ are right by D11.3, and hence equal by P4. Thus triangles COH and C′O′H′ have two corresponding angles equal and the corresponding sides OC and O′C′, opposite their right angles, equal to b. By 1.26, CH = C′H′. These perpendiculars are the heights of the respective solids, so their heights are equal in this case also.

**Step 6.** The solids have equal bases by Step 2 and equal heights by Steps 3–5. The equal-base, equal-height criterion 11.31 therefore gives S = T. Hence the parallelepipedal solid formed from the three proportional straight lines is equal to the equilateral parallelepipedal solid on the mean which is equiangular with it.
