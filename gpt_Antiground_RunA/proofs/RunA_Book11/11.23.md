## Proposition 11.23
**Statement:** To construct a solid angle out of three plane angles two of which, taken together in any manner, are greater than the remaining one: thus the three angles must be less than four right angles.

**Step 1.** Let the given angles be α, β, γ, and let R denote a right angle. Assume that each pair exceeds the remaining angle and that α + β + γ < 4R. The latter is the necessary proviso stated in the proposition, since every solid angle has its containing plane angles together less than four right angles (11.21). Subtraction gives
|α − β| < γ < min(α + β, 4R − α − β).
(CN3.) We choose a planar-chord construction followed by a perpendicular lift; this avoids cases concerning the position of a circumcentre.

**Step 2.** In a plane Π describe a circle Γ with centre O and radius r. Choose points A, B, C on Γ so that ∠AOB = α and ∠AOC = β, with B and C on the same side of OA (P3, 1.23, D1.15). Reflect C across the line OA: draw CH perpendicular to OA, and produce CH to D so that HD = HC (1.12, P2, 1.3). The right triangles OHC and OHD show that OD = OC = r and ∠AOD = β; when H = O these conclusions are immediate. Thus D belongs to Γ and lies on the opposite side of OA from C (1.4, P4, 1.13). In particular, CD is perpendicular to OA.

**Step 3.** Choose X on Γ so that ∠BOX = γ, and join the required points (1.23, P1). Put a = BX. The smaller angle between OB and OC is |α − β|, while that between OB and OD is δ = min(α + β, 4R − α − β) (1.13). Therefore Step 1 and the comparison of triangles with two equal sides give
BC < a < BD.
(1.24.) The boundary configurations require no exception to this conclusion: if B = C, the first inequality is immediate; if δ = 2R, then BD = 2r and BX < BO + OX = 2r by 1.20.

**Step 4.** We use the following projection form of 2.12 and 2.13. For a fixed segment OP = r and a point Z in Π, let u(Z) be the directed distance from O to the perpendicular projection of Z on the line OP, positive towards P. Then
PZ² − OZ² = r² − 2r·u(Z).
Here squares and products denote square and rectangular areas, with a negative directed product recording subtraction. This identity is precisely the obtuse- and acute-angle square relations of 2.12 and 2.13; the right-angle and collinear configurations follow from 1.47 and 2.4, respectively (CN2, CN3).

**Step 5.** Drop XK perpendicular to OB, and draw through K the line ℓ perpendicular to OB (1.12, 1.11). Apply Step 4 with P = B. Since OC = OX = OD = r, the inequalities BC < BX < BD imply
u(C) > u(X) > u(D),
where the projections are now taken on OB. Thus the projections of C and D lie strictly on opposite sides of K. Consequently ℓ meets the segment CD in an interior point T: the perpendicular projection lines are parallel, and their order on OB gives this interior intersection by the parallel-line angle relations and P5 (1.29, P4). Since T lies on ℓ, u(T) = u(X).

**Step 6.** The point T is strictly inside the chord CD, and hence inside Γ (3.2). Therefore OT < r (CN5). This strict inequality is what will give the constructed vertex a positive height above Π.

**Step 7.** Because CD is perpendicular to OA, T and C have the same perpendicular projection on OA. Applying Step 4 first with P = A, and then with P = B, gives
AT² − OT² = AC² − OC² = AC² − r²,
BT² − OT² = BX² − OX² = a² − r².
The second equality uses the common projection of T and X established in Step 5; the radii equalities follow from D1.15.

**Step 8.** Construct squares on r and OT, and construct a square equal to their positive difference; let its side be h. Thus h² = r² − OT² and h > 0 (1.46, 2.14, Step 6). At T erect a perpendicular to Π and mark S on it with TS = h (11.12, P2, 1.3). By D11.3 and 1.47,
OS² = OT² + TS² = r².
If T = O, the same conclusion is immediate from OS = TS = h = r. Hence OS = r; equality of square areas entails equality of their sides by 2.4 and CN5.

**Step 9.** The triangles ATS and BTS are right-angled at T (D11.3). Therefore Step 7 and 1.47 give
AS² = AT² + TS² = AT² + r² − OT² = AC²,
BS² = BT² + TS² = BT² + r² − OT² = a².
(CN2, CN3.) Consequently AS = AC and BS = BX, again by 2.4 and CN5.

**Step 10.** Compare triangles AOS and AOC. Their three corresponding sides are equal: AO is common, OS = OC = r, and AS = AC. Hence ∠AOS = ∠AOC = β (1.8). Likewise, triangles BOS and BOX have BO common, OS = OX = r, and BS = BX; hence ∠BOS = ∠BOX = γ (1.8). The remaining angle ∠AOB is α by construction. The side-side-side proposition 1.8 is the natural comparison here because all three corresponding sides have already been established equal. Finally, TS > 0 and TS is perpendicular to Π, so S is outside Π. Since OA and OB are distinct intersecting lines in Π, the three rays OA, OB, OS cannot lie in one plane (11.3). They therefore contain a solid angle whose three plane angles are exactly α, β, γ (D11.11). The required solid angle has been constructed.
