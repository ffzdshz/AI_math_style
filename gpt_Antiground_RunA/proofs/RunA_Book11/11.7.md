## Proposition 11.7
**Statement:** If two straight lines be parallel and points be taken at random on each of them, the straight line joining the points is in the same plane with the parallel straight lines.

**Step 1.** Let l and m be the given parallel straight lines, and take arbitrary points A on l and B on m. By the definition of parallel straight lines, l and m lie in a plane Π and do not meet; in particular, B is not on l. [D1.23]

**Step 2.** Join A to B. [P1] The distinct straight lines l and AB meet at A, so they lie in a plane Σ. [11.2] Path choice: use the intersecting-lines clause of 11.2 directly, since these lines already meet; no auxiliary triangle is needed.

**Step 3.** Suppose Σ and Π were distinct. Since both contain l, they intersect, and their common section is a straight line. [11.3] This common section contains l and therefore is l itself. But B belongs to Π because it lies on m, and belongs to Σ because it lies on AB. Thus B would lie on the common section l, contradicting the fact that the parallel lines l and m do not meet. [D1.23] Therefore Σ and Π are the same plane.

**Step 4.** The joining straight line AB lies in Σ by Step 2, and hence in Π by Step 3. Since Π also contains both parallel straight lines, AB is in the same plane with them, as required. [11.2, D1.23]
