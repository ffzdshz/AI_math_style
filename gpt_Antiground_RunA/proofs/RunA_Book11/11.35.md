## Proposition 11.35
**Statement:** If there be two equal plane angles, and on their vertices there be set up elevated straight lines containing equal angles with the original straight lines respectively, if on the elevated straight lines points be taken at random and perpendiculars be drawn from them to the planes in which the original angles are, and if from the points so arising in the planes straight lines be joined to the vertices of the original angles, they will contain, with the elevated straight lines, equal angles.

**Step 1.** Let the equal plane angles have vertices A and A′ and lie in planes π and π′, with their side-rays correspondingly paired. Let P and R be the given points on the elevated rays, and let PH and RK be their perpendiculars to π and π′. Join AH and A′K, the joining lines specified in the statement. These joins lie in their respective planes. We must prove ∠PAH = ∠RA′K. Every triangle used below is planar. [Hypotheses; P1; D1.7; 11.2]

**Step 2.** First normalize the lengths of the elevated segments. Interchanging the configurations if necessary, suppose AP ≤ A′R. Take Q on A′R with A′Q = AP. If Q = R, put L = K; otherwise, in triangle A′RK draw through Q the parallel to RK, meeting the side A′K at L. Then QL is perpendicular to π′. Moreover, A′Q and A′R are the same ray, as are A′L and A′K, so ∠QA′L = ∠RA′K. It therefore suffices to prove ∠PAH = ∠QA′L. [1.3; 1.31; 11.8]

**Step 3.** Draw in π a transversal through H which does not pass through A and is not parallel to either original side-line. Let it meet those complete side-lines at B and C. On the corresponding complete side-lines in π′ take B′ and C′ with A′B′ = AB and A′C′ = AC. Choose each corresponding pair of rays simultaneously in the original directions or simultaneously in the opposite directions. [P1; P2; 1.3]

Reversing one side-ray replaces an affected angle by its supplement; reversing both sides of a plane angle preserves it as a vertically opposite angle. Consequently, the hypotheses give
∠BAC = ∠B′A′C′,
∠PAB = ∠QA′B′,
and ∠PAC = ∠QA′C′,
regardless of which extensions contain B and C. [1.13; 1.15; P4; CN3]

**Step 4.** Apply side-angle-side equality to the respective pairs of triangles ABC and A′B′C′, APB and A′QB′, and APC and A′QC′. Their corresponding sides and angles are equal. In particular,
BC = B′C′, PB = QB′, and PC = QC′,
and ∠ABC = ∠A′B′C′. [1.4]

**Step 5.** Thus triangles PBC and QB′C′ have their three corresponding sides equal, so their corresponding angles are equal. In particular, ∠PBC = ∠QB′C′ and ∠PCB = ∠QC′B′. Side-side-side equality is the natural next criterion here because all three side equalities have already been obtained. [1.8]

**Step 6.** Suppose first that H is distinct from B and C. On the complete line B′C′ take X such that B′X = BH, choosing the ray B′X toward C′ exactly when the ray BH is toward C. [P2; 1.3]

The angles ∠ABH and ∠A′B′X are therefore equal, and so are ∠PBH and ∠QB′X. Indeed, these are either the corresponding equal angles at B and B′ obtained in Steps 4–5, or the supplements of those angles. This also covers H lying on an extension of BC. [1.13; P4; CN3]

**Step 7.** Triangles ABH and A′B′X have AB = A′B′, BH = B′X, and their included angles equal; hence AH = A′X. Likewise, triangles PBH and QB′X have PB = QB′, BH = B′X, and their included angles equal; hence PH = QX and ∠PHB = ∠QXB′. [1.4]

Since PH is perpendicular to π, ∠PHB is right. Therefore QX is perpendicular to XB′. [D11.3]

**Step 8.** The three corresponding sides of triangles APH and A′QX are now equal: AP = A′Q, AH = A′X, and PH = QX. Therefore
∠PAH = ∠QA′X and ∠PHA = ∠QXA′. [1.8]

The angle ∠PHA is right because PH is perpendicular to π. Thus QX is also perpendicular to XA′. The intersecting lines XA′ and XB′ are distinct and lie in π′, since A′ is not on B′C′. Consequently QX is perpendicular to π′. [D11.3; 11.4]

**Step 9.** If instead H = B, put X = B′. The equality of triangles APB and A′QB′ gives ∠PAH = ∠QA′X and makes QX perpendicular to XA′. The equality of the corresponding angles of triangles PBC and QB′C′ makes QX perpendicular to XC′, since PH is perpendicular to BC. Hence QX is perpendicular to π′. [1.4; 1.8; D11.3; 11.4]

If H = C, put X = C′ and use triangles APC and A′QC′, together with PBC and QB′C′, in exactly the same way: ∠PAH = ∠QA′X, and QX is perpendicular to both XA′ and XB′, hence to π′. [1.4; 1.8; D11.3; 11.4]

**Step 10.** In every case, QX is perpendicular to π′ and ∠PAH = ∠QA′X. But QL is also perpendicular to π′ by Step 2. If QX and QL were distinct lines, they would be parallel, although both pass through Q; this is impossible. They therefore coincide, and their intersections X and L with π′ are the same point. [11.6; D1.23; 11.1]

It follows that ∠PAH = ∠QA′L = ∠RA′K. Thus the straight lines joining the perpendicular feet to the original vertices contain equal angles with the elevated straight lines, as required. [CN1]
