## Proposition 11.6
**Statement:** If two straight lines be at right angles to the same plane, the straight lines will be parallel.

**Step 1.** Let l and m be distinct straight lines perpendicular to the same plane Π, meeting it at A and B respectively. Each therefore makes right angles with every straight line in Π through its foot [D11.3]. Our route is to establish coplanarity using 11.5 before applying a planar parallelism criterion.

**Step 2.** The feet A and B are distinct. For otherwise l and m would determine a plane [11.2], whose common section with Π would be a straight line s through their common foot [11.3]. Both l and m would be perpendicular to s [D11.3]. Choosing rays of l and m on opposite sides of s, their adjacent angles with a fixed ray of s would be two right angles; hence those rays would form one straight line [1.14]. This contradicts the distinctness of l and m.

**Step 3.** Join AB; it lies in Π [P1, D1.7]. In Π construct AC perpendicular to AB at A, with C distinct from A [1.11]. Choose a point X on m outside Π and not on l, and join AX, CX, and BC [P1]. Each triangle so formed lies in a plane [11.2].

**Step 4.** Triangle ABC is right-angled at A by construction. Since BA and BC lie in Π, triangles XBA and XBC are right-angled at B [D1.7, D11.3]. Consequently, writing YZ² for the square on YZ, the Pythagorean theorem gives
BC² = BA² + AC²,
XC² = XB² + BC²,
and XA² = XB² + BA² [1.47].

**Step 5.** Adding equal squares to the equalities in Step 4 and substituting gives
XC² = XB² + BA² + AC² = XA² + AC² [CN2, CN1].
Therefore the angle XAC is right, by the converse of the Pythagorean theorem [1.48]. This supplies the additional perpendicular needed for the coplanarity argument.

**Step 6.** At A, the straight line AC is perpendicular to AB by construction, to l because AC lies in Π [D11.3], and to AX by Step 5. Thus the three straight lines AB, l, and AX lie in one plane, say Q [11.5].

**Step 7.** Both B and X belong to Q. Their joining segment BX therefore lies in Q [D1.7], and its whole supporting straight line m lies in Q [11.1]. Hence l and m are coplanar.

**Step 8.** In Q, the transversal AB makes right angles with both l and m [D11.3]. Its interior angles on the same side therefore sum to two right angles, so l and m are parallel [1.28]. This is the direct planar criterion because the two interior right angles are already available. Thus two distinct straight lines perpendicular to the same plane are parallel.
