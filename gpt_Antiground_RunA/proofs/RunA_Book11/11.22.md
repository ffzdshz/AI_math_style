## Proposition 11.22
**Statement:** If there be three plane angles of which two, taken together in any manner, are greater than the remaining one, and they are contained by equal straight lines, it is possible to construct a triangle out of the straight lines joining the extremities of the equal straight lines.

**Step 1.** Denote the three given angles by α, β, γ, relabelled so that γ ≥ α ≥ β. Let r be the common length of all their containing straight lines, and let a, b, c respectively be the straight lines joining their extremities, drawn by P1. By hypothesis, α + β > γ.

**Step 2.** Comparing the triangles containing the given angles gives c ≥ a ≥ b: unequal angles give the corresponding strict inequalities by 1.24, while equal angles give equal opposite sides by 1.4. This is the direct comparison because the two containing sides of every angle are already equal. Our route is to verify the side inequalities and then apply 1.22; since c is greatest, only a + b > c requires proof.

**Step 3.** If γ = α, then c = a by 1.4, and therefore a + b > c by CN5. For the remaining case, suppose γ > α.

**Step 4.** Let AOB be the given angle γ, so that OA = OB = r and AB = c. Describe the circle with centre O and radius r; it passes through A and B (P3; D1.15). Within angle AOB construct a ray making angle α with OA, and let C be its intersection with the circle (1.23). Thus ∠AOC = α and OC = r; since 0 < α < γ, the points A, B, C are distinct. Join AC and BC (D1.15; P1).

**Step 5.** The remaining angle satisfies ∠COB = γ − α < β, by α + β > γ and removal of the common angle α (CN3; CN5). Comparing triangle AOC with the given triangle containing α gives AC = a by 1.4. Comparing triangle BOC with the given triangle containing β gives BC < b by 1.24, since their corresponding containing sides are all equal to r.

**Step 6.** The points A, B, C are noncollinear: otherwise the middle one would lie inside the circle by 3.2 applied to the outer two, although it lies on the circumference. Hence 1.20 applies to triangle ABC and gives AB < AC + BC. Together with Step 5, this yields c = AB < AC + BC < a + b. Thus a + b > c also in the case γ > α (1.20; CN5).

**Step 7.** In both cases, a + b > c. Since c ≥ a ≥ b and every given joining segment is positive, c + b > a and c + a > b as well (CN5). Therefore any two of a, b, c together exceed the remaining one. By 1.22, a triangle can be constructed with sides equal to a, b, c—that is, out of the straight lines joining the extremities of the given equal straight lines. This proves the proposition.
