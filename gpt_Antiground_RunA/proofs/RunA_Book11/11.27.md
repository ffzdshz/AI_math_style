## Proposition 11.27
**Statement:** On a given straight line to describe a parallelepipedal solid similar and similarly situated to a given parallelepipedal solid.

**Step 1.** Let AB be the given straight line, and let OP, OQ, OR be the three edges meeting at a vertex O of the given parallelepiped. By 11.26 construct at A, on AB, a solid angle equal to the solid angle at O, with AB corresponding to OP and its other two rays corresponding respectively to OQ and OR. Thus the three corresponding plane angles are equal. This is the direct construction available here, since the whole solid angle is given; it need not be reconstructed from its separate plane angles.

**Step 2.** By 6.12 construct lengths c and d such that
OP : AB = OQ : c, and OP : AB = OR : d.
On the two remaining rays of the copied solid angle mark C and D so that AC = c and AD = d, producing the rays and cutting off the required lengths as necessary (P2, 1.3). Consequently
OP : AB = OQ : AC = OR : AD,
where the equality of the last two ratios follows from 5.11. Moreover, by the construction in Step 1,
∠BAC = ∠POQ, ∠BAD = ∠POR, and ∠CAD = ∠QOR.

**Step 3.** Complete the solid on the three edges AB, AC, AD as follows. Through B draw, in the planes ABC and ABD, straight lines parallel respectively to AC and AD (1.31). These intersecting lines determine a plane (11.2), which is parallel to ACD by 11.15. Similarly construct through C a plane parallel to ABD, and through D a plane parallel to ABC. These three planes, together with ABC, ABD, ACD, enclose a parallelepiped S having AB, AC, AD as its edges at A. Its opposite faces are equal and parallelogrammic by 11.24.

**Step 4.** Compare the three faces of S through A with the corresponding three faces of the given solid through O. All are parallelograms (11.24). In each corresponding pair, the angles at A and O are equal by Step 1. The remaining angles therefore agree also: adjacent angles of a parallelogram are supplementary, and opposite angles are equal (1.29, 1.34). Their corresponding sides are proportional by Step 2, since opposite sides of each parallelogram are equal (1.34). Hence each of these three pairs of faces consists of similar rectilineal figures (D6.1).

**Step 5.** In either solid, the sides of a face opposite one of these three faces are respectively parallel and equal to the corresponding sides of that face: the intervening faces are parallelograms, whose opposite sides are parallel and equal (11.24, 1.34). The corresponding angles of these opposite faces are therefore equal to those of the faces already considered, by 11.10. Thus the remaining three pairs of faces inherit the equal corresponding angles and proportional corresponding sides established in Step 4. They too are similar (D6.1).

**Step 6.** The two solids are consequently contained by six respectively similar faces, and are therefore similar solid figures (D11.9). They are also similarly situated: AB, AC, AD correspond in order to OP, OQ, OR in the equal solid angles constructed by 11.26, and the parallel-plane completion of Step 3 preserves this correspondence for the incident and opposite faces. Thus on the given straight line AB a parallelepipedal solid similar and similarly situated to the given parallelepipedal solid has been described.
