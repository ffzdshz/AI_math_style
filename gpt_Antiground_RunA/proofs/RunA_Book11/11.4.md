## Proposition 11.4
**Statement:** If a straight line be set up at right angles to two straight lines which cut one another, at their common point of section, it will also be at right angles to the plane through them.

**Step 1.** Let the distinct straight lines a and b meet at O in the plane Π, and let p be perpendicular to both at O. The plane through a and b exists by 11.2. The line p cannot lie in Π: otherwise, choosing rays of a and b on opposite sides of p, their adjacent right angles with a ray of p would make those rays one straight line by 1.14, contradicting the distinctness of a and b. Moreover, p meets Π only at O; a second common point would put a segment of p in Π (D1.7), and hence the whole line there (11.1).

**Step 2.** Choose P on p, distinct from O, and produce PO through O to Q so that OQ = OP (P2, 1.3). Path choice: we shall use triangle congruence to obtain equal adjacent angles directly, rather than introduce squares of lengths.

**Step 3.** Let t be any straight line through O in Π. If t is a or b, its perpendicularity to p is given. Otherwise, choose a ray of t and points A on a and B on b on the two boundary rays of the sector containing it; join AB (P1). The chosen ray meets the interior of AB at a point T. Indeed, each base angle of triangle OAB together with angle AOB is less than two right angles (1.17). Each of the two angles into which the chosen ray divides angle AOB is smaller than the whole (CN5). Applying P5 with OA and then OB as transversal therefore gives the intersection on both ray AB and ray BA, so T lies between A and B.

**Step 4.** Join PA, QA, PB, QB, PT, and QT (P1). Every triangle used here is planar by 11.2. For X equal to A or B, the angles POX and QOX are right by the hypothesis and D1.10, and hence equal by P4. Since OP = OQ and OX is common, triangles POX and QOX are congruent by 1.4. Consequently PA = QA and PB = QB.

**Step 5.** Triangles PAB and QAB have PA = QA, PB = QB, and AB common. Therefore angle PAB equals angle QAB by 1.8. Since T lies between A and B, these are respectively angles PAT and QAT; thus angle PAT equals angle QAT.

**Step 6.** In triangles PAT and QAT, PA = QA, AT is common, and the included angles PAT and QAT are equal. Hence PT = QT by 1.4.

**Step 7.** Triangles POT and QOT now have OP = OQ, PT = QT, and OT common. Therefore angle POT equals angle QOT by 1.8. Since OP and OQ are opposite rays, these equal angles are adjacent angles on the straight line PQ. Each is consequently right by D1.10. Thus p is perpendicular to t.

**Step 8.** Every straight line in Π meeting p meets it at O, by Step 1, and every such line is perpendicular to p, by the hypothesis or Steps 3–7. Therefore p is at right angles to Π by D11.3, as required.
