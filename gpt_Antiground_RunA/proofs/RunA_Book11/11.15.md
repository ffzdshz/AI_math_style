## Proposition 11.15
**Statement:** If two straight lines meeting one another be parallel to two straight lines meeting one another, not being in the same plane, the planes through them are parallel.

**Step 1.** Let the straight lines a and b meet at A, and let c and d meet at B, with a parallel to c and b parallel to d. Let Π and Σ be their respective planes, which exist by 11.2 and are distinct by hypothesis. We choose the common-perpendicular route: we shall exhibit a straight line perpendicular to both planes and apply 11.14.

**Step 2.** The point B is outside Π. For suppose B belonged to Π. Since c is parallel to a and d is parallel to b, B lies on neither a nor b [D1.23]. The plane containing the parallel lines a and c would then share with Π both the line a and the point B outside a. These planes must coincide, since otherwise their common section would be a single straight line [11.3]. Thus c lies in Π; the same argument shows that d lies in Π. Consequently Π and Σ would contain both intersecting lines c and d and would therefore coincide, again by 11.3. This contradicts the hypothesis.

**Step 3.** Draw BH perpendicular to Π, with H in Π [11.11]. Thus BH makes right angles with every straight line in Π passing through H [D11.3].

**Step 4.** If H lies on a, take r to be a itself; otherwise, through H draw r in Π parallel to a. Similarly, take s to be b itself if H lies on b, and otherwise draw s through H in Π parallel to b [1.31].

**Step 5.** We have r parallel to c. This is the hypothesis if r equals a. Otherwise r and a are distinct parallel lines in Π, whereas c contains B outside Π; hence these three lines are not coplanar. Since r and c are both parallel to a, the spatial parallel-transitivity theorem 11.9 applies and gives r parallel to c. The identical argument gives s parallel to d [11.9].

**Step 6.** Since H and B lie respectively on the parallel lines r and c, the joining straight line BH lies in their plane [11.7]. Its angles with r at H are right angles [D11.3]; therefore its angles with c at B are also right angles [1.29]. Applying the same argument to the parallel lines s and d shows that BH also makes right angles with d at B [11.7, D11.3, 1.29].

**Step 7.** Thus BH is at right angles to both c and d at their common point B. It is therefore perpendicular to their plane Σ [11.4].

**Step 8.** The same straight line BH is perpendicular to Π by construction and to Σ by Step 7. Hence Π and Σ are parallel [11.14], as required.
