## Proposition 11.20
**Statement:** If a solid angle be contained by three plane angles, any two, taken together in any manner, are greater than the remaining one.

**Step 1.** Let the solid angle have vertex O and three noncoplanar edges in the directions OA, OB, OC. Write α = ∠AOB, β = ∠AOC, and γ = ∠BOC; take α as an arbitrary one of the three plane angles (D11.11). We shall prove β + γ > α. If β ≥ α, then β + γ > β ≥ α, since the whole is greater than its part (CN5). Thus it remains to consider α > β. Our chosen construction copies β inside α.

**Step 2.** Choose points A and B on the first two edges and join AB (P1). The intersecting lines OA and OB lie in one plane (11.2). In that plane construct a ray OD inside ∠AOB such that ∠AOD = β (1.23). Since 0 < β < α, this ray meets AB at a point D strictly between A and B.

**Step 3.** On the third edge choose C such that OC = OD, producing the edge first if necessary (P2, 1.3). Join AC and BC (P1). The noncoplanarity of the three edges ensures that A, B, C are not collinear; thus ABC is a nondegenerate triangle, and it lies in one plane (D11.11, 11.2).

**Step 4.** In triangles OAD and OAC, OA is common, OD = OC, and ∠AOD = ∠AOC by construction. Therefore their bases are equal: AD = AC (1.4).

**Step 5.** In triangle ABC, AC + BC > AB (1.20). Since D lies between A and B, AB = AD + DB. Substituting AC = AD and subtracting the equal lengths gives BC > DB (CN3).

**Step 6.** Compare triangles BOC and BOD. They have BO common and OC = OD, while their third sides satisfy BC > BD. Consequently ∠BOC > ∠BOD (1.25). This proposition applies directly because the comparison of the third sides has already been established.

**Step 7.** Add ∠AOC = ∠AOD to this strict inequality. Since OD lies inside ∠AOB, it follows that
∠AOC + ∠BOC > ∠AOD + ∠DOB = ∠AOB
(CN2, CN5). Thus β + γ > α. Because α was an arbitrary one of the three plane angles, any two of them, taken together in any manner, are greater than the remaining one.
