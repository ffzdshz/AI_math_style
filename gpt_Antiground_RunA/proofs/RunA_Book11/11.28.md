## Proposition 11.28
**Statement:** If a parallelepipedal solid be cut by a plane through the diagonals of the opposite planes, the solid will be bisected by the plane.

**Step 1.** Let the opposite faces of the parallelepiped be ABCD and EFGH, with joining edges AE, BF, CG, DH. Let the cutting plane pass through the diagonals AC and EG. By 11.24, all six faces are parallelograms, and opposite faces are equal. We shall use the direct boundary-face criterion of D11.10: compare the faces bounding the two parts.

**Step 2.** Since A and E lie in the cutting plane, the edge AE lies in it; similarly CG lies in it (11.1). The intersections of the cutting plane with the original faces are straight lines (11.3), so the section is the quadrilateral ACGE. The two parts therefore have the following boundary faces:
• first part: ABC, EFG, ABFE, BCGF, ACGE;
• second part: CDA, GHE, DCGH, ADHE, ACGE.
Here each diagonal divides its parallelogram face into the two indicated triangles (1.34).

**Step 3.** In parallelogram ABCD, the triangles ABC and CDA are equal, since its diagonal bisects it; moreover AB = CD and BC = DA (1.34). Their third side AC is common (CN4). Thus their corresponding angles are equal by 1.8, and, since their corresponding sides are equal and therefore proportional, they are also similar (D6.1). The same argument in parallelogram EFGH shows that triangles EFG and GHE are equal and similar (1.34, 1.8, D6.1).

**Step 4.** The faces ABFE and DCGH are opposite faces, as are BCGF and ADHE; hence each pair is equal (11.24). In each pair, corresponding sides are equal and parallel, being opposite sides of the adjoining parallelogram faces (1.34). Their corresponding angles are consequently equal by 11.10. Thus each pair consists of similar figures as well, since corresponding sides are equal and therefore proportional (D6.1).

**Step 5.** Finally, ACGE is the same face in both boundaries, so it is equal and similar to itself (CN4, D6.1). Consequently the two parts are bounded by five faces apiece, paired as equal and similar figures by Steps 3–4 and this common face. The two solids are therefore equal by D11.10. Since they are precisely the two parts into which the given plane cuts the parallelepiped, that plane bisects the solid.
