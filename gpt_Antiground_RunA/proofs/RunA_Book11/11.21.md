## Proposition 11.21
**Statement:** Any solid angle is contained by plane angles less than four right angles.

**Step 1.** Let S be the vertex of the given solid angle. Cut off its convex corner by a plane not through S, meeting its edge-rays, in cyclic order, at A₁, …, Aₙ, where n ≥ 3. The section is a convex polygon: its sides are the straight intersections of the cutting plane with the successive face-planes. The cut-off solid is a pyramid, and its lateral triangles SAᵢAᵢ₊₁ contain at S precisely the plane angles in question. Indices are understood cyclically. We use this section so that the direct comparison in 11.20 applies at every base vertex, treating all numbers of faces together. [D11.11, D11.12, 11.3]

**Step 2.** Let R denote a right angle, and let βᵢ = ∠Aᵢ₋₁AᵢAᵢ₊₁ be the interior angle of the section at Aᵢ. Draw the diagonals from A₁ to its nonadjacent vertices. They divide the convex section into n − 2 triangles, whose angles together constitute exactly the interior angles of the polygon. Applying the triangle-angle sum and adding gives
B := ∑ᵢ₌₁ⁿ βᵢ = 2(n − 2)R.
This uses 1.32 directly because the section has been divided into triangles. [P1, 1.32, CN2]

**Step 3.** At each Aᵢ, the three rays AᵢAᵢ₋₁, AᵢS, and AᵢAᵢ₊₁ form a solid angle contained by three plane angles: the two base rays are noncollinear in the section plane, whereas S is outside that plane. Hence 11.20 gives
∠Aᵢ₋₁AᵢS + ∠SAᵢAᵢ₊₁ > βᵢ.
Let T be the sum of the expressions on the left over all i. Adding these strict inequalities yields T > B; thus T = B + E for some positive angular excess E. [D11.11, 11.20, CN2, CN5]

**Step 4.** Put F := ∑ᵢ₌₁ⁿ ∠AᵢSAᵢ₊₁, the sum of the plane angles containing the original solid angle. The n lateral triangles have total angle sum 2nR. Their angles at S sum to F, while their remaining angles sum to T, each occurring exactly once. Therefore
F + T = 2nR.
[1.32, CN2]

**Step 5.** Substitute T = B + E and B = 2(n − 2)R into the preceding equality. It follows that
F + 2(n − 2)R + E = 2nR.
Subtracting the common magnitude 2(n − 2)R gives F + E = 4R. Since E is positive, F is a proper part of four right angles and therefore F < 4R. Thus any solid angle is contained by plane angles whose sum is less than four right angles. [CN1, CN3, CN5]
