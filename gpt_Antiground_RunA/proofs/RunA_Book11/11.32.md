## Proposition 11.32
**Statement:** Parallelepipedal solids which are of the same height are to one another as their bases.

**Step 1.** Let S and T be parallelepipedal solids with respective bases P = ABCD and Q, and with the same height h. We shall construct, adjacent to S, a solid U whose base equals Q. The equal-base theorem 11.31 will then identify U with T in magnitude, while the single-cut theorem 11.25 will give the required ratio directly.

**Step 2.** Divide Q into two triangles by drawing a diagonal (P1). Produce AB beyond B and DC beyond C (P2). Apply the first triangle to BC as an equal parallelogram BGHC, outside ABCD, with BG along AB produced. Then apply the second triangle to GH as an equal parallelogram GEFH, beyond the first, with GE along BG produced (1.44, applied twice). Thus B, G, E are collinear, C, H, F are collinear, and BC, GH, EF are parallel. Consequently their union R = BEFC is a parallelogram, and AEFD is also a parallelogram (1.30). Since the two applied parallelograms equal the two triangles constituting Q, R = Q (CN2).

**Step 3.** Extend S in the direction AB to a parallelepiped W on the enlarged base AEFD, keeping its upper face in the plane of the original upper face. Explicitly, extend the corresponding edges of the upper face and draw through E and F lines parallel to the original rising edges (P2, 1.31). The new end-face through EF is parallel to the original end-face through BC, since their two intersecting edge-directions are respectively parallel (11.15). Let U be the part of W on BEFC. The original side-face through BC separates W into S and U and is parallel to W's opposite end-faces. Moreover, S and U lie between the same lower and upper planes, so U has height h.

**Step 4.** Apply 11.25 to this division of W. Its two base portions are P and R, and its two solid portions are S and U. Therefore
P : R = S : U.

**Step 5.** Since R = Q and U and T have the same height h, the equal-base theorem gives U = T (11.31). Equal magnitudes may replace one another in these ratios (5.7), and ratios equal to the same ratio are equal (5.11). Hence
S : T = P : Q.
Therefore parallelepipedal solids of the same height are to one another as their bases.
