## Proposition 11.31
**Statement:** Parallelepipedal solids which are on equal bases and of the same height are equal to one another.

**Step 1.** Let S and T be parallelepipedal solids on equal bases and of the same perpendicular height h. Their bases are parallelograms by 11.24; height is understood as in D6.4. We shall reduce the solids to rectangular parallelepipeds and then compare them by cuts to which 11.25 applies.

**Step 2.** On each original base construct a right parallelepiped of height h. For this, erect a perpendicular to the base at one vertex (11.12), mark off length h on it (P2, 1.3), and complete the solid by parallels (1.31). The lateral edges are equal by 1.34 and are all perpendicular to the base by 11.8; the opposite face planes are parallel by 11.15. Denote these right parallelepipeds by S₀ and T₀.

**Step 3.** Since S and S₀ have the same base and height, they are equal; likewise T and T₀ are equal. Here 11.29 applies when the upper extremities lie on the same straight lines, and 11.30 applies in the complementary case. Thus S = S₀ and T = T₀. This uses the earlier 11.29 whenever its additional alignment hypothesis holds.

**Step 4.** Consider the base ABCD of S₀, with vertices in cyclic order. Draw through A a perpendicular to AB, meeting DC produced if necessary at F; through B draw its parallel, meeting the same line at G (1.11, 1.31, P2). Since AB is parallel to DC, ABGF is a rectangle by 1.29. The parallelograms ABCD and ABGF have the same base AB and lie between the same parallels AB and DC; hence they are equal by 1.35. Keeping the perpendicular edge at A, construct the right parallelepiped X on ABGF of height h by the construction of Step 2.

**Step 5.** Write A₁, B₁, C₁, D₁ for the upper vertices of S₀, and F₁, G₁ for the upper vertices above F, G in X. Regard S₀ and X as standing on their common rectangular face ABB₁A₁. Their opposite faces lie in the same plane through DC and D₁C₁, which is parallel to the common face by 11.15; consequently their heights relative to that face are equal. Moreover, D, C, F, G lie on the same straight line, and D₁, C₁, F₁, G₁ lie on the same straight line. Therefore S₀ = X by 11.29. Perform the same construction on T₀, obtaining a rectangular parallelepiped Y with T₀ = Y. By Step 4, 1.35, and CN1, the rectangular bases of X and Y are equal. Both solids still have height h.

**Step 6.** Let the adjacent sides of the rectangular base of X have lengths a, b, and those of the rectangular base of Y have lengths c, d. The rectangles are equiangular, since all right angles are equal (P4). Their equality therefore gives the reciprocal proportion

a : c = d : b

by 6.14.

**Step 7.** Construct a rectangular parallelepiped Z with perpendicular edge lengths c, b, h, using the construction of Step 2. Place X and Z together along their equal b-by-h faces, with their interiors on opposite sides; such placement does not change either solid (CN4). Their union is a rectangular parallelepiped, and the common face cuts it by a plane parallel to two opposite faces. Thus 11.25 gives

X : Z = (rectangle with sides a, b) : (rectangle with sides c, b).

These rectangles have the same height b, so 6.1 gives

X : Z = a : c.

**Step 8.** Similarly, place Y beside Z along their equal c-by-h faces (CN4). Their union is again a rectangular parallelepiped cut by the common face parallel to two opposite faces. By 11.25,

Y : Z = (rectangle with sides c, d) : (rectangle with sides c, b).

Taking d and b as the bases of these rectangles, their common height is c. Hence, by 6.1,

Y : Z = d : b.

**Step 9.** The proportion in Step 6 and the ratios in Steps 7–8 imply X : Z = Y : Z by 5.11. Magnitudes having the same ratio to the same magnitude are equal; therefore X = Y by 5.9.

**Step 10.** We have S = S₀ = X and T = T₀ = Y, while X = Y. Hence S = T by CN1. Therefore parallelepipedal solids on equal bases and of the same height are equal to one another.
