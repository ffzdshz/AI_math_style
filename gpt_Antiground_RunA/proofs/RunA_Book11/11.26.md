## Proposition 11.26
**Statement:** On a given straight line, and at a given point on it, to construct a solid angle equal to a given solid angle.

**Step 1.** Let A be the given point on the given straight line l. Write the bounding rays of the given solid angle as OU₁, OU₂, …, OUₘ. Choose the numbering so that OU₁ and OU₂ are not collinear and U₃ lies outside their plane Π; this is possible because the given angle is solid. Choose a plane Σ through l and choose one ray of l issuing from A. The required solid angle will have this ray as a bounding edge. [D11.11, P1, 11.2]

**Step 2.** On the chosen ray mark B₁ so that AB₁ = OU₁. In Σ construct the ray AB₂ so that ∠B₁AB₂ = ∠U₁OU₂, and mark B₂ so that AB₂ = OU₂. Extend the rays first if necessary. The triangles AB₁B₂ and OU₁U₂ are congruent by side-angle-side. Our construction will copy this planar frame and then restore perpendicular heights; thus the prescribed edge remains fixed throughout. Side-angle-side is the natural criterion here because precisely two sides and their included angle have been copied. [P2, 1.3, 1.23, 1.4]

**Step 3.** For each Uᵢ outside Π, draw UᵢHᵢ perpendicular to Π, with Hᵢ in Π. For each Uᵢ already in Π, put Hᵢ = Uᵢ. In particular, H₁ = U₁ and H₂ = U₂, while U₃H₃ is a nonzero perpendicular height. [11.11]

**Step 4.** Copy every Hᵢ into Σ as a point Kᵢ as follows. If Hᵢ is not on the straight line OU₁, construct ∠B₁AKᵢ = ∠U₁OHᵢ and take AKᵢ = OHᵢ. Choose Kᵢ on the same side of AB₁ as B₂ exactly when Hᵢ is on the same side of OU₁ as U₂. If Hᵢ lies on the straight line OU₁ but differs from O, mark the equal distance on the corresponding forward or opposite ray of l. If Hᵢ = O, put Kᵢ = A. Thus K₁ = B₁ and K₂ = B₂. [1.23, 1.3, P2]

**Step 5.** These constructions give one congruent copy of the entire planar configuration of the feet. Indeed, superpose the congruent base triangles AB₁B₂ and OU₁U₂, matching A with O and B₁, B₂ with U₁, U₂. Each ray AKᵢ then coincides with OHᵢ: on a specified side of a baseline, two distinct rays cannot make the same angle with that baseline, since one such angle would be a proper part of the other. The equal distances AKᵢ = OHᵢ consequently make their endpoints coincide. The points on the baseline coincide for the same reason applied to lengths. This also covers feet outside the original face angle. [1.4, CN5]

**Step 6.** Fix corresponding sides of Π and Σ in this planar comparison. Whenever Uᵢ lies outside Π, erect at Kᵢ a perpendicular to Σ and mark on it a point Vᵢ such that KᵢVᵢ = HᵢUᵢ, choosing the side corresponding to the side of Π containing Uᵢ. Whenever Uᵢ lies in Π, put Vᵢ = Kᵢ. In particular, V₁ = B₁ and V₂ = B₂. All these operations are constructions of perpendiculars and copies of given lengths. [11.12, P2, 1.3]

**Step 7.** Join A to every Vᵢ. The rays AV₁ and AV₂ are distinct rays in Σ, while V₃ lies outside Σ because its perpendicular height is nonzero. No other plane can contain both AV₁ and AV₂: otherwise its intersection with Σ would contain two intersecting straight lines, whereas the intersection of distinct planes is a straight line. Thus the constructed rays are not all coplanar and constitute a solid angle at A, with AV₁ on the prescribed line l. [P1, 11.12, 11.3, D11.11]

**Step 8.** To verify equality, use the planar superposition established in Step 5, with the chosen sides corresponding. Every Kᵢ then coincides with Hᵢ. For each lifted point, the perpendicular rays KᵢVᵢ and HᵢUᵢ coincide, since two perpendiculars to the same plane cannot issue from the same point on the same side. Their equal lengths therefore make Vᵢ coincide with Uᵢ. The points lying in the base planes already coincide. Consequently every constructed bounding ray AVᵢ coincides with its corresponding original ray OUᵢ, and the whole solid angles coincide and are equal. Hence, on the given straight line and at its given point, a solid angle equal to the given solid angle has been constructed. [11.13, CN5, CN4, D11.11]
